# The Three Formative Institutions

This is a working reference for the `renewing-the-mind` skill. It examines three institutions that train patterns of thought. The three are the school system, social media, and television with advertising. The file examines them by mechanism, not by slogan. It pairs each mechanism with the biblical category that names it, a counter-practice, and an honest note on what is contested.

## How to read this file

A common claim is that the school system is "evil and not based on first principles." This file examines that claim. It does not repeat it. Part of the claim is about mechanisms, and you can check a mechanism. Part of the claim is about moral status, which is a different kind of claim. This file keeps the two apart.

Three rules govern the analysis:

1. Mechanism before verdict. "Schools remove the fear of the LORD from the foundation of knowledge" is a claim about a mechanism, and you can check it. "Schools are evil" is a verdict about the whole institution, its intent, and its people. You can argue the first from texts and practice. The second needs more than this file can supply, and the texts do not pronounce it.
2. The texts do not mention these institutions. The school in its modern compulsory form, the algorithmic feed, and broadcast advertising are all recent. The Bible does not name them. It names categories: the fear of the LORD as the foundation of knowledge, the parent as the primary teacher, worth measured by God and not by peers, and the guarded eye. The application of those categories to these institutions is Inference, and this file labels it so.
3. Empirical claims carry a label. Several mechanisms below rest on empirical claims about how these institutions work and what they produce. Where honest researchers disagree, the claim is marked Contested. The tools in this skill cannot settle those claims. A reader who wants to reject a claim must be able to see which claim it is.

## Quotation conventions

KJV wording is quoted exactly as `scripture.ts` returns it. The source data uses curly apostrophes, and this file shows them as straight apostrophes. The wording, punctuation, and capitalization are otherwise not changed. Strong's definitions are quoted verbatim from the output of `lexicon.ts`.

## A note on tone

This file is not an argument for contempt toward teachers, students, parents, journalists, engineers, or advertisers. Each institution below is made of people who do work they believe in. The author of this file is also subject to each mechanism described here. The purpose is to name what trains the mind, so that a person can choose the training and not absorb it.

# 1. The school system

The institution under examination is compulsory, age-graded, state-regulated schooling. In this system, the law requires children to attend an institution for a fixed number of years. The system groups them by birth year, teaches them a standardized curriculum, and assesses them by comparison to their cohort. This is a modern arrangement. It is not the same thing as education, and it is not the same thing as teaching. Much of what follows applies to the structure of the system, not to a particular teacher or lesson.

The common claim is that the system is evil and not based on first principles. The mechanisms below are what that claim must rest on. This section examines each one for what the text actually names, and it gives the verdict at the end.

## The mechanisms

### M1. Knowledge is defined without the fear of the LORD

- Mechanism: The foundation of knowledge moves. The institution defines knowledge as what it can measure, test, and certify, with no reference to God. The question "what is this for, and before whom must it answer?" is not part of the curriculum. In many jurisdictions it cannot be.
- Biblical category: The fear of the LORD is not one subject among others. Proverbs 1:7 calls it the beginning of knowledge, and Proverbs 9:10 calls it the beginning of wisdom. Proverbs 2 makes the sequence explicit. The seeker searches for wisdom as for hidden treasure, and "then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God."
- Text: Proverbs 1:7: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction." Proverbs 9:10: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding." Proverbs 2:5: "Then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God."
- Counter-practice: Put the fear of the LORD back at the foundation. Do not add it as a devotional extra. Make it the frame that makes knowledge answerable. In practice, ask of any subject what it is for and to whom it must give account. Make that question part of the teaching.
- Labels: Text states: the Proverbs. Lexical: in Proverbs 1:7, "beginning" is H7225 _reshith_, "the first, in place, time, order or rank (specifically, a firstfruit)." In Proverbs 9:10, "beginning" is H8462 _techillah_, "a commencement." H1847 _daath_ is "knowledge." H3374 _yirah_ is "fear (also used as infinitive); morally, reverence." Inference: in Proverbs 1:7, _reshith_ can mean first in rank as well as first in time. On that reading, the fear of the LORD is the governing element of knowledge, not only its opening lesson. The word in Proverbs 9:10 does not carry the rank sense. Inference: a curriculum that excludes God from the foundation is the pattern that Proverbs warns against. The Proverbs do not mention curricula.
- Contested: Two questions are contested. The first is whether a given school system in fact excludes God from its foundation. The second is whether it can include him within legal limits. The answer changes by jurisdiction and by school. Some schools, including religious schools, do the opposite.

### M2. Compulsory attendance trains compliance as a virtue

- Mechanism: The law requires attendance, whatever the content or quality of the instruction. The task of the child is to be present and compliant. The habit that forms is obedience to the requirement itself, with no judgment about whether the requirement is good.
- Biblical category: Scripture treats obedience as obedience to a word, and a word has content. Saul obeyed in part. Samuel gave the verdict that obedience is measured against what God said, not against the fact that something was done. A requirement with no content cannot be obeyed in this sense. A person can only comply with it.
- Text: 1 Samuel 15:22: "And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams." Matthew 15:3: "But he answered and said unto them, Why do ye also transgress the commandment of God by your tradition?"
- Counter-practice: Make the requirement answerable. Use the tested authority of Proverbs. Let people examine a claim and hear its reasons, and let the child learn to obey a reason, not a bell. Proverbs 18:17 applies to institutions as much as to lawsuits. The first account seems just until someone searches it.
- Labels: Inference: compulsory attendance forms compliance as a disposition. The texts do not discuss attendance laws. Contested: the strength of the effect, and whether other features of schooling offset it. Rules, sport, and cooperative work are examples of such features.

### M3. Age segregation by birth year removes generational transmission

- Mechanism: The system groups children by date of birth. So almost all of the people that a child spends the day with are the same age. The older and the younger are not in the room. The social world of the child becomes a cohort, not a household or a community.
- Biblical category: Faith and skill pass from generation to generation, inside the household. The parent teaches the child, and the child learns by being present while the work is done. Psalm 78 states the purpose of the chain. The next generation must set their hope in God and not forget his works.
- Text: Deuteronomy 6:6-7: "And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up." Psalm 78:6-7: "That the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to their children: That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments:"
- Counter-practice: Use mixed-age work and apprenticeship. Put the learner in the room with the practitioner, at whatever age the work requires. The household is the primary school. Deuteronomy 6:6-7 speaks to the parent directly, not to an institution. Proverbs 22:6 frames the task as the training of a child in the way he must go.
- Labels: Text states: the commands to the parent. Inference: age-graded schooling displaces the commanded transmission. The texts do not describe schools. Contested: whether age-graded settings do harm on balance, and whether mixed-age settings can work at scale. The research literature does not give a clean answer.

### M4. Grading on a curve makes worth relative to classmates

- Mechanism: Some assessment is norm-referenced, which means that it ranks a student against the cohort. There, the measured worth of a student depends on the performance of other students. When one student improves, another student can lose rank. The standard is the group, and the institution enforces it.
- Biblical category: Paul names the practice and states a verdict. To measure yourself by yourself and to compare among yourselves is "not wise." The same passage gives the alternative: a measure that God distributes, and commendation from the Lord.
- Text: 2 Corinthians 10:12: "For we dare not make ourselves of the number, or compare ourselves with some that commend themselves: but they measuring themselves by themselves, and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise." 2 Corinthians 10:18: "For not he that commendeth himself is approved, but whom the Lord commendeth." Proverbs 21:2: "Every way of a man is right in his own eyes: but the LORD pondereth the hearts."
- Counter-practice: Use criterion-referenced work. Define the standard by what the student must produce, and let every student who meets it succeed. Where a ranking is necessary, call it a game and not a measure of worth.
- Labels: Text states: 2 Corinthians 10:12. Inference: norm-referenced grading is the practice that Paul describes. Contested: how common norm-referencing is today. Many systems use criterion-referenced grading or standards-based assessment. The "curve" belongs more to some university settings than to all schooling. This mechanism is real where it exists, but it is not universal.

### M5. The bell schedule trains obedience and punctuality, not judgment

- Mechanism: An external signal divides the day into fixed periods. Work stops and starts at the bell, whether or not the task is finished and whether or not the learner is engaged. The habit that forms is a response to the schedule, not to the work.
- Biblical category: In Scripture, wisdom works through judgment. The king must judge. The prudent man looks well to his going. The thoughts of the diligent tend to plenty. Proverbs 27:23 says, "Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds." Inference: the person who knows the state of the work can judge when to act.
- Text: Proverbs 21:5: "The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness; but of every one that is hasty only to want." Proverbs 27:12: "A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself; but the simple pass on, and are punished." Proverbs 27:23: "Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds." Isaiah 28:16: "...he that believeth shall not make haste."
- Counter-practice: Where you can, work until the task is complete. Let the task and the judgment set the interval. Where a schedule is necessary, make its reasons visible. Then people obey the schedule as a decision and not as a signal.
- Labels: Inference: the bell schedule trains a response to the schedule over judgment. Inference: Isaiah 28:16 speaks of the one who believes in the foundation stone. This file uses "shall not make haste" only as a loose parallel. The verse does not speak about schedules. Contested: the historical claim that the design of schooling copied the factory. That story is widely repeated and is too simple. The history of mass schooling is complex, and historians of education dispute the "Prussian factory model" narrative. You can argue the mechanism from the present structure without the historical claim.

### M6. Authority is procedural, not earned

- Mechanism: Authority attaches to the role. The procedure that assigns the role validates it. That procedure is the credential, the appointment, or the policy, and not shown competence or character. The authority of a teacher rests on position, not on trust that the teacher earned.
- Biblical category: Christ makes exactly this charge against the tradition of the elders. A human procedure takes the place of the commandment, and the result is worship in vain. The category is authority that cannot give an account of itself.
- Text: Matthew 15:8-9: "This people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me. But in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men." Isaiah 29:13: "Wherefore the Lord said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men:"
- Counter-practice: Use the tested authority of Proverbs. This is authority that invites examination and survives it. Paul can say "be ye followers of me" because people can inspect his conduct (1 Corinthians 11:1). That authority is earned and displayed. A procedure does not confer it.
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Inference: credentials and policy are procedural authority in this sense. Contested: whether procedural authority is illegitimate in itself. Every institution needs some procedure. The target of the text is procedure against the commandment, not procedure as such.

### M7. The mandate of the parent is outsourced to an institution

- Mechanism: The duty to teach the child moves to someone else. The parent delegates the substance of formation to a system. The role of the parent becomes the supervision of attendance and of compliance with the requirements of the system.
- Biblical category: The command speaks to the parent, in the second person, and it is continuous. It covers sitting, walking, lying down, and rising. A parent cannot discharge it by hiring an agent. Psalm 78 states the same chain. The fathers make the works of God known to the children.
- Text: Deuteronomy 6:6-7: "And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children..." Psalm 78:4-5: "We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the LORD, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done. For he established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children:" Deuteronomy 6:9: "And thou shalt write them upon the posts of thy house, and on thy gates." Ephesians 6:4: "And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord."
- Counter-practice: Make the household the primary school. Whatever other instruction a child receives, the parent keeps the substance of formation and the responsibility for it. Deuteronomy 11:18-19 repeats the command. Proverbs 3:3-4 states the promise attached: "Let not mercy and truth forsake thee: bind them about thy neck; write them upon the table of thine heart: So shalt thou find favour and good understanding in the sight of God and man."
- Labels: Text states: the commands to parents. Inference: to delegate the substance of formation goes against the address of the command. Contested: whether any delegation is permitted, and how a household that must work can do this. The text does not address teachers outside the household, and honest readers differ.

## What is contested

The mechanisms above divide into three kinds of claim. Honesty requires that you keep them apart.

Named by the text as categories: The fear of the LORD as the foundation of knowledge (Proverbs 1:7, 9:10, 2:5). The parent as the primary teacher (Deuteronomy 6:6-7). Worth measured by God and not by the cohort (2 Corinthians 10:12, Proverbs 21:2). Obedience as obedience to a word with content (1 Samuel 15:22). Authority that people can examine (Proverbs 18:17, 1 Thessalonians 5:21). These are Text states.

Inferences about the institution: These are the claims that a given school system does seven things. It removes God from the foundation. It trains compliance. It displaces generational transmission. It ranks students against each other. It forms a response to the schedule. It validates authority by procedure. It absorbs the mandate of the parent. These claims apply the categories to a modern structure. You can argue them, and the reader must check the argument.

Contested empirical claims: This file cannot settle several claims:

- Outcomes: Researchers study heavily whether schooling raises or lowers measured learning, income, and well-being, and they dispute the answer. The findings depend on the outcome, the population, and the method. Some schools teach very well. Some households teach very badly. The variable is not the building.
- Norm-referencing: Grading on a curve is real in some settings and is not universal. Many systems use criterion-referenced or standards-based assessment.
- The factory genealogy: The claim that mass schooling copied a factory model is a widely repeated historical narrative, and it is too simple. You can argue the mechanism from the present structure without it.
- Alternative feasibility: Whether household-based and apprenticeship models scale, and for whom, is a practical question with real limits. The limits include the time of the parents, income, law, and the needs of children who require specialist help. The texts do not answer it.

What the texts do not say: They do not mention schools. They do not pronounce on the moral status of an institution. They do not say that a child taught outside the household is disobedient. They do not say that a parent who uses a teacher breaks the command. Readers make those claims, and a reader who makes them must label them as such.

## Verdict on the common claim

The common claim is that the school system is evil and not based on first principles. When you examine it mechanism by mechanism, the honest verdict is mixed. It divides along the lines above:

- On first principles: M1 is the strongest part of the claim. Suppose that the system defines knowledge with no reference to God. Then by Proverbs 1:7 and 2:5, the definition begins in the wrong place. This is a real claim with real consequences, and the texts speak to it most directly.
- On the mechanisms: M2 through M7 are structural features that people can observe and argue about. Several of them are not unique to schooling. Any large institution with fixed hours, cohorts, and credentials has the same features. To call them school problems alone is not precise.
- On "evil": this claim is about the orientation and intent of the institution, and the texts in this file do not pronounce it. The texts name what a good foundation looks like, and what happens when something replaces it. Most of the disagreement lies in the gap between "this structure forms people wrongly" and "this structure is evil." This file does not close that gap. A reader who wants the stronger claim must argue it separately and must expect it to be Contested.

The useful conclusion is not a verdict on the institution. It is a question for the household: who teaches this child the foundation, and what is the foundation? The texts answer that question, and the parent can answer it whatever the system does.

# 2. Social media

The institution under examination is the platform whose product is a ranked, personalized stream of content that refreshes all the time. Its revenue depends on time and interaction, and it tunes its ranking to measured engagement. The defining feature is not the content but the loop. The loop is a feed that learns what a person responds to and supplies more of it.

This institution belongs in this file for a reason. The school system forms the foundation of a child. The feed forms the attention of an adult, all the time, in the spaces between other activities. It is the most recent of the three and the least examined. It is also the one most likely to run while you read this file.

## The mechanisms

### S1. Comparison is the business model

- Mechanism: The feed presents a continuous supply of the selected highlights of other people. The poster curates the selection, and the ranking amplifies it. So the base for comparison is not representative, and the error always points the same way. The ordinary life of the user is measured against the edited output of others.
- Biblical category: To measure yourself by yourself, and to compare among yourselves. Paul states both the practice and the verdict.
- Text: 2 Corinthians 10:12: "For we dare not make ourselves of the number, or compare ourselves with some that commend themselves: but they measuring themselves by themselves, and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise." 2 Corinthians 10:18: "For not he that commendeth himself is approved, but whom the Lord commendeth."
- Counter-practice: Choose the base for comparison on purpose, or remove it. The alternative in the text is a measure that God distributes and commendation from the Lord. Neither one is relative to the feed of anyone else.
- Labels: Text states: 2 Corinthians 10:12. Inference: a ranked feed is the practice at scale. Contested: the size of the effect on the self-assessment of any one person. The research literature reports associations of different strength and does not settle causation for all populations.

### S2. Envy is the measured product

- Mechanism: Engagement ranking rewards content that produces strong reactions, and envy is a strong reaction. The platform does not need to intend envy. It needs only to measure what holds attention. The ranking supplies more of whatever produces the reaction.
- Biblical category: Envy as a measured quantity that spreads itself. Proverbs states its internal cost. James states its social cost.
- Text: Proverbs 14:30: "A sound heart is the life of the flesh: but envy the rottenness of the bones." James 3:16: "For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work."
- Counter-practice: Notice what the feed trains. The two wisdoms test applies directly. If the feed produces envy and strife in you, the test already gives its verdict.
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: "rottenness" is H7538 _raqab_, "decay (by caries)." Caries here means decay or rot. G2205 _zelos_ carries both zeal and envy. Inference: envy is zeal turned toward the possession of another person. Inference: engagement tuning selects content that produces envy. Contested: the mechanism is plausible and partly documented. But the strength of the selection effect is disputed, and the tools here cannot verify it.

### S3. The endless scroll against the satisfied eye

- Mechanism: The feed has no natural end. There is always more, and the refresh is instant. The design removes every end point at which a person usually stops and does something else.
- Biblical category: The appetite that supply never satisfies. The eye is not satisfied with seeing, and the eyes of man are never satisfied.
- Text: Ecclesiastes 1:8: "All things are full of labour; man cannot utter it: the eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing." Proverbs 27:20: "Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied."
- Counter-practice: Restore the end point. A feed with an end is a different object from a feed without one. The user must supply the end, because the design does not. Psalm 119:37 gives the practice: "Turn away mine eyes from beholding vanity..."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Inference: infinite scroll is the mechanism that the texts describe. Contested: whether infinite scroll causes the harm or only serves an appetite that already exists. The texts describe the appetite. The design claim belongs to the reader.

### S4. The feed is an itching-ears machine

- Mechanism: The ranking function learns what a person wants and supplies it. Want is not need. The two separate in exactly the cases where a person needs correction. Over time, the supply moves toward the views and preferences that the person already has.
- Biblical category: The audience selects its teachers. People heap to themselves teachers after their own lusts, and they turn from truth to fables. The text names the mechanism: the audience selects the teaching. A ranking function automates the same mechanism.
- Text: 2 Timothy 4:3-4: "For the time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears; And they shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be turned unto fables." 2 Timothy 4:2: "Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort with all longsuffering and doctrine."
- Counter-practice: Curate your input. Choose sources by whether they will correct you, not by whether they will agree with you. Philippians 4:8 supplies the criteria for the selection. The criteria are about truth and virtue, not interest.
- Labels: Text states: 2 Timothy 4:3-4. Lexical: G2833 _knetho_, "to scratch, i.e. (by implication) to tickle." G5198 _hugiaino_, "to have sound health... figuratively, to be uncorrupt (true in doctrine)." G3454 _mythos_, "a tale, i.e. fiction ('myth')." Inference: a recommendation algorithm is this mechanism at industrial scale. Contested: the research literature disputes the strength of filter-bubble effects. Hold the claim at the level of mechanism, not size.

### S5. Performance of virtue for an audience

- Mechanism: The platform makes an audience available for every act, and the audience is measurable. The natural next step is to perform the good deed where people will count it, and to treat the count as the reward.
- Biblical category: The instruction of Christ on alms and prayer is about the audience. The instruction is not "do not be seen." It is "do not do it to be seen." It also states the accounts. Those who perform for the audience "have their reward." The attention of the audience is the whole payment.
- Text: Matthew 6:1: "Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, to be seen of them: otherwise ye have no reward of your Father which is in heaven." Matthew 6:2: "Therefore when thou doest thine alms, do not sound a trumpet before thee, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, that they may have glory of men. Verily I say unto you, They have their reward." John 12:43: "For they loved the praise of men more than the praise of God."
- Counter-practice: Do the good deed in private: "let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doeth..." The practice is not secrecy for its own sake. It removes the audience from the transaction.
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Inference: measurable audiences produce this effect. Contested: whether public good done for mixed motives is worse than private good not done at all. The text addresses motive. The application to a particular post is the judgment of the reader.

### S6. The erosion of the private life

- Mechanism: When posting is normal, the default assumption becomes that people do things to be witnessed. The category of the unwitnessed act, the act with no record, shrinks. With it shrinks the practice of doing good where no one will know.
- Biblical category: The Father who sees in secret. The text places the reward with the one who sees what is not shown. This assumes that other people do not witness a large part of life.
- Text: Matthew 6:6: "But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly." Matthew 6:4: "That thine alms may be in secret: and thy Father which seeth in secret himself shall reward thee openly."
- Counter-practice: Return to unwitnessed work. Keep some part of the day, and some part of the good that you do, unrecorded on purpose. Proverbs 4:23 states the general form: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."
- Labels: Text states: Matthew 6. Inference: platforms erode the category of the unwitnessed act. Contested: whether it does harm to share good work in a given case. The text addresses the motive of the actor, and the application by the reader is an inference.

### S7. The shortened horizon of the reaction

- Mechanism: The loop rewards an immediate response: the post, the reply, the reaction. The interval between stimulus and response gets shorter. The disposition that forms is speed, the answer before the hearing.
- Biblical category: The sequence in James and the proverb on wrath. Be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath. The one who is hasty of spirit exalts folly.
- Text: James 1:19: "Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:" Proverbs 14:29: "He that is slow to wrath is of great understanding: but he that is hasty of spirit exalteth folly."
- Counter-practice: Put a delay before you respond. The counter is a procedure, and the text supplies the order. Hear, then speak, and let wrath come last, if at all.
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Inference: platform design shortens the interval. Contested: the size of the effect, and whether it changes by person and context.

### A note on the fast

This file includes the fast as a practice, not as a command. Scripture commands the fear of the LORD and the guarding of the heart. It does not command a fast from any particular medium. The purpose of a deliberate fast is diagnostic. It shows what the input did to you, which is hard to see while the input runs. Anyone who turns the fast itself into a rule moves into E5 in `thought-patterns.md`.

## What is contested

- The mechanics: Three claims concern public engineering practice. Major platforms rank by predicted engagement. Infinite scroll and notifications exist to extend sessions. Ranking is personalized. These claims are widely documented, but the tools in this skill cannot verify them. Each use carries the label Inference or Contested.
- The effects: The research on well-being, attention, and polarization reports a range of findings. The size of the effects is disputed, and so is their direction for different populations. A file like this one must not overstate them. The mechanism claim is stronger than the size claim, and this file keeps to the mechanism.
- The benefits: Social media connects isolated people and spreads information quickly. It also lets people organize in ways that are otherwise not possible. A treatment that lists only the harms is not honest. This file does not ask "is it bad?" It asks "what does it train, and who chose that?"
- The texts do not mention any of this: The Bible names the categories. They are comparison, envy, the unsatisfied eye, the itching ear, the performed virtue, and the unwitnessed act. The application to a feed is an inference by the reader.

# 3. Television and advertising

The institution under examination is broadcast and streaming video, and the advertising that funds it. The medium delivers a continuous sequence of images and sounds to a receiver. The practice pays for attention by attaching desire to products. This file treats the two together, because the second is the business model of the first.

This institution belongs in this file for a reason. It is the most passive of the three. The viewer does not have to do anything, and that is exactly what the texts on hearing and doing address.

## The mechanisms

### T1. The manufacture of desire for what was not wanted

- Mechanism: Advertising does more than present wants that already exist. It creates new ones. It installs a need that the viewer did not have, together with the product that satisfies it. The viewer then experiences the installed desire as his or her own.
- Biblical category: The eyes of man are never satisfied. The three channels of desire are the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life. They are the address where the installation happens.
- Text: Proverbs 27:20: "Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied." 1 John 2:16: "For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world." Genesis 3:6: "And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat."
- Counter-practice: Ask of a want when it arrived. A desire that appeared during the advertising, and that was not present before, is a manufactured one. The text treats the appetite as something to examine, not something to obey.
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Inference: advertising manufactures want. The history of the industry documents the practice of creating demand, and the texts name the appetite that it addresses. Contested: the research literature disputes the strength of the effect of advertising on behavior, and the tools here cannot measure it.

### T2. Passive reception instead of action

- Mechanism: The medium delivers to a receiver who does not have to act. Nothing in the transaction depends on the viewer doing anything. The disposition that forms is that of the hearer who does not do.
- Biblical category: James names exactly this figure. The hearer of the word who is not a doer beholds himself in a glass, goes his way, and forgets what manner of man he was. The text calls the condition self-deception.
- Text: James 1:22: "But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves." James 1:23-24: "For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass: For he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was." James 1:25: "But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed."
- Counter-practice: Pair consumption with action. The counter to the hearer is the doer, and the blessing in the text attaches to the deed, not the hearing. In practice, consume with a pen, or with a next action, or not at all.
- Labels: Text states: the passage. Inference: a receptive medium forms the disposition of the hearer only. Contested: whether television viewing is passive by nature. Some viewing is active. The claim that the medium determines the disposition is a medium-theory claim, and honest readers dispute it.

### T3. The image substituted for the word

- Mechanism: The main carrier of meaning moves from the word to the image. The image is faster, more immediate, and harder to examine. You must interpret it before you can argue with it, and it arrives with its interpretation built in.
- Biblical category: Creation is by word: "And God said, Let there be light: and there was light." John opens with the same instrument: "In the beginning was the Word..." The word is the main instrument of making and of revelation. The text states the pattern at the foundation of both.
- Text: Genesis 1:3: "And God said, Let there be light: and there was light." John 1:1: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."
- Counter-practice: Prefer the word where the word will carry the meaning. The counter is not the rejection of images, because Scripture uses them. The counter is the refusal to let the image do the work that the word must do.
- Labels: Text states: Genesis 1:3 and John 1:1. Inference: a revelation by word implies a preference for formation by word, and media that put the image first displace it. The texts do not discuss media. The argument from creation by word to media practice is a chain of inferences.
- Contested: Media theory contests the claim that images are less truthful or harder to examine than words by nature. Honest note: Scripture does not forbid images as such. The second commandment forbids making an image for worship and bowing to it: "Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them..." (Exodus 20:5). The target of the prohibition is the worship, not representation. To use that commandment against visual media in general reads too much into it.

### T4. The appetite as the selling point

- Mechanism: The advertiser attaches the product to an appetite, not to a use. Food, drink, and bodies are sold as desire. The pitch addresses hunger, attraction, and status, not function.
- Biblical category: The three-channel structure of Genesis 3:6 and 1 John 2:16. The appetite is addressed directly. The account of the fall in the text shows the sequence from seeing to taking.
- Text: Genesis 3:6: "And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat." 1 John 2:16: "For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world."
- Counter-practice: Separate the appetite from the product. Ask what the thing does, apart from what it promises to make the viewer feel.
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Inference: advertising addresses these three channels. Contested: how well appetite-based advertising works, and how much it creates demand and how much it only channels demand.

### T5. The normalization of what Scripture calls sin through repetition

- Mechanism: What people see again and again becomes ordinary. A person rejects a behavior on first sight, but accepts it after enough exposures. The acceptance happens without a decision.
- Biblical category: The progressive exposure of Psalm 1. First walking in the counsel, then standing in the way, then sitting in the seat. The verbs are a sequence, and the sequence is the mechanism. Paul states the same law for companionship: evil associations corrupt good manners.
- Text: Psalm 1:1: "Blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly, nor standeth in the way of sinners, nor sitteth in the seat of the scornful." 1 Corinthians 15:33: "Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners." Proverbs 4:23: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."
- Counter-practice: Choose what to consume. Do not receive what is served. The pattern in the text is the decision made in advance. "I will set no wicked thing before mine eyes..." (Psalm 101:3). "I made a covenant with mine eyes..." (Job 31:1). Daniel "purposed in his heart" not to be defiled by the portion that was served (Daniel 1:8).
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G3657 _homilia_, "companionship ('homily'), i.e. (by implication) intercourse." The "evil communications" of 1 Corinthians 15:33 are evil companionships, not evil speech. Inference: repeated exposure makes things normal. Contested: the size of the desensitization effect, and whether it changes by content and viewer.

### A note on the medium

The counter-practice is not the rejection of all video or all advertising. Scripture does not command that, and the target of the second commandment is worship, not representation. The texts do command the guarded eye and the examined appetite. Those commands apply to whatever is served.

## What is contested

- The manufacture of desire: The creation of demand by advertising is a documented industry practice with a history. The research literature contests the strength of its effect on any one purchase or preference.
- Medium theory: One family of claims says that a medium itself forms a disposition. Examples are that television makes people passive and that images displace words. These claims come from media theory, and people dispute them. This file uses the mechanism as an Inference and does not present it as established.
- Normalization: The claim that repeated exposure makes behavior normal is plausible and partly supported. Researchers debate the size of the effect.
- The texts do not mention television or advertising: The Bible names the appetite, the guarded eye, the covenant, the progression of exposure, and the hearer who does not do. The application to a medium is the inference of the reader. When a reader uses it to correct someone else, the reader must label it as an inference.

# The common mechanism

The three institutions look unrelated. One is compulsory, one is voluntary, and one is ambient. They differ in law, in medium, and in the age of the person they address. But the same three moves appear in each. The moves are the reason that the three belong in one file.

## The shared structure

1. Each captures attention. The school captures the hours of the child by law. The feed captures the minutes of the adult by reward. The broadcast captures the room by default. Attention is the asset in all three cases. The funding of the school, the revenue of the platform, and the rate of the advertiser all depend on it. The capture is not a side effect. It is the business of the institution.

2. Each defines worth by comparison to others. The school ranks the student against the cohort. The feed ranks the user by counts and measures the user against curated highlights. The advertiser shows the viewer the life that the product promises and measures the viewer against it. In each case, the standard is other people. The institution supplies the standard, and the person does not choose it.

3. Each removes the fear of the LORD from the foundation. The school defines knowledge with no reference to God. The feed ranks content by desire, not by truth. The advertisement attaches worth to a product, not to God. None of the three denies God. Each builds the foundation somewhere else. Proverbs 1:7 and 2:5 warn against this. Romans 1:25 describes it as changing the truth of God into a lie and serving the creature more than the Creator.

### Why the three belong together

The three do not need to coordinate, and they do not need a common author. They come together because of three shared features. They all compete for the same scarce resource, which is attention. They all use the same cheap metric, which is comparison. They all work without reference to the same foundation, which is the fear of the LORD. All three train a person formed by them in the same direction, three times over.

## The single counter-principle

The principle is this. Because the fear of the LORD is the foundation of knowledge, the word renews the mind, and you guard attention as the first asset.

Four texts carry the principle:

- The foundation: Proverbs 1:7: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction." Proverbs 2:5: "Then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God." The foundation is not a subject to add to a curriculum. It is the element that makes knowledge knowledge. Lexical: in Proverbs 1:7, "beginning" is H7225 _reshith_, "the first, in place, time, order or rank." Inference: in this verse, the word can mean first in rank as well as first in time.

- The renewal: Romans 12:2: "And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God." The word for renewing is G342 _anakainosis_, "renovation." The word for transformed is G3339 _metamorphoo_. The mind is not emptied but renovated. The result is the ability to test: "that ye may prove." That ability is the opposite of the disposition that all three institutions form, which is to receive what is served.

- The attention: Proverbs 4:23: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life." 2 Corinthians 10:5: "Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ;" The Greek for "imaginations" is G3053 _logismos_, "computation, i.e. (figuratively) reasoning (conscience, conceit)." It is the word for reasoning. Reasoning is what the mind does when something captures it, and it is what the text says to take captive.

- The orientation: Colossians 3:2: "Set your affection on things above, not on things on the earth." Matthew 6:21: "For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also." The orientation is the decision that comes before the exposure. The institutions work to make that decision unnecessary.

## What this does not mean

- It does not mean that the three institutions are equally harmful, or that they are a conspiracy. Incentive brings them together, not coordination.
- It does not mean that withdrawal is the answer. The counter-principle is a foundation and a discipline, not a location. Daniel served in Babylon and purposed in his heart about the food. Joseph served in Egypt. The household is the primary school, but the household is not a wall.
- It does not mean that the mechanisms are beyond examination. Every claim in this file carries a label for a reason. The categories are Text states, the applications are Inference, and the empirical claims are Contested. A reader who disagrees with an application can find it and attack it. That is the point.
