# The Three Formative Institutions

Working reference for the `renewing-the-mind` skill. It examines three institutions that
train patterns of thought — the school system, social media, and television and advertising —
by mechanism rather than by slogan. Each mechanism is paired with the biblical category that
names it, a counter-practice, and an honest note on what is contested.

## How to read this file

The commission for this skill included a working hypothesis: that the school system is "evil
and not based on first principles." That hypothesis is examined here, not repeated. It is
partly a claim about mechanisms, which can be checked, and partly a claim about moral status,
which is a different kind of claim. This file keeps the two apart.

Three rules govern the analysis.

1. **Mechanism before verdict.** "Schools remove the fear of the LORD from the foundation of
   knowledge" is a checkable claim about a mechanism. "Schools are evil" is a verdict about
   the whole institution, its intent, and its people. The first can be argued from texts and
   practice. The second requires more than this file can supply, and the texts do not
   pronounce it.
2. **The texts do not mention these institutions.** The school in its modern compulsory form,
   the algorithmic feed, and broadcast advertising are all recent. The Bible does not address
   them by name. What it does is name _categories_ — the fear of the LORD as the foundation of
   knowledge, the parent as the primary teacher, worth measured by God rather than by peers,
   the guarded eye. Applying those categories to these institutions is _Inference_, and is
   labelled as such throughout.
3. **Empirical claims are labelled.** Several mechanisms below rest on empirical claims about
   how these institutions work and what they produce. Those claims are marked _Contested_
   where honest researchers disagree, and the tools bundled with this skill cannot settle
   them. A reader who wants to reject a claim should be able to see exactly which claim it is.

## Labels

| Label           | Meaning                                                                                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Text states** | The passage says this, in these words. Checkable against the KJV.                          |
| **Lexical**     | A claim about the underlying Hebrew or Greek, verified against the bundled lexicon.        |
| **Inference**   | Reasoning from the text by a fallible reader. Disagreeable.                                |
| **Contested**   | An empirical or historical claim the tools cannot settle, and that honest readers dispute. |

## Quotation conventions

KJV wording is quoted exactly as returned by `scripture.ts`. The source data uses curly
apostrophes; they are rendered here as straight apostrophes. Wording, punctuation, and
capitalisation are otherwise unaltered. Strong's definitions are quoted verbatim from
`lexicon.ts` output.

## A note on tone

This file is not an argument for contempt toward teachers, students, parents, journalists,
engineers, or advertisers. Each institution below is made of people doing work they believe
in, and each mechanism described is one that the author of this file is also subject to. The
purpose is to name what trains the mind so that the training can be chosen rather than
absorbed.

# 1. The school system

**The institution under examination.** Compulsory, age-graded, state-regulated schooling:
a system in which children are required by law to attend an institution for a fixed number
of years, grouped by birth year, taught a standardised curriculum, and assessed by
comparison to their cohort. This is a modern arrangement. It is not the same thing as
education, and it is not the same thing as teaching. Much of what follows applies to the
system's _structure_ rather than to any particular teacher or lesson.

**The working hypothesis.** That the system is evil and not based on first principles. The
mechanisms below are what that hypothesis would have to rest on. Each is examined for what
the text actually names, and the verdict is given at the end of the section.

## The mechanisms

### M1. Knowledge is defined without the fear of the LORD

- **Mechanism.** The foundation of knowledge is relocated. Knowledge is defined as what can
  be measured, tested, and certified by the institution, with no reference to God. The
  question "what is this for, and before whom is it answerable?" is not part of the
  curriculum, and in many jurisdictions cannot be.
- **Biblical category.** The fear of the LORD is not one subject among others; it is the
  _beginning_ — the firstfruit and rank-one element — of knowledge and wisdom. Proverbs 2
  makes the sequence explicit: the seeker who searches for wisdom as for hidden treasure
  "then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God."
- **Text.** _Proverbs 1:7_ — "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools
  despise wisdom and instruction." _Proverbs 9:10_ — "The fear of the LORD is the beginning
  of wisdom: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding." _Proverbs 2:5_ — "Then shalt
  thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God."
- **Counter-practice.** Put the fear of the LORD back at the foundation — not as a devotional
  add-on but as the frame that makes knowledge answerable. Practically: ask of any subject
  what it is for and to whom it is accountable, and make that question part of the teaching.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the Proverbs. _Lexical_: H7225 _reshith_, "the first, in place,
  time, order or rank (specifically, a firstfruit)" — the word means first in rank, not merely
  first in time, so the fear of the LORD is knowledge's governing element, not its opening
  lesson. H1847 _daath_, "knowledge"; H3374 _yirah_, "fear (also used as infinitive);
  morally, reverence". _Inference_: that a curriculum which excludes God from the foundation
  is the pattern Proverbs warns against. The Proverbs do not mention curricula.
- **Contested.** _Contested_: whether a given school system in fact excludes God from its
  foundation, and whether it could include him within legal constraints. The claim varies by
  jurisdiction and by school. Some schools, including religious schools, do the opposite.

### M2. Compulsory attendance trains compliance as a virtue

- **Mechanism.** Attendance is required regardless of the content or quality of the
  instruction. The child's task is to be present and compliant, and the habit formed is
  obedience to the requirement itself, detached from any judgement about whether the
  requirement is good.
- **Biblical category.** Scripture treats obedience as obedience _to a word_ — it has content.
  Samuel's verdict on Saul's partial obedience is that obedience is measured against what was
  said, not against the fact that something was done. A requirement with no content cannot be
  obeyed in this sense; it can only be complied with.
- **Text.** _1 Samuel 15:22_ — "And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt
  offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better
  than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams." _Matthew 15:3_ — "But he answered and
  said unto them, Why do ye also transgress the commandment of God by your tradition?"
- **Counter-practice.** Make the requirement answerable. The tested authority of Proverbs:
  let a claim be examined and its reasons given, and let the child learn to obey a reason
  rather than a bell. Proverbs 18:17 applies to institutions as much as to lawsuits — the
  first account seems just until it is searched.
- **Labels.** _Inference_: that compulsory attendance forms compliance as a disposition. The
  texts do not discuss attendance laws. _Contested_: the strength of the effect and whether
  other features of schooling (rules, sport, cooperative work) offset it.

### M3. Age segregation by birth year removes generational transmission

- **Mechanism.** Children are grouped by date of birth, so the people a child spends the day
  with are almost all the same age. The older and younger are removed from the room. The
  child's social world becomes a cohort rather than a household or a community.
- **Biblical category.** The transmission of faith and skill is _generational_ and
  _household-based_: the parent teaches the child, and the child learns by being present
  while the work is done. Psalm 78 states the purpose of the chain: that the next generation
  might set their hope in God and not forget his works.
- **Text.** _Deuteronomy 6:6-7_ — "And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be
  in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of
  them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou
  liest down, and when thou risest up." _Psalm 78:6-7_ — "That the generation to come might
  know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to
  their children: That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but
  keep his commandments:"
- **Counter-practice.** Mixed-age work and apprenticeship: put the learner in the room with
  the practitioner, at whatever age the work requires. The household is the primary school
  (Deuteronomy 6:6-7 addresses the parent directly, not an institution), and Proverbs 22:6
  frames the task as training a child in the way he should go.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the commands to the parent. _Inference_: that age-graded
  schooling displaces the commanded transmission. The texts do not describe schools.
  _Contested_: whether age-graded settings are harmful on balance, and whether mixed-age
  settings are feasible at scale; the research literature does not give a clean answer.

### M4. Grading on a curve makes worth relative to classmates

- **Mechanism.** Where assessment is norm-referenced — ranked against the cohort — the
  student's measured worth is a function of other students' performance. One student's
  improvement can lower another's rank. The standard is the group, and it is enforced by the
  institution.
- **Biblical category.** Paul names the practice and states a verdict: measuring oneself by
  oneself and comparing among oneself is "not wise." The alternative in the same passage is a
  measure distributed by God and commendation from the Lord.
- **Text.** _2 Corinthians 10:12_ — "For we dare not make ourselves of the number, or compare
  ourselves with some that commend themselves: but they measuring themselves by themselves,
  and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise." _2 Corinthians 10:18_ — "For not
  he that commendeth himself is approved, but whom the Lord commendeth." _Proverbs 21:2_ —
  "Every way of a man is right in his own eyes: but the LORD pondereth the hearts."
- **Counter-practice.** Criterion-referenced work: define the standard by what must be
  produced, and let every student who meets it succeed. Where ranking is unavoidable, name it
  as a game rather than as a measure of worth.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: 2 Corinthians 10:12. _Inference_: that norm-referenced grading is
  the practice Paul describes. _Contested_: how common norm-referencing is today — many
  systems use criterion-referenced grading or standards-based assessment, and the "curve" is
  more a feature of some university settings than of all schooling. This mechanism is real
  where it exists and is not universal.

### M5. The bell schedule trains obedience and punctuality rather than judgement

- **Mechanism.** The day is divided into fixed periods by an external signal. Work stops and
  starts at the bell, regardless of whether the task is finished or the learner is engaged.
  The habit formed is responsiveness to the schedule rather than to the work.
- **Biblical category.** Wisdom in Scripture is exercised in _judgement_ — the king is to
  judge, the prudent man looks well to his going, the diligent thinks about his work. The
  Proverbs praise the person who considers the state of his flocks and the face of his
  fields, which is a judgement about when to act.
- **Text.** _Proverbs 21:5_ — "The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness; but
  of every one that is hasty only to want." _Proverbs 27:12_ — "A prudent man foreseeth the
  evil, and hideth himself; but the simple pass on, and are punished." _Isaiah 28:16_ — "he
  that believeth shall not make haste."
- **Counter-practice.** Work to completion where possible; let the task and the judgement
  set the interval. Where a schedule is needed, make its reasons visible so the schedule is
  obeyed as a decision rather than as a signal.
- **Labels.** _Inference_: that the bell schedule trains schedule-responsiveness over
  judgement. _Contested_: the historical claim that schooling was modelled on the factory.
  That story is widely repeated and is oversimplified — the genealogy of mass schooling is
  complex, and the "Prussian factory model" narrative is disputed by historians of education.
  The mechanism can be argued from present structure without the historical claim.

### M6. Authority is procedural rather than earned

- **Mechanism.** Authority attaches to the role, and the role is validated by the procedure
  that assigns it — the credential, the appointment, the policy — rather than by demonstrated
  competence or character. A teacher's authority rests on position rather than on being
  trusted with it.
- **Biblical category.** Christ's charge against the tradition of the elders is exactly this:
  a human procedure has taken the place of the commandment, and the result is worship in
  vain. The category is authority that cannot give an account of itself.
- **Text.** _Matthew 15:8-9_ — "This people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and
  honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me. But in vain they do worship
  me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men." _Isaiah 29:13_ — "Wherefore the Lord
  said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour
  me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the
  precept of men:"
- **Counter-practice.** The tested authority of Proverbs: authority that invites examination
  and survives it. Paul can say "be ye followers of me" because his conduct is inspectable
  (1 Corinthians 11:1); that is authority earned and displayed, not conferred by a procedure.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Inference_: that credentialing and policy are
  procedural authority in this sense. _Contested_: whether procedural authority is
  illegitimate in itself — some procedure is necessary for any institution, and the text's
  target is procedure _against_ the commandment, not procedure as such.

### M7. The parent's mandate is outsourced to an institution

- **Mechanism.** The duty to teach the child is transferred. The parent delegates the
  substance of formation to a system, and the parent's role becomes supervision of attendance
  and compliance with the system's requirements.
- **Biblical category.** The command is addressed to the parent, in the second person, and it
  is continuous — sitting, walking, lying down, rising. It is not a duty that can be
  discharged by engaging an agent. Psalm 78 states the same chain: the fathers make them
  known to the children.
- **Text.** _Deuteronomy 6:6-7_ — "And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be
  in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children..." _Psalm 78:4-5_ —
  "We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises
  of the LORD, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done. For he
  established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our
  fathers, that they should make them known to their children:" _Deuteronomy 6:9_ — "And thou
  shalt write them upon the posts of thy house, and on thy gates." _Ephesians 6:4_ — "And, ye
  fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and
  admonition of the Lord."
- **Counter-practice.** The household as the primary school. Whatever other instruction a
  child receives, the parent retains the substance of formation and the responsibility for
  it. Deuteronomy 11:18-19 repeats the command, and Proverbs 3:3-4 states the promise
  attached: "Let not mercy and truth forsake thee: bind them about thy neck; write them upon
  the table of thine heart: So shalt thou find favour and good understanding in the sight of
  God and man."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the commands to parents. _Inference_: that delegating the
  substance of formation violates the command's address. _Contested_: whether any delegation
  is permissible, and how a household that must work can do this. The text does not address
  the question of teachers outside the household, and honest readers differ.

## What is contested

The mechanisms above divide into three kinds of claim, and honesty requires keeping them
apart.

**Named by the text as categories.** The fear of the LORD as the foundation of knowledge
(Proverbs 1:7; 9:10; 2:5). The parent as the primary teacher (Deuteronomy 6:6-7). Worth
measured by God rather than by the cohort (2 Corinthians 10:12; Proverbs 21:2). Obedience as
obedience to a word with content (1 Samuel 15:22). Authority that can be examined (Proverbs
18:17; 1 Thessalonians 5:21). These are _Text states_.

**Inferences about the institution.** That a given school system removes God from the
foundation, trains compliance, displaces generational transmission, ranks students
relatively, forms schedule-responsiveness, validates authority procedurally, and absorbs the
parent's mandate. These are reasoned applications of the categories to a modern structure.
They are arguable, and the argument is the reader's to check.

**Contested empirical claims.** Several claims cannot be settled here:

- **Outcomes.** Whether schooling raises or lowers measured learning, income, and wellbeing
  is heavily studied and genuinely disputed; the findings depend on the outcome, the
  population, and the method. Some schools teach very well. Some households teach very badly.
  The variable is not the building.
- **Norm-referencing.** Grading on a curve is real in some settings and not universal. Many
  systems use criterion-referenced or standards-based assessment.
- **The factory genealogy.** The claim that mass schooling was designed on a factory model is
  a widely repeated historical narrative and is oversimplified. The mechanism can be argued
  from present structure without it.
- **Alternative feasibility.** Whether household-based and apprenticeship models scale, and
  for whom, is a practical question with real constraints — parental time, income, law, and
  the needs of children who require specialist help. The texts do not answer it.

**What the texts do not say.** They do not mention schools. They do not pronounce on the
moral status of an institution. They do not say that a child taught outside the household is
disobedient, or that a parent who uses a teacher has broken the command. Those claims are
made by readers, and should be labelled as such when made.

## Verdict on the commissioning hypothesis

The hypothesis was that the school system is evil and not based on first principles. Examined
mechanism by mechanism, the honest verdict is mixed, and it divides along the lines above.

- **On first principles:** the strongest part of the hypothesis is M1. If knowledge is
  defined without reference to God, then by Proverbs 1:7 and 2:5 the definition has begun in
  the wrong place. This is a real and consequential claim, and it is the one the texts speak
  to most directly.
- **On the mechanisms:** M2 through M7 are structural features that can be observed and
  argued about. Several of them are not unique to schooling — any large institution with
  fixed hours, cohorts, and credentials has the same features. Naming them as school
  problems alone would be imprecise.
- **On "evil":** this is a claim about the institution's orientation and intent, and it is not
  one the texts in this file pronounce. What the texts do is name what a _good_ foundation
  looks like, and what happens when it is replaced. The gap between "this structure forms
  people wrongly" and "this structure is evil" is where most of the disagreement lies, and
  this file does not close it. A reader who wants the stronger claim should argue it
  separately, and should expect it to be _Contested_.

The useful conclusion is not a verdict on the institution but a question for the household:
**who is teaching this child the foundation, and what is the foundation?** That question is
answerable from the texts, and it is answerable by the parent regardless of what the system
does.

# 2. Social media

**The institution under examination.** Platforms whose product is a ranked, personalised,
continuously refreshed stream of content, whose revenue depends on time and interaction, and
whose ranking is optimised against measured engagement. The defining feature is not the
content but the _loop_: a feed that learns what a person responds to and supplies more of it.

**Why it belongs in this file.** The school system forms a child's foundation; the feed forms
an adult's attention, continuously, in the spaces between other activities. It is the most
recent and the least examined of the three, and it is the one most likely to be running while
this file is being read.

## The mechanisms

### S1. Comparison is the business model

- **Mechanism.** The feed presents a continuous supply of other people's selected highlights.
  Because the selection is curated by the poster and amplified by the ranking, the comparison
  base is systematically unrepresentative. The user's ordinary life is measured against
  others' edited output.
- **Biblical category.** Measuring oneself by oneself, and comparing among oneself. Paul
  states both the practice and the verdict.
- **Text.** _2 Corinthians 10:12_ — "For we dare not make ourselves of the number, or compare
  ourselves with some that commend themselves: but they measuring themselves by themselves,
  and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise." _2 Corinthians 10:18_ — "For not
  he that commendeth himself is approved, but whom the Lord commendeth."
- **Counter-practice.** Choose the comparison base deliberately, or remove it. The text's
  alternative is a measure distributed by God and commendation from the Lord, which is not
  relative to anyone else's feed.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: 2 Corinthians 10:12. _Inference_: that a ranked feed is the
  practice at scale. _Contested_: the size of the effect on any individual's self-assessment.
  The research literature reports associations of varying strength and does not settle
  causation for all populations.

### S2. Envy is the measured product

- **Mechanism.** Engagement ranking rewards content that produces strong reactions, and envy
  is a strong reaction. The platform does not need to intend envy; it needs only to measure
  what holds attention. Whatever produces the reaction is supplied more.
- **Biblical category.** Envy as a measured, self-propagating quantity. Proverbs states its
  internal cost; James states its social cost.
- **Text.** _Proverbs 14:30_ — "A sound heart is the life of the flesh: but envy the
  rottenness of the bones." _James 3:16_ — "For where envying and strife is, there is
  confusion and every evil work."
- **Counter-practice.** Notice what the feed is training. The two wisdoms test applies
  directly: if consuming it produces envy and strife, the test has already returned its
  verdict.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: H7538 _raqab_, "decay (by caries)" —
  the KJV's "rottenness" is dental decay; G2205 _zelos_ carries both zeal and envy, so envy is
  zeal turned toward another's possession. _Inference_: that engagement optimisation selects
  for envy-producing content. _Contested_: the mechanism is plausible and partly documented,
  but the strength of the selection effect is disputed and cannot be verified with the tools
  here.

### S3. The endless scroll against the satisfied eye

- **Mechanism.** The feed has no natural end. There is always more, and the refresh is
  instantaneous. The design removes every terminal point at which a person would ordinarily
  stop and do something else.
- **Biblical category.** The appetite that is never satisfied by supply. The eye is not
  satisfied with seeing, and the eyes of man are never satisfied.
- **Text.** _Ecclesiastes 1:8_ — "All things are full of labour; man cannot utter it: the eye
  is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing." _Proverbs 27:20_ — "Hell
  and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied."
- **Counter-practice.** Restore the terminal point. A feed with an end is a different object
  from a feed without one, and the end has to be supplied by the user rather than the design.
  Psalm 119:37 gives the practice: "Turn away mine eyes from beholding vanity."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Inference_: that infinite scroll is the mechanism
  the texts describe. _Contested_: whether infinite scroll causes the harm or merely
  accommodates an existing appetite. The texts describe the appetite; the design claim is the
  reader's.

### S4. The feed is an itching-ears machine

- **Mechanism.** The ranking function learns what a person _wants_ and supplies it. Want is
  not need, and the two diverge in exactly the cases where correction is required. Over time
  the supply converges on the person's existing views and preferences.
- **Biblical category.** The demand-side selection of teachers: people heap to themselves
  teachers after their own lusts, and turn from truth to fables. The text names the
  mechanism — the audience selects the teaching — and it is the same mechanism a ranking
  function automates.
- **Text.** _2 Timothy 4:3-4_ — "For the time will come when they will not endure sound
  doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching
  ears; And they shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be turned unto fables."
  _2 Timothy 4:2_ — "Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke,
  exhort with all longsuffering and doctrine."
- **Counter-practice.** Curated input: choose sources by whether they will correct you, not
  by whether they will agree with you. Philippians 4:8 supplies the selection criteria, and
  the criteria are about truth and virtue rather than interest.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: 2 Timothy 4:3-4. _Lexical_: G2833 _knetho_, "to scratch, i.e.
  (by implication) to tickle"; G5198 _hugiaino_, "to have sound health... figuratively, to be
  uncorrupt (true in doctrine)"; G3454 _mythos_, "a tale, i.e. fiction ('myth')". _Inference_:
  that a recommendation algorithm is this mechanism at industrial scale. _Contested_: the
  strength of filter-bubble effects is disputed in the research literature. Hold the claim at
  the level of mechanism, not magnitude.

### S5. Performance of virtue for an audience

- **Mechanism.** The platform makes an audience available for every act, and the audience is
  measurable. The natural next step is to perform the good deed where it will be counted, and
  to treat the count as the reward.
- **Biblical category.** Christ's instruction on alms and prayer is precisely about the
  audience. The instruction is not "do not be seen" but "do not do it _to be seen_", and it
  states the accounting: those who perform for the audience "have their reward" — the
  audience's attention is the whole payment.
- **Text.** _Matthew 6:1_ — "Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, to be seen of
  them: otherwise ye have no reward of your Father which is in heaven." _Matthew 6:2_ —
  "Therefore when thou doest thine alms, do not sound a trumpet before thee, as the hypocrites
  do in the synagogues and in the streets, that they may have glory of men. Verily I say unto
  you, They have their reward." _John 12:43_ — "For they loved the praise of men more than the
  praise of God."
- **Counter-practice.** The private good deed: "let not thy left hand know what thy right
  hand doeth." The practice is not secrecy for its own sake but the removal of the audience
  from the transaction.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Inference_: that measurable audiences produce this
  effect. _Contested_: whether public good done for mixed motives is worse than private good
  not done at all. The text addresses motive, and the application to any particular post is
  the reader's judgement.

### S6. The erosion of the private life

- **Mechanism.** When posting is normal, the default assumption becomes that things are done
  to be witnessed. The category of the unwitnessed act — the act with no record — shrinks, and
  with it the practice of doing good where no one will know.
- **Biblical category.** The Father who sees in secret. The text locates the reward with the
  one who sees what is not shown, which presupposes that a substantial part of life is
  unwitnessed by other people.
- **Text.** _Matthew 6:6_ — "But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou
  hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in
  secret shall reward thee openly." _Matthew 6:4_ — "That thine alms may be in secret: and thy
  Father which seeth in secret himself shall reward thee openly."
- **Counter-practice.** The return to unwitnessed work: keep some part of the day, and some
  part of the good done, deliberately unrecorded. Proverbs 4:23 states the general form —
  "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: Matthew 6. _Inference_: that platforms erode the unwitnessed
  category. _Contested_: whether sharing good work does harm in a given case; the text
  addresses the motive of the actor, and the reader's application is an inference.

### S7. The shortened horizon of the reaction

- **Mechanism.** The loop rewards immediate response: the post, the reply, the reaction. The
  interval between stimulus and response is compressed, and the disposition formed is
  speed — the answer before the hearing.
- **Biblical category.** The sequence in James and the proverb on wrath: swift to hear, slow
  to speak, slow to wrath; the one who is hasty of spirit exalts folly.
- **Text.** _James 1:19_ — "Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear,
  slow to speak, slow to wrath:" _Proverbs 14:29_ — "He that is slow to wrath is of great
  understanding: but he that is hasty of spirit exalteth folly."
- **Counter-practice.** Insert a delay before responding. The counter is procedural, and the
  text supplies the order: hear, then speak, and let wrath come last if at all.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Inference_: that platform design compresses the
  interval. _Contested_: the magnitude of the effect and whether it differs by person and
  context.

| Counter-practice                   | Grounding                                            | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **The private good deed**          | Matthew 6:3-4, 6:6                                   | Remove the audience from the transaction. The Father who sees in secret is the stated reward.                                                                                                                                  |
| **The deliberate fast**            | 1 Corinthians 9:25-27; Daniel 1:8                    | Self-control for a purpose, and a decision made in advance about what will not be received. **A note on the fast.** The fast is included as a practice, not as a command. Scripture
commands the fear of the LORD and the guarding of the heart; it does not command a fast from
any particular medium. The purpose of a deliberate fast is diagnostic: to find out what the
input has been doing, which is hard to see while it is running. Anyone who turns the fast
itself into a rule has moved into E5 in `thought-patterns.md`.

## What is contested

- **The mechanics.** That major platforms rank by predicted engagement, that infinite scroll
  and notifications are designed to extend sessions, and that ranking is personalised are
  claims about public engineering practice. They are widely documented and are not verifiable
  with the tools bundled here. They are marked _Inference_ or _Contested_ at each use.
- **The effects.** The research on wellbeing, attention, and polarisation reports a range of
  findings, and the size of the effects — and their direction for different populations — is
  genuinely disputed. A file like this should not overstate them. The mechanism claim is
  stronger than the magnitude claim, and this file keeps to the mechanism.
- **The benefits.** Social media connects isolated people, distributes information quickly,
  and allows organisation that would otherwise be impossible. A treatment that lists only the
  harms is not honest. The question this file asks is not "is it bad?" but "what is it
  training, and who chose that?"
- **The texts do not mention any of this.** The Bible names the categories — comparison,
  envy, the unsatisfied eye, the itching ear, the performed virtue, the unwitnessed act — and
  the application to a feed is an inference by the reader.

# 3. Television and advertising

**The institution under examination.** Broadcast and streaming video, and the advertising
that funds it: a medium that delivers a continuous sequence of images and sounds to a
receiver, and a practice that pays for attention by attaching desire to products. The two are
treated together because the second is the business model of the first.

**Why it belongs in this file.** It is the oldest of the three and the one whose mechanisms
are most settled. It is also the one whose training is most passive: the viewer is not
required to do anything, which is exactly what the texts on hearing and doing address.

## The mechanisms

### T1. The manufacture of desire for what was not wanted

- **Mechanism.** Advertising does not only present existing wants; it creates new ones. A need
  the viewer did not have is installed, along with the product that satisfies it. The
  installed desire is then experienced as the viewer's own.
- **Biblical category.** The eyes of man are never satisfied, and the three channels of
  desire — the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, the pride of life — are the address at
  which the installation happens.
- **Text.** _Proverbs 27:20_ — "Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are
  never satisfied." _1 John 2:16_ — "For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and
  the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world."
  _Genesis 3:6_ — "And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was
  pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit
  thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat."
- **Counter-practice.** Ask of a want when it arrived. A desire that appeared during the
  advertising and was not present before is a manufactured one, and the text treats the
  appetite as something to be examined rather than obeyed.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Inference_: that advertising manufactures want —
  the practice of creating demand is documented in the industry's own history, and the texts
  name the appetite it addresses. _Contested_: the strength of advertising's effect on
  behaviour is disputed in the research literature, and the tools here cannot measure it.

### T2. Passive reception instead of action

- **Mechanism.** The medium delivers to a receiver who is not required to act. Nothing in the
  transaction depends on the viewer doing anything, and the disposition formed is that of the
  hearer who does not do.
- **Biblical category.** James names exactly this figure: the hearer of the word who is not a
  doer, who beholds himself in a glass and goes his way and forgets what manner of man he was.
  The text calls the condition self-deception.
- **Text.** _James 1:22_ — "But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your
  own selves." _James 1:23-24_ — "For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is
  like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass: For he beholdeth himself, and goeth
  his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was." _James 1:25_ — "But whoso
  looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful
  hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed."
- **Counter-practice.** Pair consumption with action. The counter to the hearer is the doer,
  and the text's blessing is attached to the deed, not the hearing. Practically: consume with
  a pen, or with a next action, or not at all.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passage. _Inference_: that a receptive medium forms the
  hearer-only disposition. _Contested_: whether television viewing is inherently passive —
  some viewing is active, and the claim that the medium determines the disposition is a
  medium-theory claim that honest readers dispute.

### T3. The image substituted for the word

- **Mechanism.** The primary carrier of meaning shifts from the word to the image. The image
  is faster, more immediate, and harder to examine — it must be interpreted before it can be
  argued with, and it arrives with its interpretation built in.
- **Biblical category.** Creation is by word: "And God said, Let there be light: and there was
  light." John opens with the same instrument: "In the beginning was the Word." The word is
  the primary instrument of making and of revelation, and the pattern is stated at the
  foundation of both.
- **Text.** _Genesis 1:3_ — "And God said, Let there be light: and there was light."
  _John 1:1_ — "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was
  God."
- **Counter-practice.** Prefer the word where the word will carry it. The counter is not the
  rejection of images — Scripture uses them — but the refusal to let the image do the work
  that the word must do.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: Genesis 1:3 and John 1:1. _Inference_: that a word-based
  revelation implies a preference for word-based formation, and that image-dominant media
  displace it. The texts do not discuss media, and the argument from creation-by-word to
  media practice is a chain of inferences.
- **Contested.** The claim that images are inherently less truthful or less examinable than
  words is a contested claim in media theory. _Honest note_: Scripture does not forbid images
  as such. The second commandment forbids making an image for worship and bowing to it —
  "Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them" (Exodus 20:5) — and the
  prohibition's target is the worship, not representation. Using that commandment against
  visual media generally over-reads it.

### T4. The appetite as the selling point

- **Mechanism.** The product is attached to an appetite rather than to a use. Food, drink, and
  bodies are sold as desire; the pitch addresses hunger, attraction, and status rather than
  function.
- **Biblical category.** The three-channel structure of Genesis 3:6 and 1 John 2:16. The
  appetite is addressed directly, and the text's account of the fall shows the sequence from
  seeing to taking.
- **Text.** _Genesis 3:6_ — "And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that
  it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the
  fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat."
  _1 John 2:16_ — "For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the
  eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world."
- **Counter-practice.** Separate the appetite from the product. Ask what the thing does, apart
  from what it promises to make the viewer feel.
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Inference_: that advertising addresses these three
  channels. _Contested_: the effectiveness of appetite-based advertising and the degree to
  which it creates rather than channels demand.

### T5. The normalisation of what Scripture calls sin through repetition

- **Mechanism.** What is shown repeatedly becomes ordinary. A behaviour that would be
  rejected on first sight is accepted after enough exposures, and the acceptance happens
  without a decision.
- **Biblical category.** The progressive exposure of Psalm 1: walking in the counsel, then
  standing in the way, then sitting in the seat. The verbs are a sequence, and the sequence is
  the mechanism. Paul states the same law for companionship: evil associations corrupt good
  manners.
- **Text.** _Psalm 1:1_ — "Blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly,
  nor standeth in the way of sinners, nor sitteth in the seat of the scornful." _1 Corinthians
  15:33_ — "Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners." _Proverbs 4:23_ —
  "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."
- **Counter-practice.** Choose what to consume rather than receiving what is served. The
  text's pattern is the decision made in advance: "I will set no wicked thing before mine
  eyes" (Psalm 101:3), "I made a covenant with mine eyes" (Job 31:1), and Daniel "purposed in
  his heart" not to be defiled by the portion that was served (Daniel 1:8).
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G3657 _homilia_, "companionship
  ('homily'), i.e. (by implication) intercourse" — 1 Corinthians 15:33's "evil
  communications" is evil companionships, not evil speech. _Inference_: that repeated
  exposure normalises. _Contested_: the magnitude of the desensitisation effect and whether it
  varies by content and viewer.

## Counter-practices

| Counter-practice                             | Grounding                                                 | Notes                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Set no wicked thing before the eyes**      | Psalm 101:3; Psalm 119:37; Matthew 6:22-23                | A decision made before the occasion, not during it. The single eye is the positive image.                                                       |
| **The covenant of Job 31:1**                 | Job 31:1                                                  | "I made a covenant with mine eyes" — a standing decision, made once and held, about what the eyes will not look upon.                           |

**A note on the medium.** The counter-practice is not the rejection of all video or all
advertising. Scripture does not command that, and the second commandment's target is worship
rather than representation. What the texts do command is the guarded eye and the examined
appetite, and those commands apply to whatever is being served.

## What is contested

- **The manufacture of desire.** That advertising creates demand is a documented industry
  practice with a history; the strength of its effect on any individual purchase or
  preference is contested in the research literature.
- **Medium theory.** The claim that a medium itself forms a disposition — that television
  makes people passive, that images displace words — is a family of claims associated with
  media theory and is disputed. This file uses the mechanism as an _Inference_ and does not
  present it as established.
- **Normalisation.** The claim that repeated exposure normalises behaviour is plausible and
  partly supported, and the effect sizes are debated.
- **The texts do not mention television or advertising.** The Bible names the appetite, the
  guarded eye, the covenant, the progression of exposure, and the hearer who does not do. The
  application to a medium is the reader's inference, and it should be labelled as one when it
  is used to correct someone else.

# The common mechanism

The three institutions look unrelated. One is compulsory, one is voluntary, one is ambient.
They differ in law, in medium, and in the age of the person they address. But the same three
moves appear in each, and the moves are the reason they belong in one file.

## The shared structure

**1. Each captures attention.** The school captures the child's hours by law; the feed
captures the adult's minutes by reward; the broadcast captures the room by default. Attention
is the asset in all three cases — the school's funding and the platform's revenue and the
advertiser's rate all depend on it. The capture is not incidental to the institution; it is
the institution's business.

**2. Each defines worth by comparison to others.** The student is ranked against the cohort;
the user is ranked by counts and measured against curated highlights; the viewer is shown the
life that the product promises and is measured against it. In each case the standard is other
people, and the standard is supplied by the institution rather than chosen by the person.

**3. Each removes the fear of the LORD from the foundation.** The school defines knowledge
without reference to God; the feed ranks content by desire rather than by truth; the
advertisement attaches worth to a product rather than to God. None of the three denies God.
Each simply builds the foundation somewhere else, which is what Proverbs 1:7 and 2:5 warn
against, and what Romans 1:25 describes as changing the truth of God into a lie and serving
the creature rather than the Creator.

**Why the three belong together.** They do not need to coordinate. They do not need a common
author. They converge because they are all optimised for the same scarce resource
(attention), they all use the same cheap metric (comparison), and they all operate without
reference to the same foundation (the fear of the LORD). A person formed by all three has
been trained in the same direction three times.

## The single counter-principle

**Because the fear of the LORD is the foundation of knowledge, the mind is renewed by the word
and the attention is guarded as the first asset.**

Four texts carry the principle.

- **The foundation.** _Proverbs 1:7_ — "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge:
  but fools despise wisdom and instruction." _Proverbs 2:5_ — "Then shalt thou understand the
  fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God." The foundation is not a subject to be
  added to a curriculum; it is the element that makes knowledge knowledge. H7225 _reshith_
  means first in rank as well as in time.

- **The renewal.** _Romans 12:2_ — "And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed
  by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and
  perfect, will of God." The word for renewing is G342 _anakainosis_, renovation; the word for
  transformed is G3339 _metamorphoo_. The mind is not emptied but renovated, and the result is
  the ability to test — "that ye may prove" — which is the opposite of the receive-what-is-
  served disposition all three institutions form.

- **The attention.** _Proverbs 4:23_ — "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are
  the issues of life." _2 Corinthians 10:5_ — "Casting down imaginations, and every high thing
  that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought
  to the obedience of Christ." The Greek for "imaginations" is G3053 _logismos_, "computation,
  i.e. (figuratively) reasoning (conscience, conceit)" — the word for reasoning, which is what
  the mind does when it is captured and what the text says to take captive.

- **The orientation.** _Colossians 3:2_ — "Set your affection on things above, not on things
  on the earth." _Matthew 6:21_ — "For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also."
  The orientation is the decision that precedes the exposure, and it is the thing the
  institutions are designed to make unnecessary.

## What this does not mean

- It does not mean the three institutions are equally harmful, or that they are a conspiracy.
  They converge by incentive, not by coordination.
- It does not mean withdrawal is the answer. The counter-principle is a foundation and a
  discipline, not a location. Daniel served in Babylon and purposed in his heart about the
  food; Joseph served in Egypt; the household is the primary school but the household is not
  a wall.
- It does not mean the mechanisms are beyond examination. Every claim in this file carries a
  label for a reason: the categories are _Text states_, the applications are _Inference_, and
  the empirical claims are _Contested_. A reader who disagrees with an application can find
  it and attack it, which is the point.
