# Foundations of Renewing the Mind

Reference file for the `renewing-the-mind` skill. It holds the Scripture readings behind the
rules in `SKILL.md`: why the skill always ends with a replacement, who renews, the manner of
correction, the mandate, who the skill is for, and the note on the three sources.

## Removal is not enough (Rule one)

Matthew 12:43-45 records the warning. An unclean spirit goes out of a man. Verse 44 says that the spirit returns and "findeth it empty, swept, and garnished." The spirit then brings seven other spirits, and "the last state of that man is worse than the first."

Read it in context. Jesus applies the warning to "this wicked generation" (12:45). Verse 28 says what had come to them: "if I cast out devils by the Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God is come unto you." Verse 41 says "a greater than Jonas is here." The house is clean, but it is empty. It has no occupant. Reading: the passage points first to receiving Christ, not to a better idea.

Reading: this skill borrows the shape of the warning. A false pattern that you tear out and do not replace leaves a vacancy. Something fills it. For this reason, the skill always ends with a replacement and never with a deletion. A true sentence does not fill the house by itself. See "Who renews" below.

Romans 12:2 states the same shape in one verse: "And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind..." The negative command and the positive command arrive together.

## Renewal, not victory (Rule three)

2 Timothy 2:24-26 defines the manner: "In meekness instructing those that oppose themselves..." Galatians 6:1 says to restore in meekness. 1 Peter 3:15 says to give an answer with meekness and fear. James 1:19 says to be swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to wrath.

A person can correctly name the error of another person and do it with contempt. That person produces the fruit that the catalog condemns. Before you use this skill on anyone other than yourself, read the section "Guards against false detection" in `thought-patterns.md`.

## The mandate

Scripture commands the renewal of the mind. It is a command, not a mood. But the KJV verb is passive: "be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind" (Romans 12:2). Reading: the person obeys, and God does the transforming.

2 Corinthians 10:3-5 describes a warfare. Verse 4 says that the weapons "are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds". Their use is to cast down imaginations and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, "and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ". In context, Paul defends his ministry against opponents in Corinth (10:2, 10:10-12). Reading: the passage also applies to the thoughts of a believer. The power is God's, not the power of a technique.

Proverbs 4:23 states the stakes: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."

The supporting texts are Romans 12:1-2, Isaiah 26:3, Philippians 2:5, Philippians 4:8, Colossians 3:1-2, 1 Peter 1:13, 2 Timothy 1:7, and Titus 2:6. Several of them name God as the one who acts. "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace" (Isaiah 26:3). "God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind" (2 Timothy 1:7).

## Who renews

Scripture says: God renews. The KJV uses "renewing" twice. Romans 12:2 is one. Titus 3:5 is the other: "Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;" In the New Testament, "renewed" appears three times, and each time it is passive. 2 Corinthians 4:16 says "the inward man is renewed day by day". Ephesians 4:23 says "be renewed in the spirit of your mind". The new man "is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him" (Colossians 3:10). 2 Corinthians 3:18 says that we "are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the LORD."

Scripture says: the person also acts. Philippians 2:12-13: "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure." John 15:5: "for without me ye can do nothing."

Reading: the eight steps in `SKILL.md` are the human side of a work that God does. They are not a technique that renews by its own power. So do them before God, in prayer. The replacement is truth for the Spirit to use, not a mantra.

Disputed: Christian traditions differ on how the two works relate. Some stress that God's work comes first and makes the person's work possible (Philippians 2:13). Others stress that the person cooperates with grace (Philippians 2:12). Both hold that God renews and that the person must act.

## Who this is for

Romans 12:1 addresses "brethren, by the mercies of God". In Ephesians 4:20-24, "be renewed" follows "ye have not so learned Christ", and it comes with "put on the new man, which after God is created in righteousness and true holiness." 1 Corinthians 2:14 says, "But the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God".

The skill assumes that the user is a Christian, so the renewal that Romans 12:2 describes applies to them.

## The sources

Reading: this skill infers that three institutions shape most of the patterns in the catalog. The three are the school system, social media, and television with advertising. The skill infers that each one captures attention, defines worth by comparison to other people, and removes the foundation that Proverbs 1:7 names. That verse says, "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge..."

`institutions.md` sets out the mechanisms, the counter-practices, and the parts that are contested. That file labels each claim as Scripture says, Reading, or Disputed.

The contested parts need a note. There are three kinds of claim here.

- Scripture says: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge" (Proverbs 1:7). "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom" (Proverbs 9:10). Hold this as firm.
- Reading: a given school or medium leaves that foundation out. Each case needs evidence. Scripture also shows God giving real knowledge to people trained in a pagan school. Daniel was taught "the learning and the tongue of the Chaldeans" (Daniel 1:4), and "God gave them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom" (Daniel 1:17).
- Disputed: the empirical claims about the outcomes of schooling. They change by school and by country, and some schools teach well. Treat them as testable.

Do not merge the three.
