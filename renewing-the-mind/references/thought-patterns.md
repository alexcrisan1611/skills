# Thought Patterns: The Catalog

Working reference for the `renewing-the-mind` skill. It names malformed and inaccurate
patterns of thought, states what each one lies about, shows its fruit, and pairs it with a
counter-truth and a verified text. It is a reference document, not a devotional.

## Method and labels

Four labels are used throughout. They are load-bearing; do not strip them.

| Label           | Meaning                                                                                         |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Text states** | The passage says this, in these words. Checkable against the KJV.                               |
| **Lexical**     | A claim about the underlying Hebrew or Greek, verified against the bundled lexicon.             |
| **Inference**   | Reasoning from the text by a fallible reader. Disagreeable.                                     |
| **Contested**   | An empirical or historical claim that the tools cannot settle, and that honest readers dispute. |

Three rules govern everything here.

1. **Applications are inferences, not Scripture.** The text names a pattern. The claim that
   a particular modern institution or habit is that pattern is an inference, and it is
   labelled as one.
2. **Description is not prescription.** Several of the best passages in this catalog describe
   a failure — Judges 17:6, 1 Samuel 15, Psalm 73. The text is reporting what happened, not
   commanding it. Those entries say so.
3. **Verify before inferring.** KJV wording is quoted exactly as returned by `scripture.ts`.
   Where a 1611 word does argumentative work it was checked against `lexicon.ts`. The
   checked words are listed in the appendix.

## Quotation conventions

KJV wording is quoted exactly as returned by the tool. The source data uses curly
apostrophes; they are rendered here as straight apostrophes. Wording, punctuation, and
capitalisation are otherwise unaltered. Strong's definitions are quoted verbatim from
`lexicon.ts` output, including its own abbreviations.

## How to use this catalog

Each entry has the same seven parts: **description**, **the lie**, **cause**, **fruit**,
**counter-truth**, **counter-text**, **labels**. The cause is included because a pattern that
is merely condemned cannot be replaced. A pattern is attractive for a reason, and the reason
has to be answered.

Repair procedure:

1. **Name the pattern.** Use the biblical name where one fits. Naming is half the work;
   an unnamed pattern operates as a mood rather than a claim.
2. **State the lie in one sentence.** If it cannot be stated in one sentence, the pattern
   has not been isolated.
3. **Name the cause.** What does this thought buy the person who holds it? Fear, status,
   relief, permission?
4. **Check the fruit.** Apply the two wisdoms test below.
5. **Replace, do not merely delete.** A vacated pattern leaves the cause unaddressed.
   The counter-truth is the replacement.
6. **Expect resistance.** The pattern will re-argue. Replacement is a practice, not an event.

## What this catalog is not

It is not a licence to diagnose other people. Every entry describes an error that the
reader is also capable of, and the guards section at the end is mandatory reading, not an
appendix of courtesies. A catalog of thought errors used as a weapon produces exactly the
fruit that the catalog condemns — see F1 and the guards section.

# Group A — Truth and evidence

These are the failures that corrupt the _input_: what a person accepts as true, and how they
decide. Every later group depends on this one, because a person who cannot classify cannot
correct.

## A1 · Calling evil good

_Modern name: moral inversion._

- **Description.** The classifying faculty is reversed. The text lists four swaps — evil and
  good, darkness and light, bitter and sweet. The third pair is sensory: bitterness is a
  taste, so the inversion is not only a change of vocabulary but a claimed change of
  experience.
- **The lie.** "The label determines the value. If I rename it, I have changed it."
- **Cause.** Renaming removes the need to repent. It is cheaper than reform and it keeps the
  approval of whichever group supplied the new label.
- **Fruit.** The loss of the ability to detect error at all. A person who has renamed the
  evil cannot repent of it, because they no longer believe it is there.
- **Counter-truth.** Good is not defined by the namer. The standard is God's own character,
  and the text does not observe the inversion neutrally — it pronounces woe on it.
- **Counter-text.** _Isaiah 5:20_ — "Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that
  put darkness for light, and light for darkness; that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for
  bitter!"
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the inversion and the woe. _Inference_: that this pattern is
  upstream of the others. _Contested_: which contemporary practices fall under it — that
  question is not settled by this text and requires the guards section.
- **Related.** A2, D1, E5.

## A2 · Every man right in his own eyes

_Modern name: relativism and self-referential truth._

- **Description.** Each person's own assessment becomes the final court. The refrain in
  Judges is given twice, at the beginning and the end of the period, as the explanation for
  the chaos between. Proverbs states the same condition in the singular.
- **The lie.** "Sincerity is the same as correctness. If it seems right to me, it is right
  for me."
- **Cause.** It ends every argument without requiring anyone to change. It also costs
  nothing to hold, since it never has to survive an external test.
- **Fruit.** The condition Judges describes: no acknowledged authority, every assessment
  private, and the events the book records as the result.
- **Counter-truth.** The text does not leave the private assessment unchallenged. Proverbs
  adds the external check: the LORD weighs the hearts. The alternative to self-reference is
  not another private opinion but counsel and a judge who sees what the person cannot.
- **Counter-text.** _Judges 17:6_ — "In those days there was no king in Israel, but every man
  did that which was right in his own eyes." _Judges 21:25_ — "In those days there was no
  king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes." _Proverbs 12:15_ —
  "The way of a fool is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise."
  _Proverbs 21:2_ — "Every way of a man is right in his own eyes: but the LORD pondereth the
  hearts."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the refrain and the two Proverbs. _Description, not prescription_:
  Judges reports the condition; it does not command it. _Inference_: that "no king" and
  "every man right in his own eyes" are connected as cause and symptom.
- **Related.** A5, A4, D1.

## A3 · Answering before hearing

_Modern name: premature conclusion._

- **Description.** A verdict is issued before the evidence is in. The text names the
  sequence, not merely the outcome: the answer comes _before_ the hearing.
- **The lie.** "A fast answer is a competent answer. Speed is evidence of understanding."
- **Cause.** Speed signals confidence, and confidence is rewarded socially. Waiting looks
  like ignorance, and ignorance is uncomfortable.
- **Fruit.** The text states it directly: "it is folly and shame unto him." The shame is
  the later discovery of what the earlier answer missed.
- **Counter-truth.** The remedy is procedural, and James gives the sequence: swift to hear,
  slow to speak. Proverbs gives the corresponding virtue — the prudent man looks well to his
  going, while the simple believes every word.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 18:13_ — "He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it
  is folly and shame unto him." _Proverbs 14:15_ — "The simple believeth every word: but the
  prudent man looketh well to his going." _James 1:19_ — "Wherefore, my beloved brethren,
  let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:"
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the Proverbs and James. _Inference_: that the modern "hot take"
  is this pattern at scale. _Contested_: the claim that particular platforms measurably
  increase it — the tools here cannot settle that, and the research literature disputes the
  size of the effect.
- **Related.** A4, A8, E2.

## A4 · First in his own cause

_Modern name: confirmation bias and motivated reasoning._

- **Description.** The text describes a legal procedure. The first party to speak "seemeth
  just" — the word is _seemeth_, an appearance — and the case appears closed until the other
  party examines him. The verse is a warning about the appearance created by speaking first.
- **The lie.** "My account of events is the whole account. The case is closed after my
  version."
- **Cause.** The first version is the one that feels like the truth, because it is the one
  the person has already lived through and rehearsed. Later information arrives as an
  interruption.
- **Fruit.** Unjust judgement, self-justification, and an inability to hear correction.
  The person is not lying; they are reporting a genuinely partial view as if it were total.
- **Counter-truth.** The text prescribes the procedure by describing it: the neighbour comes
  and searches him. The counter-practice is to seek the other side before forming the
  verdict, and to treat one's own case as a case, not as a fact.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 18:17_ — "He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but
  his neighbour cometh and searcheth him." _Proverbs 18:13_ — "He that answereth a matter
  before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the courtroom description. _Inference_: the mapping onto
  confirmation bias and motivated reasoning — the text does not use those terms, and the
  mechanism is named by the reader.
- **Related.** A3, D2, D5.

## A5 · Measuring yourself by yourself

_Modern name: relative judgement and social comparison._

- **Description.** The standard of measurement is the measuring group itself. Paul describes
  the practice and then states a verdict on it: those who do it "are not wise." The
  surrounding verses supply the alternative — a measure distributed by God, and commendation
  that comes from the Lord rather than from oneself.
- **The lie.** "My standing is defined by the group I am in. If I am ahead of them, I am
  fine."
- **Cause.** It is the cheapest available standard. It requires no external reference, no
  examination, and no work — only a comparison group, which is now supplied continuously.
- **Fruit.** Two errors from one root: inflation when the comparison favours the person and
  deflation when it does not. Both are false. It also makes envy structural, because the
  group's success is by definition one's own loss.
- **Counter-truth.** The measurement is not self-issued. The text points to a measure
  assigned by God and to the Lord's commendation, which is the only approval that is not
  relative.
- **Counter-text.** _2 Corinthians 10:12_ — "For we dare not make ourselves of the number,
  or compare ourselves with some that commend themselves: but they measuring themselves by
  themselves, and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise." _2 Corinthians
  10:17-18_ — "But he that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord. For not he that commendeth
  himself is approved, but whom the Lord commendeth."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the practice and the verdict "are not wise", and the alternative
  in 10:17-18. _Lexical_: G3358 _metron_, "a measure ('metre'), literally or figuratively;
  by implication, a limited portion (degree)". _Inference_: that this is the engine of the
  comparison economy — see B1 and the social media section of `institutions.md`.
- **Related.** B1, C1, C3.

## A6 · Judging by appearance

_Modern name: the halo effect._

- **Description.** A judgement is formed from the visible surface — countenance, stature,
  presentation — and treated as a judgement of the person. The LORD corrects Samuel for
  using the criterion that comes naturally to humans, and states the difference: man looks
  on the outward appearance, the LORD looks on the heart.
- **The lie.** "What is visible is what is real. Presentation is character."
- **Cause.** Appearance is available immediately and costs nothing to read. It is also a
  genuinely useful signal in some contexts, which is what makes the error attractive — the
  pattern is a real signal treated as a total one.
- **Fruit.** Wrong appointments, wrong trusts, and the promotion of the impressive over the
  competent. It also produces self-condemnation in those who know they do not present well.
- **Counter-truth.** The text commands judgement, not its suspension: "judge righteous
  judgment." The correction is to the _criterion_, not to the act of judging.
- **Counter-text.** _1 Samuel 16:7_ — "But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his
  countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD
  seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on
  the heart." _John 7:24_ — "Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous
  judgment."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: both verses. _Lexical_: H4758 _mareh_, "a view (the act of
  seeing); also an appearance (the thing seen), whether (real) a shape (especially if
  handsome, comeliness; often plural the looks), or (mental) a vision" — the word carries
  the sense of comeliness, which is exactly the surface that misleads.
- **Related.** A7, C3, D5.

## A7 · Partiality

_Modern name: in-group bias._

- **Description.** Judgement is weighted by who the person is rather than by what is true or
  just. James gives a concrete scene: the well-dressed visitor is seated well, the poor
  visitor is seated low, and the assembly has become "judges of evil thoughts." Leviticus
  states the prohibition in both directions — neither the poor nor the mighty are to be
  favoured.
- **The lie.** "Loyalty to my group is the same as justice."
- **Cause.** Partiality buys belonging. It is the price of admission to a group, and the
  group is often the source of safety.
- **Fruit.** The text names it: sin, and conviction as transgressors. It also destroys the
  credibility of the judge, because a partial judge cannot be trusted on any matter.
- **Counter-truth.** The royal law is the standard: love thy neighbour as thyself. The text
  also supplies a striking word for what impartiality is — G87 _adiakritos_, whose
  dictionary sense is "undistinguished, i.e. (actively) impartial." The wisdom from above
  does not sort people by face.
- **Counter-text.** _James 2:1_ — "My brethren, have not the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ,
  the Lord of glory, with respect of persons." _James 2:9_ — "But if ye have respect to
  persons, ye commit sin, and are convinced of the law as transgressors." _Leviticus 19:15_
  — "Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of the
  poor, nor honor the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy
  neighbour."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G4382 _prosopolepsia_, "partiality,
  i.e. favoritism"; G4381 _prosopoleptes_, "an accepter of a face (individual), i.e.
  (specially), one exhibiting partiality" — literally a receiver of faces; H4856 _massa_,
  "partiality (as a lifting up)". _Inference_: that the Hebrew idiom behind "respect the
  person" is the lifting of a face (H5375 _nasa_, "to lift"; H6440 _panim_, "the face") —
  the tool confirms both words and their glosses but does not map them to this verse, so the
  verse-level mapping is the reader's.
- **Related.** A6, A5, D2.

## A8 · The unverified report

_Modern name: accepting a claim without testing it._

- **Description.** A claim is accepted because of its source, its confidence, or its
  convenience, without a test. Deuteronomy supplies a test for prophecy and makes it
  falsifiable by outcome. John commands the testing of spirits. Paul commands the testing of
  all things.
- **The lie.** "Believing is the same as agreeing. Testing is unfaithfulness."
- **Cause.** Testing is expensive and exposes the tester. Acceptance is cheap and keeps the
  peace. There is also a real virtue nearby — trust — which the pattern exploits by
  confusing trust with credulity.
- **Fruit.** Gullibility and cynicism in alternation, often in the same person: uncritical
  acceptance of favoured sources and uncritical rejection of disfavoured ones. The ability
  to distinguish is lost in both directions.
- **Counter-truth.** The text does not merely permit testing; it commands it, and it supplies
  a criterion (the outcome) and a disposition (hold fast that which is good). Faith in the
  biblical sense is not bare belief — G4102 _pistis_ carries "constancy" as part of its own
  definition, so testing is not the opposite of faith but a way of exercising it.
- **Counter-text.** _Deuteronomy 18:22_ — "When a prophet speaketh in the name of the LORD,
  if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which the LORD hath not
  spoken, but the prophet hath spoken it presumptuously: thou shalt not be afraid of him."
  _1 John 4:1_ — "Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of
  God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world." _1 Thessalonians 5:21_ —
  "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the three passages. _Lexical_: G1381 _dokimazo_, "to test
  (literally or figuratively); by implication, to approve"; G3985 _peirazo_, "to test
  (objectively), i.e. endeavor, scrutinize, entice, discipline"; G4102 _pistis_, "persuasion,
  i.e. credence; moral conviction (of religious truth, or the truthfulness of God or a
  religious teacher), especially reliance upon Christ for salvation; abstractly, constancy in
  such profession". _Inference_: that the reverse error — rejecting a claim because of its
  source rather than testing it — is the same pattern with the sign flipped.
- **Related.** A3, E7, E2.

# Group B — Desire and comparison

These patterns corrupt the _want_. They do not argue that a thing is true; they make it feel
necessary. Every one of them is downstream of A5, because a desire is only disordered when
it has been measured against someone else's portion.

## B1 · Envy

_Modern name: comparison-driven discontent._

- **Description.** Envy is grief at another's good. Proverbs states its physical effect;
  James states its social effect; Psalm 73 gives the full first-person account of the
  process, including the near-fall, the turning point, and the recovery.
- **The lie.** "Their gain is my loss. If they have it, I have been deprived."
- **Cause.** Envy is a form of attention, and attention is a form of importance. It also
  supplies a reason for one's own position that does not require action — the problem is
  another person's fortune, not one's own conduct.
- **Fruit.** The text is specific. Proverbs 14:30 calls it "the rottenness of the bones" —
  the word for rottenness is decay by caries. James 3:16: "where envying and strife is,
  there is confusion and every evil work." Psalm 73 records the self-diagnosis: "So foolish
  was I, and ignorant: I was as a beast before thee."
- **Counter-truth.** The psalm's turn is not psychological but spatial and theological: "Until
  I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end." The recovery is a change of
  vantage, not a change of feeling, and it ends in a different measurement — God as the
  portion, rather than the neighbour's portion as the measure.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 14:30_ — "A sound heart is the life of the flesh: but envy the
  rottenness of the bones." _James 3:14-16_ — "But if ye have bitter envying and strife in
  your hearts, glory not, and lie not against the truth. This wisdom descendeth not from
  above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish. For where envying and strife is, there is
  confusion and every evil work." _Psalm 73:17-19_ — "Until I went into the sanctuary of God;
  then understood I their end. Surely thou didst set them in slippery places: thou castedst
  them down into destruction. How are they brought into desolation, as in a moment! they are
  utterly consumed with terrors." _Psalm 73:25-26_ — "Whom have I in heaven but thee? and
  there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee. My flesh and my heart faileth: but God
  is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: all four passages, including the sequence in Psalm 73.
  _Lexical_: H7068 _qinah_, "jealousy or envy"; H7538 _raqab_, "decay (by caries)"; G2205
  _zelos_, "properly, heat, i.e. (figuratively) 'zeal' (in a favorable sense, ardor; in an
  unfavorable one, jealousy, as of a husband (figuratively, of God), or an enemy, malice)".
  The Hebrew and Greek words for envy are the same words as zeal and jealousy — envy is not a
  separate faculty but zeal turned toward another's possession. H4832 _marpe_, "properly,
  curative, i.e. literally (concretely) a medicine, or (abstractly) a cure; figuratively
  (concretely) deliverance, or (abstractly) placidity" — so the "sound heart" of Proverbs
  14:30 is literally the medicine of the flesh. _Inference_: that envy is the affective
  engine of the comparison economy.
- **Related.** A5, B3, F1.

## B2 · The three channels of desire

_Modern name: appetite, aesthetics, status._

- **Description.** Genesis 3:6 and 1 John 2:16 each list three channels. Genesis gives them
  in narrative order: good for food, pleasant to the eyes, desired to make one wise. John
  gives them in summary: the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, the pride of life. The
  mapping of one list onto the other is an inference, but the three-fold structure appears in
  both texts independently.
- **The lie.** "What I want is what I need. The strength of the wanting is evidence of the
  worth of the thing."
- **Cause.** Each channel answers a real need at a counterfeit address. Food answers hunger,
  beauty answers the love of order, wisdom answers the desire to understand. The pattern does
  not create a need; it redirects one.
- **Fruit.** The sequence in Genesis 3:6 is stated as a chain — saw, took, ate, gave. The
  desire did not stay internal. James states the same chain in general terms: every man is
  drawn away of his own lust and enticed, and the finished product is death.
- **Counter-truth.** The text does not command the removal of desire but its reordering.
  The single eye of Matthew 6:22-23 is the counter-image: an eye that is not divided between
  the channel and its counterfeit.
- **Counter-text.** _Genesis 3:6_ — "And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food,
  and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took
  of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did
  eat." _1 John 2:16_ — "For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust
  of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world." _1 John
  2:17_ — "And the world passeth away, and the lust thereof: but he that doeth the will of
  God abideth for ever." _James 1:14_ — "But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of
  his own lust, and enticed." _James 1:15_ — "Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth
  forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the two three-fold lists. _Inference_: the mapping between them.
  _Contested_: the claim that advertising targets exactly these three channels is an
  inference from marketing practice, and the strength of advertising's effect on behaviour is
  disputed in the research literature — see `institutions.md`.
- **Related.** B3, B7, C5.

## B3 · The eyes never satisfied

_Modern name: manufactured want._

- **Description.** The appetite does not terminate. Proverbs pairs the unsatisfied eye with
  hell and destruction — two things that are never full. Ecclesiastes states the same of both
  eye and ear: seeing is not satisfied by seeing, hearing is not filled by hearing.
- **The lie.** "The next acquisition will settle it."
- **Cause.** The appetite is real and its satisfaction is real, which is what makes the
  pattern credible. A satisfied hunger is genuine evidence — for the wrong conclusion, since
  the next hunger arrives.
- **Fruit.** Consumption that increases the appetite it feeds. The want is not reduced by
  supply; it is trained by it.
- **Counter-truth.** Contentment is not the suppression of desire but its relocation. The
  text does not say the eye should stop seeing; it says the person should stop feeding on
  what cannot fill. Psalm 119:37 gives the practice: turn the eyes away from beholding
  vanity.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 27:20_ — "Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of
  man are never satisfied." _Ecclesiastes 1:8_ — "All things are full of labour; man cannot
  utter it: the eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing."
  _Psalm 119:37_ — "Turn away mine eyes from beholding vanity; and quicken thou me in thy
  way." _Hebrews 13:5_ — "Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with
  such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Psalm 119, Hebrews. _Inference_: that
  a manufactured-want industry exists and that this is its mechanism. _Contested_: the size
  of the effect on any given person; the tools here cannot measure it.
- **Related.** B2, B4, B7.

## B4 · The deceitfulness of riches

_Modern name: — (the biblical name is the diagnosis)._

- **Description.** The text does not call riches evil; it calls them _deceitful_. In the
  parable of the sower, the deceit works alongside the cares of the world and the lusts of
  other things, and the stated result is that the word is choked and becomes unfruitful.
  Paul charges the rich not to be highminded and not to trust in uncertain riches. The psalm
  gives the same instruction in the imperative: if riches increase, set not your heart upon
  them.
- **The lie.** "Money is neutral. More money will solve the anxiety that less money caused."
- **Cause.** Money genuinely solves real problems. The deceit is not that it is useless but
  that it is treated as a foundation rather than an instrument. The anxiety it relieves is
  real, which makes the relief feel like security.
- **Fruit.** Choked word, no fruit. Highmindedness. Trust placed in a thing the text itself
  labels uncertain.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is not poverty and it is not guilt. Paul's charge does not
  tell the rich to divest; it tells them where to put their trust and then tells them to
  enjoy what God gives. The psalm's counter is a direction of the heart, not a liquidation
  of the assets.
- **Counter-text.** _Mark 4:19_ — "And the cares of this world, and the deceitfulness of
  riches, and the lusts of other things entering in, choke the word, and it becometh
  unfruitful." _1 Timothy 6:17_ — "Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not
  highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all
  things to enjoy;" _Psalm 62:10_ — "Trust not in oppression, and become not vain in robbery:
  if riches increase, set not your heart upon them."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: all three passages. _Lexical_: G539 _apate_, "delusion" — the
  KJV's "deceitfulness" is the word for delusion. _Inference_: that "uncertain riches" names
  the mechanism, since the uncertainty is what the trust ignores. _Note_: Mark 4:19 is a
  description of a class of hearer, not a command to the rich to become poor.
- **Related.** B5, B7, F7.

## B5 · Haste to be rich

_Modern name: the get-rich-quick reflex._

- **Description.** Three Proverbs name speed as the fault, not ambition. The faithful man
  abounds; the one who makes haste to be rich shall not be innocent. The thoughts of the
  diligent tend to plenty; the hasty tend to want. Wealth gathered by vanity diminishes;
  wealth gathered by labour increases.
- **The lie.** "Speed is the same as skill. The slow path is for people without vision."
- **Cause.** The slow path is genuinely slower, and patience is genuinely hard. The pattern
  also borrows the vocabulary of faith — vision, boldness, favour — which makes caution look
  like unbelief.
- **Fruit.** The text states three outcomes: not innocent, want, and diminution. Note the
  word: Proverbs 21:5 ends in _want_, which in 1611 means lack, not desire — the hasty end
  in poverty, not merely in unfulfilled longing.
- **Counter-truth.** The text's alternative is not passivity but labour gathered over time,
  and the count-the-cost procedure of Luke 14:28-30, which treats the unfinished tower as a
  public failure.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 28:20_ — "A faithful man shall abound with blessings: but he
  that maketh haste to be rich shall not be innocent." _Proverbs 21:5_ — "The thoughts of
  the diligent tend only to plenteousness; but of every one that is hasty only to want."
  _Proverbs 13:11_ — "Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished: but he that gathereth by
  labour shall increase." _Luke 14:28_ — "For which of you, intending to build a tower,
  sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it?"
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the Proverbs and Luke. _Lexical_: H213 _uts_, "to press; (by
  implication) to be close, hurry, withdraw"; the "want" of Proverbs 21:5 belongs to the
  KJV's lack-family — `lexicon.ts english "want"` returns 21 entries, all of them lack,
  deficiency, or poverty words, and none of them a desire-word.
- **Related.** B4, B6, F3.

## B6 · Bread of deceit

_Modern name: short-term gain from deception._

- **Description.** A gain obtained by deception is described as sweet at the moment of
  eating and gravel afterwards. The text does not deny the sweetness; it states its duration.
- **The lie.** "The gain is real. The method is a detail."
- **Cause.** Deception works immediately. The gravel arrives later, and later is
  systematically discounted.
- **Fruit.** The aftertaste the text names, plus the collapse of trust, which is the asset
  the deception spent. Proverbs 12:19 states the asymmetry: the lip of truth is established
  for ever, the lying tongue is but for a moment.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is not merely honesty as a rule but truth as a durable
  asset. Paul's counter-practice is stated: renouncing the hidden things of dishonesty, not
  handling the word of God deceitfully, commending by manifestation of the truth.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 20:17_ — "Bread of deceit is sweet to a man; but afterwards his
  mouth shall be filled with gravel." _Proverbs 12:19_ — "The lip of truth shall be
  established for ever: but a lying tongue is but for a moment." _2 Corinthians 4:2_ — "But
  have renounced the hidden things of dishonesty, not walking in craftiness, nor handling the
  word of God deceitfully; but by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every
  man's conscience in the sight of God."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: all three. _Lexical_: H8267 _sheqer_, "an untruth; by
  implication, a sham (often adverbial)" — the word covers both the lie and the thing that
  is a sham.
- **Related.** B5, E4, E7.

## B7 · Covetousness

_Modern name: — (the biblical name is the diagnosis)._

- **Description.** Luke 12:15 contains both the command and the reason, and the reason quotes
  the lie directly: a man's life does not consist in the abundance of his possessions. The
  parable that follows ends with the question that the covetous man cannot answer: whose
  shall those things be?
- **The lie.** "Life consists in the abundance of the things which he possesseth." The text
  quotes it in order to deny it.
- **Cause.** Possession is visible evidence of security, and security is a real good. The
  pattern substitutes the evidence for the thing.
- **Fruit.** The parable's verdict: "Thou fool." Also the inability to answer the ownership
  question, because the covetous man has been treating himself as the owner.
- **Counter-truth.** 1 John 2:15-17 gives the boundary and the reason: the things of the
  world are not of the Father, and the world passes away. The counter is not the absence of
  goods but the absence of love for the world, and the text states the test as a direction of
  love, not a level of wealth.
- **Counter-text.** _Luke 12:15_ — "And he said unto them, Take heed, and beware of
  covetousness: for a man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he
  possesseth." _Luke 12:20_ — "But God said unto him, Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be
  required of thee: then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided?" _1 John
  2:15_ — "Love not the world, neither the things that are in the world. If any man love the
  world, the love of the Father is not in him."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G4124 _pleonexia_, "avarice, i.e. (by
  implication) fraudulency, extortion" — the word is literally "having more"; its dictionary
  sense extends to fraud and extortion, which connects B7 to B6.
- **Related.** B3, B4, B6.

# Group C — Fear and the opinion of others

These patterns corrupt the _social reference_. They answer the question "what will they
think?" before the question "what is true?" and then mistake the answer for a moral fact.

## C1 · The fear of man

_Modern name: social conformity and bandwagon pressure._

- **Description.** The text names the mechanism in two words: a snare. The fear of human
  disapproval is not a feeling to be managed; it is a trap with a trigger. The same verse
  gives the alternative — trust in the LORD — and the alternative's outcome: safe.
- **The lie.** "The cost of their disapproval is real, and the cost of obedience is
  theoretical."
- **Cause.** Disapproval has immediate, material costs: exclusion, loss of work, loss of
  standing. The fear is proportionate to real stakes, which is what makes it a snare rather
  than a phobia.
- **Fruit.** Compromised confession. John 12:42-43 records it exactly: many believed, but
  did not confess, because of the Pharisees, "for they loved the praise of men more than the
  praise of God." The failure is a ranking failure, not an absence of love for God.
  Galatians 1:10 states the terminal cost: to please men is to cease to be the servant of
  Christ.
- **Counter-truth.** The text does not command the absence of fear but its relocation. The
  one who trusts is safe; the one who manages appearances is caught. The counter-practice is
  to price the two costs honestly, which the fear of man never does.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 29:25_ — "The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth
  his trust in the LORD shall be safe." _John 12:42-43_ — "Nevertheless among the chief
  rulers also many believed on him; but because of the Pharisees they did not confess him,
  lest they should be put out of the synagogue: For they loved the praise of men more than
  the praise of God." _Galatians 1:10_ — "For do I now persuade men, or God? or do I seek to
  please men? for if I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: H4034 _magorah_, "affright"; H4170
  _moqesh_, "a noose (for catching animals) (literally or figuratively); by implication, a
  hook (for the nose)" — the snare has a hook in it. _Inference_: that "bandwagon pressure"
  is this pattern at scale.
- **Related.** A5, C2, C3.

## C2 · Following a multitude

_Modern name: herd behaviour and social proof._

- **Description.** The command has two clauses. The first forbids following a crowd _to do
  evil_. The second forbids speaking in a cause so as to "decline after many" — that is,
  letting the number of voices bend testimony. The second clause is the sharper one: a
  majority does not make a statement true.
- **The lie.** "Many people cannot be wrong about this."
- **Cause.** The crowd is a genuine source of information in many domains, and copying
  successful behaviour is often rational. The pattern is that rational heuristic applied
  where it does not apply — to matters of truth and judgement.
- **Fruit.** Wrested judgement, and testimony that bends with the count. Also the erosion of
  the individual's ability to report what they actually saw.
- **Counter-truth.** The text supplies a gate with a count on it, and the count is small:
  "few there be that find it." The counter-practice is to ask what the multitude is being
  used to establish, and whether the number is relevant to the claim.
- **Counter-text.** _Exodus 23:2_ — "Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil; neither
  shalt thou speak in a cause to decline after many to wrest judgment:" _Matthew 7:13-14_ —
  "Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth
  to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and
  narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it." _Proverbs
  29:25_ — "The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall
  be safe."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: Exodus and Matthew. _Inference_: that modern "social proof" is
  this pattern, and that the second clause of Exodus 23:2 is about testimony rather than
  behaviour.
- **Related.** C1, C3, E2.

## C3 · The praise of men

_Modern name: — (the biblical name is the diagnosis)._

- **Description.** John states the motive plainly: they loved the praise of men more than
  the praise of God. Proverbs supplies the diagnostic image: "As the fining pot for silver,
  and the furnace for gold; so is a man to his praise." Praise is the crucible — it is
  applied heat, and it reveals what is in the metal.
- **The lie.** "Approval is a good in itself, and more of it is better."
- **Cause.** Praise is pleasant, cheap to receive, and often accurate. It is also the
  currency of belonging, which makes it hard to refuse.
- **Fruit.** The loss of the ability to receive correction, because correction and praise
  come from the same sources and the person has learned to need the source. Also the
  substitution of the audience's judgement for one's own.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is not the rejection of praise but the reordering of its
  source. John 5:44 states the mechanism: receiving honour one from another makes belief
  difficult, because the honour has already been paid out by the time the true honour is
  offered. The counter-practice is to let praise test rather than feed.
- **Counter-text.** _John 12:43_ — "For they loved the praise of men more than the praise of
  God." _Proverbs 27:21_ — "As the fining pot for silver, and the furnace for gold; so is a
  man to his praise." _John 5:44_ — "How can ye believe, which receive honour one of
  another, and seek not the honour that cometh from God only?"
- **Labels.** _Text states_: all three. _Inference_: the expansion of Proverbs 27:21 from
  its compressed form — the KJV clause does not spell out the comparison, and the reading
  "praise is a test" is the reader's. _Note_: the verse is a proverb, so it states a
  tendency, not a mechanism that fires identically in every case.
- **Related.** C1, C4, D6.

## C4 · Flattery

_Modern name: manipulation through praise._

- **Description.** Flattery is praise with a purpose. The Hebrew behind "flattereth" means
  to be smooth — the flatterer is the one who smooths. Proverbs states the effect: he
  spreads a net for the feet. Psalm 12 adds the internal form: flattering lips and a double
  heart speak together. Daniel records the political use: the corrupt are corrupted by
  flatteries.
- **The lie.** "The compliment is disinterested."
- **Cause.** Flattery is pleasant and often contains something true, which is what makes it
  hard to detect. It also arrives attached to a real offer.
- **Fruit.** The net, and the double heart — the flatterer's own, and the flattered person's
  growing dependence on the smoothing. Daniel 11:32 gives the contrast in the same verse:
  those who do wickedly are corrupted by it, while those who know God are strong and do
  exploits.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is not suspicion of all praise — that would be C1's
  opposite error — but the question of what the praise is attached to. The text's own test
  is whether the person giving it is spreading something for the feet.
- **Counter-text.** _Psalm 12:2-3_ — "They speak vanity every one with his neighbour: with
  flattering lips and with a double heart do they speak. The LORD shall cut off all
  flattering lips, and the tongue that speaketh proud things:" _Proverbs 29:5_ — "A man that
  flattereth his neighbour spreadeth a net for his feet." _Daniel 11:32_ — "And such as do
  wickedly against the covenant shall he corrupt by flatteries: but the people that do know
  their God shall be strong, and do exploits."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: H2505 _chalaq_, "to be smooth
  (figuratively); by implication (as smooth stones were used for lots) to apportion or
  separate" — the KJV gloss list includes "flatter" and "divide" from the same root, which is
  the double heart in a single word.
- **Related.** C3, C1, E4.

## C5 · Fear and anxiety

_Modern name: catastrophizing._

- **Description.** The texts do not forbid prudence; they forbid the anxious rehearsal of
  what has not happened. Matthew 6 names the objects — food, drink, clothing, the morrow —
  and supplies a test: which of you by taking thought can add one cubit unto his stature?
  Philippians 4 supplies the replacement practice: prayer with thanksgiving, then a
  deliberate list of what to think on. Peter supplies the disposal: casting all your care
  upon him.
- **The lie.** "Worry is a form of control. If I rehearse the disaster, I will be ready for
  it."
- **Cause.** Worry is a genuine problem-solving attempt aimed at a problem that cannot be
  solved by rehearsal. It also provides the feeling of responsibility, which is why it feels
  like a duty.
- **Fruit.** The mind occupied by the imagined, the body taxed, and the day's work reduced.
  Psalm 94:19 states the condition and the remedy together: "In the multitude of my thoughts
  within me thy comforts delight my soul."
- **Counter-truth.** The distinction that matters is between prudence and anxiety: the
  prudent man foresees the evil and hides himself (Proverbs 27:12), while the anxious man
  foresees and stays. The text gives four practices: prayer with thanksgiving (Philippians
  4:6), the thought-list (4:8), casting (1 Peter 5:7), and the comfort that answers the
  multitude of thoughts (Psalm 94:19).
- **Counter-text.** _Philippians 4:6_ — "Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by
  prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God."
  _Philippians 4:8_ — "Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are
  honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are
  lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any
  praise, think on these things." _Matthew 6:27_ — "Which of you by taking thought can add
  one cubit unto his stature?" _1 Peter 5:7_ — "Casting all your care upon him; for he
  careth for you." _Psalm 94:19_ — "In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts
  delight my soul."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G3309 _merimnao_, "to be anxious
  about"; G3308 _merimna_, "solicitude", from G3307 _merizo_ "through the idea of
  distraction" — anxiety in the Greek is a mind divided by distraction, which links it to
  the double-mindedness of F5. The KJV's "careful" at Philippians 4:6 is the anxious sense,
  not the attentive sense. _Inference_: the prudence/anxiety distinction — the text does not
  draw it explicitly, and Proverbs 27:12 is the reader's warrant for it.
- **Related.** F5, C6, B3.

## C6 · Unbelief and limiting God

_Modern name: learned helplessness and defeatism._

- **Description.** Psalm 78 records a generation that "turned back and tempted God, and
  limited the Holy One of Israel." Hebrews 3:12 warns against the same condition and calls
  it an evil heart of unbelief that departs from the living God. The two texts describe one
  pattern from two ends: the limit imposed, and the departure that follows.
- **The lie.** "God has a ceiling, and I have already measured it. Hoping for more is
  presumption."
- **Cause.** The limit is usually derived from experience — a real disappointment treated as
  a permanent measurement. It protects against hoping and being disappointed again, which is
  a genuine form of self-protection.
- **Fruit.** The psalm's catalogue: turning back, tempting God, limiting him. Hebrews states
  the endpoint: departing from the living God. The pattern does not merely under-estimate;
  it walks away.
- **Counter-truth.** Hebrews gives the instruction with the warning: "Take heed." The psalm
  gives the alternative in its own summary of the purpose of transmission: "That they might
  set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments." The
  counter is the memory of what was actually done, which is what the limit had erased.
- **Counter-text.** _Psalm 78:41_ — "Yea, they turned back and tempted God, and limited the
  Holy One of Israel." _Hebrews 3:12_ — "Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an
  evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God." _Psalm 78:7_ — "That they might
  set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments:"
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: H6696 _tsur_, "to cramp, i.e. confine
  (in many applications, literally and figuratively, formative or hostile)" — the same verb
  is used of besieging a city; the KJV gloss list includes "besiege", "inclose", "lay
  siege". G570 _apistia_, "faithlessness, i.e. (negatively) disbelief (lack of Christian
  faith), or (positively) unfaithfulness (disobedience)" — in the Greek, unbelief and
  unfaithfulness are one word. _Inference_: that the besieging image is the point of the
  psalm's "limited".
- **Related.** F4, C5, A5.

# Group D — Self-deception

These patterns corrupt the _audit_. The person is not only wrong; they are unable to find
the error, because the instrument doing the finding is the instrument that is wrong.

## D1 · The deceitful heart

_Modern name: self-flattery._

- **Description.** Jeremiah states the condition and the consequence: the heart is deceitful
  above all things, and the question that follows — who can know it? — is not rhetorical
  comfort but a statement of limit. Psalm 36 describes the mechanism: a man flatters himself
  in his own eyes _until_ his iniquity is found to be hateful. Proverbs 30:12 gives the
  result: a generation pure in its own eyes, and not washed.
- **The lie.** "I can audit myself."
- **Cause.** Self-assessment is the only assessment most people have practised, and it has
  a built-in advantage: the auditor and the defendant are the same person, and the auditor
  knows the defendant's story from the inside.
- **Fruit.** Unexamined error, sudden exposure, and an inability to receive correction. The
  psalm's "until" is the operative word — the discovery happens, but not by self-audit.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is not more self-examination; it is examination by another.
  Psalm 139 asks God to do what the heart cannot do for itself: "Search me, O God, and know
  my heart: try me, and know my thoughts." Proverbs states the alternative to self-trust:
  he that trusteth in his own heart is a fool.
- **Counter-text.** _Jeremiah 17:9_ — "The heart is deceitful above all things, and
  desperately wicked: who can know it?" _Psalm 36:2_ — "For he flattereth himself in his own
  eyes, until his iniquity be found to be hateful." _Proverbs 30:12_ — "There is a generation
  that are pure in their own eyes, and yet is not washed from their filthiness." _Proverbs
  28:26_ — "He that trusteth in his own heart is a fool: but whoso walketh wisely, he shall
  be delivered." _Psalm 139:23_ — "Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my
  thoughts:"
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: H6121 _aqob_, "fraudulent or tracked"
  — "deceitful" is a fraudulence word, and it is related to the heel (H6117, to supplant).
  H605 _anash_, "to be frail, feeble, or (figuratively) melancholy", whose KJV glosses
  include "desperate(-ly wicked), incurable, sick, woeful". _Honest note_: the KJV's
  "desperately wicked" renders a word whose dictionary sense is frailty and incurability, not
  intensity of evil. The claim that the heart is beyond human self-repair is _Text states_;
  the claim that it is maximally evil is not what the lexicon says, and should not be
  preached from this verse. H3820 _leb_, "the heart; also used (figuratively) very widely for
  the feelings, the will and even the intellect" — in the Hebrew, the heart is not the
  emotions as opposed to the mind; it includes the intellect, and the KJV gloss list includes
  "mind(-ed)". This matters for the whole skill: renewing the mind is heart work in the
  biblical vocabulary.
- **Related.** A2, D2, F8.

## D2 · The beam and the mote

_Modern name: projection and the fundamental attribution error._

- **Description.** The passage forbids a judgement that has not first passed through
  self-examination, and it does so with a size ratio: a timber beam against a dry straw. The
  final verse commands the correction of the brother _after_ the beam is removed — the
  procedure enables the correction, it does not cancel it.
- **The lie.** "My vision is clear because my error is small."
- **Cause.** Other people's faults are visible; one's own are not, for the structural reason
  that one sees one's own behaviour from the inside, as intention, and other people's from
  the outside, as act. The pattern is a real asymmetry misread as a moral difference.
- **Fruit.** Hypocrisy, the double standard, and correction that cannot be received because
  it is delivered by a person with an unaddressed beam. The text's word for it is
  "hypocrite" — the actor wearing a mask.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter-procedure is stated: first cast out the beam, then see
  clearly to cast out the mote. Galatians 6:1 supplies the manner — restore in the spirit of
  meekness, considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted. The goal is the brother's
  restoration, not the beam-holder's vindication.
- **Counter-text.** _Matthew 7:3_ — "And why beholdest thou the mote that is in thy
  brother's eye, but considerest not the beam that is in thine own eye?" _Matthew 7:5_ —
  "Thou hypocrite, first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see
  clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye." _Galatians 6:1_ — "Brethren, if a
  man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of
  meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passage. _Lexical_: G1385 _dokos_, "a stick of timber";
  G2595 _karphos_, "a dry twig or straw"; G3358 _metron_, "a measure ('metre'), literally or
  figuratively; by implication, a limited portion (degree)" — the same word for measure as
  2 Corinthians 10:12 and the two wisdoms test. _Inference_: that Matthew 7:1 does not forbid
  judgement as such; John 7:24 commands it, and 7:5 commands it after the beam is out. The
  passage forbids a _procedure_, not the faculty.
- **Related.** A4, D5, and the guards section.

## D3 · Excusing with the sluggard's reason

_Modern name: rationalisation and the lion in the street._

- **Description.** The sluggard gives a reason: there is a lion in the way. The proverb does
  not say the lion is imaginary. It says the risk is _used_ as a reason not to move. The
  second proverb repeats it with the outcome attached: "I shall be slain in the streets."
- **The lie.** "A valid reason and a covering reason are the same thing."
- **Cause.** Rationalisation works because it uses true premises. There are real lions. The
  error is not in the premise but in the inference, which is why it survives self-examination
  — the person can point to the lion.
- **Fruit.** The door on its hinges — motion without progress. And the diagnostic in verse
  16: the sluggard is "wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can render a reason." The
  defence is not laziness but superior reasoning, which is why it must be answered with an
  argument rather than an exhortation.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is the same foresight used differently. Proverbs 27:12 has
  the prudent man foresee the evil and hide himself; the simple pass on and are punished. The
  difference between the two is not whether the risk was seen but what was done after seeing
  it. The counter-practice is to ask what the lion would require if it were real, and whether
  the plan includes that.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 26:13_ — "The slothful man saith, There is a lion in the way; a
  lion is in the streets." _Proverbs 26:16_ — "The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit than
  seven men that can render a reason." _Proverbs 22:13_ — "The slothful man saith, There is a
  lion without, I shall be slain in the streets." _Proverbs 27:12_ — "A prudent man
  foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself; but the simple pass on, and are punished."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the proverbs. _Inference_: that the mechanism is rationalisation
  rather than fear — the text does not name the mechanism, and the reading is the reader's.
- **Related.** A4, D4, F3.

## D4 · Partial obedience

_Modern name: self-serving interpretation._

- **Description.** 1 Samuel 15 is the canon's most detailed case study of self-serving
  interpretation, and it is _description_, not command. The sequence: Saul claims full
  obedience (15:13); the evidence contradicts him audibly (15:14); he reinterprets the
  remainder as a higher obedience (15:15, "to sacrifice unto the LORD thy God"); he restates
  the claim flatly (15:20); and Samuel gives the verdict (15:22-23).
- **The lie.** "The part I did is the whole. The part I did not do is a special case."
- **Cause.** The reinterpretation is not random; it keeps the parts of the command that are
  cheap and converts the remainder into something the person was already going to do. It
  preserves both the appearance of obedience and the desired outcome.
- **Fruit.** Rejection. The text's verdict is severe and explicit: "Because thou hast
  rejected the word of the LORD, he hath also rejected thee from being king."
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is stated in the same passage: "to obey is better than
  sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams." The counter-practice is to check the
  original instruction against the report, and to treat the gap as the object of attention
  rather than as a special case.
- **Counter-text.** _1 Samuel 15:13_ — "And Samuel came to Saul: and Saul said unto him,
  Blessed be thou of the LORD: I have performed the commandment of the LORD." _1 Samuel
  15:22_ — "And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and
  sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice,
  and to hearken than the fat of rams." _1 Samuel 15:23_ — "For rebellion is as the sin of
  witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry. Because thou hast rejected the
  word of the LORD, he hath also rejected thee from being king."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the narrative. _Description, not prescription_: the passage
  reports what Saul did; it does not command it. _Inference_: that the mechanism generalises
  to any instruction with an inconvenient remainder.
- **Related.** D3, D1, A2.

## D5 · Evil surmisings

_Modern name: hostile attribution and mind-reading._

- **Description.** Paul lists "evil surmisings" among the fruits of a certain kind of
  argumentation, alongside envy, strife, and railings. The word for surmisings is suspicion.
  The pattern is the assignment of a hostile motive that was never stated and never verified.
- **The lie.** "I know what they meant. My inference about their motive is knowledge."
- **Cause.** Motive-inference is fast, and it is often right, which is what makes it
  credible. It is also useful: a hostile motive justifies a hostile response in advance.
- **Fruit.** Strife, railings, and the breakdown of trust. The damage is disproportionate,
  because the accusation cannot be answered — the accused is asked to prove a negative.
- **Counter-truth.** The text reserves the reading of motive to God: the LORD pondereth the
  hearts, and man looks on the outward appearance. The counter-practice is to judge the act
  and its fruit, which are available, and to state the inferred motive as an inference.
  1 Corinthians 13:5 supplies the disposition: charity "thinketh no evil."
- **Counter-text.** _1 Timothy 6:4_ — "He is proud, knowing nothing, but doting about
  questions and strifes of words, whereof cometh envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings,"
  _1 Corinthians 13:5_ — "Doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily
  provoked, thinketh no evil;" _Proverbs 21:2_ — "Every way of a man is right in his own
  eyes: but the LORD pondereth the hearts." _1 Samuel 16:7_ — "for man looketh on the outward
  appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G5283 _hyponoia_, "suspicion" — the
  KJV's "surmisings" is suspicion; G3552 _noseo_, "to be sick, i.e. (by implication, of a
  diseased appetite) to hanker after (figuratively, to harp upon)" — the KJV's "doting"
  describes a diseased appetite harping on a subject. _Inference_: the distinction between
  discernment of acts and mind-reading of motives — the text does not draw the line
  explicitly, and the reading is the reader's.
- **Related.** D2, A6, C4.

## D6 · Pride deceiving the heart

_Modern name: — (the biblical name is the diagnosis)._

- **Description.** Obadiah names the mechanism: "The pride of thine heart hath deceived
  thee." The deception has content — the security of a high position ("Who shall bring me
  down to the ground?"). Proverbs states the pattern in general form: pride goes before
  destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.
- **The lie.** "My position is secure because it is high."
- **Cause.** Height is genuinely protective in many systems, and the proud person's
  assessment of their position is usually accurate at the moment they make it. The error is
  the extrapolation.
- **Fruit.** Destruction and the fall. The text gives the counter in the same form:
  Proverbs 18:12 — "Before destruction the heart of man is haughty, and before honour is
  humility."
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is not the estimate of low position but the transfer of
  trust. The pride of the heart deceives because the heart is doing the estimating. The
  remedy is a different estimator.
- **Counter-text.** _Obadiah 1:3_ — "The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee, thou that
  dwellest in the clefts of the rock, whose habitation is high; that saith in his heart, Who
  shall bring me down to the ground?" _Obadiah 1:4_ — "Though thou exalt thyself as the
  eagle, and though thou set thy nest among the stars, thence will I bring thee down, saith
  the LORD." _Proverbs 16:18_ — "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit
  before a fall." _Proverbs 18:12_ — "Before destruction the heart of man is haughty, and
  before honour is humility."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Note_: Proverbs 16:18 and 18:12 are proverbs —
  they state tendencies, not a mechanism that fires identically in every case, and they are
  not promises that every proud person falls immediately.
- **Related.** A5, C3, F8.

## D7 · Making provision for the flesh

_Modern name: designing your own environment to fail._

- **Description.** The command is not "avoid the flesh" but "make not provision for it."
  Provision is logistics — supply, arrangement, forethought. The verse forbids arranging the
  circumstances in advance on behalf of the appetite. The positive half of the same verse
  gives the replacement supply: "put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ."
- **The lie.** "I can be near the occasion and still be free of it."
- **Cause.** The provision is usually made for an innocent reason — the route passes the
  place, the meeting is at the hour, the device is in the room. Each step is defensible and
  the arrangement is not.
- **Fruit.** The planned fall. The failure is not a moment of weakness; it is a decision
  already made and stored in the environment, waiting for the moment of weakness to arrive.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is a different provision, not a deprivation: put on Christ,
  which is the same kind of act — deliberate supply in advance. Proverbs 4:23 states the
  general form: keep thy heart with all diligence. Psalm 101:3 and Job 31:1 supply the
  specific practice — a decision made about the eyes before the occasion arrives.
- **Counter-text.** _Romans 13:14_ — "But put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not
  provision for the flesh, to fulfil the lusts thereof." _Proverbs 4:23_ — "Keep thy heart
  with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life." _Psalm 101:3_ — "I will set no
  wicked thing before mine eyes: I hate the work of them that turn aside; it shall not cleave
  to me." _Job 31:1_ — "I made a covenant with mine eyes; why then should I think upon a
  maid?"
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G4307 _pronoia_, "forethought, i.e.
  provident care or supply" — the same word is used positively of providence, so the verse
  forbids forethought _on behalf of_ the flesh, not forethought as such. G4561 _sarx_,
  "flesh (as stripped of the skin), i.e. (strictly) the meat of an animal (as food), or (by
  extension) the body (as opposed to the soul (or spirit), or as the symbol of what is
  external, or as the means of kindred), or (by implication) human nature (with its frailties
  (physically or morally) and passions), or (specially), a human being (as such)". _Inference_:
  that the modern "environment design" practice is the same mechanism, read in either
  direction.
- **Related.** D3, F6, B2.

# Group E — Words and information

These patterns corrupt the _medium_. They are failures in how words are produced, consumed,
and inherited. They matter because the previous groups travel through them.

## E1 · Vain jangling and endless questions

_Modern name: analysis paralysis and doomscrolling._

- **Description.** Four texts name a family of failures: babble (1 Timothy 1:6), a diseased
  appetite for questions (6:4), questions that "gender strifes" (2 Timothy 2:23), and
  questions that are "unprofitable and vain" (Titus 3:9). The common feature is not the
  difficulty of the questions but their fruit.
- **The lie.** "More questions is the same as more understanding. Engagement is progress."
- **Cause.** Questioning feels like diligence and looks like humility. It also defers the
  cost of a conclusion, which is the real attraction — a person with an open question cannot
  be held to an answer.
- **Fruit.** The list in 1 Timothy 6:4: envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings. Also the
  navigational failure of 1 Timothy 1:6 — "some having swerved have turned aside."
- **Counter-truth.** The text supplies an end, not merely a limit: "the end of the
  commandment is charity out of a pure heart." The questions are to be measured against that
  end. The instruction in 2 Timothy and Titus is _avoid_, which is a command to disengage —
  the counter-practice is not more analysis but action.
- **Counter-text.** _1 Timothy 1:6_ — "From which some having swerved have turned aside unto
  vain jangling;" _1 Timothy 1:5_ — "Now the end of the commandment is charity out of a pure
  heart, and of a good conscience, and of faith unfeigned:" _1 Timothy 6:4_ — "He is proud,
  knowing nothing, but doting about questions and strifes of words, whereof cometh envy,
  strife, railings, evil surmisings," _2 Timothy 2:23_ — "But foolish and unlearned questions
  avoid, knowing that they do gender strifes." _Titus 3:9_ — "But avoid foolish questions,
  and genealogies, and contentions, and strivings about the law; for they are unprofitable
  and vain."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G3150 _mataiologia_, "random talk, i.e.
  babble"; G3552 _noseo_, "to be sick, i.e. (by implication, of a diseased appetite) to
  hanker after (figuratively, to harp upon)". _Inference_: that the "doomscroll" is this
  pattern with an infinite supply — the text names the appetite, not the medium.
- **Related.** E2, E3, A3.

## E2 · Itching ears

_Modern name: echo chambers and algorithmic feeds._

- **Description.** The mechanism in the text is demand-side: people "after their own lusts
  shall heap to themselves teachers." The selection is driven by desire, and the supply is
  multiplied. The result is stated: they will not endure sound doctrine, and they turn from
  truth to fables.
- **The lie.** "What I want to hear is what I need to hear."
- **Cause.** The tickle is pleasant and the sound doctrine is not. "Itching" is the lexicon's
  word for it — the desire for the scratch, which is satisfied by the scratch and returns.
- **Fruit.** Intolerance of sound doctrine, and the exchange of truth for fiction. The word
  for fables is _mythos_ — the same word that gives "myth".
- **Counter-truth.** The counter-practice is stated in the same passage: preach the word,
  reprove, rebuke, exhort. The test of a teacher is not whether the teaching is agreeable but
  whether it is sound — _hugiaino_, healthy. A diagnostic follows from this: if nothing you
  read contradicts you, you are being tickled, not taught. That diagnostic is an _Inference_.
- **Counter-text.** _2 Timothy 4:3-4_ — "For the time will come when they will not endure
  sound doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having
  itching ears; And they shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be turned unto
  fables." _2 Timothy 4:2_ — "Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove,
  rebuke, exhort with all longsuffering and doctrine." _2 Timothy 3:16_ — "All scripture is
  given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction,
  for instruction in righteousness:"
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G2833 _knetho_, "to scratch, i.e. (by
  implication) to tickle"; G5198 _hugiaino_, "to have sound health, i.e. be well (in body);
  figuratively, to be uncorrupt (true in doctrine)"; G3454 _mythos_, "a tale, i.e. fiction
  ('myth')". _Inference_: that a recommendation algorithm is this mechanism at industrial
  scale, because the ranking function learns preference, and preference is not need.
  _Contested_: the strength of filter-bubble and echo-chamber effects on individuals is
  disputed in the research literature; the tools here cannot measure it, and the claim should
  be held at the level of mechanism, not magnitude.
- **Related.** E1, E5, A8.

## E3 · Talebearing and whispering

_Modern name: gossip and rumour._

- **Description.** Four texts describe the same traffic. The talebearer's words are "as
  wounds" and go "into the innermost parts of the belly" — the damage is internal and
  lasting. The whisperer "separateth chief friends." Where there is no talebearer, the strife
  ceases. Leviticus states the prohibition directly.
- **The lie.** "Passing it on is not the same as inventing it."
- **Cause.** The information is often true, which is what makes the pattern defensible to the
  person carrying it. It also purchases attention and a temporary intimacy with the listener.
- **Fruit.** Separated friends, kindled strife, and wounds that go down into the innermost
  parts. The word for the whisperer also means to grumble and rebel — the whisper is a form
  of insurrection, not merely idle talk.
- **Counter-truth.** The remedy in the text is subtraction, not correction: remove the wood
  and the fire goes out. The counter-practice is to refuse the role of carrier, which is
  different from refusing to hear — the text's instruction is to the talebearer, and the
  listener's part is to stop being the fuel.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 16:28_ — "A froward man soweth strife: and a whisperer
  separateth chief friends." _Proverbs 18:8_ — "The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and
  they go down into the innermost parts of the belly." _Proverbs 26:20_ — "Where no wood is,
  there the fire goeth out: so where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth." _Leviticus
  19:16_ — "Thou shalt not go up and down as a talebearer among thy people: neither shalt
  thou stand against the blood of thy neighbour: I am the LORD."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: H5372 _nirgan_, "a slanderer"; H7400
  _rakil_, "a scandal-monger (as travelling about)"; H7279 _ragan_, "to grumble, i.e. rebel"
  — the KJV renders H7279 as "whisperer" and the dictionary sense is murmuring and rebellion.
- **Related.** E4, D5, F6.

## E4 · Swelling words

_Modern name: hype and jargon._

- **Description.** The image is inflation — a thing larger than its content. 2 Peter calls
  them "great swelling words of vanity"; the lexicon's sense for "swelling" is "bulging over."
  Ephesians calls the same phenomenon "vain words", and the word for vain is _empty_. Swelling
  and empty are the same object seen from two sides: it looks large and contains nothing.
- **The lie.** "The size of the language is the size of the thing."
- **Cause.** Large language signals confidence, and confidence is often a genuine signal of
  competence. The pattern is that signal detached from the thing it was supposed to indicate.
- **Fruit.** Allurement through lusts (2 Peter 2:18), and in Jude the motive is named: the
  people who use it have "men's persons in admiration because of advantage." The swelling
  words are not vanity in the sense of uselessness; they are a sales instrument.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is not crude speech but plain speech. Matthew 5:37 gives the
  standard: let your communication be Yea, yea; Nay, nay — the words should carry exactly
  what is promised. Note the false friend: the KJV's "communication" here is speech, while in
  1 Corinthians 15:33 the same English word renders _homilia_, which means companionship, not
  speech.
- **Counter-text.** _2 Peter 2:18_ — "For when they speak great swelling words of vanity,
  they allure through the lusts of the flesh, through much wantonness, those that were clean
  escaped from them who live in error." _Jude 1:16_ — "These are murmurers, complainers,
  walking after their own lusts; and their mouth speaketh great swelling words, having men's
  persons in admiration because of advantage." _Ephesians 5:6_ — "Let no man deceive you
  with vain words: for because of these things cometh the wrath of God upon the children of
  disobedience." _Matthew 5:37_ — "But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for
  whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G5246 _hyperonkos_, "bulging over, i.e.
  (figuratively) insolent"; G2756 _kenos_, "empty (literally or figuratively)"; G3657
  _homilia_, "companionship ('homily'), i.e. (by implication) intercourse" — the KJV renders
  it "communication" at 1 Corinthians 15:33, where the sense is association, not speech.
  _Inference_: that technical jargon is not automatically swelling words; the test is whether
  the term adds information. The text does not draw that line.
- **Related.** E1, E6, E7.

## E5 · Tradition and the commandments of men

_Modern name: institutional inertia, and "we have always done it this way."_

- **Description.** The texts do not condemn tradition as such. They condemn tradition set
  _against_ the commandment: "ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own
  tradition." The human rule is used to nullify the divine rule. Isaiah adds a second form:
  the fear of God itself can be "taught by the precept of men" — reverence transmitted as a
  technique.
- **The lie.** "Age is authority. We have always done it this way."
- **Cause.** Inherited practice is usually the compressed wisdom of people who faced the same
  problem. The pattern is that wisdom detached from its reason, which then cannot be
  re-examined and cannot be told apart from the rule that merely protects the institution.
- **Fruit.** Worship in vain, the commandment made of none effect, and being carried off as
  plunder (Colossians 2:8 — the verb is a looting word, "to lead away as booty").
- **Counter-truth.** The test in Colossians is the source: after the tradition of men, after
  the rudiments of the world, or after Christ. The counter-practice is to ask what a rule was
  for, and whether the answer is still true — which the rule itself cannot supply.
- **Counter-text.** _Matthew 15:3_ — "But he answered and said unto them, Why do ye also
  transgress the commandment of God by your tradition?" _Matthew 15:6_ — "And honour not his
  father or his mother, he shall be free. Thus have ye made the commandment of God of none
  effect by your tradition." _Matthew 15:9_ — "But in vain they do worship me, teaching for
  doctrines the commandments of men." _Mark 7:9_ — "And he said unto them, Full well ye reject
  the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition." _Colossians 2:8_ — "Beware
  lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men,
  after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ." _Isaiah 29:13_ — "Wherefore the
  Lord said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do
  honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by
  the precept of men:"
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G3862 _paradosis_, "transmission, i.e.
  (concretely) a precept; specially, the Jewish traditionary law" — the word is transmission,
  so the authority claimed is the fact that it was handed down; G1778 _entalma_, "an
  injunction, i.e. religious precept". _Inference_: the application to schooling is the
  reader's, and it is partly _Contested_ — see `institutions.md`. The text's own target is
  religious tradition, not education as such.
- **Related.** E6, E7, A1.

## E6 · Philosophy and vain deceit

_Modern name: — (the biblical name is the diagnosis)._

- **Description.** Colossians 2:8 gives a warning and a test. The warning is that a person
  can be carried off as plunder; the test is the source — "after the tradition of men, after
  the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ." The verse is not a general prohibition
  of thinking; it is a criterion for systems.
- **The lie.** "A system's sophistication is evidence of its truth."
- **Cause.** Coherent systems are impressive, and coherence is a genuine virtue. The pattern
  is coherence treated as sufficient, when the text's test is the source.
- **Fruit.** Being spoiled — carried off as booty. The word describes a person being
  plundered while believing they are being educated.
- **Counter-truth.** The criterion is stated: after Christ. The same letter supplies the
  positive content elsewhere, and the counter-practice is to ask of any system what it takes
  as its first principle, and whether that first principle is Christ or a rudiment.
- **Counter-text.** _Colossians 2:8_ — "Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and
  vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after
  Christ." _Colossians 2:18_ — "Let no man beguile you of your reward in a voluntary humility
  and worshipping of angels, intruding into those things which he hath not seen, vainly
  puffed up by his fleshly mind,"
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passage. _Lexical_: G5385 _philosophia_, "'philosophy',
  i.e. (specially), Jewish sophistry" — the dictionary's specific sense is Jewish sophistry,
  so the verse's target is a particular religious-philosophical syncretism, not philosophy as
  an academic discipline; G4747 _stoicheion_, "something orderly in arrangement, i.e. (by
  implication) a serial (basal, fundamental, initial) constituent (literally), proposition
  (figuratively)" — the "rudiments" are elementary constituents, the ABCs of a system;
  G4812 _sylagogeo_, "to lead away as booty, i.e. (figuratively) seduce". _Honest note_: the
  verse is often preached as a blanket condemnation of philosophy; the lexical data does not
  support that. It condemns a system whose elementary principles are not Christ.
- **Related.** E5, E7, E1.

## E7 · Science falsely so called

_Modern name: claims wearing the authority of method without the method._

- **Description.** Paul instructs Timothy to avoid "oppositions of science falsely so called."
  The fault is in the naming: something is called knowledge that is not knowledge. The Greek
  word rendered "science" here is _gnosis_, the ordinary word for knowledge — not a term for
  the modern institution.
- **The lie.** "The label 'study' or 'science' or 'research' transfers authority to the claim."
- **Cause.** The method has earned its authority by working. The pattern borrows that
  authority without doing the work — a claim is presented in the grammar of findings while
  the method that would produce a finding is absent.
- **Fruit.** "Oppositions" — the counterfeit produces contention rather than knowledge. It
  also produces the opposite error in the same person: the rejection of verified findings
  because they arrive from a distrusted source. That is A8 with the sign flipped, and it is
  the same failure.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is the test: prove all things; hold fast that which is good.
  The counter-practice is to ask what the claim is actually based on, and to accept or reject
  on that basis rather than on the label.
- **Counter-text.** _1 Timothy 6:20_ — "O Timothy, keep that which is committed to thy trust,
  avoiding profane and vain babblings, and oppositions of science falsely so called:"
  _1 Thessalonians 5:21_ — "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good." _Proverbs
  14:15_ — "The simple believeth every word: but the prudent man looketh well to his going."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passage. _Lexical_: G1108 _gnosis_, "knowing (the act), i.e.
  (by implication) knowledge", KJV gloss "knowledge, science"; G5581 _pseudonymos_, "untruly
  named". _Honest note_: this verse is frequently used to dismiss science as an institution.
  The lexical data does not support that reading — the target is claims that are untruly
  named knowledge, not knowledge obtained by testing. _Inference_: that the same criticism
  applies in the other direction, to claims made in the name of "research" or "data" without
  the method.
- **Related.** A8, E4, E5.

# Group F — Character of the thinker

These patterns corrupt the _person doing the thinking_. They are not errors in a particular
judgement; they are conditions that determine what kind of judgements a person can make. The
first entry is the test for the rest.

## F1 · The two wisdoms

_Modern name: — (the test is the biblical name)._

- **Description.** James 3:13-18 presents two wisdoms and gives each one's fruit. The one
  "descendeth not from above" is earthly, sensual, devilish, and produces confusion and every
  evil work. The one from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, easy to be entreated,
  full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy. The test is
  fruit-based: a wisdom is known by what it produces.
- **The lie.** "The quality of an argument is independent of its effect. A true idea can
  produce chaos and still be true."
- **Cause.** The pattern is attractive because some true statements do disturb. The text does
  not say every disturbing truth is false; it says the wisdom from above has a particular
  character, and the character is testable.
- **Fruit.** The text names it directly: "where envying and strife is, there is confusion and
  every evil work." The word for confusion is instability and disorder. The wisdom from above
  produces the opposite list.
- **Counter-truth.** The order in verse 17 matters: "first pure, then peaceable." Purity
  precedes peace; peace is not obtained by abandoning purity. The counter-practice is the
  full list, not one item from it.
- **Counter-text.** _James 3:14-16_ — "But if ye have bitter envying and strife in your
  hearts, glory not, and lie not against the truth. This wisdom descendeth not from above,
  but is earthly, sensual, devilish. For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and
  every evil work." _James 3:17_ — "But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then
  peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without
  partiality, and without hypocrisy." _James 3:18_ — "And the fruit of righteousness is sown
  in peace of them that make peace."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passage. _Lexical_: G5591 _psychikos_, "sensitive, i.e.
  animate (in distinction on the one hand from G4152 (pneumatikos), which is the higher or
  renovated nature; and on the other from G5446 (physikos), which is the lower or bestial
  nature)" — the KJV's "sensual" means soulish or merely animate, not sexual; it is the
  middle level, life without the Spirit; G181 _akatastasia_, "instability, i.e. disorder" —
  the KJV's "confusion" is instability; G2138 _eupeithes_, "good for persuasion, i.e.
  (intransitively) complaint", KJV "easy to be intreated" — the definition as returned reads
  oddly (apparently for "compliant"), and is quoted as returned rather than smoothed;
  G87 _adiakritos_, "properly, undistinguished, i.e. (actively) impartial". _Description_:
  the passage is instruction to wisdom-claimants, and the test is applied to a wisdom, not
  to a person's standing. See the two wisdoms test below.
- **Related.** the two wisdoms test; all groups.

## F2 · Despising the day of small things

_Modern name: — (the biblical name is the diagnosis)._

- **Description.** The question is rhetorical: "who hath despised the day of small things?"
  The context is the rebuilding of the temple, where the foundation had been laid and the
  older men wept because it was smaller than the first house. The text pronounces a blessing
  on those who see the plummet in the builder's hand — the measuring instrument, in use.
- **The lie.** "Small work is not real work. A serious person waits for a serious stage."
- **Cause.** Small work is invisible, and visibility is a genuine good. The pattern trades
  the work for the appearance of the work, which is why it can look like high standards.
- **Fruit.** Abandoned foundations and the refusal to begin. Also the inability to improve,
  since improvement requires a first small version to improve.
- **Counter-truth.** The text's counter is the plummet in the hand — measurement and progress
  on a small structure. Proverbs 24:3-4 gives the sequence: build, establish, fill. Luke 16:10
  states the rule: faithfulness in the least is the same disposition as faithfulness in much.
- **Counter-text.** _Zechariah 4:10_ — "For who hath despised the day of small things? for
  they shall rejoice, and shall see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel with those seven;
  they are the eyes of the LORD, which run to and fro through the whole earth." _Proverbs
  24:3-4_ — "Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established: And
  by knowledge shall the chambers be filled with all precious and pleasant riches." _Luke
  16:10_ — "He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that
  is unjust in the least is unjust also in much."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Inference_: the application of Zechariah's
  temple-rebuilding scene to ordinary work — the text's own subject is the temple, and the
  generalisation is the reader's.
- **Related.** F3, F4, B5.

## F3 · Complacency and ease

_Modern name: — (the biblical name is the diagnosis)._

- **Description.** Amos pronounces woe on those "at ease in Zion" who trust in the mountain.
  Proverbs states the mechanism: the turning away of the simple shall slay them, and the
  prosperity of fools shall destroy them. The word for "at ease" means secure, and in a bad
  sense haughty.
- **The lie.** "Comfort is safety."
- **Cause.** Ease is pleasant and is usually earned. The pattern is earned rest treated as a
  permanent condition, which removes the pressure that produced the learning.
- **Fruit.** The text names it: destruction, and being slain by one's own prosperity. The
  mechanism is that prosperity removes the feedback that would have corrected the person.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is not the rejection of rest but the rejection of false
  security: "let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall." The counter-practice
  is to keep a live test in place during good conditions.
- **Counter-text.** _Amos 6:1_ — "Woe to them that are at ease in Zion, and trust in the
  mountain of Samaria, which are named chief of the nations, to whom the house of Israel
  came!" _Proverbs 1:32_ — "For the turning away of the simple shall slay them, and the
  prosperity of fools shall destroy them." _1 Corinthians 10:12_ — "Wherefore let him that
  thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: H7600 _sha'anan_, "secure; in a bad
  sense, haughty". _Inference_: that the mechanism is feedback removal — the text states the
  outcome, not the mechanism.
- **Related.** F4, D6, C6.

## F4 · Hasting in the day of adversity

_Modern name: — (the biblical name is the diagnosis)._

- **Description.** The proverb is diagnostic rather than exhortative: "If thou faint in the
  day of adversity, thy strength is small." It does not say the strength is small because of
  the adversity; it says the adversity revealed what was already true.
- **The lie.** "The pressure is proof that the work is wrong. Difficulty is a signal to
  stop."
- **Cause.** Difficulty is genuinely a signal in some cases, and quitting is genuinely right
  in some cases. The pattern applies a sometimes-valid rule universally, and it does so at
  the moment when judgement is most impaired.
- **Fruit.** Abandoned work at the moment it is most needed, and the misreading of a test as
  a verdict. James supplies the correct reading of the same event: the trying of faith works
  patience.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is the distinction between a test and a verdict. The proverb
  states that the day of adversity _reveals_ strength; James states that the trial
  _produces_ endurance. Isaiah 28:16 supplies the disposition: he that believeth shall not
  make haste.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 24:10_ — "If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength
  is small." _James 1:2-4_ — "My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers
  temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience
  have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing." _Isaiah 28:16_
  — "Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD, Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a
  tried stone, a precious corner stone, a sure foundation: he that believeth shall not make
  haste."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: H7503 _raphah_, "to slacken (in many
  applications, literal or figurative)" — the KJV's "faint" is to slacken, and the same word
  is rendered "slothful" elsewhere in the KJV gloss list. The failure is the loosening of
  grip, not an emotional collapse. _Inference_: that "faint" and "slothful" are one condition
  rather than two — the lexicon supports the shared word, and the identification is the
  reader's.
- **Related.** C6, F3, B5.

## F5 · Wavering and double-mindedness

_Modern name: — (the biblical name is the diagnosis)._

- **Description.** James describes a man who asks and does not receive, and gives the reason:
  he asks "nothing wavering," and the wavering man is like a wave driven and tossed. The
  condition is named at the end: "A double minded man is unstable in all his ways."
- **The lie.** "I can keep both options and postpone the judgement. Suspended judgement is
  neutral."
- **Cause.** Postponing a decision avoids the cost of being wrong. The pattern is attractive
  precisely because both options remain available, and the unavailability of one option is
  what makes decisions expensive.
- **Fruit.** Instability in all his ways, and nothing received. Note that the instability is
  general — the text says _all_ his ways, not only the matter in question.
- **Counter-truth.** The remedy in the text is not more information but asking in faith. The
  counter-practice is to close the question, which the text treats as a condition of
  receiving. Elijah states the same demand in the form of a question: "How long halt ye
  between two opinions?"
- **Counter-text.** _James 1:5-6_ — "If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that
  giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him. But let him ask
  in faith, nothing wavering. For he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea driven with the
  wind and tossed." _James 1:8_ — "A double minded man is unstable in all his ways."
  _1 Kings 18:21_ — "And Elijah came unto all the people, and said, How long halt ye between
  two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him. And the people
  answered him not a word."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: G1252 _diakrino_, "to separate
  thoroughly, i.e. (literally and reflexively) to withdraw from, or (by implication) oppose;
  figuratively, to discriminate (by implication, decide), or (reflexively) hesitate" — the
  word is built on _krino_, to judge, so the wavering man is one who cannot judge;
  G1374 _dipsychos_, "two-spirited, i.e. vacillating (in opinion or purpose)".
- **Related.** C5, F1, A2.

## F6 · Bad company

_Modern name: social contagion._

- **Description.** Proverbs states the rule: the one who walks with wise men will be wise,
  and the companion of fools will be destroyed. The second proverb is more specific — do not
  make friendship with an angry man, lest thou learn his ways and get a snare to thy soul.
  Paul states the principle in a sentence: "evil communications corrupt good manners."
- **The lie.** "I can be near without being changed. My character is independent of my
  company."
- **Cause.** Companionship is a genuine good, and loyalty is a genuine virtue. The pattern is
  loyalty to a person extended to imitation of the person, which is what proximity does
  regardless of intention.
- **Fruit.** Destruction, learned ways, a snare to the soul, and corrupted manners. The psalm
  gives the positive form of the same law: blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel
  of the ungodly.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is not the rejection of people but the choice of counsel.
  The text's verbs are walk, stand, sit — degrees of proximity — and the blessed man's
  alternative is delight in the law, which occupies the place the counsel would have taken.
- **Counter-text.** _Proverbs 13:20_ — "He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a
  companion of fools shall be destroyed." _1 Corinthians 15:33_ — "Be not deceived: evil
  communications corrupt good manners." _Proverbs 22:24-25_ — "Make no friendship with an
  angry man; and with a furious man thou shalt not go: Lest thou learn his ways, and get a
  snare to thy soul." _Psalm 1:1-2_ — "Blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of
  the ungodly, nor standeth in the way of sinners, nor sitteth in the seat of the scornful.
  But his delight is in the law of the LORD; and in his law doth he meditate day and night."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Lexical_: H7462 _ra'ah_, "to tend a flock; i.e.
  pasture it; intransitively, to graze (literally or figuratively); generally to rule; by
  extension, to associate with (as a friend)" — the verb for walking with a companion is the
  shepherding verb: you graze where they graze. G3657 _homilia_, "companionship ('homily'),
  i.e. (by implication) intercourse" — the KJV's "evil communications" at 1 Corinthians 15:33
  means evil companionships, not evil speech. This is a 1611 false friend; the verse is not
  about bad language.
- **Related.** D7, E3, F1.

## F7 · Broken cisterns

_Modern name: substitutes that cannot hold value._

- **Description.** Jeremiah states two evils in one sentence: forsaking the fountain of
  living waters, and hewing out cisterns. The cisterns are not merely inadequate; they are
  broken, so they cannot hold what is put in them. And they are _hewn_ — labour has been
  invested in the substitute.
- **The lie.** "I can store the living water in a container of my own design."
- **Cause.** The cistern is designed by the person who needs it, which makes it feel
  appropriate. The work of hewing it is also real work, which gives the substitute the
  appearance of a serious project.
- **Fruit.** Thirst, and labour without supply. The water is not lost because it was never
  given; it is lost because the vessel cannot hold it.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is the fountain, and the text's own image for it is
  springing, not stored: the water given by Christ is "a well of water springing up into
  everlasting life." A spring does not need a cistern.
- **Counter-text.** _Jeremiah 2:13_ — "For my people have committed two evils; they have
  forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns,
  that can hold no water." _John 4:14_ — "But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall
  give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of
  water springing up into everlasting life."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Inference_: that the pattern generalises from
  idols to any substitute source — the text's own subject is the forsaking of God for idols,
  and the generalisation is the reader's.
- **Related.** F8, B7, B4.

## F8 · Feeding on ashes

_Modern name: deception that arrives with self-justification._

- **Description.** Isaiah 44 describes the maker of an idol, who uses half the wood for fuel
  and the other half for a god, and then cannot ask whether the thing in his own hand is a
  lie. The verse names the mechanism: "a deceived heart hath turned him aside." The deception
  is what prevents the diagnosis.
- **The lie.** "The substitute is working."
- **Cause.** The substitute genuinely provides something — the idol-maker is fed and warmed
  by the same tree. The deception is not that the object does nothing; it is that the object
  is credited with what the real source provided.
- **Fruit.** The inability to self-diagnose — "he cannot deliver his soul, nor say, Is there
  not a lie in my right hand?" The lie is _in his right hand_: it is what he is holding and
  using. And the food is ashes.
- **Counter-truth.** The counter is the question the deceived person cannot ask, which is why
  it must be asked by another. The chapter's own remedy is forgiveness and return — the
  deception is not answered by shame but by the offer that makes return possible — and John
  supplies the practice: keep yourselves from idols.
- **Counter-text.** _Isaiah 44:20_ — "He feedeth on ashes: a deceived heart hath turned him
  aside, that he cannot deliver his soul, nor say, Is there not a lie in my right hand?"
  _Isaiah 44:22_ — "I have blotted out, as a thick cloud, thy transgressions, and, as a
  cloud, thy sins: return unto me; for I have redeemed thee." _1 John 5:21_ — "Little
  children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen." _Proverbs 4:23_ — "Keep thy heart with all
  diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."
- **Labels.** _Text states_: the passages. _Inference_: the generalisation from carved idols
  to modern substitutes. _Note_: the verse's own subject is the idol-maker; the application
  to other substitutes is the reader's move and should be labelled as such when used.
- **Related.** D1, F7, D6.

# The two wisdoms test

James 3:13-18 is the working checklist for this skill. It is used because it does not ask
whether a thought _feels_ true or whether its holder is sincere. It asks what the thought
produces. A pattern of thought is a wisdom, and a wisdom is known by its fruit.

## The text

_James 3:13_ — "Who is a wise man and endued with knowledge among you? let him shew out of a
good conversation his works with meekness of wisdom."

_James 3:14_ — "But if ye have bitter envying and strife in your hearts, glory not, and lie
not against the truth."

_James 3:15_ — "This wisdom descendeth not from above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish."

_James 3:16_ — "For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work."

_James 3:17_ — "But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and
easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without
hypocrisy."

_James 3:18_ — "And the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace of them that make peace."

## The checklist

**Fails the test — the wisdom from below.** A pattern fails if its characteristic output
includes:

| Marker                      | What to look for                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bitter envying**          | The thought is powered by comparison. Someone else's good is felt as one's own loss.                                                                        |
| **Strife**                  | The thought produces quarrels, factions, or a permanent opponent.                                                                                           |
| **Boasting / glorying**     | The thought's function is to establish the holder's superiority. Verse 14 says "glory not" — if the thought cannot be held without glorying, it has failed. |
| **Lying against the truth** | The thought requires the suppression of known facts.                                                                                                        |
| **Confusion**               | The Greek is _akatastasia_, instability and disorder. The thought produces agitation rather than settled judgement.                                         |
| **Every evil work**         | The fruit includes acts that the person would condemn in someone else.                                                                                      |

**Passes the test — the wisdom from above.** A pattern passes if its characteristic output
includes:

| Marker                            | What to look for                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pure**                          | First in the list. It does not require a compromise of what is known to be right.                                          |
| **Peaceable**                     | It produces peace — and note the order: purity first, then peace. Peace bought by impurity is not this.                    |
| **Gentle**                        | The manner is not coercive.                                                                                                |
| **Easy to be intreated**          | The Greek word means persuadable. The holder can be moved by a better argument — this is the direct opposite of A3 and A4. |
| **Full of mercy and good fruits** | It produces concrete good, not only correct analysis.                                                                      |
| **Without partiality**            | It does not sort people by face or group (A7).                                                                             |
| **Without hypocrisy**             | It is the same in private as in public.                                                                                    |

## How to run the test on a thought

1. **State the thought as a sentence.** Not "I feel bad about work" but "I am being
   disrespected, and that means I should withdraw." A pattern that cannot be stated as a
   sentence cannot be tested.
2. **Name what it produces.** Not what it _could_ produce, and not what it produces in
   theory — what it actually produces in this person's week. Confusion, strife, and envy are
   observable.
3. **Ask the source question.** Does this descend from above, or is it earthly, soulish,
   demonic? The three words in verse 15 are not synonyms; they are a descent: worldly,
   merely natural, and finally diabolical. The test is not whether the thought is clever but
   where it comes from.
4. **Apply the verse 14 warning.** If the thought cannot be held without glorying — without
   making its holder superior — the text has already given the verdict.
5. **Apply the purity test first.** Because purity comes first in verse 17, a proposed peace
   that requires impurity fails at the first item. This is where the test does its hardest
   work.
6. **Apply the teachability test.** "Easy to be intreated" is a property of the _person
   holding the thought_, not of the thought alone. A person who cannot be moved on a matter
   is holding a wisdom from below, whatever the content.
7. **Replace, then re-run the test later.** The fruit appears over time. A thought that
   passes today may show its fruit in a month, and the test is repeatable.

## Limits of the test — read before using it

- **The test is diagnostic, not judicial.** It evaluates a _pattern_, not a person's standing.
  James is addressing people who claim wisdom; the test tells you what kind of wisdom you are
  holding, not whether someone is saved.
- **The test is not a licence for contempt.** "This thought fails the test" is a statement
  about the thought. It is not a statement that its holder is a fool. Every pattern in this
  catalog is available to the reader.
- **A true statement can be held in a wisdom from below.** The test does not replace
  truth-tracking; it adds a second question — how the truth is being held and what it is
  producing.
- **Some fruit takes time.** Judgement, in the sense of premature conclusion (A3), is
  specifically forbidden here. Do not run the test on a person's fruit after one
  conversation.
- **Disagreement is not strife.** The text condemns _strife_, not difference. Two people can
  hold different views peaceably, and the guards section below governs how to handle the
  difference.

# Guards against false detection

This section is required, not decorative. A catalog of thought errors has a predictable
failure mode: it becomes an instrument for contempt. The person who learns the patterns can
begin to see them everywhere, in everyone, and the skill becomes a machine for producing
arrogance, legalism, conspiracy thinking, and spiritual pride. Those are themselves patterns
from the catalog — D6, A7, and F1 respectively.

Read this before applying any entry to another person.

## The keystone: love is the first principle of motive

The governing rule of the whole skill family is 1 Corinthians 13. The word rendered "charity"
is _agape_, benevolent love; in 1611 "charity" meant that, not almsgiving. The passage makes
love constitutive of the act's value, not an addition to it.

_1 Corinthians 13:1_ — "Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not
charity, I am become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal."

_1 Corinthians 13:2_ — "And though I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries,
and all knowledge; and though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not
charity, I am nothing."

_1 Corinthians 13:3_ — "And though I bestow all my goods to feed the poor, and though I give
my body to be burned, and have not charity, it profiteth me nothing."

**Rule.** If a reading of this catalog produces cruelty, the reading is wrong. The catalog is
not an exception to the keystone.

## The guards

**1. Own beam first.** _Matthew 7:3-5._ The procedure is stated: first cast out the beam out
of thine own eye, _then_ shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote. The order is not
optional, and the beam is the larger object. Applied here: before diagnosing another person's
pattern, name one's own.

**2. Knowledge inflates; love builds.** _1 Corinthians 8:1_ — "Now as touching things offered
unto idols, we know that we all have knowledge. Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth."
The Greek for "puffeth up" is _physioo_, to inflate; "edifieth" is _oikodomeo_, to build a
house. The two verbs describe two different results. A person who has learned this catalog
and become inflated by it has produced the first result and not the second.

**3. Disputable matters are not the same as errors.** _Romans 14:1_ — "Him that is weak in the
faith receive ye, but not to doubtful disputations." _Romans 14:3_ — "Let not him that eateth
despise him that eateth not; and let not him which eateth not judge him that eateth: for God
hath received him." _Romans 14:5_ — "Let every man be fully persuaded in his own mind."
_Romans 14:13_ — "Let us not therefore judge one another any more: but judge this rather, that
no man put a stumblingblock or an occasion to fall in his brother's way." The chapter
distinguishes matters of conscience from matters of command, and forbids both directions of
contempt — despising and judging. A great deal of what this catalog's readers will be tempted
to correct is in this category.

**4. Instruct in meekness, not in triumph.** _2 Timothy 2:24-25_ — "And the servant of the
Lord must not strive; but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient, In meekness
instructing those that oppose themselves; if God peradventure will give them repentance to the
acknowledging of the truth." Note the phrase "those that oppose themselves" — the person in
error is described as opposing their own interest, which is an argument for pity rather than
contempt. Note also who is credited with the change: "if God peradventure will give them
repentance."

**5. Restore in meekness, considering thyself.** _Galatians 6:1_ — "Brethren, if a man be
overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness;
considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted." The instruction contains its own guard: the
corrector is told to consider himself. The Greek word for meekness is _prautes_, mildness and
humility.

**6. Answer with meekness and fear.** _1 Peter 3:15_ — "But sanctify the Lord God in your
hearts: and be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the
hope that is in you with meekness and fear:" The manner is commanded as part of the answer.
"Fear" here is not fear of the person; it is the disposition that keeps the answerer aware of
whose presence they are in.

**7. Be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath.** _James 1:19_ — "Wherefore, my beloved
brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:" The three-part
sequence is the counter-procedure to A3 and A4. Wrath is placed last and made slow, not
eliminated — the point is the sequence, not the absence of any reaction.

**8. Hear the other side first.** _Proverbs 18:17_ — "He that is first in his own cause
seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him." Applied to the catalog: before
concluding that a person's thought is a pattern from this list, let their own account be
searched. The first account always seems just.

**9. The goal is renewal, not victory.** The purpose of the skill is stated in its name:
renewal. A corrected person is the goal; a defeated person is not. Romans 12:2 gives the
mechanism — "be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind" — and transformation is not the
same as refutation. Where the choice is between winning the argument and the person being
renewed, the text chooses the person. This is an _Inference_ from the purpose of the skill and
the shape of the passages above; it is not a verse.

## Over-detection failure modes

These are the ways the catalog itself goes wrong. Each one is a pattern from the catalog
wearing the catalog's clothes.

| Failure mode                                          | What it looks like                                                                                                                                  | The pattern it actually is                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Conspiracy where there is incompetence**            | Reading coordination and intent into outcomes produced by drift, error, or ordinary self-interest. Motive is inferred and then treated as evidence. | D5 (evil surmisings), A4 (first in his own cause)                    |
| **Treating preference as sin**                        | Converting a disputable matter into a command. Food, dress, schooling, music, and scheduling are the classic cases.                                 | E5 (the commandments of men), A7 (partiality toward one's own group) |
| **Treating every disagreement as a spiritual defect** | The other person's pattern is diagnosed from their conclusion. Because they disagree, they must be in one of the patterns.                          | A3 (answering before hearing), D2 (beam and mote)                    |
| **Diagnosing a person from one conversation**         | A single remark is read as a settled character condition.                                                                                           | A6 (judging by appearance), D5                                       |
| **Using the test to win**                             | The two wisdoms test is invoked as a verdict against an opponent rather than as a check on oneself.                                                 | D6 (pride), F1 failed by the tester                                  |
| **Contempt as discernment**                           | The tone carries superiority while the words carry analysis.                                                                                        | 1 Corinthians 8:1 — inflation, not edification                       |

**The single check.** If using this catalog makes a person more able to see their own beam
and more patient with those who oppose themselves, it is being used correctly. If it makes
them more confident of their own standing and more contemptuous of others, it is being used as
a wisdom from below, and the test in James 3 has already returned its verdict on the user.
