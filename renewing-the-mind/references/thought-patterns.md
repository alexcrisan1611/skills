# Thought Patterns: The Catalog

This is a working reference for the `renewing-the-mind` skill. It names malformed and inaccurate patterns of thought and states what each one lies about. It shows the fruit of each pattern, and it pairs each one with a counter-truth and a verified text. It is a reference document, not a devotional.

## Method and labels

This file uses four labels. The labels carry weight. Do not remove them.

| Label       | Meaning                                                                                         |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Text states | The passage says this, in these words. You can check it against the KJV.                        |
| Lexical     | A claim about the Hebrew or Greek underneath, verified against the bundled lexicon.             |
| Inference   | Reasoning from the text by a fallible reader. You can disagree with it.                         |
| Contested   | An empirical or historical claim that the tools cannot settle, and that honest readers dispute. |

Three rules govern everything here:

1. Applications are inferences, not Scripture. The text names a pattern. The claim that a particular modern institution or habit is that pattern is an inference, and this file labels it as one.
2. Description is not prescription. Several of the best passages in this catalog describe a failure. Examples are Judges 17:6, 1 Samuel 15, and Psalm 73. The text reports what happened. It does not command it. Those entries say so.
3. Verify before you infer. KJV wording is quoted exactly as `scripture.ts` returns it. Where a 1611 word does work in the argument, it was checked against `lexicon.ts`. The appendix lists the checked words.

## Quotation conventions

KJV wording is quoted exactly as the tool returns it. The source data uses curly apostrophes, and this file shows them as straight apostrophes. The wording, punctuation, and capitalization are otherwise not changed. Strong's definitions are quoted verbatim from the output of `lexicon.ts`. The quotes keep the abbreviations of the tool.

## How to use this catalog

Each entry has the same seven parts: description, the lie, cause, fruit, counter-truth, counter-text, and labels. The entry includes the cause because you cannot replace a pattern that you only condemn. A pattern attracts a person for a reason, and you must answer the reason.

Repair procedure:

1. Name the pattern. Use the biblical name where one fits. The name does half the work. A pattern with no name acts as a mood and not as a claim.
2. State the lie in one sentence. If you cannot state it in one sentence, you did not yet isolate the pattern.
3. Name the cause. Ask what this thought buys the person who holds it. It can be fear, status, relief, or permission.
4. Check the fruit. Apply the two wisdoms test below.
5. Replace the pattern. Do not only delete it. A vacated pattern leaves the cause unanswered. The counter-truth is the replacement.
6. Expect resistance. The pattern will argue again. Replacement is a practice, not an event.

## What this catalog is not

It is not a license to diagnose other people. Every entry describes an error that the reader can also make. You must read the guards section at the end. It is not an appendix of courtesies. A catalog of thought errors used as a weapon produces exactly the fruit that the catalog condemns. See F1 and the guards section.

# Group A: Truth and evidence

These failures corrupt the input. The input is what a person accepts as true, and how the person decides. Every later group depends on this one, because a person who cannot classify cannot correct.

## A1 · Calling evil good

Modern name: moral inversion.

- Description: The faculty that classifies is reversed. The text lists the swaps: evil and good, darkness and light, bitter and sweet. The third pair is sensory. Bitterness is a taste. So the inversion is not only a change of vocabulary. It claims a change of experience.
- The lie: "The label determines the value. If I rename it, I change it."
- Cause: A new name removes the need to repent. It is cheaper than reform, and it keeps the approval of the group that supplied the new label.
- Fruit: The loss of the ability to detect error at all. A person who renamed the evil cannot repent of it, because that person no longer believes it is there.
- Counter-truth: The namer does not define good. The standard is the character of God. The text does not observe the inversion neutrally. It pronounces woe on it.
- Counter-text: Isaiah 5:20: "Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness; that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!"
- Labels: Text states: the inversion and the woe. Inference: this pattern comes before the others. Contested: which present-day practices fall under it. This text does not settle that question, and the question needs the guards section.
- Related: A2, D1, E5.

## A2 · Every man right in his own eyes

Modern name: relativism and self-referential truth.

- Description: The assessment of each person becomes the final court. Judges gives the refrain twice, at the beginning and the end of the period. It gives the refrain as the explanation for the chaos between. Proverbs states the same condition in the singular.
- The lie: "Sincerity is the same as correctness. If it seems right to me, it is right for me."
- Cause: It ends every argument, and nobody has to change. It also costs nothing to hold, because it never has to survive an external test.
- Fruit: The condition that Judges describes. Nobody acknowledges an authority, every assessment is private, and the book records the events that result.
- Counter-truth: The text does not leave the private assessment unchallenged. Proverbs adds the external check: the LORD weighs the hearts. The alternative to self-reference is not another private opinion. It is counsel, and a judge who sees what the person cannot see.
- Counter-text: Judges 17:6: "In those days there was no king in Israel, but every man did that which was right in his own eyes." Judges 21:25: "In those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes." Proverbs 12:15: "The way of a fool is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise." Proverbs 21:2: "Every way of a man is right in his own eyes: but the LORD pondereth the hearts."
- Labels: Text states: the refrain and the two Proverbs. Description, not prescription: Judges reports the condition. It does not command it. Inference: "no king" and "every man did that which was right in his own eyes" connect as cause and symptom.
- Related: A5, A4, D1.

## A3 · Answering before hearing

Modern name: premature conclusion.

- Description: A person gives a verdict before the evidence is in. The text names the sequence, not only the outcome. The answer comes before the hearing.
- The lie: "A fast answer is a competent answer. Speed is evidence of understanding."
- Cause: Speed signals confidence, and people reward confidence. A wait looks like ignorance, and ignorance is uncomfortable.
- Fruit: The text states it directly: "it is folly and shame unto him." The shame is the later discovery of what the earlier answer missed.
- Counter-truth: The remedy is a procedure, and James gives the sequence: swift to hear, slow to speak. Proverbs gives the matching virtue. The prudent man looks well to his going, but the simple believes every word.
- Counter-text: Proverbs 18:13: "He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him." Proverbs 14:15: "The simple believeth every word: but the prudent man looketh well to his going." James 1:19: "Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:"
- Labels: Text states: the Proverbs and James. Inference: the modern "hot take" is this pattern at scale. Contested: the claim that particular platforms increase it by a measurable amount. The tools here cannot settle that, and the research literature disputes the size of the effect.
- Related: A4, A8, E2.

## A4 · First in his own cause

Modern name: confirmation bias and motivated reasoning.

- Description: The text describes a legal procedure. The first party to speak "seemeth just." The word is "seemeth," which is an appearance. The case appears closed until the other party examines him. The verse warns about the appearance that the first speaker creates.
- The lie: "My account of events is the whole account. The case is closed after my version."
- Cause: The first version feels like the truth, because the person lived through it and rehearsed it. Later information arrives as an interruption.
- Fruit: Unjust judgment, self-justification, and an inability to hear correction. The person does not lie. The person reports a partial view as if it were the whole view.
- Counter-truth: The text prescribes the procedure when it describes it. The neighbor comes and searches him. The counter-practice is to seek the other side before you form the verdict. Treat your own case as a case, not as a fact.
- Counter-text: Proverbs 18:17: "He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him." Proverbs 18:13: "He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him."
- Labels: Text states: the courtroom description. Inference: the match to confirmation bias and motivated reasoning. The text does not use those terms. The reader names the mechanism.
- Related: A3, D2, D5.

## A5 · Measuring yourself by yourself

Modern name: relative judgment and social comparison.

- Description: The measuring group itself is the standard of measurement. Paul describes the practice and then states a verdict on it. Those who do it "are not wise." The verses around it supply the alternative. The alternative is a measure that God distributes, and commendation that comes from the Lord, not from yourself.
- The lie: "The group I am in defines my standing. If I am ahead of them, I am fine."
- Cause: It is the cheapest standard available. It needs no external reference, no examination, and no work. It needs only a comparison group, and the feed now supplies one all the time.
- Fruit: Two errors from one root. The person inflates when the comparison favors him and deflates when it does not. Both are false. It also makes envy part of the structure, because the success of the group is by definition the loss of the person.
- Counter-truth: The person does not issue his own measurement. The text points to a measure that God assigns and to the commendation of the Lord. That commendation is the only approval that is not relative.
- Counter-text: 2 Corinthians 10:12: "For we dare not make ourselves of the number, or compare ourselves with some that commend themselves: but they measuring themselves by themselves, and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise." 2 Corinthians 10:17-18: "But he that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord. For not he that commendeth himself is approved, but whom the Lord commendeth."
- Labels: Text states: the practice, the verdict "are not wise", and the alternative in 10:17-18. Lexical: "measuring" in 10:12 is G3354 _metreo_, "to measure (i.e. ascertain in size by a fixed standard)." The "measure" of 10:13 and 10:15 is G3358 _metron_, "a measure ('metre'), literally or figuratively; by implication, a limited portion (degree)". Inference: this is the engine of the comparison economy. See B1 and the social media section of `institutions.md`.
- Related: B1, C1, C3.

## A6 · Judging by appearance

Modern name: the halo effect.

- Description: A person forms a judgment from the visible surface and treats it as a judgment of the person. The surface is countenance, stature, and presentation. The LORD corrects Samuel for the use of the criterion that comes naturally to humans. He states the difference: man looks on the outward appearance, and the LORD looks on the heart.
- The lie: "What is visible is what is real. Presentation is character."
- Cause: Appearance is available at once and costs nothing to read. In some contexts it is also a useful signal, and that is what makes the error attractive. The pattern treats a real signal as the whole signal.
- Fruit: Wrong appointments, wrong trust, and the promotion of the impressive over the competent. It also produces self-condemnation in those who know that they do not present well.
- Counter-truth: The text commands judgment, not its suspension: "judge righteous judgment." The correction is to the criterion, not to the act of judging.
- Counter-text: 1 Samuel 16:7: "But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart." John 7:24: "Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment."
- Labels: Text states: both verses. Lexical: H4758 _mareh_, "a view (the act of seeing); also an appearance (the thing seen), whether (real) a shape (especially if handsome, comeliness; often plural the looks), or (mental) a vision". The word carries the sense of comeliness, which is exactly the surface that misleads.
- Related: A7, C3, D5.

## A7 · Partiality

Modern name: in-group bias.

- Description: A person weighs a judgment by who the person is, not by what is true or just. James gives a concrete scene. The assembly seats the well-dressed visitor well and seats the poor visitor low. The assembly then becomes "judges of evil thoughts." Leviticus states the prohibition in both directions. You must not favor the poor or the mighty.
- The lie: "Loyalty to my group is the same as justice."
- Cause: Partiality buys belonging. It is the price of admission to a group, and the group is often the source of safety.
- Fruit: The text names it: sin, and conviction as transgressors. It also destroys the credibility of the judge, because nobody can trust a partial judge on any matter.
- Counter-truth: The royal law is the standard: love thy neighbor as thyself. The text also supplies a striking word for impartiality. G87 _adiakritos_ has the dictionary sense "undistinguished, i.e. (actively) impartial." The wisdom from above does not sort people by face.
- Counter-text: James 2:1: "My brethren, have not the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with respect of persons." James 2:9: "But if ye have respect to persons, ye commit sin, and are convinced of the law as transgressors." Leviticus 19:15: "Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honor the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G4382 _prosopolepsia_, "partiality, i.e. favoritism." The verb behind "have respect to persons" in James 2:9 is G4380 _prosopolepteo_, "to favor an individual, i.e. show partiality." G4380 comes from G4381 _prosopoleptes_, "an accepter of a face (individual), i.e. (specially), one exhibiting partiality." Literally, it is a receiver of faces. H4856 _massa_, "partiality (as a lifting up)". Inference: the Hebrew idiom behind "respect the person" is the lifting of a face (H5375 _nasa_, "to lift", and H6440 _panim_, "the face"). The tool confirms both words and their glosses. It does not map them to this verse, so the mapping to the verse belongs to the reader.
- Related: A6, A5, D2.

## A8 · The unverified report

Modern name: acceptance of a claim without a test.

- Description: A person accepts a claim because of its source, its confidence, or its convenience, without a test. Deuteronomy supplies a test for prophecy. The test can prove a prophecy false by its outcome. John commands people to try the spirits. Paul commands people to prove all things.
- The lie: "To believe is the same as to agree. A test is unfaithfulness."
- Cause: A test is expensive, and it exposes the tester. Acceptance is cheap, and it keeps the peace. A real virtue is also nearby. That virtue is trust, and the pattern exploits it by confusing trust with credulity.
- Fruit: Gullibility and cynicism take turns, often in the same person. The person accepts favored sources without criticism and rejects disfavored ones without criticism. The person loses the ability to distinguish in both directions.
- Counter-truth: The text does more than permit a test. It commands one. It supplies a criterion, which is the outcome, and a disposition, which is to hold fast that which is good. Faith in the biblical sense is not bare belief. G4102 _pistis_ carries "constancy" as part of its own definition. So a test is not the opposite of faith. It is a way to exercise faith.
- Counter-text: Deuteronomy 18:22: "When a prophet speaketh in the name of the LORD, if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which the LORD hath not spoken, but the prophet hath spoken it presumptuously: thou shalt not be afraid of him." 1 John 4:1: "Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world." 1 Thessalonians 5:21: "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good."
- Labels: Text states: the three passages. Lexical: G1381 _dokimazo_, "to test (literally or figuratively); by implication, to approve." G3985 _peirazo_, "to test (objectively), i.e. endeavor, scrutinize, entice, discipline." G4102 _pistis_, "persuasion, i.e. credence; moral conviction (of religious truth, or the truthfulness of God or a religious teacher), especially reliance upon Christ for salvation; abstractly, constancy in such profession". Inference: the reverse error is the same pattern with the sign reversed. The reverse error is to reject a claim because of its source and not to test it.
- Related: A3, E7, E2.

# Group B: Desire and comparison

These patterns corrupt the want. They do not argue that a thing is true. They make it feel necessary. Several of them come after A5, because they measure a desire against the portion of someone else. B2 and B3 show that desire can go wrong with no comparison at all.

## B1 · Envy

Modern name: discontent driven by comparison.

- Description: Envy is grief at the good of another person. Proverbs states its physical effect, and James states its social effect. Psalm 73 gives the full first-person account of the process. The account includes the near-fall, the turning point, and the recovery.
- The lie: "Their gain is my loss. If they have it, someone took it from me."
- Cause: Envy is a form of attention, and attention is a form of importance. It also supplies a reason for your own position that needs no action. The problem becomes the fortune of another person, not your own conduct.
- Fruit: The text is specific. Proverbs 14:30 calls it "the rottenness of the bones." The word for rottenness means decay or rot. James 3:16 says: "For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work." Psalm 73 records the self-diagnosis: "So foolish was I, and ignorant: I was as a beast before thee."
- Counter-truth: The turn in the psalm is not psychological. It is spatial and theological: "Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end." The recovery is a change of vantage point, not a change of feeling. It ends in a different measurement. God is the portion. The portion of the neighbor is no longer the measure.
- Counter-text: Proverbs 14:30: "A sound heart is the life of the flesh: but envy the rottenness of the bones." James 3:14-16: "But if ye have bitter envying and strife in your hearts, glory not, and lie not against the truth. This wisdom descendeth not from above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish. For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work." Psalm 73:17-19: "Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end. Surely thou didst set them in slippery places: thou castedst them down into destruction. How are they brought into desolation, as in a moment! they are utterly consumed with terrors." Psalm 73:25-26: "Whom have I in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee. My flesh and my heart faileth: but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever."
- Labels: Text states: all four passages, and the sequence in Psalm 73. Lexical: H7068 _qinah_, "jealousy or envy." H7538 _raqab_, "decay (by caries)." G2205 _zelos_, "properly, heat, i.e. (figuratively) 'zeal' (in a favorable sense, ardor; in an unfavorable one, jealousy, as of a husband (figuratively, of God), or an enemy, malice)". The Hebrew and Greek words for envy are the same words as zeal and jealousy. Inference: envy is not a separate faculty. It is zeal turned toward the possession of another person. Lexical: "sound" in Proverbs 14:30 is H4832 _marpe_, "properly, curative, i.e. literally (concretely) a medicine, or (abstractly) a cure; figuratively (concretely) deliverance, or (abstractly) placidity". The word describes the heart. A "sound heart" is a heart of healing or calm, and the verse calls that heart "the life of the flesh." Inference: envy is the emotional engine of the comparison economy.
- Related: A5, B3, F1.

## B2 · The three channels of desire

Modern name: appetite, aesthetics, status.

- Description: Genesis 3:6 and 1 John 2:16 each list three channels. Genesis gives them in narrative order: good for food, pleasant to the eyes, desired to make one wise. John gives them in summary: the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, the pride of life. The match of one list to the other is an inference. But the threefold structure appears in both texts independently.
- The lie: "What I want is what I need. The strength of the want is evidence of the worth of the thing."
- Cause: Each channel answers a real need at a counterfeit address. Food answers hunger. Beauty answers the love of order. Wisdom answers the desire to understand. The pattern does not create a need. It redirects one.
- Fruit: Genesis 3:6 states the sequence as a chain: saw, took, ate, gave. The desire did not stay inside. James states the same chain in general terms. Every man is drawn away of his own lust and enticed, and the finished product is death.
- Counter-truth: The text does not command the removal of desire. It commands a new order for desire. The single eye of Matthew 6:22-23 is the counter-image. It is an eye that is not divided between the channel and its counterfeit.
- Counter-text: Genesis 3:6: "And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat." 1 John 2:16: "For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world." 1 John 2:17: "And the world passeth away, and the lust thereof: but he that doeth the will of God abideth for ever." James 1:14: "But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed." James 1:15: "Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death."
- Labels: Text states: the two threefold lists. Inference: the match between them. Contested: the claim that advertising targets exactly these three channels. That claim is an inference from marketing practice. The research literature also disputes the strength of the effect of advertising on behavior. See `institutions.md`.
- Related: B3, B7, C5.

## B3 · The eyes never satisfied

Modern name: manufactured want.

- Description: The appetite has no end. Proverbs pairs the unsatisfied eye with hell and destruction, two things that are never full. Ecclesiastes states the same of both eye and ear. Seeing does not satisfy the eye, and hearing does not fill the ear.
- The lie: "The next acquisition will settle it."
- Cause: The appetite is real, and its satisfaction is real. That is what makes the pattern credible. A satisfied hunger is real evidence, but for the wrong conclusion, because the next hunger arrives.
- Fruit: Consumption that increases the appetite that it feeds. Supply does not reduce the want. Supply trains it.
- Counter-truth: Contentment is not the suppression of desire. It moves desire to a new place. The text does not say that the eye must stop seeing. It says that the person must stop feeding on what cannot fill. Psalm 119:37 gives the practice: turn the eyes away from beholding vanity.
- Counter-text: Proverbs 27:20: "Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied." Ecclesiastes 1:8: "All things are full of labour; man cannot utter it: the eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing." Psalm 119:37: "Turn away mine eyes from beholding vanity; and quicken thou me in thy way." Hebrews 13:5: "Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee."
- Labels: Text states: Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Psalm 119, Hebrews. Inference: an industry of manufactured want exists, and this is its mechanism. Contested: the size of the effect on a given person. The tools here cannot measure it.
- Related: B2, B4, B7.

## B4 · The deceitfulness of riches

Modern name: none. The biblical name is the diagnosis.

- Description: The text does not call riches evil. It calls them deceitful. In the parable of the sower, the deceit works beside the cares of the world and the lusts of other things. The stated result is that the word is choked and becomes unfruitful. Paul charges the rich not to be highminded and not to trust in uncertain riches. The psalm gives the same instruction as a command: if riches increase, set not your heart upon them.
- The lie: "Money is neutral. More money will solve the anxiety that less money caused."
- Cause: Money does solve real problems. The deceit is not that money is useless. The deceit is that the person treats it as a foundation and not as an instrument. The anxiety that it relieves is real, so the relief feels like security.
- Fruit: A choked word and no fruit. Highmindedness. Trust in a thing that the text itself calls uncertain.
- Counter-truth: The counter is not poverty, and it is not guilt. The charge of Paul does not tell the rich to give everything away. It tells them where to put their trust, and then it tells them to enjoy what God gives. The counter in the psalm is a direction of the heart, not a sale of the assets.
- Counter-text: Mark 4:19: "And the cares of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches, and the lusts of other things entering in, choke the word, and it becometh unfruitful." 1 Timothy 6:17: "Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy;" Psalm 62:10: "Trust not in oppression, and become not vain in robbery: if riches increase, set not your heart upon them."
- Labels: Text states: all three passages. Lexical: G539 _apate_, "delusion." The "deceitfulness" of the KJV is the word for delusion. Inference: "uncertain riches" names the mechanism, because the trust ignores the uncertainty. Note: Mark 4:19 describes a class of hearer. It does not command the rich to become poor.
- Related: B5, B7, F7.

## B5 · Haste to be rich

Modern name: the get-rich-quick reflex.

- Description: Three Proverbs name speed as the fault, not ambition. The faithful man abounds, but the one who makes haste to be rich shall not be innocent. The thoughts of the diligent tend to plenty, but the hasty tend to want. Wealth gathered by vanity diminishes, but wealth gathered by labor increases.
- The lie: "Speed is the same as skill. The slow path is for people without vision."
- Cause: The slow path is in fact slower, and patience is in fact hard. The pattern also borrows the vocabulary of faith: vision, boldness, favor. That vocabulary makes caution look like unbelief.
- Fruit: The text states three outcomes: not innocent, want, and decrease. Note the word. Proverbs 21:5 ends in "want." In 1611, "want" means lack, not desire. The hasty end in poverty, not only in unfulfilled longing.
- Counter-truth: The alternative in the text is not passivity. It is labor gathered over time. It is also the count-the-cost procedure of Luke 14:28-30, which treats the unfinished tower as a public failure.
- Counter-text: Proverbs 28:20: "A faithful man shall abound with blessings: but he that maketh haste to be rich shall not be innocent." Proverbs 21:5: "The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness; but of every one that is hasty only to want." Proverbs 13:11: "Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished: but he that gathereth by labour shall increase." Luke 14:28: "For which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it?"
- Labels: Text states: the Proverbs and Luke. Lexical: H213 _uts_, "to press; (by implication) to be close, hurry, withdraw". Lexical: `lexicon.ts english "want"` returns 21 entries. The entries that mean a need are lack, deficiency, or poverty words, for example H4270 _machsor_, "deficiency; hence, impoverishment". The other entries come from "wanton" or from idioms. None of the 21 is a plain word for desire. Inference: the "want" of Proverbs 21:5 belongs to the family of lack words.
- Related: B4, B6, F3.

## B6 · Bread of deceit

Modern name: short-term gain from deception.

- Description: The text describes a gain from deception as sweet at the moment of eating and as gravel afterwards. The text does not deny the sweetness. It states how long the sweetness lasts.
- The lie: "The gain is real. The method is a detail."
- Cause: Deception works at once. The gravel arrives later, and people always give less weight to later.
- Fruit: The aftertaste that the text names. Also the collapse of trust, which is the asset that the deception spent. Proverbs 12:19 states the asymmetry. The lip of truth is established for ever, but the lying tongue is but for a moment.
- Counter-truth: The counter is more than honesty as a rule. It is truth as an asset that lasts. Paul states his counter-practice. He renounced the hidden things of dishonesty. He did not handle the word of God deceitfully. He commended himself by manifestation of the truth.
- Counter-text: Proverbs 20:17: "Bread of deceit is sweet to a man; but afterwards his mouth shall be filled with gravel." Proverbs 12:19: "The lip of truth shall be established for ever: but a lying tongue is but for a moment." 2 Corinthians 4:2: "But have renounced the hidden things of dishonesty, not walking in craftiness, nor handling the word of God deceitfully; but by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God."
- Labels: Text states: all three. Lexical: H8267 _sheqer_, "an untruth; by implication, a sham (often adverbial)". The word covers both the lie and the thing that is a sham.
- Related: B5, E4, E7.

## B7 · Covetousness

Modern name: none. The biblical name is the diagnosis.

- Description: Luke 12:15 contains both the command and the reason. The reason states the lie directly in order to deny it: a man's life does not consist in the abundance of his possessions. The parable after it ends with a question that the covetous man cannot answer. Whose shall those things be?
- The lie: A man's life consists in the abundance of the things that he possesses. Luke 12:15 states this lie in order to deny it.
- Cause: Possession is visible evidence of security, and security is a real good. The pattern puts the evidence in the place of the thing.
- Fruit: The verdict of the parable: "Thou fool." Also the inability to answer the question of ownership, because the covetous man treated himself as the owner.
- Counter-truth: 1 John 2:15-17 gives the boundary and the reason. The things of the world are not of the Father, and the world passes away. The counter is not the absence of goods. It is the absence of love for the world. The text states the test as a direction of love, not a level of wealth.
- Counter-text: Luke 12:15: "And he said unto them, Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth." Luke 12:20: "But God said unto him, Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee: then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided?" 1 John 2:15: "Love not the world, neither the things that are in the world. If any man love the world, the love of the Father is not in him."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G4124 _pleonexia_, "avarice, i.e. (by implication) fraudulency, extortion". Inference: the word is built on the idea of having more. Its dictionary sense extends to fraud and extortion, and that connects B7 to B6.
- Related: B3, B4, B6.

# Group C: Fear and the opinion of others

These patterns corrupt the social reference. They answer the question "what will they think?" before the question "what is true?" Then they mistake the answer for a moral fact.

## C1 · The fear of man

Modern name: social conformity and bandwagon pressure.

- Description: The text names the mechanism in two words: a snare. The fear of human disapproval is not a feeling to manage. It is a trap with a trigger. The same verse gives the alternative, which is trust in the LORD. It also gives the outcome of the alternative: safe.
- The lie: "The cost of their disapproval is real, and the cost of obedience is theoretical."
- Cause: Disapproval has immediate, material costs: exclusion, loss of work, and loss of standing. The fear matches real stakes. That is what makes it a snare and not a phobia.
- Fruit: A compromised confession. John 12:42-43 records it exactly. Many believed, but they did not confess, because of the Pharisees, "For they loved the praise of men more than the praise of God." The failure is a failure of rank. It is not an absence of love for God. Galatians 1:10 states the final cost. To please men is to stop being the servant of Christ.
- Counter-truth: The text does not command the absence of fear. It moves the fear to a new object. The one who trusts is safe. The one who manages appearances is caught. The counter-practice is to price the two costs honestly, and the fear of man never does that.
- Counter-text: Proverbs 29:25: "The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe." John 12:42-43: "Nevertheless among the chief rulers also many believed on him; but because of the Pharisees they did not confess him, lest they should be put out of the synagogue: For they loved the praise of men more than the praise of God." Galatians 1:10: "For do I now persuade men, or God? or do I seek to please men? for if I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: "fear" in Proverbs 29:25 is H2731 _charadah_, "fear, anxiety." The Hebrew phrase is _cherdat adam_. "Snare" is H4170 _moqesh_, "a noose (for catching animals) (literally or figuratively); by implication, a hook (for the nose)". The snare has a hook in it. Inference: "bandwagon pressure" is this pattern at scale.
- Related: A5, C2, C3.

## C2 · Following a multitude

Modern name: herd behavior and social proof.

- Description: The command has two clauses. The first forbids following a crowd to do evil. The second forbids speaking in a cause so as "to decline after many". That is, it forbids letting the number of voices bend testimony. The second clause is the sharper one. A majority does not make a statement true.
- The lie: "Many people cannot be wrong about this."
- Cause: In many areas, the crowd is a real source of information, and to copy successful behavior is often rational. The pattern applies that rational rule of thumb where it does not apply. It applies it to matters of truth and judgment.
- Fruit: Wrested judgment, and testimony that bends with the count. Also, the person slowly loses the ability to report what he actually saw.
- Counter-truth: The text supplies a gate with a count on it, and the count is small: "few there be that find it." The counter-practice is to ask what the multitude is used to establish, and whether the number matters to the claim.
- Counter-text: Exodus 23:2: "Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil; neither shalt thou speak in a cause to decline after many to wrest judgment:" Matthew 7:13-14: "Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it." Proverbs 29:25: "The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe."
- Labels: Text states: Exodus and Matthew. Inference: modern "social proof" is this pattern. Inference: the second clause of Exodus 23:2 is about testimony, not behavior.
- Related: C1, C3, E2.

## C3 · The praise of men

Modern name: none. The biblical name is the diagnosis.

- Description: John states the motive plainly. They loved the praise of men more than the praise of God. Proverbs supplies the diagnostic image: "As the fining pot for silver, and the furnace for gold; so is a man to his praise." Praise is the crucible. It is applied heat, and it shows what is in the metal.
- The lie: "Approval is a good in itself, and more of it is better."
- Cause: Praise is pleasant, cheap to receive, and often accurate. It is also the currency of belonging, so it is hard to refuse.
- Fruit: The loss of the ability to receive correction. Correction and praise come from the same sources, and the person learned to need the source. Also, the judgment of the audience takes the place of the judgment of the person.
- Counter-truth: The counter is not the rejection of praise. It is a new order for its source. John 5:44 states the mechanism. When people receive honor one from another, belief becomes difficult. The honor is already paid out by the time the true honor is offered. The counter-practice is to let praise test you and not feed you.
- Counter-text: John 12:43: "For they loved the praise of men more than the praise of God." Proverbs 27:21: "As the fining pot for silver, and the furnace for gold; so is a man to his praise." John 5:44: "How can ye believe, which receive honour one of another, and seek not the honour that cometh from God only?"
- Labels: Text states: all three. Inference: the expansion of Proverbs 27:21 from its short form. The KJV clause does not spell out the comparison, and the reading "praise is a test" belongs to the reader. Note: the verse is a proverb, so it states a tendency. It does not state a mechanism that works the same way in every case.
- Related: C1, C4, D6.

## C4 · Flattery

Modern name: manipulation through praise.

- Description: Flattery is praise with a purpose. The Hebrew behind "flattereth" means to be smooth. The flatterer is the one who makes things smooth. Proverbs states the effect: he spreads a net for the feet. Psalm 12 adds the internal form. Flattering lips and a double heart speak together. Daniel records the political use. The wicked are corrupted by flatteries.
- The lie: "The compliment has no motive behind it."
- Cause: Flattery is pleasant and often contains something true, so it is hard to detect. It also arrives attached to a real offer.
- Fruit: The net, and the double heart. The double heart is the heart of the flatterer, and also the growing dependence of the flattered person on the smoothing. Daniel 11:32 gives the contrast in the same verse. Those who do wickedly are corrupted by it, but those who know God are strong and do exploits.
- Counter-truth: The counter is not suspicion of all praise. That is the opposite error to C1. The counter is to ask what the praise is attached to. The test in the text is whether the person who gives the praise spreads something for the feet.
- Counter-text: Psalm 12:2-3: "They speak vanity every one with his neighbour: with flattering lips and with a double heart do they speak. The LORD shall cut off all flattering lips, and the tongue that speaketh proud things:" Proverbs 29:5: "A man that flattereth his neighbour spreadeth a net for his feet." Daniel 11:32: "And such as do wickedly against the covenant shall he corrupt by flatteries: but the people that do know their God shall be strong, and do exploits."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: H2505 _chalaq_, "to be smooth (figuratively); by implication (as smooth stones were used for lots) to apportion or separate". The KJV gloss list includes "flatter" and "divide" from the same root. Speculation: the two senses in one root echo the double heart of Psalm 12. A shared root does not prove a shared meaning in a given verse.
- Related: C3, C1, E4.

## C5 · Fear and anxiety

Modern name: catastrophizing.

- Description: The texts do not forbid prudence. They forbid the anxious rehearsal of what did not happen. Matthew 6 names the objects: food, drink, clothing, and the morrow. It supplies a test: "Which of you by taking thought can add one cubit unto his stature?" Philippians 4 supplies the replacement practice. That practice is prayer with thanksgiving, then a deliberate list of what to think on. Peter supplies the disposal: "Casting all your care upon him..."
- The lie: "Worry is a form of control. If I rehearse the disaster, I will be ready for it."
- Cause: Worry is a real attempt to solve a problem, but rehearsal cannot solve the problem it aims at. It also provides the feeling of responsibility, and that is why it feels like a duty.
- Fruit: The imagined fills the mind, the body pays a price, and the work of the day shrinks. Psalm 94:19 states the condition and the remedy together: "In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul."
- Counter-truth: The distinction that matters is between prudence and anxiety. The prudent man foresees the evil and hides himself (Proverbs 27:12). The anxious man foresees and stays. The text gives four practices:
  1. Prayer with thanksgiving (Philippians 4:6).
  2. The list of what to think on (4:8).
  3. Casting the care on God (1 Peter 5:7).
  4. The comfort that answers the multitude of thoughts (Psalm 94:19).
- Medical note: Persistent anxiety can be a medical condition. Prayer does not replace a physician. See "Where this skill refuses" in `SKILL.md`.
- Counter-text: Philippians 4:6: "Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God." Philippians 4:8: "Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things." Matthew 6:27: "Which of you by taking thought can add one cubit unto his stature?" 1 Peter 5:7: "Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you." Psalm 94:19: "In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G3309 _merimnao_, "to be anxious about." G3308 _merimna_, "solicitude", from G3307 _merizo_ "through the idea of distraction". Inference: in the Greek, anxiety is a mind divided by distraction, and that links it to the double-mindedness of F5. The "careful" of the KJV at Philippians 4:6 has the anxious sense, not the attentive sense. Inference: the distinction between prudence and anxiety. The text does not draw it explicitly. Proverbs 27:12 is the warrant of the reader for it.
- Related: F5, C6, B3.

## C6 · Unbelief and limiting God

Modern name: learned helplessness and defeatism.

- Description: Psalm 78 records a generation that "turned back and tempted God, and limited the Holy One of Israel." Hebrews 3:12 warns against the same condition. It calls it an evil heart of unbelief that departs from the living God. The two texts describe one pattern from two ends: the limit that the person imposes, and the departure that follows.
- The lie: "God has a ceiling, and I already measured it. To hope for more is presumption."
- Cause: The limit usually comes from experience. The person treats a real disappointment as a permanent measurement. The limit protects against hope and a second disappointment, and that is a real form of self-protection.
- Fruit: The list in the psalm: turning back, tempting God, limiting him. Hebrews states the end point: departing from the living God. The pattern does more than underestimate. It walks away.
- Counter-truth: Hebrews gives the instruction with the warning: "Take heed..." The psalm gives the alternative in its own summary of why the works must pass to the next generation: "That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments:" The counter is the memory of what God actually did. The limit erased that memory.
- Counter-text: Psalm 78:41: "Yea, they turned back and tempted God, and limited the Holy One of Israel." Hebrews 3:12: "Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God." Psalm 78:7: "That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments:"
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: "limited" is H8428 _tavah_, "to grieve", with the KJV gloss "limit (by confusion with H8427 (תָּוָה))." H8427 means "to mark out, i.e. (primitive) scratch or (definite) imprint". Inference: the lexicon itself treats "limited" as an uncertain rendering. The verb can mean that they grieved God, or that they set a mark or boundary for him. Either reading fits the pattern of a person who sets a ceiling on God. G570 _apistia_, "faithlessness, i.e. (negatively) disbelief (lack of Christian faith), or (positively) unfaithfulness (disobedience)". In the Greek, unbelief and unfaithfulness are one word.
- Related: F4, C5, A5.

# Group D: Self-deception

These patterns corrupt the audit. The person is not only wrong. The person cannot find the error, because the instrument that does the finding is the instrument that is wrong.

## D1 · The deceitful heart

Modern name: self-flattery.

- Description: Jeremiah states the condition and the consequence. The heart is deceitful above all things. The question that follows, "who can know it?", is not a rhetorical comfort. It is a statement of limit. Psalm 36 describes the mechanism. A man flatters himself in his own eyes until his iniquity is found to be hateful. Proverbs 30:12 gives the result: a generation pure in its own eyes, and not washed.
- The lie: "I can audit myself."
- Cause: Self-assessment is the only assessment that most people practiced. It has a built-in advantage. The auditor and the defendant are the same person, and the auditor knows the story of the defendant from the inside.
- Fruit: Unexamined error, sudden exposure, and an inability to receive correction. The "until" of the psalm is the word that does the work. The discovery happens, but not by self-audit.
- Counter-truth: The counter is not more self-examination. It is examination by another. Psalm 139 asks God to do what the heart cannot do for itself: "Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts:" Proverbs states the alternative to self-trust. He that trusteth in his own heart is a fool.
- Counter-text: Jeremiah 17:9: "The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?" Psalm 36:2: "For he flattereth himself in his own eyes, until his iniquity be found to be hateful." Proverbs 30:12: "There is a generation that are pure in their own eyes, and yet is not washed from their filthiness." Proverbs 28:26: "He that trusteth in his own heart is a fool: but whoso walketh wisely, he shall be delivered." Psalm 139:23: "Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts:"
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: H6121 _aqob_, "(transitive) fraudulent or (intransitive) tracked". "Deceitful" is a word of fraud, and it comes from H6117, "to seize by the heel; figuratively, to circumvent". The KJV glosses of H6117 include "supplant". Lexical: H605 _anash_, "to be frail, feeble, or (figuratively) melancholy". Its KJV glosses include "desperate(-ly wicked), incurable, sick, woeful". Honest note: the "desperately wicked" of the KJV renders a word whose dictionary sense is frailty and incurability, not intensity of evil. The claim that the heart is beyond human self-repair is Text states. The claim that it is evil to the maximum is not what the lexicon says. Do not preach that claim from this verse. Lexical: H3820 _leb_, "the heart; also used (figuratively) very widely for the feelings, the will and even the intellect". In the Hebrew, the heart is not the emotions as opposed to the mind. It includes the intellect, and the KJV gloss list includes "mind(-ed)". This matters for the whole skill. In the biblical vocabulary, the renewal of the mind is heart work.
- Related: A2, D2, F8.

## D2 · The beam and the mote

Modern name: projection and the actor-observer asymmetry.

- Description: The passage forbids a judgment that did not first pass through self-examination. It does so with a ratio of size: a timber beam against a dry straw. The final verse commands the correction of the brother after the beam is out. The procedure makes the correction possible. It does not cancel it.
- The lie: "My vision is clear because my error is small."
- Cause: You can see the faults of other people, but not your own. The reason is structural. You see your own behavior from the inside, as intention. You see the behavior of other people from the outside, as act. Psychologists call this the actor-observer asymmetry (Jones and Nisbett, 1971). The pattern misreads a real asymmetry as a moral difference.
- Fruit: Hypocrisy, the double standard, and correction that nobody can receive, because a person with a beam still in place delivers it. The word of the text for this is "hypocrite." Inference: the Greek word first named a stage actor, and so a person who wears a mask.
- Counter-truth: The text states the counter-procedure. First cast out the beam, then see clearly to cast out the mote. Galatians 6:1 supplies the manner. Restore in the spirit of meekness, and consider yourself, lest you also be tempted. The goal is the restoration of the brother, not the vindication of the one who held the beam.
- Counter-text: Matthew 7:3: "And why beholdest thou the mote that is in thy brother's eye, but considerest not the beam that is in thine own eye?" Matthew 7:5: "Thou hypocrite, first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye." Galatians 6:1: "Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted."
- Labels: Text states: the passage. Lexical: G1385 _dokos_, "a stick of timber." G2595 _karphos_, "a dry twig or straw." The "measure" of Matthew 7:2 is G3358 _metron_, "a measure ('metre'), literally or figuratively; by implication, a limited portion (degree)". This is the same word as the "measure" of 2 Corinthians 10:13 and 10:15. The "measuring" of 10:12 is the related verb G3354 _metreo_. Inference: Matthew 7:1 does not forbid judgment as such. John 7:24 commands it, and Matthew 7:5 commands it after the beam is out. The passage forbids a procedure, not the faculty.
- Related: A4, D5, and the guards section.

## D3 · Excusing with the sluggard's reason

Modern name: rationalization and the lion in the street.

- Description: The sluggard gives a reason: there is a lion in the way. The proverb does not say that the lion is imaginary. It says that the sluggard uses the risk as a reason not to move. The second proverb repeats it with the outcome attached: "I shall be slain in the streets."
- The lie: "A valid reason and a covering reason are the same thing."
- Cause: Rationalization works because it uses true premises. There are real lions. The error is not in the premise but in the inference. That is why it survives self-examination. The person can point to the lion.
- Fruit: The door on its hinges, which is motion without progress. Also the diagnosis in verse 16. The sluggard is "wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can render a reason." The defense is not laziness but superior reasoning. So you must answer it with an argument, not with an exhortation.
- Counter-truth: The counter is the same foresight, used in a different way. In Proverbs 27:12, the prudent man foresees the evil and hides himself, but the simple pass on and are punished. The difference between the two is not whether they saw the risk. It is what they did after they saw it. The counter-practice is to ask what the lion requires if it is real, and whether the plan includes that.
- Counter-text: Proverbs 26:13: "The slothful man saith, There is a lion in the way; a lion is in the streets." Proverbs 26:16: "The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can render a reason." Proverbs 22:13: "The slothful man saith, There is a lion without, I shall be slain in the streets." Proverbs 27:12: "A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself; but the simple pass on, and are punished."
- Labels: Text states: the proverbs. Inference: the mechanism is rationalization, not fear. The text does not name the mechanism, and the reading belongs to the reader.
- Related: A4, D4, F3.

## D4 · Partial obedience

Modern name: self-serving interpretation.

- Description: 1 Samuel 15 is the most detailed case study of self-serving interpretation in the canon. It is description, not command. The sequence has five steps:
  1. Saul claims full obedience (15:13).
  2. The evidence contradicts him, and it is audible (15:14).
  3. He reinterprets the remainder as a higher obedience (15:15, "to sacrifice unto the LORD thy God").
  4. He states the claim again, flatly (15:20).
  5. Samuel gives the verdict (15:22-23).
- The lie: "The part I did is the whole. The part I did not do is a special case."
- Cause: The reinterpretation is not random. It keeps the cheap parts of the command. It changes the remainder into something that the person was already going to do. It keeps both the appearance of obedience and the desired outcome.
- Fruit: Rejection. The verdict of the text is severe and explicit: "Because thou hast rejected the word of the LORD, he hath also rejected thee from being king."
- Counter-truth: The same passage states the counter: "to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams." The counter-practice is to compare the original instruction with the report. Treat the gap as the object of attention, not as a special case.
- Counter-text: 1 Samuel 15:13: "And Samuel came to Saul: and Saul said unto him, Blessed be thou of the LORD: I have performed the commandment of the LORD." 1 Samuel 15:22: "And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams." 1 Samuel 15:23: "For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry. Because thou hast rejected the word of the LORD, he hath also rejected thee from being king."
- Labels: Text states: the narrative. Description, not prescription: the passage reports what Saul did. It does not command it. Inference: the mechanism applies to any instruction with an inconvenient remainder.
- Related: D3, D1, A2.

## D5 · Evil surmisings

Modern name: hostile attribution and mind-reading.

- Description: Paul lists "evil surmisings" among the fruits of a certain kind of argument, beside envy, strife, and railings. The word for surmisings is suspicion. The pattern assigns a hostile motive that nobody stated and nobody verified.
- The lie: "I know what they meant. My inference about their motive is knowledge."
- Cause: An inference about motive is fast, and it is often right. That is what makes it credible. It is also useful, because a hostile motive justifies a hostile response in advance.
- Fruit: Strife, railings, and the breakdown of trust. The damage is out of proportion, because nobody can answer the accusation. The accused must prove a negative.
- Counter-truth: The text reserves the reading of motive to God. The LORD pondereth the hearts, and man looks on the outward appearance. The counter-practice is to judge the act and its fruit, which you can see. State the inferred motive as an inference. 1 Corinthians 13:5 supplies the disposition. Charity "thinketh no evil".
- Counter-text: 1 Timothy 6:4: "He is proud, knowing nothing, but doting about questions and strifes of words, whereof cometh envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings," 1 Corinthians 13:5: "Doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil;" Proverbs 21:2: "Every way of a man is right in his own eyes: but the LORD pondereth the hearts." 1 Samuel 16:7: "...for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G5283 _hyponoia_, "suspicion." The "surmisings" of the KJV are suspicion. G3552 _noseo_, "to be sick, i.e. (by implication, of a diseased appetite) to hanker after (figuratively, to harp upon)". The "doting" of the KJV describes a diseased appetite that harps on a subject. Inference: the distinction between discernment of acts and mind-reading of motives. The text does not draw the line explicitly, and the reading belongs to the reader.
- Related: D2, A6, C4.

## D6 · Pride deceiving the heart

Modern name: none. The biblical name is the diagnosis.

- Description: Obadiah names the mechanism: "The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee..." The deception has content. It is the security of a high position: "Who shall bring me down to the ground?" Proverbs states the pattern in general form. Pride goes before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.
- The lie: "My position is secure because it is high."
- Cause: In many systems, height does protect. The proud person usually assesses the position accurately at the moment of the assessment. The error is the extension of that moment into the future.
- Fruit: Destruction and the fall. The text gives the counter in the same form. Proverbs 18:12 says, "Before destruction the heart of man is haughty, and before honour is humility."
- Counter-truth: The counter is not an estimate of low position. It is a transfer of trust. The pride of the heart deceives because the heart makes the estimate. The remedy is a different estimator.
- Counter-text: Obadiah 1:3: "The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee, thou that dwellest in the clefts of the rock, whose habitation is high; that saith in his heart, Who shall bring me down to the ground?" Obadiah 1:4: "Though thou exalt thyself as the eagle, and though thou set thy nest among the stars, thence will I bring thee down, saith the LORD." Proverbs 16:18: "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall." Proverbs 18:12: "Before destruction the heart of man is haughty, and before honour is humility."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Note: Proverbs 16:18 and 18:12 are proverbs. They state tendencies, not a mechanism that works the same way in every case. They are not promises that every proud person falls at once.
- Related: A5, C3, F8.

## D7 · Making provision for the flesh

Modern name: a personal environment designed to fail.

- Description: The command is not "avoid the flesh". It is "make not provision for the flesh". Provision is logistics: supply, arrangement, and forethought. The verse forbids an arrangement of the circumstances in advance on behalf of the appetite. The positive half of the same verse gives the replacement supply: "But put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ..."
- The lie: "I can be near the occasion and still be free of it."
- Cause: The person usually makes the provision for an innocent reason. The route passes the place. The meeting is at the hour. The device is in the room. You can defend each step, but you cannot defend the arrangement.
- Fruit: The planned fall. The failure is not a moment of weakness. It is a decision already made and stored in the environment. It waits for the moment of weakness to arrive.
- Counter-truth: The counter is a different provision, not a deprivation. Put on Christ. That is the same kind of act, a deliberate supply in advance. Proverbs 4:23 states the general form: "Keep thy heart with all diligence..." Psalm 101:3 and Job 31:1 supply the specific practice. It is a decision about the eyes, made before the occasion arrives.
- Counter-text: Romans 13:14: "But put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh, to fulfil the lusts thereof." Proverbs 4:23: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life." Psalm 101:3: "I will set no wicked thing before mine eyes: I hate the work of them that turn aside; it shall not cleave to me." Job 31:1: "I made a covenant with mine eyes; why then should I think upon a maid?"
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G4307 _pronoia_, "forethought, i.e. provident care or supply". The same word has a positive use for providence. So the verse forbids forethought on behalf of the flesh, not forethought as such. G4561 _sarx_, "flesh (as stripped of the skin), i.e. (strictly) the meat of an animal (as food), or (by extension) the body (as opposed to the soul (or spirit), or as the symbol of what is external, or as the means of kindred), or (by implication) human nature (with its frailties (physically or morally) and passions), or (specially), a human being (as such)". Inference: the modern practice of environment design is the same mechanism, read in either direction.
- Related: D3, F6, B2.

# Group E: Words and information

These patterns corrupt the medium. They are failures in how people produce, consume, and inherit words. They matter because the earlier groups travel through them.

## E1 · Vain jangling and endless questions

Modern name: analysis paralysis and doomscrolling.

- Description: Four texts name a family of failures:
  1. Babble (1 Timothy 1:6).
  2. A diseased appetite for questions (6:4).
  3. Questions that "gender strifes" (2 Timothy 2:23).
  4. Questions that are "unprofitable and vain" (Titus 3:9).

  The common feature is not the difficulty of the questions. It is their fruit.
- The lie: "More questions is the same as more understanding. Engagement is progress."
- Cause: Questions feel like diligence and look like humility. They also delay the cost of a conclusion, and that is the real attraction. Nobody can hold a person with an open question to an answer.
- Fruit: The list in 1 Timothy 6:4: envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings. Also the failure of direction in 1 Timothy 1:6: "some having swerved have turned aside..."
- Counter-truth: The text supplies an end, not only a limit: "the end of the commandment is charity out of a pure heart..." Measure the questions against that end. The instruction in 2 Timothy and Titus is "avoid", which is a command to disengage. The counter-practice is not more analysis. It is action.
- Counter-text: 1 Timothy 1:6: "From which some having swerved have turned aside unto vain jangling;" 1 Timothy 1:5: "Now the end of the commandment is charity out of a pure heart, and of a good conscience, and of faith unfeigned:" 1 Timothy 6:4: "He is proud, knowing nothing, but doting about questions and strifes of words, whereof cometh envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings," 2 Timothy 2:23: "But foolish and unlearned questions avoid, knowing that they do gender strifes." Titus 3:9: "But avoid foolish questions, and genealogies, and contentions, and strivings about the law; for they are unprofitable and vain."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G3150 _mataiologia_, "random talk, i.e. babble." G3552 _noseo_, "to be sick, i.e. (by implication, of a diseased appetite) to hanker after (figuratively, to harp upon)". Inference: the "doomscroll" is this pattern with an infinite supply. The text names the appetite, not the medium.
- Related: E2, E3, A3.

## E2 · Itching ears

Modern name: echo chambers and algorithmic feeds.

- Description: In the text, the demand side drives the mechanism. People "after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers". Desire drives the selection, and the supply multiplies. The text states the result. They will not endure sound doctrine, and they turn from truth to fables.
- The lie: "What I want to hear is what I need to hear."
- Cause: The tickle is pleasant, and sound doctrine is not. "Itching" is the word of the lexicon for it. It is the desire for the scratch. The scratch satisfies it, and then it returns.
- Fruit: Intolerance of sound doctrine, and the exchange of truth for fiction. The word for fables is _mythos_, the same word that gives "myth".
- Counter-truth: The same passage states the counter-practice: preach the word, reprove, rebuke, exhort. The test of a teacher is not whether the teaching is agreeable. It is whether the teaching is sound. The word is _hugiaino_, healthy. A diagnosis follows from this. If nothing you read contradicts you, something tickles you and does not teach you. That diagnosis is an Inference.
- Counter-text: 2 Timothy 4:3-4: "For the time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears; And they shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be turned unto fables." 2 Timothy 4:2: "Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort with all longsuffering and doctrine." 2 Timothy 3:16: "All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness:"
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G2833 _knetho_, "to scratch, i.e. (by implication) to tickle." G5198 _hugiaino_, "to have sound health, i.e. be well (in body); figuratively, to be uncorrupt (true in doctrine)." G3454 _mythos_, "a tale, i.e. fiction ('myth')". Inference: a recommendation algorithm is this mechanism at industrial scale, because the ranking function learns preference, and preference is not need. Contested: the research literature disputes the strength of filter-bubble and echo-chamber effects on individuals. The tools here cannot measure it. Hold the claim at the level of mechanism, not size.
- Related: E1, E5, A8.

## E3 · Talebearing and whispering

Modern name: gossip and rumor.

- Description: Four texts describe the same traffic. The words of the talebearer are "as wounds", and they go "into the innermost parts of the belly". The damage is internal, and it lasts. The whisperer "separateth chief friends." Where there is no talebearer, the strife ceases. Leviticus states the prohibition directly.
- The lie: "To pass it on is not the same as to invent it."
- Cause: The information is often true. That is what lets the person who carries it defend the pattern. It also buys attention and a short-lived closeness with the listener.
- Fruit: Separated friends, kindled strife, and wounds that go down into the innermost parts. Speculation: some readers connect the word for the whisperer with a verb that means to grumble and rebel. On that reading, the whisper is a form of rebellion, not only idle talk. The bundled lexicon does not confirm the connection (see Labels).
- Counter-truth: The remedy in the text is subtraction, not correction. Remove the wood, and the fire goes out. The counter-practice is to refuse the role of carrier. That is different from a refusal to hear. The instruction of the text is to the talebearer. The part of the listener is to stop being the fuel.
- Counter-text: Proverbs 16:28: "A froward man soweth strife: and a whisperer separateth chief friends." Proverbs 18:8: "The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly." Proverbs 26:20: "Where no wood is, there the fire goeth out: so where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth." Leviticus 19:16: "Thou shalt not go up and down as a talebearer among thy people: neither shalt thou stand against the blood of thy neighbour: I am the LORD."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: the "whisperer" of Proverbs 16:28 is H5372 _nirgan_, "a slanderer". Its KJV glosses are "talebearer, whisperer." H7400 _rakil_, "a scandal-monger (as travelling about)". H7279 _ragan_, "to grumble, i.e. rebel", has the KJV gloss "murmur". Inference: H5372 and H7279 share the same consonants, and many readers derive the whisperer from the verb to grumble and rebel. The bundled lexicon derives H5372 "from an unused root meaning to roll to pieces". So the link to rebellion is not confirmed.
- Related: E4, D5, F6.

## E4 · Swelling words

Modern name: hype and jargon.

- Description: The image is inflation, a thing larger than its content. 2 Peter calls them "great swelling words of vanity". The sense of the lexicon for "swelling" is "bulging over." Ephesians calls the same thing "vain words", and the word for vain is "empty". Swelling and empty are the same object seen from two sides. It looks large and contains nothing.
- The lie: "The size of the language is the size of the thing."
- Cause: Large language signals confidence, and confidence is often a real signal of competence. The pattern cuts that signal off from the thing that it must indicate.
- Fruit: Allurement through lusts (2 Peter 2:18). In Jude, the text names the motive. The people who use it have "men's persons in admiration because of advantage." The swelling words are not vanity in the sense of uselessness. They are a sales instrument.
- Counter-truth: The counter is not crude speech but plain speech. Matthew 5:37 gives the standard: "But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay..." The words must carry exactly what they promise. Note the false friend. The "communication" of the KJV here is speech. In 1 Corinthians 15:33, the same English word renders _homilia_, which means companionship, not speech.
- Counter-text: 2 Peter 2:18: "For when they speak great swelling words of vanity, they allure through the lusts of the flesh, through much wantonness, those that were clean escaped from them who live in error." Jude 1:16: "These are murmurers, complainers, walking after their own lusts; and their mouth speaketh great swelling words, having men's persons in admiration because of advantage." Ephesians 5:6: "Let no man deceive you with vain words: for because of these things cometh the wrath of God upon the children of disobedience." Matthew 5:37: "But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G5246 _hyperonkos_, "bulging over, i.e. (figuratively) insolent." G2756 _kenos_, "empty (literally or figuratively)." G3657 _homilia_, "companionship ('homily'), i.e. (by implication) intercourse". The KJV renders it "communication" at 1 Corinthians 15:33, where the sense is association, not speech. Inference: technical jargon is not swelling words by default. The test is whether the term adds information. The text does not draw that line.
- Related: E1, E6, E7.

## E5 · Tradition and the commandments of men

Modern name: institutional inertia, and "we always did it this way."

- Description: The texts do not condemn tradition as such. They condemn tradition set against the commandment: "ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition." People use the human rule to cancel the divine rule. Isaiah adds a second form. The fear of God itself can be "taught by the precept of men". That is reverence passed on as a technique.
- The lie: "Age is authority. We always did it this way."
- Cause: An inherited practice is usually the compressed wisdom of people who faced the same problem. The pattern cuts that wisdom off from its reason. After that, nobody can examine it again, and nobody can tell it apart from a rule that only protects the institution.
- Fruit: Worship in vain, and the commandment made of none effect. Also, the person is carried off as plunder. In Colossians 2:8, "spoil" is a looting word, "to lead away as booty".
- Counter-truth: The test in Colossians is the source: "after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ." The counter-practice is to ask what a rule was for, and whether the answer is still true. The rule itself cannot supply that answer.
- Counter-text: Matthew 15:3: "But he answered and said unto them, Why do ye also transgress the commandment of God by your tradition?" Matthew 15:6: "And honour not his father or his mother, he shall be free. Thus have ye made the commandment of God of none effect by your tradition." Matthew 15:9: "But in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men." Mark 7:9: "And he said unto them, Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition." Colossians 2:8: "Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ." Isaiah 29:13: "Wherefore the Lord said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men:"
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G3862 _paradosis_, "transmission, i.e. (concretely) a precept; specially, the Jewish traditionary law". The word means transmission. So the authority that it claims is the fact that someone handed it down. G1778 _entalma_, "an injunction, i.e. religious precept". Inference: the application to schooling belongs to the reader, and it is partly Contested. See `institutions.md`. The target of the text itself is religious tradition, not education as such.
- Related: E6, E7, A1.

## E6 · Philosophy and vain deceit

Modern name: none. The biblical name is the diagnosis.

- Description: Colossians 2:8 gives a warning and a test. The warning is that someone can carry a person off as plunder. The test is the source: "after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ." The verse is not a general prohibition of thought. It is a criterion for systems.
- The lie: "The sophistication of a system is evidence of its truth."
- Cause: Coherent systems are impressive, and coherence is a real virtue. The pattern treats coherence as enough, but the test of the text is the source.
- Fruit: The person is spoiled, which means carried off as booty. The word describes a person who is plundered while he believes that he is educated.
- Counter-truth: The text states the criterion: after Christ. The same letter supplies the positive content in other places. The counter-practice is to ask of any system what it takes as its first principle. Then ask whether that first principle is Christ or a rudiment.
- Counter-text: Colossians 2:8: "Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ." Colossians 2:18: "Let no man beguile you of your reward in a voluntary humility and worshipping of angels, intruding into those things which he hath not seen, vainly puffed up by his fleshly mind,"
- Labels: Text states: the passage. Lexical: G5385 _philosophia_, "'philosophy', i.e. (specially), Jewish sophistry". The specific sense in the dictionary is Jewish sophistry. So the target of the verse is a particular religious and philosophical mixture, not philosophy as an academic discipline. G4747 _stoicheion_, "something orderly in arrangement, i.e. (by implication) a serial (basal, fundamental, initial) constituent (literally), proposition (figuratively)". The "rudiments" are elementary parts, the ABCs of a system. G4812 _sylagogeo_, "to lead away as booty, i.e. (figuratively) seduce". Honest note: preachers often use the verse as a blanket condemnation of philosophy. The lexical data does not support that. The verse condemns a system whose elementary principles are not Christ.
- Related: E5, E7, E1.

## E7 · Science falsely so called

Modern name: claims that wear the authority of method without the method.

- Description: Paul tells Timothy to avoid "oppositions of science falsely so called". The fault is in the name. People call something knowledge that is not knowledge. The Greek word rendered "science" here is _gnosis_, the ordinary word for knowledge. It is not a term for the modern institution.
- The lie: "The label 'study' or 'science' or 'research' transfers authority to the claim."
- Cause: The method earned its authority because it works. The pattern borrows that authority and does not do the work. A person presents a claim in the grammar of findings, but the method that produces a finding is absent.
- Fruit: "Oppositions". The counterfeit produces contention, not knowledge. It also produces the opposite error in the same person. That error is the rejection of verified findings because they come from a distrusted source. That is A8 with the sign reversed, and it is the same failure.
- Counter-truth: The counter is the test: "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good." The counter-practice is to ask what the claim actually rests on. Accept or reject it on that basis, not on the label.
- Counter-text: 1 Timothy 6:20: "O Timothy, keep that which is committed to thy trust, avoiding profane and vain babblings, and oppositions of science falsely so called:" 1 Thessalonians 5:21: "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good." Proverbs 14:15: "The simple believeth every word: but the prudent man looketh well to his going."
- Labels: Text states: the passage. Lexical: G1108 _gnosis_, "knowing (the act), i.e. (by implication) knowledge", with the KJV gloss "knowledge, science". G5581 _pseudonymos_, "untruly named". Honest note: people often use this verse to dismiss science as an institution. The lexical data does not support that reading. The target is claims that are untruly named knowledge, not knowledge that comes from a test. Inference: the same criticism applies in the other direction, to claims made in the name of "research" or "data" without the method.
- Related: A8, E4, E5.

# Group F: Character of the thinker

These patterns corrupt the person who does the thinking. They are not errors in one judgment. They are conditions that decide what kind of judgments a person can make. The first entry is the test for the rest.

## F1 · The two wisdoms

Modern name: none. The test is the biblical name.

- Description: James 3:13-18 presents two wisdoms and gives the fruit of each one. The first "descendeth not from above". It is earthly, sensual, devilish, and it produces confusion and every evil work. The wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy. The test is based on fruit. You know a wisdom by what it produces.
- The lie: "The quality of an argument does not depend on its effect. A true idea can produce chaos and still be true."
- Cause: The pattern attracts because some true statements do disturb. The text does not say that every disturbing truth is false. It says that the wisdom from above has a particular character, and you can test the character.
- Fruit: The text names it directly: "For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work." The word for confusion means instability and disorder. The wisdom from above produces the opposite list.
- Counter-truth: The order in verse 17 matters: "first pure, then peaceable". Purity comes before peace. A person does not get peace by giving up purity. The counter-practice is the full list, not one item from it.
- Counter-text: James 3:14-16: "But if ye have bitter envying and strife in your hearts, glory not, and lie not against the truth. This wisdom descendeth not from above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish. For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work." James 3:17: "But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy." James 3:18: "And the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace of them that make peace."
- Labels: Text states: the passage. Lexical: G5591 _psychikos_, "sensitive, i.e. animate (in distinction on the one hand from G4152 (πνευματικός), which is the higher or renovated nature; and on the other from G5446 (φυσικός), which is the lower or bestial nature)". The "sensual" of the KJV means soulish or only animate, not sexual. It is the middle level, life without the Spirit. G181 _akatastasia_, "instability, i.e. disorder". The "confusion" of the KJV is instability. G2138 _eupeithes_, "good for persuasion, i.e. (intransitively) complaint", with the KJV gloss "easy to be intreated". The definition reads oddly, probably for "compliant". This file quotes it as the tool returns it and does not smooth it. G87 _adiakritos_, "properly, undistinguished, i.e. (actively) impartial". Description: the passage instructs people who claim wisdom. The test applies to a wisdom, not to the standing of a person. See the two wisdoms test below.
- Related: the two wisdoms test, and all groups.

## F2 · Despising the day of small things

Modern name: none. The biblical name is the diagnosis.

- Description: The question is rhetorical: "For who hath despised the day of small things?" The context is the rebuilding of the temple. When the builders laid the foundation, the old men who saw the first house wept (Ezra 3:12). Haggai 2:3 asks whether the new house is "in comparison of it as nothing". Zechariah 4:10 answers that "they shall rejoice, and shall see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel". The plummet is the measuring instrument, in use. Text states: the verse predicts joy. It does not pronounce a formal blessing.
- The lie: "Small work is not real work. A serious person waits for a serious stage."
- Cause: Small work is invisible, and visibility is a real good. The pattern trades the work for the appearance of the work. That is why it can look like high standards.
- Fruit: Abandoned foundations and the refusal to begin. Also the inability to improve, because improvement needs a first small version to improve.
- Counter-truth: The counter in the text is the plummet in the hand, which is measurement and progress on a small structure. Proverbs 24:3-4 gives the sequence: build, establish, fill. Luke 16:10 states the rule. Faithfulness in the least is the same disposition as faithfulness in much.
- Counter-text: Zechariah 4:10: "For who hath despised the day of small things? for they shall rejoice, and shall see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel with those seven; they are the eyes of the LORD, which run to and fro through the whole earth." Ezra 3:12: "But many of the priests and Levites and chief of the fathers, who were ancient men, that had seen the first house, when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes, wept with a loud voice; and many shouted aloud for joy:" Proverbs 24:3-4: "Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established: And by knowledge shall the chambers be filled with all precious and pleasant riches." Luke 16:10: "He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Inference: the application of the temple scene in Zechariah to ordinary work. The subject of the text itself is the temple. The general application belongs to the reader.
- Related: F3, F4, B5.

## F3 · Complacency and ease

Modern name: none. The biblical name is the diagnosis.

- Description: Amos pronounces woe on those "at ease in Zion" who trust in the mountain. Proverbs states the mechanism. The turning away of the simple shall slay them, and the prosperity of fools shall destroy them. The word for "at ease" means secure, and in a bad sense, haughty.
- The lie: "Comfort is safety."
- Cause: Ease is pleasant, and people usually earn it. The pattern treats earned rest as a permanent condition. That removes the pressure that produced the learning.
- Fruit: The text names it: destruction, and death by your own prosperity. The mechanism is that prosperity removes the feedback that corrects the person.
- Counter-truth: The counter is not the rejection of rest. It is the rejection of false security: "let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall." The counter-practice is to keep a live test in place while conditions are good.
- Counter-text: Amos 6:1: "Woe to them that are at ease in Zion, and trust in the mountain of Samaria, which are named chief of the nations, to whom the house of Israel came!" Proverbs 1:32: "For the turning away of the simple shall slay them, and the prosperity of fools shall destroy them." 1 Corinthians 10:12: "Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: H7600 _sha'anan_, "secure; in a bad sense, haughty". Inference: the mechanism is the removal of feedback. The text states the outcome, not the mechanism.
- Related: F4, D6, C6.

## F4 · Fainting in the day of adversity

Modern name: none. The biblical name is the diagnosis.

- Description: The proverb diagnoses. It does not exhort: "If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small." It does not say that the strength is small because of the adversity. It says that the adversity showed what was already true.
- The lie: "The pressure is proof that the work is wrong. Difficulty is a signal to stop."
- Cause: In some cases, difficulty is a real signal, and to quit is the right choice. The pattern applies a rule that is sometimes valid to every case. It does so at the moment when judgment is weakest.
- Fruit: Work abandoned at the moment when it is most needed, and the misreading of a test as a verdict. James supplies the correct reading of the same event. The trying of faith works patience.
- Counter-truth: The counter is the distinction between a test and a verdict. The proverb states that the day of adversity reveals strength. James states that the trial produces endurance. Isaiah 28:16 supplies the disposition: "he that believeth shall not make haste."
- Counter-text: Proverbs 24:10: "If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small." James 1:2-4: "My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing." Isaiah 28:16: "Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD, Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone, a sure foundation: he that believeth shall not make haste."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: H7503 _raphah_, "to slacken (in many applications, literal or figurative)". The "faint" of the KJV is to slacken. The KJV gloss list for the same word also has "slothful". The failure is a loosened grip, not an emotional collapse. Inference: "faint" and "slothful" are one condition, not two. The lexicon supports the shared word, and the identification belongs to the reader.
- Related: C6, F3, B5.

## F5 · Wavering and double-mindedness

Modern name: none. The biblical name is the diagnosis.

- Description: James describes a man who asks and does not receive, and he gives the reason. The man must ask "nothing wavering", and the wavering man is like a wave driven and tossed. The text names the condition at the end: "A double minded man is unstable in all his ways."
- The lie: "I can keep both options and delay the judgment. Suspended judgment is neutral."
- Cause: A delayed decision avoids the cost of being wrong. The pattern attracts because both options stay available. The loss of one option is what makes decisions expensive.
- Fruit: Instability in all his ways, and nothing received. Note that the instability is general. The text says "all his ways", not only the matter in question.
- Counter-truth: The remedy in the text is not more information. It is to ask in faith. The counter-practice is to close the question, and the text treats that as a condition of receiving. Elijah states the same demand as a question: "How long halt ye between two opinions?"
- Counter-text: James 1:5-6: "If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him. But let him ask in faith, nothing wavering. For he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed." James 1:8: "A double minded man is unstable in all his ways." 1 Kings 18:21: "And Elijah came unto all the people, and said, How long halt ye between two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him. And the people answered him not a word."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: G1252 _diakrino_, "to separate thoroughly, i.e. (literally and reflexively) to withdraw from, or (by implication) oppose; figuratively, to discriminate (by implication, decide), or (reflexively) hesitate". In James 1:6, the sense is to hesitate. Speculation: the word is built on _krino_, to judge, and some readers take the wavering man as a man who cannot judge. The parts of a word do not fix its meaning in a verse, so hold this loosely. G1374 _dipsychos_, "two-spirited, i.e. vacillating (in opinion or purpose)".
- Related: C5, F1, A2.

## F6 · Bad company

Modern name: social contagion.

- Description: Proverbs states the rule. The one who walks with wise men will be wise, and the companion of fools will be destroyed. The second proverb is more specific. Do not make friendship with an angry man, lest thou learn his ways and get a snare to thy soul. Paul states the principle in a sentence: "evil communications corrupt good manners."
- The lie: "I can be near without being changed. My character does not depend on my company."
- Cause: Companionship is a real good, and loyalty is a real virtue. The pattern extends loyalty to a person into imitation of the person. Nearness does that, whatever the intention.
- Fruit: Destruction, learned ways, a snare to the soul, and corrupted manners. The psalm gives the positive form of the same law: "Blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly..."
- Counter-truth: The counter is not the rejection of people. It is the choice of counsel. The verbs of the text are walk, stand, and sit, which are degrees of nearness. The alternative of the blessed man is delight in the law. The law takes the place of the counsel.
- Counter-text: Proverbs 13:20: "He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed." 1 Corinthians 15:33: "Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners." Proverbs 22:24-25: "Make no friendship with an angry man; and with a furious man thou shalt not go: Lest thou learn his ways, and get a snare to thy soul." Psalm 1:1-2: "Blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly, nor standeth in the way of sinners, nor sitteth in the seat of the scornful. But his delight is in the law of the LORD; and in his law doth he meditate day and night."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Lexical: in Proverbs 13:20, "walketh" is H1980 _halak_, "to walk (in a great variety of applications, literally and figuratively)". The "companion" is H7462 _ra'ah_, "to tend a flock; i.e. pasture it; intransitively, to graze (literally or figuratively); generally to rule; by extension, to associate with (as a friend)". The word for the companion is the shepherding verb. Inference: you graze where your companions graze. G3657 _homilia_, "companionship ('homily'), i.e. (by implication) intercourse". The "evil communications" of the KJV at 1 Corinthians 15:33 means evil companionships, not evil speech. This is a 1611 false friend. The verse is not about bad language.
- Related: D7, E3, F1.

## F7 · Broken cisterns

Modern name: substitutes that cannot hold value.

- Description: Jeremiah states two evils in one sentence. The people forsook the fountain of living waters, and they hewed out cisterns. The cisterns are not only inadequate. They are broken, so they cannot hold what goes into them. They are also hewn, so the people put labor into the substitute.
- The lie: "I can store the living water in a container of my own design."
- Cause: The person who needs the cistern designs it, so it feels right. The work to hew it is also real work. That gives the substitute the appearance of a serious project.
- Fruit: Thirst, and labor without supply. The water is not lost because God never gave it. It is lost because the vessel cannot hold it.
- Counter-truth: The counter is the fountain. The image of the text for it is a spring, not a store. The water that Christ gives is "a well of water springing up into everlasting life." A spring does not need a cistern.
- Counter-text: Jeremiah 2:13: "For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water." John 4:14: "But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Inference: the pattern extends from idols to any substitute source. The subject of the text itself is the forsaking of God for idols, and the extension belongs to the reader.
- Related: F8, B7, B4.

## F8 · Feeding on ashes

Modern name: deception that arrives with self-justification.

- Description: Isaiah 44 describes the maker of an idol. He uses half the wood for fuel and the other half for a god. Then he cannot ask whether the thing in his own hand is a lie. The verse names the mechanism: "a deceived heart hath turned him aside". The deception is what prevents the diagnosis.
- The lie: "The substitute works."
- Cause: The substitute does provide something. The same tree feeds and warms the maker of the idol. The deception is not that the object does nothing. It is that the person gives the object credit for what the real source provided.
- Fruit: The inability to diagnose yourself: "he cannot deliver his soul, nor say, Is there not a lie in my right hand?" The lie is in his right hand. It is what he holds and uses. And the food is ashes.
- Counter-truth: The counter is the question that the deceived person cannot ask, so another person must ask it. The remedy in the chapter itself is forgiveness and return. Shame does not answer the deception. The offer that makes return possible answers it. John supplies the practice: "keep yourselves from idols."
- Counter-text: Isaiah 44:20: "He feedeth on ashes: a deceived heart hath turned him aside, that he cannot deliver his soul, nor say, Is there not a lie in my right hand?" Isaiah 44:22: "I have blotted out, as a thick cloud, thy transgressions, and, as a cloud, thy sins: return unto me; for I have redeemed thee." 1 John 5:21: "Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen." Proverbs 4:23: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."
- Labels: Text states: the passages. Inference: the extension from carved idols to modern substitutes. Note: the subject of the verse itself is the maker of the idol. The application to other substitutes is a move of the reader, and a reader who uses it must label it as such.
- Related: D1, F7, D6.

# The two wisdoms test

James 3:13-18 is the working checklist for this skill. This file uses it because it does not ask whether a thought feels true or whether the person who holds it is sincere. It asks what the thought produces. A pattern of thought is a wisdom, and you know a wisdom by its fruit.

## The text

> 13 Who is a wise man and endued with knowledge among you? let him shew out of a good conversation his works with meekness of wisdom.
> 14 But if ye have bitter envying and strife in your hearts, glory not, and lie not against the truth.
> 15 This wisdom descendeth not from above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish.
> 16 For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.
> 17 But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy.
> 18 And the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace of them that make peace.

## The checklist

The wisdom from below fails the test. A pattern fails if its usual output includes these markers:

| Marker                  | What to look for                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bitter envying          | Comparison drives the thought. The person feels the good of someone else as his own loss.                                                                 |
| Strife                  | The thought produces quarrels, factions, or a permanent opponent.                                                                                        |
| Boasting or glorying    | The function of the thought is to establish the superiority of the holder. Verse 14 says "glory not". If you cannot hold the thought without glorying, it fails. |
| Lying against the truth | The thought requires the suppression of known facts.                                                                                                     |
| Confusion               | The Greek is _akatastasia_, instability and disorder. The thought produces agitation, not settled judgment.                                             |
| Every evil work         | The fruit includes acts that the person condemns in someone else.                                                                                        |

The wisdom from above passes the test. A pattern passes if its usual output includes these markers:

| Marker                        | What to look for                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pure                          | First in the list. It does not require a compromise of what you know to be right.                                     |
| Peaceable                     | It produces peace. Note the order: purity first, then peace. Peace bought by impurity is not this.                   |
| Gentle                        | The manner does not force.                                                                                           |
| Easy to be intreated          | The Greek word means persuadable. A better argument can move the holder. This is the direct opposite of A3 and A4.   |
| Full of mercy and good fruits | It produces concrete good, not only correct analysis.                                                                |
| Without partiality            | It does not sort people by face or group (A7).                                                                       |
| Without hypocrisy             | It is the same in private as in public.                                                                              |

## How to run the test on a thought

1. State the thought as a sentence. Do not write "I feel bad about work." Write "People disrespect me, and that means I must withdraw." You cannot test a pattern that you cannot state as a sentence.
2. Name what it produces. Do not name what it can produce, or what it produces in theory. Name what it actually produces in the week of this person. You can observe confusion, strife, and envy.
3. Ask the source question. Does this descend from above, or is it earthly, soulish, and demonic? The three words in verse 15 are not synonyms. They are a descent: worldly, then only natural, then diabolical. The test is not whether the thought is clever. It is where the thought comes from.
4. Apply the warning of verse 14. Suppose that you cannot hold the thought without glorying, that is, without making its holder superior. Then the text already gave the verdict.
5. Apply the purity test first. Purity comes first in verse 17. So a proposed peace that requires impurity fails at the first item. Here the test does its hardest work.
6. Apply the teachability test. "Easy to be intreated" is a property of the person who holds the thought, not of the thought alone. A person whom nothing can move on a matter holds a wisdom from below, whatever the content.
7. Replace the thought, then do the test again later. The fruit appears over time. A thought that passes today can show its fruit in a month, and you can repeat the test.

## Limits of the test: read before you use it

- The test diagnoses. It does not judge. It evaluates a pattern, not the standing of a person. James speaks to people who claim wisdom. The test tells you what kind of wisdom you hold, not whether someone is saved.
- The test is not a license for contempt. "This thought fails the test" is a statement about the thought. It does not state that its holder is a fool. The reader can fall into every pattern in this catalog.
- A person can hold a true statement in a wisdom from below. The test does not replace the search for truth. It adds a second question: how does the person hold the truth, and what does it produce?
- Some fruit takes time. Judgment in the sense of premature conclusion (A3) is forbidden here. Do not do the test on the fruit of a person after one conversation.
- Disagreement is not strife. The text condemns strife, not difference. Two people can hold different views in peace. The guards section below governs how to handle the difference.

# Guards against false detection

This section is required, not decorative. A catalog of thought errors has a predictable failure. It becomes an instrument for contempt. The person who learns the patterns can begin to see them everywhere, in everyone. Then the skill becomes a machine that produces four failures. Each failure is itself a pattern from the catalog:

- Arrogance is D6 (pride deceiving the heart).
- Legalism is E5 (the commandments of men), with A7 (partiality toward your own group).
- Conspiracy thinking is D5 (evil surmisings).
- Spiritual pride is F1, the wisdom from below that glories (James 3:14).

Read this section before you apply any entry to another person.

## The keystone: love is the first principle of motive

The governing rule of the whole skill family is 1 Corinthians 13. The word rendered "charity" is _agape_, benevolent love. In 1611, "charity" meant that, not almsgiving. The passage makes love part of what gives the act its value. Love is not an extra.

> 1 Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not charity, I am become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal.
> 2 And though I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries, and all knowledge; and though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not charity, I am nothing.
> 3 And though I bestow all my goods to feed the poor, and though I give my body to be burned, and have not charity, it profiteth me nothing.

Rule: if a reading of this catalog produces cruelty, the reading is wrong. The catalog is not an exception to the keystone.

## The guards

### 1. Own beam first

Matthew 7:3-5 states the procedure: "first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote..." The order is not optional, and the beam is the larger object. Apply it here. Before you diagnose the pattern of another person, name your own.

### 2. Knowledge inflates, and love builds

1 Corinthians 8:1: "Now as touching things offered unto idols, we know that we all have knowledge. Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth." The Greek for "puffeth up" is _physioo_, "to inflate". "Edifieth" is _oikodomeo_, "to be a house-builder". The two verbs describe two different results. A person who learned this catalog and became inflated by it produced the first result and not the second.

### 3. Disputable matters are not the same as errors

Romans 14:1: "Him that is weak in the faith receive ye, but not to doubtful disputations." Romans 14:3: "Let not him that eateth despise him that eateth not; and let not him which eateth not judge him that eateth: for God hath received him." Romans 14:5: "...Let every man be fully persuaded in his own mind." Romans 14:13: "Let us not therefore judge one another any more: but judge this rather, that no man put a stumblingblock or an occasion to fall in his brother's way."

The chapter separates matters of conscience from matters of command. It forbids contempt in both directions: despising and judging. Much of what the readers of this catalog will want to correct is in this category.

### 4. Instruct in meekness, not in triumph

2 Timothy 2:24-25: "And the servant of the Lord must not strive; but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient, In meekness instructing those that oppose themselves; if God peradventure will give them repentance to the acknowledging of the truth;"

Note the phrase "those that oppose themselves." The text describes the person in error as one who opposes his own interest. That is an argument for pity, not contempt. Note also who gets the credit for the change: "if God peradventure will give them repentance".

### 5. Restore in meekness, and consider yourself

Galatians 6:1: "Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted." The instruction contains its own guard. It tells the corrector to consider himself. The Greek word for meekness is _praiotes_, "gentleness, by implication, humility".

### 6. Answer with meekness and fear

1 Peter 3:15: "But sanctify the Lord God in your hearts: and be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you with meekness and fear:" The text commands the manner as part of the answer. "Fear" here is not fear of the person. It is the disposition that keeps the one who answers aware of whose presence he is in.

### 7. Be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath

James 1:19: "Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:" The three-part sequence is the counter-procedure to A3 and A4. The text puts wrath last and makes it slow. It does not remove it. The point is the sequence, not the absence of any reaction.

### 8. Hear the other side first

Proverbs 18:17: "He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him." Apply it to the catalog. Before you conclude that the thought of a person is a pattern from this list, let someone search the account of that person. The first account always seems just.

### 9. The goal is renewal, not victory

The name of the skill states its purpose: renewal. A corrected person is the goal. A defeated person is not. Romans 12:2 gives the mechanism: "...be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind..." Transformation is not the same as refutation. Where the choice is between winning the argument and the renewal of the person, the text chooses the person. Inference: this comes from the purpose of the skill and the shape of the passages above. It is not a verse.

## Over-detection failure modes

These are the ways that the catalog itself goes wrong. Each one is a pattern from the catalog in the clothes of the catalog.

| Failure mode                                     | What it looks like                                                                                                                                       | The pattern it actually is                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Conspiracy where there is incompetence           | The person reads coordination and intent into outcomes that drift, error, or ordinary self-interest produced. The person infers motive and treats it as evidence. | D5 (evil surmisings), A4 (first in his own cause)                 |
| Treating preference as sin                       | The person turns a disputable matter into a command. Food, dress, schooling, music, and schedules are the classic cases.                                  | E5 (the commandments of men), A7 (partiality toward your own group) |
| Treating every disagreement as a spiritual defect | The person diagnoses the pattern of the other person from his conclusion. Because the other person disagrees, he must be in one of the patterns.          | A3 (answering before hearing), D2 (beam and mote)                 |
| Diagnosing a person from one conversation        | The person reads a single remark as a settled condition of character.                                                                                    | A6 (judging by appearance), D5                                    |
| Using the test to win                            | The person uses the two wisdoms test as a verdict against an opponent, not as a check on himself.                                                        | D6 (pride), F1 failed by the tester                               |
| Contempt as discernment                          | The tone carries superiority while the words carry analysis.                                                                                             | 1 Corinthians 8:1: inflation, not edification                     |

### The single check

Suppose that the catalog makes a person more able to see his own beam and more patient with those who oppose themselves. Then the person uses it correctly. Suppose that it makes the person more confident of his own standing and more contemptuous of others. Then the person uses it as a wisdom from below, and the test in James 3 already gave its verdict on the user.
