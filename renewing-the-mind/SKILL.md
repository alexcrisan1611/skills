# Renewing the Mind

## What this skill does

This skill finds broken patterns of thought. It then rebuilds them.

It exists because three forces train almost everyone into false patterns before the age of
twenty. Those forces are the school system, social media, and television with advertising. A
person does not choose most of their assumptions. They absorb them, and then they defend them
as if they had chosen them.

The skill has one purpose. It replaces a false pattern with a true one. It is not a tool for
winning arguments, and it is not a tool for contempt.

## Rule one: removal is not enough

Matthew 12:43-45 records the warning. An unclean spirit leaves a man. The man sweeps the house
and garnishes it, and the spirit returns with seven others, and the last state is worse than
the first.

A false pattern that is torn out and not replaced leaves a vacancy. Something fills it. This
is the reason the skill always ends with a replacement and never with a deletion.

Romans 12:2 states the same shape in one verse. "Be not conformed to this world, but be ye
transformed by the renewing of your mind." The negative command and the positive command
arrive together.

## Rule two: the beam comes first

Matthew 7:1-5. The person removing a mote from a brother's eye must first remove the beam
from his own.

Apply this literally to yourself before you apply anything here to another person. The catalog
in this skill exists to correct the reader, not to diagnose the reader's opponents. When you
use it on someone else, you will usually find what you are looking for, and you will usually be
wrong.

## Rule three: the goal is renewal, not victory

2 Timothy 2:24-26 defines the manner. "In meekness instructing those that oppose themselves."
Galatians 6:1 says restore in meekness. 1 Peter 3:15 says give an answer with meekness and
fear. James 1:19 says be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath.

A person who correctly names another person's error and does it with contempt has produced the
fruit that the catalog condemns. Read `references/thought-patterns.md`, section "Guards against
false detection", before using this skill on anyone other than yourself.

## The mandate

Scripture commands the renewal of the mind as an act, not as a mood.

2 Corinthians 10:3-5 describes the work. The weapons are not carnal, and they are used to cast
down imaginations and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, and to
bring every thought into captivity.

Proverbs 4:23 states the stakes. "Keep thy heart with all diligence, for out of it are the
issues of life."

The supporting texts are Romans 12:1-2, Isaiah 26:3, Philippians 2:5, Philippians 4:8,
Colossians 3:1-2, 1 Peter 1:13, 2 Timothy 1:7, and Titus 2:6.

## The deconstruction procedure

Run these eight steps in order. Steps 1 through 6 take the pattern apart. Steps 7 and 8 put
something true in its place.

**Step 1. Write the thought in one sentence.**
If you cannot state it in one sentence, you do not yet know what you believe. The act of
writing it down exposes most of the vagueness at once.

**Step 2. Name the source.**
Ask where the thought came from. The home, the classroom, the feed, the screen, a peer group,
or your own invention. A thought with no traceable source is usually a thought absorbed whole.
You do not have to accept a thought just because you cannot remember choosing it.

**Step 3. Expose the assumption underneath.**
Ask what must be true for the thought to hold. Most false patterns rest on one unexamined
premise. Find the premise and the thought often falls without further argument.

**Step 4. Run the three tests.**
The tests are true, wise, and loving. Details are below. All three must pass. A thought can be
true and still be unwise to speak, and it can be wise and still be loveless.

**Step 5. Trace the fruit.**
Ask what the thought produces when it runs for a year. Matthew 7:15-20 gives the rule. "By
their fruits ye shall know them." A pattern of thought is known by its output, and not by how
reasonable it feels from inside.

**Step 6. Find the counterfeit.**
Most lies are a truth with something added or removed. Name the truth the lie is imitating.
This step does more work than any other, because it converts a fight into a repair. For example,
the fear of man is a corrupted form of the fear of God. Envy is a corrupted form of a real
desire for good. The correction is not to kill the desire. The correction is to reattach it to
the right object.

**Step 7. Replace it, and write the replacement down.**
State the counter-truth in one sentence and attach the verse that carries it. A replacement
that lives only in your head will not survive the next encounter with the old pattern. Write
it. Say it aloud. Return to it.

**Step 8. Change the input.**
Thought follows diet. Philippians 4:8 lists what to hold in the mind, and Psalm 101:3 says, "I
will set no wicked thing before mine eyes." Job 31:1 records a covenant made with the eyes
before the temptation arrived. Remove the source where you can, and reduce it where you cannot.
A pattern that keeps returning is usually a pattern that keeps being fed.

## The three tests

**Test one: is it true?**
Philippians 4:8 gives the list. "Whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest,
whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely,
whatsoever things are of good report, if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise,
think on these things."
Ask whether the thought survives contact with the text and with the facts. A thought that
requires you to ignore a fact fails.

**Test two: is it wise?**
James 3:17 gives the profile. "The wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable,
gentle, and easy to be entreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and
without hypocrisy."
Ask whether the thought produces peace or confusion. Ask whether it makes you easier to reason
with or harder to reason with.

**Test three: is it loving?**
1 Corinthians 13:4-7 gives the anatomy. Charity suffers long, is kind, envies not, vaunts not
itself, is not puffed up, does not behave itself unseemly, seeks not her own, is not easily
provoked, thinks no evil, rejoices not in iniquity but rejoices in the truth, bears all things,
believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.
Ask what the thought does to your treatment of people. A true thought held without love still
fails this test, because the test is about the effect on you and not only about the truth of
the claim.

## The two wisdoms test

James 3:13-18 sets two wisdoms against each other, and it supplies a practical test.

The wisdom that is earthly, sensual, and devilish produces envy, strife, confusion, boasting,
and every evil work. The wisdom from above produces purity, peace, gentleness, mercy, good
fruits, impartiality, and sincerity.

Run the test on a pattern of thought by asking what it produces in the group where it is held.
A belief that reliably produces suspicion, division, and self-congratulation fails, even when
nobody can find the error in its logic. The full procedure is in
`references/thought-patterns.md`.

## The sources

Three institutions shape most of the patterns in the catalog. Each one captures attention,
defines worth by comparison to other people, and removes the foundation that Proverbs 1:7
names. That verse says, "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge."

The three are the school system, social media, and television with advertising. The mechanisms,
the counter-practices, and the parts that are genuinely contested are set out in
`references/institutions.md`.

A note on the contested parts. The theological claim is strong and the texts speak to it
directly. Knowledge that is defined without the fear of the LORD rests on a broken foundation,
and Proverbs 1:7 and Proverbs 9:10 say so. The empirical claims about schooling outcomes vary
by school and by country. Some schools teach well. Treat the theological claim as firm and the
empirical claims as testable, and do not merge the two.

## Output format

When answering from this skill, use this shape:

1. The pattern, named in one sentence.
2. The source, where it can be traced.
3. The assumption underneath.
4. The three tests, with the result of each.
5. The fruit.
6. The counterfeit, which is the truth it imitates.
7. The replacement, with the verse.
8. The change of input.
9. Confidence. Mark what the text states and what you inferred.

Keep the output short. A long answer about a single pattern is usually a sign that the pattern
was not found.

## Where this skill refuses

This skill does not do three things.

It does not diagnose other people. It has no authority to name a person's spiritual condition,
and the texts forbid the attempt in the manner most people attempt it.

It does not resolve disputable matters. Romans 14 draws a line between matters of sin and
matters of preference. A person who treats a preference as a sin has added a rule that God did
not give, and that is its own error.

It does not give medical advice. Anxiety, depression, and intrusive thought have medical
dimensions. A person in distress needs a physician as well as a pastor. Say so plainly when
that is what you see.
