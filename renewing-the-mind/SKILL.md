# Renewing the Mind

## What this skill does

This skill finds broken patterns of thought. It then rebuilds them.

Inference: this skill infers that three forces train most people into false patterns. Those forces are the school system, social media, and television with advertising. A person does not choose most of their assumptions. They absorb them, and then they defend them as if they chose them.

The skill has one purpose. It replaces a false pattern with a true one. It is not a tool to win arguments, and it is not a tool for contempt.

## Rule one: removal is not enough

Matthew 12:43-45 records the warning. An unclean spirit goes out of a man. Verse 44 says that the spirit returns and "findeth it empty, swept, and garnished." The spirit then brings seven other spirits, and "the last state of that man is worse than the first."

Inference: a false pattern that you tear out and do not replace leaves a vacancy. Something fills it. For this reason, the skill always ends with a replacement and never with a deletion.

Romans 12:2 states the same shape in one verse: "And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind..." The negative command and the positive command arrive together.

## Rule two: the beam comes first

Matthew 7:1-5 gives the order. The person who removes a mote from the eye of a brother must first remove the beam from his own eye.

Apply this literally to yourself before you apply anything here to another person. The catalog in this skill exists to correct the reader. It does not exist to diagnose the opponents of the reader. When you use it on someone else, you will usually find what you look for, and you will usually be wrong.

## Rule three: the goal is renewal, not victory

2 Timothy 2:24-26 defines the manner: "In meekness instructing those that oppose themselves..." Galatians 6:1 says to restore in meekness. 1 Peter 3:15 says to give an answer with meekness and fear. James 1:19 says to be swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to wrath.

A person can correctly name the error of another person and do it with contempt. That person produces the fruit that the catalog condemns. Before you use this skill on anyone other than yourself, read the section "Guards against false detection" in `references/thought-patterns.md`.

## The mandate

Scripture commands the renewal of the mind as an act, not as a mood.

2 Corinthians 10:3-5 describes the work. The weapons are not carnal. Their use is to cast down imaginations and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God. Their use is also to bring every thought into captivity.

Proverbs 4:23 states the stakes: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."

The supporting texts are Romans 12:1-2, Isaiah 26:3, Philippians 2:5, Philippians 4:8, Colossians 3:1-2, 1 Peter 1:13, 2 Timothy 1:7, and Titus 2:6.

## The deconstruction procedure

Do these eight steps in order. Steps 1 through 6 take the pattern apart. Steps 7 and 8 put something true in its place.

### Step 1. Write the thought in one sentence

If you cannot state the thought in one sentence, you do not yet know what you believe. When you write it down, most of the vagueness shows at once.

### Step 2. Name the source

Ask where the thought came from. The source can be the home, the classroom, the feed, the screen, a peer group, or your own invention. A thought with no traceable source is usually a thought that you absorbed whole. You do not have to accept a thought only because you cannot remember that you chose it.

### Step 3. Expose the assumption underneath

Ask what must be true for the thought to hold. Most false patterns rest on one unexamined premise. Find the premise, and the thought often falls with no more argument.

### Step 4. Do the three tests

The tests are true, wise, and loving. The details are in "The three tests" below. All three must pass. A thought can be true and still be unwise to speak. A thought can be wise and still be loveless.

### Step 5. Trace the fruit

Ask what the thought produces when it runs for a year. Matthew 7:20 gives the rule: "Wherefore by their fruits ye shall know them." You know a pattern of thought by its output. You do not know it by how reasonable it feels from inside.

### Step 6. Find the counterfeit

Most lies are a truth with something added or removed. Name the truth that the lie imitates. This step does more work than any other step, because it changes a fight into a repair. For example, the fear of man is a corrupted form of the fear of God. Envy is a corrupted form of a real desire for good. The correction does not kill the desire. The correction attaches the desire again to the right object.

### Step 7. Replace the thought, and write the replacement down

State the counter-truth in one sentence, and attach the verse that carries it. A replacement that lives only in your head will not survive the next meeting with the old pattern. Write it. Say it aloud. Return to it.

### Step 8. Change the input

Thought follows diet. Philippians 4:8 lists what to hold in the mind. Psalm 101:3 says, "I will set no wicked thing before mine eyes..." Job 31:1 records a covenant with the eyes, made before the temptation arrived. Remove the source where you can, and reduce it where you cannot. A pattern that keeps returning is usually a pattern that something keeps feeding.

## The three tests

### Test one: is it true?

Philippians 4:8 gives the list: "...whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things."

Ask whether the thought survives contact with the text and with the facts. A thought that requires you to ignore a fact fails.

### Test two: is it wise?

James 3:17 gives the profile: "But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy."

Ask whether the thought produces peace or confusion. Ask whether it makes you easier or harder to reason with.

### Test three: is it loving?

1 Corinthians 13:4-7 gives the anatomy. Charity suffers long and is kind. Charity does not envy, does not vaunt itself, and is not puffed up. It does not behave itself unseemly, does not seek her own, and is not easily provoked. It thinks no evil. It does not rejoice in iniquity, but it rejoices in the truth. It bears, believes, hopes, and endures all things.

Ask what the thought does to your treatment of people. A true thought that you hold without love still fails this test. The test is about the effect on you, not only about the truth of the claim.

## The two wisdoms test

James 3:13-18 sets two wisdoms against each other, and it supplies a practical test.

The wisdom that is earthly, sensual, and devilish produces envy, strife, confusion, boasting, and every evil work. The wisdom from above produces purity, peace, gentleness, mercy, good fruits, impartiality, and sincerity.

To do the test on a pattern of thought, ask what the pattern produces in the group that holds it. A belief that reliably produces suspicion, division, and self-congratulation fails, even when nobody can find the error in its logic. The full procedure is in `references/thought-patterns.md`.

## The sources

Inference: this skill infers that three institutions shape most of the patterns in the catalog. The three are the school system, social media, and television with advertising. The skill infers that each one captures attention, defines worth by comparison to other people, and removes the foundation that Proverbs 1:7 names. That verse says, "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge..."

`references/institutions.md` sets out the mechanisms, the counter-practices, and the parts that are contested. That file labels each claim as Text states, Inference, or Contested.

The contested parts need a note. The theological claim is strong, and the texts speak to it directly. Proverbs 1:7 and Proverbs 9:10 say that knowledge without the fear of the LORD rests on a broken foundation. The empirical claims about the outcomes of schooling are different. They change by school and by country, and some schools teach well. Treat the theological claim as firm and the empirical claims as testable. Do not merge the two.

## Output format

When you answer from this skill, use this shape:

1. The pattern, named in one sentence.
2. The source, where you can trace it.
3. The assumption underneath.
4. The three tests, with the result of each.
5. The fruit.
6. The counterfeit, which is the truth that it imitates.
7. The replacement, with the verse.
8. The change of input.
9. Confidence. Mark what the text states and what you inferred.

Keep the output short. A long answer about a single pattern usually shows that you did not find the pattern.

## Where this skill refuses

This skill does not do three things.

It does not diagnose other people. It has no authority to name the spiritual condition of a person. The texts forbid the attempt in the manner that most people attempt it.

It does not resolve disputable matters. Romans 14 draws a line between matters of sin and matters of preference. A person who treats a preference as a sin adds a rule that God did not give. That is its own error.

It does not give medical advice. Anxiety, depression, and intrusive thought have medical dimensions. A person in distress needs a physician as well as a pastor. When that is what you see, say so plainly.
