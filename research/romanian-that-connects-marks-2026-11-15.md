What the reading suggests:

- None of the "translated" marks fell on a phrase calque of the "face sens" kind. They fell on standard interface words: salvată, acțiune, actualizare, succes, Creează, card, maximum.
- "Too long" and "nerdy" are one pattern: long bookish neologisms (simplificate, gestionezi, economisești, remedierea, funcționat) where Alex wants a short, old, everyday word. Mark 8 says so directly.
- The "we", truth, positive-wording and cookie-cutter groups are about voice and honesty. They would apply in any language.
- Marks 17 and 6–7 pull in different directions on "specific": exact about what the company does, warm about who the reader is.

## When Jesus words things negatively (lookups for the rule Alex asked for)

KJV text from `scripture.ts lookup`. The patterns are a Reading from these verses, not from every saying of Jesus. A counter-example would be a "Fear not" with nothing after it, or an invitation worded as a negative.

- To someone already afraid or grieving, the negative names what they feel and a reason, promise or deed follows: Luke 12:32; Matthew 14:27; Mark 5:36; Luke 8:50; Luke 5:10; Luke 7:13-14; John 14:1, 27; Matthew 10:28-31.
- A prohibition comes with its replacement or its reason: Matthew 6:19-20; Matthew 6:31-34; John 20:27; Matthew 7:1-2.
- Invitations and gifts are positive: Matthew 11:28-30; Matthew 7:7-8; Matthew 7:12; Luke 8:48.
- Real trouble and real danger are said plainly: John 16:33; Luke 13:3; Luke 14:28; John 5:14.
- The hardest negatives go to those who harm others while looking good, not to the weak: Matthew 23:13 against John 8:10-11 and Matthew 12:20.
