## The regress and where it stops

An argument cannot go back forever. Each justification needs another one, and an endless chain
justifies nothing. Reasoning stops at what nobody can reduce further.

Scripture opens at that point. Scripture says: Genesis 1:1 says, "In the beginning God created the
heaven and the earth." Psalm 90:2 says, "even from everlasting to everlasting, thou art God."
Reading: the regress stops at God, who is before all things (Psalm 90:2). Genesis 1:1 begins
with him and does not argue for him. Scripture says: the act has a stated reason, "for thy
pleasure they are and were created" (Revelation 4:11).

- Word meaning: the KJV word "beginning" in Genesis 1:1 comes from a Hebrew family that includes
  H7225 _reshiyth_, defined as "the first, in place, time, order or rank (specifically, a
  firstfruit)". The word carries first place in order, and not only first place in time.

Aristotle used the word _archai_ for starting points that nobody can demonstrate. Disputed, in
the narrow sense: his accounts differ across his works, but the term itself is standard. The
Greek word is G746 _archē_, defined as "a commencement, or (concretely) chief (in various
applications of order, time, place, or rank)".

Hebrews 5:12 is the one verse in the KJV that contains the phrase "first principles". A text
search returns exactly one verse. It reads, "For when for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye
have need that one teach you again which be the first principles of the oracles of God; and are
become such as have need of milk, and not of strong meat."

- Word meaning: the word behind "principles" there is G4747 _stoicheion_, defined as "something
  orderly in arrangement, i.e. (by implication) a serial (basal, fundamental, initial)
  constituent (literally), proposition (figuratively)". The gloss list for the entry is
  "element, principle, rudiment".
- Word meaning: the Greek phrase in 5:12 is _ta stoicheia tes arches_. "Principles" is G4747, and
  "first" is G746 _archē_.
- Word meaning: the same word G4747 is used for the first principles "of the oracles of God"
  (Hebrews 5:12) and for "the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ" (Colossians 2:8).
  Galatians 4:9 calls them "the weak and beggarly elements". So the word alone does not tell you
  whether a starting point is sound. Colossians 2:8 warns against "philosophy and vain deceit"
  that rests on the wrong ones.
- Scripture says: the passage from Hebrews 5:12 to 6:2 continues with the list. Verses 6:1 and 6:2
  name the items: "repentance from dead works, and of faith toward God, Of the doctrine of
  baptisms, and of laying on of hands, and of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal
  judgment." That is six items.
- Scripture says: the first principles of Hebrews are "the principles of the doctrine of Christ"
  (6:1). The writer calls the readers "such as have need of milk" (5:12) and tells them to "go
  on unto perfection" (6:1).
- Reading: the passage describes doctrine, not method. Using the phrase for a reasoning
  method is an analogy, and the text does not make that move.

Word meaning: verse 6:1 uses G746 _archē_ alone for "principles". This is the same word that Aristotle
used. It is also the word behind "first" in 5:12, so 6:1 does not bring in a new word. The first
entry in the KJV gloss list for G746 is "beginning". Newer translations call the passage "the
elementary doctrine" or "the foundation".

One tool limit applies here. The supplied Strong's data has no verse-level tagging. A verse to
Strong's mapping is a standard concordance association and not a tool finding.

## Orientation before technique

Method is not the first requirement. The starting condition of the thinker is.

Scripture says: Proverbs 1:7 says, "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools
despise wisdom and instruction." Proverbs 9:10 says, "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of
wisdom: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding."

Look at what the claim is about. Both verses attach the beginning to a disposition and not to a
procedure.

Disputed: a method that works without that orientation still produces results that look like
knowledge. Readers disagree about what to call them. One reading says that the facts are real
knowledge on the wrong floor. Another reading says that Proverbs 1:7 withholds the word
"knowledge" from them, because they lack the beginning. Both readings agree that the floor is
wrong.

- Word meaning: "instruction" in Proverbs 1:7 is among the KJV renderings of H4148 _musar_, defined as
  "properly, chastisement; figuratively, reproof, warning or instruction; also restraint". The
  word carries correction as well as teaching. A learner who refuses correction is not in the
  category.

Proverbs 8:22 to 8:31 places wisdom at the creation. Wisdom speaks: "The LORD possessed me in the
beginning of his way, before his works of old" (8:22). The passage ends, "Then I was by him, as
one brought up with him: and I was daily his delight, rejoicing always before him; Rejoicing in
the habitable part of his earth; and my delights were with the sons of men" (8:30-31).

Reading: the result for reasoning is large. If the order of the world comes before the human
mind and does not depend on it, then an explanation can fit the world. The world makes sense
because a mind made it. It is stable because that mind does not change. On this account,
reasoning is discovery and not invention.

- Disputed: the KJV says that wisdom was "possessed" (8:22). The Hebrew H7069 _qanah_ is defined
  as "to erect, i.e. create; by extension, to procure, especially by purchase (causatively,
  sell); by implication to own". Readers have long disputed whether the sense in this verse is
  possession or creation. The Greek Septuagint (LXX) reads _ektisen_, "created", and the first
  sense in the lexicon entry is "create". The dispute does not touch the point above. That point
  rests on the whole passage and not on one verb.

## The limit of the instrument

A method that cannot state its own limit will overstate its results.

Scripture says: Isaiah 55:8-9 states the distance: "For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither
are your ways my ways, saith the LORD. For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my
ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts."

Scripture says: Romans 11:33-36 closes a long argument with the same note: "O the depth of the
riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways
past finding out! For who hath known the mind of the Lord? or who hath been his counsellor?"

1 Corinthians 13:9-12 states the condition of the knower: "For we know in part, and we prophesy
in part... For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part;
but then shall I know even as also I am known."

- Word meaning: "glass" is G2072 _esoptron_, "a mirror (for looking into)". "Darkly" is G135
  _ainigma_, "an obscure saying ('enigma'), i.e. (abstractly) obscureness". The image is a mirror
  in poor light, and not total darkness.
- Scripture says: the partial knowing is temporary. Verse 13:12 ends, "then shall I know even as
  also I am known." Reading: partial knowledge is the current condition and not the
  permanent one. It does not follow that the creature will know as God knows. His ways stay
  "past finding out" (Romans 11:33).

Scripture says: Deuteronomy 29:29 draws the boundary in one sentence: "The secret things belong unto
the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for
ever, that we may do all the words of this law." The verse pairs a limit with a grant. The limit
is real, but it is not total. God gives the revealed things for action, and the purpose clause
at the end says so.

- Word meaning: "secret" is among the renderings of H5641 _sathar_, "to hide (by covering), literally
  or figuratively".

Job 38 to 41 gives the longest treatment. God answers Job out of a whirlwind with questions and
not with explanations. Scripture says:

- Job 38:4 reads, "Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? declare, if thou hast
  understanding."
- Job 38:33 reads, "Knowest thou the ordinances of heaven? canst thou set the dominion thereof in
  the earth?"
- Job 42:3 reads, "therefore have I uttered that I understood not; things too wonderful for me,
  which I knew not."
- Job 42:5-6 reads, "I have heard of thee by the hearing of the ear: but now mine eye seeth thee.
  Wherefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes."

Scripture says: the book never tells Job why he suffered. It shows who is speaking. Reading: the
response to the limit is not an answer. It is a corrected posture.

Reading: these texts give a rule of method. When a question has no available answer, a
confident story about the answer is worse than the admission, because people use the story.

## The failure modes

Six failure modes do most of the damage. Each one has a test.

1. The inherited assumption. A claim arrived without a decision. It came from a family, a school,
   a profession, or a feed. It feels like a fact because nobody examined it. The test: name the
   source. If the answer is "everyone knows", the claim is in this category. Reading:
   Proverbs 18:17 gives the correction: "He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his
   neighbour cometh and searcheth him." The first account of anything sounds just. Only the
   opposing account tests it.

2. The substituted authority. Someone answers the question "is it true?" with "who says so?". The
   authority can be a person, a credential, a market, a majority, or a study. Authority is
   evidence, but it is not the claim itself. The test: state the claim as one sentence. Then ask
   what supports the sentence apart from the reputation of the speaker. Scripture says: the
   Bereans tested what Paul preached against the scriptures. They "received the word with all
   readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so" (Acts
   17:11). Reading: the standard they used was Scripture, not the standing of the speaker.

3. The worship of an analogy. Someone applies a model that fitted one situation to another
   situation. The map becomes the territory. Financial models, biological metaphors, military
   language, and software terms all produce this error. The test: name the point where the
   analogy breaks. An analogy with no stated limit works as a proof, and it cannot carry that
   weight.

4. The false dichotomy. Someone offers two options and refuses a third. "We cut the budget or we
   miss the date." Both branches are usually real. The frame itself is the error. The test: ask
   what the two options share. Attack the shared premise first.

5. The undisclosed premise. The argument skips a step. The conclusion rests on a claim that
   nobody stated and nobody defended. The test: write the argument as numbered lines, one claim
   a line. A gap is visible on paper and invisible in speech.

6. Haste. The conclusion arrives before the evidence. Haste is not only a vice. Pressure produces
   it, and it produces work that fails every test above. Reading: Isaiah 28:16 gives a
   counter by analogy: "Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a
   precious corner stone, a sure foundation: he that believeth shall not make haste."

   - Scripture says: the verse sets trust in God's stone against the boast of the rulers, "we have
     made lies our refuge" (28:15). The New Testament applies the stone to Christ: "he that
     believeth on him shall not be confounded" (1 Peter 2:6). Reading: the use of the verse
     for the pace of reasoning is an analogy.
   - Word meaning: "make haste" is among the renderings of H2363 _chush_, defined as "to hurry;
     figuratively, to be eager with excitement or enjoyment".
   - Scripture says: the verse puts belief in the foundation and the pace of action in the same
     sentence.

Reading: Luke 14:28-30 shows the cost of the opposite. Scripture says: the builder who starts
without a count "is not able to finish it", and the people who see the half-built tower mock
him. Jesus tells the story about the cost of following him (14:27, 14:33), so its use for
planning is an analogy.

## The tests

Six tests appear in the text. They are cheap to run, and they separate a position from an
opinion.

- Acts 17:11. "These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word
  with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so."
  The test: check the claim against the source, every day, and be willing to find it false.
- 1 Thessalonians 5:21. "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good."
  - Word meaning: "Prove" is among the renderings of G1381 _dokimazo_, defined as "to test (literally
    or figuratively); by implication, to approve". The word covers testing and approving
    together. A test that can never approve anything is not this word.
- 1 John 4:1. "Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God:
  because many false prophets are gone out into the world." The verse is first about claims of
  inspiration. Its use for persuasive claims of any kind is an inference.
- Job 34:3. "For the ear trieth words, as the mouth tasteth meat." In 1611 English, "meat" means
  food of any kind. The image is tasting and not reading. This is a note on English usage. The
  Hebrew word, H398, means "to eat".
- Proverbs 18:13. "He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto
  him." The test: give no verdict before you hear the whole case.
- Proverbs 18:17 is the charter for adversarial review. "He that is first in his own cause
  seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him." The neighbor is the part of the case
  that the first speaker did not present. A review that reads only the summary of the author has
  not begun.

Reading: these texts give a practical rule. A person who does not profit from the claim tests
it, and the test runs before the decision and not after it.

## The mind as the instrument under repair

A person does the reasoning. A damaged instrument produces damaged output, and no method repairs
the instrument by itself.

Scripture treats the condition of the mind as part of the work and not as a step before it.

- Philippians 2:5. "Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus." Reading: the
  verse is a command to have a mind that was first in Christ. So the right mind is given and not
  self-generated.
- 1 Corinthians 2:16. "For who hath known the mind of the Lord, that he may instruct him? But we
  have the mind of Christ." Scripture says: look at the order. The verse first denies that a human
  mind can instruct the Lord. Then it says, "we have the mind of Christ."
- Romans 12:2. "And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your
  mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God."
  - Word meaning: "renewing" is G342 _anakainosis_, "renovation". "Prove" is G1381 _dokimazo_ again.
    So the testing faculty itself is the thing under repair.
- 2 Corinthians 10:3-5. The weapons are not carnal, and the work is "Casting down imaginations,
  and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into
  captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ".
  - Word meaning: "imaginations" is G3053 _logismos_, defined as "computation, i.e. (figuratively)
    reasoning (conscience, conceit)". The word means reasonings. So the verse targets arguments,
    and not only pictures in the mind.
- Isaiah 26:3. "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he
  trusteth in thee."
  - Word meaning: "stayed" is among the renderings of H5564 _samak_, "to prop (literally or
    figuratively); reflexively, to lean upon or take hold of". The word is about a support and
    not about a feeling.
- Colossians 3:1-2. "If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above, where
  Christ sitteth on the right hand of God. Set your affection on things above, not on things on
  the earth." Scripture says: the verse directs attention, and it gives the direction as a command.
- Philippians 4:8. "Whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things
  are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of
  good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things."
  - Word meaning: "think on" is G3049 _logizomai_, defined as "to take an inventory, i.e. estimate
    (literally or figuratively)". The command is closer to taking stock than to daydreaming.
- Jeremiah 17:9. "The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know
  it?" Scripture says: the verse calls the heart deceitful and asks who can know it. Reading: the
  instrument carries a bias toward its own account, and it cannot audit itself.
- Romans 1:21. "Because that, when they knew God, they glorified him not as God, neither were
  thankful; but became vain in their imaginations, and their foolish heart was darkened."
  Ephesians 4:18 speaks of people "Having the understanding darkened". 1 Corinthians 2:14 says,
  "But the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God: for they are foolishness
  unto him: neither can he know them, because they are spiritually discerned." Scripture says: those
  who knew God and did not glorify him as God had their heart darkened, and the natural man
  cannot know the things of the Spirit.
  Reading: the damage is moral and not only a matter of limits. The text names the repair as
  the work of God: "the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost" (Titus 3:5).

Reading: some practical results are ordinary. Sleep, fear, appetite, debt, and loyalty all
shape judgment before the reasoning starts. Pride and the fear of others shape it too: "The fear
of man bringeth a snare" (Proverbs 29:25). A frightened person will find the conclusion that ends
the fear, and then build the argument for it. The method cannot repair this. So the method must
name the condition of the thinker as an input, and the thinker must ask God for wisdom. "If any of
you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it
shall be given him." (James 1:5)
