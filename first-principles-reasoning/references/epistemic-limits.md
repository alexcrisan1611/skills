# The Limits of the Method

Reference file for the `first-principles-reasoning` skill.

People use a method past its boundary when the method cannot name that boundary, and the results
look confident. This file sets out what Scripture states about the boundary. It also shows a
working procedure on the near side of the boundary.

Verses are quoted from the local KJV with `../scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts`.
Definitions are copied from `../scripture-foundations/scripts/lexicon.ts`. The labels are "Text
states", "Lexical", "Inference", and "Contested".

## Why a limit belongs in the method

People say "we do not know" in three different situations, and each one needs a different
response.

| Situation                                              | What is true               | The correct response                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| The information exists and is not collected            | The gap is a cost problem  | Collect it, or price the ignorance and act         |
| The system is too complex for the available model      | The gap is a scale problem | Model the part that matters and state the residual |
| The question is outside the jurisdiction of the method | The gap is structural      | Name it, stop, and act on what is revealed         |

The first two are ordinary engineering. The third is the subject of this file. A method that
treats the third case as the first will spend a career on data that cannot settle the question.

Inference: to recognize the third case is part of the method and not a failure of it. A surgeon
who names the limit of the operation did not fail at surgery.

## Job 38 to 41: the answer that is not an explanation

The book sets up a question. Text states: Job loses his children, his wealth, and his health. He
asks why, many times and at length.

The reader learns the reason in chapters 1 and 2. Text states: Job 1:8 reads, "And the LORD said
unto Satan, Hast thou considered my servant Job, that there is none like him in the earth, a
perfect and an upright man, one that feareth God, and escheweth evil?" Job 2:3 repeats the
assessment and adds, "and still he holdeth fast his integrity, although thou movedst me against
him, to destroy him without cause." Job never hears this exchange. He reasons from the evidence
that he has, and that evidence does not include the cause.

Then God answers. The answer is a sequence of questions about the created order.

| Verse     | The question                                                                               | The domain                         |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Job 38:4  | "Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth?"                                | Presence at the founding           |
| Job 38:11 | "Hitherto shalt thou come, but no further"                                                 | The limits assigned to the sea     |
| Job 38:18 | "Hast thou perceived the breadth of the earth? declare if thou knowest it all."            | Extent of the earth                |
| Job 38:33 | "Knowest thou the ordinances of heaven? canst thou set the dominion thereof in the earth?" | Governance of the heavens          |
| Job 38:36 | "Who hath put wisdom in the inward parts? or who hath given understanding to the heart?"   | The origin of understanding itself |
| Job 38:41 | "who provideth for the raven his food?"                                                    | Provision for creatures            |
| Job 40:2  | "Shall he that contendeth with the Almighty instruct him?"                                 | Standing to bring the case         |

Text states: the questions are not rhetorical decoration. Each one names a thing that Job did not
do and cannot do.

Text states: Job gives his first response in 40:4-5: "Behold, I am vile; what shall I answer
thee? I will lay mine hand upon my mouth. Once have I spoken; but I will not answer: yea, twice;
but I will proceed no further." He gives his second response in 42:3: "Who is he that hideth
counsel without knowledge? therefore have I uttered that I understood not; things too wonderful
for me, which I knew not."

Three observations follow:

1. Text states: the book never gives Job a reason for his suffering. The reader has one, and Job
   does not.
2. Text states: in 42:3, Job names his error as speech about what he did not understand. Text
   states: in 42:7, the LORD tells Eliphaz, "ye have not spoken of me the thing that is right, as
   my servant Job hath." Inference: so the error is not the asking. The LORD approves what Job
   spoke, and the book keeps the speeches of Job. God first speaks to Job in chapter 38, and He
   says, "I will demand of thee, and answer thou me" (38:3).
3. Inference: the sequence settles jurisdiction before it settles content. The question of Job
   assumes a standing to evaluate the government of the world. The questions of God remove that
   assumption.

Inference: some "why" questions have answers that live outside the system under discussion. For
those questions, the honest reply is a boundary. A boundary is information. It changes behavior,
because it stops the spending.

Job 28 gives the same note from the human side. Text states: "Whence then cometh wisdom? and
where is the place of understanding? Seeing it is hid from the eyes of all living, and kept close
from the fowls of the air" (28:20-21). Job 23:8-10 records the search without the finding, and
then the confidence that survives it: "Behold, I go forward, but he is not there; and backward,
but I cannot perceive him... But he knoweth the way that I take: when he hath tried me, I shall
come forth as gold."

## Isaiah 55:8-9: the distance claim

Text states: the two verses state a difference in kind. "For my thoughts are not your thoughts,
neither are your ways my ways, saith the LORD. For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so
are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts."

Look at three things in how the passage makes the claim:

1. Text states: the comparison is spatial and graded. The heavens are higher than the earth, and
   the difference in thought is like that. The verse does not say that the two are unrelated. It
   says that the distance is large and has a direction.
2. Text states: the limit sits inside an invitation. Verse 55:1 opens, "Ho, every one that
   thirsteth, come ye to the waters." Verses 55:6-7 call the wicked to return. Verses 55:10-11
   promise that the word will do what God sends it to do. The limit is in the middle of an
   address that asks for a response.
3. Inference: the limit does not cancel the communication. The same passage that says that the
   thoughts are not ours says that the word goes out and does its work. The limit is on
   comprehension and not on reception.

The practical rule that follows is modest. An inference from Scripture carries a label, because
the difference in Isaiah 55:8 sits between the text and the model that the reader makes of it.

## Romans 11:33-36: the doxology that ends the argument

Paul spends three chapters on the hardest question in the letter, the standing of Israel. The
argument does not end with a solution. Text states: it ends with this. "O the depth of the riches
both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past
finding out! For who hath known the mind of the Lord? or who hath been his counsellor? Or who hath
first given to him, and it shall be recompensed unto him again? For of him, and through him, and
to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever. Amen."

The three questions have a single answer: nobody. Nobody knew the mind, counseled the Lord, or
gave first.

Inference: the passage models a change of output at the boundary. When the argument reaches a
certain depth, the correct product is not a conclusion. It is an acknowledgment of depth and a
doxology (a short statement of praise). That is not an escape from reasoning, because the
argument arrived there by reasoning. It is the reported result.

Inference: the business use of that move is limited, and the limit needs a name. A firm cannot
run on doxology. What carries over is narrower. When a question has no available answer, the
report says so, and the decision goes ahead on the revealed portion.

## 1 Corinthians 13:9-12: the partial knower

Text states: "For we know in part, and we prophesy in part. But when that which is perfect is
come, then that which is in part shall be done away... For now we see through a glass, darkly;
but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known."

- Lexical: "glass" is G2072 _esoptron_, "a mirror (for looking into)". "Darkly" is G135
  _ainigma_, "an obscure saying ('enigma'), i.e. (abstractly) obscureness". The image is a
  reflection in a mirror. The dimness is in the seeing and not in the object.
- Text states: the passage makes three comparisons. A child against a man (13:11), a mirror
  against a face (13:12), and a part against a whole (13:9-10, 13:12).
- Text states: the passage states the direction of travel. The partial passes away, and knowledge
  becomes complete.

The text permits neither of two errors at this verse:

- A claim of completeness now. Verse 13:9 states the contrary in the present tense.
- A view that partial knowledge is worthless. Verse 13:12 states that God knows the partial
  knower. Chapter 13 describes love, and the command follows in 14:1: "Follow after charity".
  Verse 13:13 closes with "faith, hope, charity". All three work under partial knowledge.

Inference: the limit sits in the middle of the love chapter, so the limit is practical. Love
works with partial knowledge. A person who waits for complete knowledge before acting will not
act, and the waiting is itself a decision.

## Deuteronomy 29:29: the boundary with a purpose clause

Text states: "The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are
revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law."

- Lexical: "secret" is among the renderings of H5641 _sathar_, "to hide (by covering), literally
  or figuratively".
- Text states: the sentence divides all knowledge into two categories and gives each one an
  owner.
- Text states: the purpose clause gives the use of the second category. The revealed things
  belong to us "that we may do all the words of this law". The grant is the size of an action.

Inference: the division does not forbid curiosity. It is a division of labor. The question "who
owns this?" has an answer, and the answer sets what the reader does next. This verse does not
forbid curiosity about the secret things. It does rule out a business plan built on them.

A practical test follows. A question is answerable for action when an answer will change what the
asker does. If no available answer changes any action, the question belongs to the other category
for now. The correct output is to record it and move on.

## Ecclesiastes: the experimenter

Ecclesiastes records a man who runs a deliberate program of experiments on the largest questions
and reports the results.

### The method he states

- Ecclesiastes 1:13: "And I gave my heart to seek and search out by wisdom concerning all things
  that are done under heaven: this sore travail hath God given to the sons of man to be exercised
  therewith."
- Ecclesiastes 1:17: "And I gave my heart to know wisdom, and to know madness and folly."
- Ecclesiastes 2:1: "I said in mine heart, Go to now, I will prove thee with mirth, therefore
  enjoy pleasure."
- Ecclesiastes 7:23: "All this have I proved by wisdom: I said, I will be wise; but it was far
  from me."

Text states: the verbs are the verbs of a test. He seeks, searches, gives his heart, and proves.

### The experiments and the verdicts

| Experiment                                     | Location | Verdict                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pleasure and mirth                             | 2:1-3    | "this also is vanity"                                                   |
| Great works, houses, vineyards, gardens, pools | 2:4-6    | The work was real, and 2:11 calls it vanity                             |
| Accumulated labor and its fruit                | 2:10-11  | "all was vanity and vexation of spirit"                                 |
| The search to know wisdom, madness, and folly  | 1:17-18  | "in much wisdom is much grief"                                          |
| Wisdom compared with folly                     | 2:12-16  | "wisdom excelleth folly", yet "how dieth the wise man? as the fool."    |
| Wealth and increase                            | 5:10     | "He that loveth silver shall not be satisfied with silver"              |
| Right work and the envy it draws               | 4:4      | "for this a man is envied of his neighbour. This is also vanity"        |

Text states: the verdicts are mostly negative, and he records them as results and not as
opinions. He ran the experiment, looked at the output, and wrote down what he found.

### The limits he hits

The book is as careful about its limits as about its findings.

- Ecclesiastes 3:11: "also he hath set the world in their heart, so that no man can find out the
  work that God maketh from the beginning to the end."
- Ecclesiastes 3:14: "I know that, whatsoever God doeth, it shall be for ever: nothing can be put
  to it, nor any thing taken from it: and God doeth it, that men should fear before him."
- Ecclesiastes 7:23-24: "I said, I will be wise; but it was far from me. That which is far off,
  and exceeding deep, who can find it out?"
- Ecclesiastes 8:16-17: "Then I beheld all the work of God, that a man cannot find out the work
  that is done under the sun: because though a man labour to seek it out, yet he shall not find
  it; yea farther; though a wise man think to know it, yet shall he not be able to find it."
- Ecclesiastes 11:5: "As thou knowest not what is the way of the spirit... even so thou knowest
  not the works of God who maketh all."
- Ecclesiastes 9:11: "the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, neither yet
  bread to the wise, nor yet riches to men of understanding, nor yet favour to men of skill; but
  time and chance happeneth to them all."
- Ecclesiastes 12:12: "of making many books there is no end; and much study is a weariness of
  the flesh."

### What he does with the limits

He does not stop work, and he does not stop testing. He acts under uncertainty. Text states:
Ecclesiastes 11:6 reads, "In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine
hand: for thou knowest not whether shall prosper, either this or that, or whether they both shall
be alike good."

Text states: the conclusion states the duty that survives every limit. Ecclesiastes 12:13-14
reads, "Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for
this is the whole duty of man. For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret
thing, whether it be good, or whether it be evil."

Inference: the book teaches a method in four parts:

1. Run the experiment.
2. Record the negative result.
3. Name the limit that the experiment cannot cross.
4. Act inside the limit, because the limit does not suspend the duty.

Contested: readers differ on how much of Ecclesiastes is prescription. One reading takes the
vanity verdicts as the settled position of the Preacher. Another reading takes them as the record
of a search that 12:13 corrects. The difference matters for how a reader applies the book. This
file treats the experiments as results and 12:13-14 as the conclusion, and it labels that choice
as a reading. It does not claim that the text settles the choice.

## A working procedure at the limit

When a question resists an answer, classify the resistance before you do more work.

Question 1. Is the information unavailable, or only expensive? The information is expensive when
a purchase, a study, or a month of measurement will produce it. Buy it, or price the ignorance
and go ahead.

Question 2. Is the system too complex for the available model? Complexity is real in weather,
markets, and long-run social systems. Model the part that carries the decision, and name the
residual. Do not present the residual as measured.

Question 3. Is the question outside the jurisdiction of the method? The sign is that no amount of
the available evidence settles it. Questions about the purpose of a life, the reason for
undeserved suffering, and the government of the world sit here. Name the question, state the
limit, and act on what is revealed. Deuteronomy 29:29 governs this step.

Then do three things in order:

1. Write the limit down. Put the question that has no answer in the record of the decision, so
   that the next reader does not spend the same budget to find it again.
2. Write down what is revealed. Record the portion that is the size of an action, with its label.
3. Set the review. If new evidence can reach the question, name the evidence and the date. If new
   evidence cannot reach it, say so.

## Two errors at the boundary

Error one: false certainty. People fill the gap with a story, because a story is more useful than
a blank. The story then runs the strategy. The cost of the failure grows with the load that the
gap carried.

Error two: total skepticism. People extend the limit on one question to every question. Nobody
can act under total skepticism, so the position collapses into paralysis or into unexamined
convention. Proverbs 1:7 places knowledge on a foundation and not on doubt.

A third error needs a line of its own: activity in place of knowledge. People produce studies,
workshops, and dashboards because nobody can answer the question and the work is visible. The
output is motion, and the question stays open.

One test separates these three errors from honest work. Did the last deliverable name the limit
or hide it?
