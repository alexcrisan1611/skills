# The Limits of the Method

Reference file for the `first-principles-reasoning` skill.

A method that cannot name its own boundary will be used past the boundary, and the results will
look confident. This file sets out what Scripture states about the boundary, and what a working
procedure looks like on the near side of it.

Verses are quoted from the local KJV with
`skills/scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts`. Definitions are copied from
`skills/scripture-foundations/scripts/lexicon.ts`. Labels are **Text states**, **Lexical**,
**Inference**, and **Contested**.

## Why a limit belongs in the method

Three different situations get called "we do not know", and they need different responses.

| Situation                                              | What is true               | The correct response                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| The information exists and is not collected            | The gap is a cost problem  | Collect it, or price the ignorance and act         |
| The system is too complex for the available model      | The gap is a scale problem | Model the part that matters and state the residual |
| The question is outside the jurisdiction of the method | The gap is structural      | Name it, stop, and act on what is revealed         |

The first two are ordinary engineering. The third is the subject of this file. A method that
treats the third case as the first will spend a career collecting data that cannot settle the
question.

**Inference:** recognising the third case is part of the method rather than a failure of it. A
surgeon who names the limit of the operation has not failed at surgery.

## Job 38 to 41: the answer that is not an explanation

The book sets up a question. Job loses his children, his wealth, and his health. He asks why,
repeatedly and at length. **(Text states)**

The reader is told the reason in chapters 1 and 2. Job 1:8 reads, "And the LORD said unto
Satan, Hast thou considered my servant Job, that there is none like him in the earth, a
perfect and an upright man, one that feareth God, and escheweth evil?" Job 2:3 repeats the
assessment and adds, "and still he holdeth fast his integrity, although thou movedst me
against him, to destroy him without cause." **(Text states)** Job never hears this exchange.
He reasons from the evidence available to him, and the evidence available to him does not
include the cause.

Then God answers. The answer is a sequence of questions about the created order.

| Verse     | The question                                                                               | The domain                         |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Job 38:4  | "Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth?"                                | Presence at the founding           |
| Job 38:11 | "Hitherto shalt thou come, but no further"                                                 | The limits assigned to the sea     |
| Job 38:18 | "Hast thou perceived the breadth of the earth? declare if thou knowest it all."            | Extent of the earth                |
| Job 38:33 | "Knowest thou the ordinances of heaven? canst thou set the dominion thereof in the earth?" | Governance of the heavens          |
| Job 38:36 | "Who hath put wisdom in the inward parts? or who hath given understanding to the heart?"   | The origin of understanding itself |
| Job 38:41 | "who provideth for the raven his food?"                                                    | Provision for creatures            |
| Job 40:2  | "Shall he that contendeth with the Almighty instruct him?"                                 | Standing to bring the case         |

**(Text states)** The questions are not rhetorical decoration. Each one names a thing Job did
not do and cannot do.

Job's first response is in 40:4-5: "Behold, I am vile; what shall I answer thee? I will lay
mine hand upon my mouth. Once have I spoken; but I will not answer: yea, twice; but I will
proceed no further." His second response is in 42:3: "Who is he that hideth counsel without
knowledge? therefore have I uttered that I understood not; things too wonderful for me, which
I knew not." **(Text states)**

Three observations follow.

1. **Text states:** the book never supplies Job with a reason for his suffering. The reader has
   one, and Job does not.
2. **Text states:** Job's error is named in 42:3 as speaking about what he did not understand.
   The error is not the asking. God invites the asking earlier in the book, and the book
   preserves Job's speeches.
3. **Inference:** the sequence establishes jurisdiction before it settles content. Job's
   question presupposes a standing to evaluate the government of the world. The questions
   remove the presupposition.

**Inference:** some "why" questions have answers that live outside the system under
discussion. For those questions, the honest reply is a boundary. A boundary is information,
and it changes behaviour, because it stops the expenditure.

Job 28 supplies the same note from the human side. "Whence then cometh wisdom? and where is
the place of understanding? Seeing it is hid from the eyes of all living, and kept close from
the fowls of the air" (28:20-21). And Job 23:8-10 records the searching without the finding,
then the confidence that survives it: "Behold, I go forward, but he is not there; and backward,
but I cannot perceive him... But he knoweth the way that I take: when he hath tried me, I
shall come forth as gold." **(Text states)**

## Isaiah 55:8-9: the distance claim

The two verses state a difference in kind. "For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are
your ways my ways, saith the LORD. For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways
higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts." **(Text states)**

Three things are worth noticing about how the claim is made.

1. **Text states:** the comparison is spatial and graded. The heavens are higher than the earth,
   and the difference in thought is like that. The verse does not say the two are unrelated. It
   says the distance is large and directional.
2. **Text states:** the limit sits inside an invitation. Verse 55:1 opens, "Ho, every one that
   thirsteth, come ye to the waters." Verses 55:6-7 call the wicked to return. Verses 55:10-11
   promise that the word will accomplish what it is sent to do. The limit is stated in the
   middle of an address that asks for a response.
3. **Inference:** the limit does not cancel the communication. The same passage that says the
   thoughts are not ours says that the word goes out and does its work. What is limited is
   comprehension rather than reception.

The practical rule that follows is modest. An inference drawn from Scripture carries a label,
because the difference named in Isaiah 55:8 sits between the text and the reader's model of it.

## Romans 11:33-36: the doxology that ends the argument

Paul has spent three chapters on the hardest question in the letter, the standing of Israel.
The argument does not end with a solution. It ends with this. "O the depth of the riches both
of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past
finding out! For who hath known the mind of the Lord? or who hath been his counsellor? Or who
hath first given to him, and it shall be recompensed unto him again? For of him, and through
him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever. Amen." **(Text states)**

The three questions have a single answer. None. Nobody has known the mind, counselled the
Lord, or given first.

**Inference:** the passage models a change of output at the boundary. When the argument
reaches a certain depth, the correct product is not a conclusion. It is an acknowledgement of
depth plus a doxology. That is not an escape from reasoning, because the argument arrived
there by reasoning. It is the reported result.

The business application of that move is limited, and the limit is worth naming. A firm cannot
run on doxology. What carries over is narrower. When a question has no available answer, the
report says so, and the decision proceeds on the revealed portion. **(Inference)**

## 1 Corinthians 13:9-12: the partial knower

"For we know in part, and we prophesy in part. But when that which is perfect is come, then
that which is in part shall be done away... For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then
face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known." **(Text
states)**

- **Lexical:** "glass" is G2072 _esoptron_, "a mirror (for looking into)". "Darkly" is G135
  _ainigma_, "an obscure saying ('enigma'), i.e. (abstractly) obscureness". The image is an
  image seen in a mirror, and the dimness is in the seeing rather than in the object.
- **Text states:** the passage makes three comparisons. A child against a man (13:11), a mirror
  against a face (13:12), and a part against a whole (13:9-10, 13:12).
- **Text states:** the direction of travel is stated. The partial passes away, and knowledge is
  completed.

Two errors are available at this verse, and the text permits neither.

- **Claiming completeness now.** Verse 13:9 states the contrary in the present tense.
- **Treating partial knowledge as worthless.** Verse 13:12 states that the partial knower is
  known, and the chapter is a command to love with what is available. Verse 13:13 closes with
  "faith, hope, charity", and the three operate under partial knowledge by definition.

**Inference:** the limit is stated in the middle of the love chapter, so the limit is
practical. Love works with partial knowledge. A person who waits for complete knowledge before
acting will not act, and the waiting is itself a decision.

## Deuteronomy 29:29: the boundary with a purpose clause

"The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong
unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law." **(Text
states)**

- **Lexical:** "secret" is among the renderings of H5641 _sathar_, "to hide (by covering),
  literally or figuratively".
- **Text states:** the sentence divides all knowledge into two categories and assigns each one.
- **Text states:** the purpose clause supplies the use of the second category. The revealed
  things belong to us "that we may do all the words of this law". The grant is action-sized.

**Inference:** the division is not a prohibition on curiosity. It is a division of labour. The
question "who owns this?" has an answer, and the answer determines what the reader does next.
Curiosity about the secret things is not forbidden here. Building a business plan on them is.

A practical test follows. A question is answerable for action when an answer will change what
the asker does. If no available answer changes any action, the question belongs to the other
category for now. The correct output is to record it and move.

## Ecclesiastes: the experimenter

Ecclesiastes records a man running a deliberate program of experiments on the largest
questions, and reporting the results.

### The method he states

- Ecclesiastes 1:13: "And I gave my heart to seek and search out by wisdom concerning all
  things that are done under heaven: this sore travail hath God given to the sons of man to be
  exercised therewith."
- Ecclesiastes 1:17: "And I gave my heart to know wisdom, and to know madness and folly."
- Ecclesiastes 2:1: "I said in mine heart, Go to now, I will prove thee with mirth, therefore
  enjoy pleasure."
- Ecclesiastes 7:23: "All this have I proved by wisdom: I said, I will be wise; but it was far
  from me."

**(Text states)** The verbs are the verbs of a test. He seeks, searches, gives his heart, and
proves.

### The experiments and the verdicts

| Experiment                                     | Location | Verdict                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pleasure and mirth                             | 2:1-3    | "this also is vanity"                                      |
| Great works, houses, vineyards, gardens, pools | 2:4-6    | The work was real, and 2:11 calls it vanity                |
| Accumulated labour and its fruit               | 2:10-11  | "all was vanity and vexation of spirit"                    |
| Wisdom compared with folly                     | 1:17-18  | "in much wisdom is much grief"                             |
| Wealth and increase                            | 5:10     | "He that loveth silver shall not be satisfied with silver" |
| Right work and its reward                      | 4:4      | "This is also vanity and vexation of spirit"               |

**(Text states)** The verdicts are negative, and they are recorded as results rather than as
opinions. He ran the experiment, looked at the output, and wrote down what he found.

### The limits he hits

The book is as careful about its limits as its findings.

- Ecclesiastes 3:11: "also he hath set the world in their heart, so that no man can find out
  the work that God maketh from the beginning to the end."
- Ecclesiastes 3:14: "I know that, whatsoever God doeth, it shall be for ever: nothing can be
  put to it, nor any thing taken from it: and God doeth it, that men should fear before him."
- Ecclesiastes 7:23-24: "I said, I will be wise; but it was far from me. That which is far off,
  and exceeding deep, who can find it out?"
- Ecclesiastes 8:16-17: "Then I beheld all the work of God, that a man cannot find out the work
  that is done under the sun: because though a man labour to seek it out, yet he shall not find
  it; yea farther; though a wise man think to know it, yet shall he not be able to find it."
- Ecclesiastes 11:5: "As thou knowest not what is the way of the spirit... even so thou knowest
  not the works of God who maketh all."
- Ecclesiastes 9:11: "the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, neither yet
  bread to the wise, nor yet riches to men of understanding, nor yet favour to men of skill;
  but time and chance happeneth to them all."
- Ecclesiastes 12:12: "of making many books there is no end; and much study is a weariness of
  the flesh."

### What he does with the limits

He does not stop working, and he does not stop testing. He acts under uncertainty. Ecclesiastes
11:6 reads, "In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine hand: for thou
knowest not whether shall prosper, either this or that, or whether they both shall be alike
good." **(Text states)**

The conclusion states the duty that survives every limit. Ecclesiastes 12:13-14 reads, "Let us
hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the
whole duty of man. For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing,
whether it be good, or whether it be evil." **(Text states)**

**Inference:** the method lesson in the book has four parts. Run the experiment. Record the
negative result. Name the limit that the experiment cannot cross. Then act inside the limit,
because the limit does not suspend the duty.

**Contested:** readers differ on how much of Ecclesiastes is prescription. One reading takes
the vanity verdicts as the Preacher's settled position. Another reads them as the record of a
search that is corrected by 12:13. The difference matters for how the book is applied. This
file treats the experiments as results and 12:13-14 as the conclusion, and it labels the choice
as a reading. It does not claim that the text settles the choice.

## A working procedure at the limit

When a question resists an answer, classify the resistance before doing more work.

**Question 1. Is the information unavailable, or merely expensive?**
The information is expensive when a purchase, a study, or a month of measurement will produce
it. Buy it, or price the ignorance and proceed.

**Question 2. Is the system too complex for the available model?**
Complexity is real in weather, markets, and long-run social systems. Model the part that
carries the decision and name the residual. Do not present the residual as measured.

**Question 3. Is the question outside the jurisdiction of the method?**
The tell is that no amount of the available evidence settles it. Questions about the purpose of
a life, the reason for undeserved suffering, and the government of the world sit here. Name
the question, state the limit, and act on what is revealed. **Deuteronomy 29:29 governs this
step.**

Then do three things in order.

1. **Write the limit down.** Put the unanswerable question in the record of the decision, so
   the next reader does not spend the same budget rediscovering it.
2. **Write down what is revealed.** The action-sized portion, with its label.
3. **Set the review.** If the question is the kind that new evidence can reach, name the
   evidence and the date. If it is not, say that it is not.

## Two errors at the boundary

**Error one: false certainty.** The gap gets filled with a story because a story is more
useful than a blank. The story then runs the strategy. The failure is expensive in proportion
to how load-bearing the gap was.

**Error two: total scepticism.** The limit on one question gets extended to every question.
Nobody can act under total scepticism, so the position collapses into either paralysis or
unexamined convention. Proverbs 1:7 places knowledge on a foundation rather than on doubt.

A third error deserves a line of its own. **Substituting activity for knowledge.** Studies,
workshops, and dashboards get produced because the question cannot be answered and the work is
visible. The output is motion, and the question remains open.

The test that separates the three from honest work is simple. Did the last deliverable name
the limit or hide it?
