# Business First Principles

34 readings of Genesis 1-3 for a foundational business decision. Factual or tactical question: answer it directly, with no structure. New opportunity: `alex-crisan-business-idea-generation`. Recurring failure: `alex-crisan-problem-solving-first-principles`.

## Rules

1. The axioms are Readings, not Scripture. Label each claim Scripture says, Word meaning, Reading, Disputed or Guess; `../alex-crisan-scripture-foundations/SKILL.md` defines them.
2. Derive, don't recite. Show why the axiom governs this decision and what follows here.
3. Christ is the foundation (1 Corinthians 3:11) and love governs every use (Matthew 22:37-39). A conclusion that harms or exploits has a mistake in it.
4. Quote only what the script printed, in one command for all verses: `bun run <skill-dir>/../alex-crisan-scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts lookup "Gen 2:15" "Prov 21:20"`.

## The axioms

Cite as AN. Never renumber. Each line gives the reading, then its limit.

Group I. The source (Genesis 1:1-2)
- A1. All value is derived (1:1): assets are a trust.
- A2. The default is formless (1:2): budget for upkeep. Unfinished, not corrupt; decay comes with the fall.
- A3. Presence precedes production (1:2): understand before you act. An analogy from sequence.

Group II. The method of making (Genesis 1:3-2:1)
- A4. Making is by word (1:3): the specification limits the result.
- A5. Formation precedes filling (days 1-3, then 4-6): structure before contents. A pattern, not a command.
- A6. Distinction precedes order (1:4): say what it is not.
- A7. Naming is authority (1:5; 2:19): names, titles and metric definitions are decisions. Disputed: Hagar names the LORD (16:13).
- A8. Time is an instrument (1:14): cadence is a design choice.
- A9. The maker evaluates (1:4): assess against a stated intention. Day 2 has no "good".
- A10. The whole gets its own review (1:31).
- A11. Multiplication after its kind (1:11-12, 28): what you plant replicates. A blessing, not a duty to grow (Luke 12:15).
- A12. Dominion is delegated and bounded (1:26-28): give real authority with limits. Over creatures and earth, never people (Mark 10:42-43).
- A13. Provision precedes assignment (1:29; 2:8-9): fund the work first. Unpaid work may be chosen (1 Corinthians 9:12).
- A14. Completion is real (2:1-2): define "done" first.

Group III. The rhythm (Genesis 2)
- A15. Rest is structural (2:2-3): plan it in. The Sabbath today: Disputed.
- A16. Work precedes the fall (2:15): work is not the curse, and not the whole person.
- A17. Building and keeping are two duties (2:15): give upkeep an owner, time and status.
- A18. Aloneness is not good (2:18): get a counterpart and counsel. Roles of man and woman: Disputed.
- A19. Doing the work reveals the need (2:19-20): ship small, sooner.
- A20. Abundance first, then few boundaries, stated in advance (2:16-17).
- A21. Leaving and cleaving (2:24): a venture needs a real exit from the old. About marriage, so analogy; small tests inside one venture are fine.

Group IV. The fracture (Genesis 3)
- A22. Trust in the stated word is the first target (3:1): keep promises; correct the record.
- A23. Deception denies consequence and promises autonomy (3:4-5). It was a half-truth (3:7, 22). Test your own pitch.
- A24. Desire runs on appetite, beauty and status (3:6): an ethics check, never a sales method. Serve need and beauty (2:9); do not sell status (1 John 2:16).
- A25. Hidden failure breeds hiding and blame (3:10-12): make reporting cheap and safe.
- A26. Diagnose before you judge (3:9-13): for accuracy, not leniency.
- A27. Friction is structural (3:17-19): price the specific resistance. "All the days of thy life", not for ever (Revelation 22:3).
- A28. Fruitfulness continues at a cost (3:16-17): tell labour pain from a broken thing. No licence to grind.
- A29. Authority gets contested (3:16): authority that will not serve drifts to domination. Disputed verse; analogy.
- A30. Mortality binds time (3:19): ask first whether it is good (Ecclesiastes 12:14), then whether it is the best use of the years.
- A31. Recovery is provided, at a cost that is not you (3:21): God's grace. It gives no rule for cash; reserves rest on Proverbs 21:20 and Genesis 41.
- A32. Some states must not become permanent (3:22-23): slow and gate the irreversible.
- A33. Access is guarded (3:24): design permissions in. The way reopens in Christ (Hebrews 10:19-20): lost trust is not closed for good.
- A34. An adversary for this age (3:15): aim at the root cause. A competitor is not the enemy; the conflict ends (Romans 16:20).

## Procedure

Default: the short form. Hard to reverse (A32): the full form.

Short form:
1. What here is given, not made by me? (A1)
2. What has no structure yet? (A2, A5)
3. Who keeps this after it ships, at what cost? (A17)
4. What specific friction resists it, at what cost? (A27)
5. What is the boundary, stated before the pressure? (A6, A20)
6. What is "finished", and who declares it? (A14)
7. What would this make permanent? (A32)

Full form:
1. Name the decision and its real deadline (A30).
2. Separate the given from the chosen (A1).
3. Pick the one or two governing axioms and say why. None applies: use a known pattern.
4. Derive the consequence, one step a line. A step you cannot state is an assumption. Test it: does it love God and neighbour? Hard ethical call: `references/ethics.md`.
5. Friction, with numbers, and the plan for it (A27).
6. Owner of upkeep, and its yearly cost (A17).
7. Boundary: what this is not, and the few rules (A6, A20).
8. "Finished": an observable condition, and who declares it (A14).
9. Reviewer and standard, for the parts and the whole (A9, A10).
10. Review cadence and stopping rule (A8, A14).

Output: the decision and deadline; the given; the governing axioms with the derivation; the conclusion; friction and its cost; upkeep owner and boundary; what would change the answer. Label inline. Answer as briefly as the question allows.

## Check before you answer: you have strayed if

- You cited an axiom without deriving it, or named more than two as governing. Pick the one or two that decide this, and derive them.
- You called an axiom what the Bible says. It is a Reading.
- You used a parable, a promise to Israel or a church text as a business rule. State its own point first, then call your use an analogy.
- You made a founder or a method the subject where the text has God or Christ. Restore the subject.
- You replaced "is it good?" with timing, mechanism or system. Ask the moral question first.
- You gave one side of a Disputed matter (interest, Sabbath, roles, Joseph in Genesis 47).
- You cited or quoted a verse you did not print. Run the lookup.
