# Crisis Response

Reference file for the `problem-solving-first-principles` skill.

A crisis compresses the time available for every decision. The method does not change. The
order changes, because some decisions stop harm and other decisions do not.

This file works from two narratives. Acts 27 is a physical crisis at sea. Nehemiah 4 is a
sustained threat against a building project. Verses are quoted from
`skills/scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts`. Labels are **Text states**, **Lexical**,
**Inference**, and **Contested**.

## The shape of a crisis

A crisis has four properties, and each one changes the work. **(Inference)**

1. **Harm is active.** Something is still breaking, so the first move is a stop rather than a
   study.
2. **The evidence is perishable.** Logs rotate, memories fade, and the people who know leave
   the room. The record competes with the fix for the same hours.
3. **The decisions arrive faster than the information.** A leader acts on partial evidence,
   and the text of Acts 27 shows a warning delivered on exactly that basis.
4. **The asset is often already lost.** Once the loss is accepted, the remaining decisions are
   about people and evidence. Much of the difficulty in a crisis comes from refusing to accept
   the loss.

## Acts 27: the sequence

**The warning, and its rejection.**

- Acts 27:9-10: "Now when much time was spent, and when sailing was now dangerous, because the
  fast was now already past, Paul admonished them, And said unto them, Sirs, I perceive that
  this voyage will be with hurt and much damage, not only of the lading and ship, but also of
  our lives."
- Acts 27:11: "Nevertheless the centurion believed the master and the owner of the ship, more
  than those things which were spoken by Paul."

**(Text states)** The warning failed on the standing of the speaker rather than on the evidence.
The owner of the ship had an interest in the voyage, and his account carried more weight.

**The first moves in the storm.**

- Acts 27:18-19: "And we being exceedingly tossed with a tempest, the next day they lightened
  the ship; And the third day we cast out with our own hands the tackling of the ship."
- Acts 27:20: "And when neither sun nor stars in many days appeared, and no small tempest lay
  on us, all hope that we should be saved was then taken away."

**(Text states)** The first moves reduce weight and throw away the means of navigation. The
response to an overwhelming load is removal rather than addition.

- **Lexical:** "taken away" in 27:20 is G4014 _periaireo_, "to remove all around, i.e. unveil,
  cast off (anchor); figuratively, to expiate".

**The word in the crisis.**

- Acts 27:22-26: "And now I exhort you to be of good cheer: for there shall be no loss of any
  man's life among you, but of the ship. For there stood by me this night the angel of God,
  whose I am, and whom I serve, Saying, Fear not, Paul; thou must be brought before Caesar: and,
  lo, God hath given thee all them that sail with thee. Wherefore, sirs, be of good cheer: for I
  believe God, that it shall be even as it was told me. Howbeit we must be cast upon a certain
  island."

**(Text states)** Three features of that message matter in a crisis.

1. The message states the loss. The ship is gone in the forecast.
2. The message states the preservation. No life is lost.
3. The message names a condition. "we must be cast upon a certain island."

A message that promises no loss at all is not the message in this chapter. **(Inference)**

**The escape attempt.**

- Acts 27:30-32: "And as the shipmen were about to flee out of the ship, when they had let down
  the boat into the sea, under colour as though they would have cast anchors out of the
  foreship, Paul said to the centurion and to the soldiers, Except these abide in the ship, ye
  cannot be saved. Then the soldiers cut off the ropes of the boat, and let her fall off."

**(Text states)** The people with the best knowledge of the vessel try to leave first, and they
disguise the move as a task. The correction is physical rather than rhetorical. The soldiers cut
the ropes.

**The people are restored for the work.**

- Acts 27:33-36: "And while the day was coming on, Paul besought them all to take meat, saying,
  This day is the fourteenth day that ye have tarried and continued fasting, having taken
  nothing. Wherefore I pray you to take some meat: for this is for your health: for there shall
  not an hair fall from the head of any of you. And when he had thus spoken, he took bread, and
  gave thanks to God in presence of them all: and when he had broken it, he began to eat. Then
  were they all of good cheer, and they also took some meat."

**(Text states)** Food is treated as a matter of health and of morale. The leader eats first.
Note that "meat" here means food of any kind, in the 1611 sense. **(Lexical)**

**The decisive people decision.**

- Acts 27:42-44: "And the soldiers' counsel was to kill the prisoners, lest any of them should
  swim out, and escape. But the centurion, willing to save Paul, kept them from their purpose;
  and commanded that they which could swim should cast themselves first into the sea, and get to
  land: And the rest, some on boards, and some on broken pieces of the ship. And so it came to
  pass, that they escaped all safe to land."

**(Text states)** In the last hour the asset is gone, and the decision is about people. The
soldiers propose a policy that protects an institution at the cost of the people in it. The
centurion refuses, and the outcome is the one promised in 27:22.

## Nehemiah 4: the sequence

The threat here is sustained rather than sudden, and the chapter shows what a long crisis costs.

**The immediate response.**

- Nehemiah 4:8-9: "And conspired all of them together to come and to fight against Jerusalem,
  and to hinder it. Nevertheless we made our prayer unto our God, and set a watch against them
  day and night, because of them."

**(Text states)** Two responses, joined, and the watch is set day and night.

**The placement and the message.**

- Nehemiah 4:13-14: "Therefore set I in the lower places behind the wall, and on the higher
  places, I even set the people after their families with their swords, their spears, and their
  bows. And I looked, and rose up, and said unto the nobles, and to the rulers, and to the rest
  of the people, Be not ye afraid of them: remember the LORD, which is great and terrible, and
  fight for your brethren, your sons, and your daughters, your wives, and your houses."

**(Text states)** The defenders go to the low places, which are the weak points. The units are
families, which are the strongest available bond. The speech names what is being defended
rather than what is feared.

**The split and the rally rule.**

- Nehemiah 4:16-18: "And it came to pass from that time forth, that the half of my servants
  wrought in the work, and the other half of them held both the spears, the shields, and the
  bows, and the habergeons... They which builded on the wall, and they that bare burdens, with
  those that laded, every one with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other hand
  held a weapon."
- Nehemiah 4:19-20: "And I said unto the nobles, and to the rulers, and to the rest of the
  people, The work is great and large, and we are separated upon the wall, one far from another.
  In what place therefore ye hear the sound of the trumpet, resort ye thither unto us: our God
  shall fight for us."

**(Text states)** The workers are spread out, so the plan includes a signal and a meeting point.
A distributed team in a crisis needs one agreed rally rule.

**The strain, the report, and the warning network.**

- Nehemiah 4:10: "And Judah said, The strength of the bearers of burdens is decayed, and there
  is much rubbish; so that we are not able to build the wall."
- Nehemiah 4:12: "And it came to pass, that when the Jews which dwelt by them came, they said
  unto us ten times, From all places whence ye shall return unto us they will be upon you."
- Nehemiah 4:21-23: "So we laboured in the work: and half of them held the spears from the
  rising of the morning till the stars appeared... So neither I, nor my brethren, nor my
  servants, nor the men of the guard which followed me, none of us put off our clothes, saving
  that every one put them off for washing."

**(Text states)** Three internal facts sit inside the chapter: exhaustion, repeated warnings,
and a prolonged schedule with no relief. A crisis plan that assumes full capacity will fail on
the exhaustion line rather than on the threat.

- **Lexical:** "watch" in 4:9 is among the renderings of H4929 _mishmereth_, "a guard (the man,
  the post or the prison); a deposit (figuratively); also (as observed) a usage (abstractly), or
  an example (concretely)". The word covers the guard post and the duty.

## The decisions that belong in the first hour

These decisions reduce harm or preserve the means of recovery. None of them requires the cause.
**(Inference)**

| #   | Decision                                             | The text behind it              | What waiting costs                             |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Stop the active harm                                 | Acts 27:18-19, lighten the ship | The damage compounds while the meeting runs    |
| 2   | Protect the people first                             | Acts 27:42-43                   | Injury and loss of trust                       |
| 3   | Accept the loss that is already certain              | Acts 27:22, "but of the ship"   | Energy spent defending an unsaveable asset     |
| 4   | Name one speaker and one message                     | Acts 27:22-25 and Nehemiah 4:14 | Two accounts circulate and neither is believed |
| 5   | Post defenders at the weak points                    | Nehemiah 4:13                   | The threat finds the unguarded place           |
| 6   | Keep the people together, and stop the private exits | Acts 27:30-32                   | The people with the exit leave first           |
| 7   | Restore the people's capacity to work                | Acts 27:33-36                   | Exhaustion decides the outcome                 |
| 8   | Set one rally rule for dispersed people              | Nehemiah 4:19-20                | Separated teams make separate choices          |
| 9   | Start the record                                     | Acts 27:27-28, 27:37            | The record is gone within days                 |

**The order matters.** Items 1 to 3 come before any communication, because a message that
contradicts the visible facts costs credibility that the response needs later. **(Inference)**

## The decisions that wait

These decisions produce no additional harm when they wait one day or one week. **(Inference)**

- The root cause. The cause is found after the situation is stable, and the diagnosis procedure
  in `references/diagnosis.md` is built for that.
- The blame and the verdict. The business axiom is A26. Judgment that arrives first produces the
  wrong verdict and teaches people to hide.
- Who was right about the warning. The record settles that question later, and the question
  distracts the room during the response.
- The policy change. A policy written in the middle of a crisis encodes the panic.
- The permanent fix. It needs the classification step.
- The post-mortem, the performance review, and the message to the market.
- The reorganisation. Crises are a bad time to redesign the organization chart, because the
  people who know the work are busy with the work.

**The test for the boundary between the two lists.** Does the delay produce more harm? If the
answer is no, the decision waits. If the answer is yes, the decision belongs in the first hour.
**(Inference)**

## The record

The narrative in Acts 27 includes three measurements that a later storyteller has no reason to
add.

- Verse 27:27: "when the fourteenth night was come" and "about midnight".
- Verse 27:28: "sounded, and found it twenty fathoms: and when they had gone a little further,
  they sounded again, and found it fifteen fathoms."
- Verse 27:37: "And we were in all in the ship two hundred threescore and sixteen souls."

**(Text states)** Times, soundings, and a headcount. The record was kept while the ship was
breaking up.

Three rules follow for a crisis log. **(Inference)**

1. **Write time and observation together.** A fact without a time is not usable in the review.
2. **Count the people.** The headcount in verse 27:37 is the number the response is accountable
   for.
3. **Keep the raw record.** The interpretation is written later, and it is written on top of the
   raw record.

## The stopping rule

An emergency needs an end date. The body axiom B10 in `body-first-principles` states the shape.
Acute inflammation heals, and chronic inflammation destroys the tissue it was sent to protect.
Set the date when the emergency posture ends and the normal posture resumes. **(Inference)**

The stopping rule has three parts.

1. The condition that ends the emergency.
2. The date of the review that confirms it.
3. The named person who declares the end.

Without the third part the emergency ends by exhaustion rather than by decision.

## Failure modes in a crisis

| Failure                                    | What it looks like                                         | The text that names it                                              |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fighting for the asset                     | Effort spent on a loss that is already certain             | Acts 27:22, the ship is given up in the forecast                    |
| Abandoning the people with the asset       | The people closest to the exit leave first                 | Acts 27:30, the sailors lower the boat                              |
| Punishing the people to protect the system | A policy applied at the cost of the people inside it       | Acts 27:42, the soldiers' counsel                                   |
| Running the crisis alone                   | The leader holds every decision and becomes the bottleneck | Exodus 18:18, "too heavy for thee"                                  |
| Leaving the post for the negotiation       | The leader attends the meeting and the work stops          | Nehemiah 6:3, "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down" |
| Pretending the asset is safe               | A message that contradicts the visible facts               | Acts 27:22 states the loss and the preservation together            |
| No end date                                | The emergency becomes the operating mode                   | Body axiom B10                                                      |

**(Text states)** for each cited verse. The mapping from the verse to the failure mode is an
inference.

Note the sixth row. In Acts the message includes bad news. A crisis message that reports only
good news trains the audience to discount the next message. **(Inference)**

## After the crisis

The order after the emergency ends is fixed.

1. **Close the losses.** Account for the people, the money, and the commitments. Acts 27 ends
   with everyone safe and the ship and cargo lost. **(Text states)**
2. **Debrief the warning.** Find the warning in the record and ask how it can arrive earlier or
   be weighed differently. In Acts 27 the warning was right and the source was discounted.
3. **Run the diagnosis procedure.** Classify the failure before writing the fix.
4. **Write the permanent change.** The policy, the gate, or the capacity model that the
   classification calls for.
5. **Set the next test.** Name the event that will show whether the fix holds.

**Inference:** a crisis that ends without a written change has spent its cost and kept nothing.
The people who lived through it carry the lesson, and the lesson leaves when they do.

Nehemiah 6:15-16 closes its own crisis with a date and an outcome: "So the wall was finished in
the twenty and fifth day of the month Elul, in fifty and two days. And it came to pass, that
when all our enemies heard thereof... they were much cast down in their own eyes: for they
perceived that this work was wrought of our God." **(Text states)**
