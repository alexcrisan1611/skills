# Business Idea Generation

A skill that finds business opportunities, tests them, and turns them into a decision, with a method that rests on Scripture.

## What it does

The skill defines an opportunity as a change in the world that makes some work newly worth doing. It finds those changes, tests them against a seven-step procedure, and gives a structured answer. The answer ends with a test that has a threshold, a date, and a stopping rule.

The skill keeps two capacities apart. The first is reading the times, from 1 Chronicles 12:32. The second is knowing what to do. A reading with no owner, no action, no trigger, no cost, and no stopping rule is only a report. The conversion test in the skill finds which of these answers is missing.

Each claim in the skill carries one of four labels:

- Text states: the KJV verse says it, and the file quotes the verse.
- Lexical: a Strong's entry says it, copied from the lexicon tool in `scripture-foundations`.
- Inference: a reader drew it, and the file shows how.
- Contested: faithful readers or practitioners disagree, and the file gives the range.

Every business application is an inference. The skill says "this suggests". It does not say that the Bible commands a business practice.

## When to use it

Use the skill when you:

- Ask for business ideas, or ask what to build next.
- Want to find a gap or a niche in a market.
- Ask whether an idea is any good.
- Want help to read a trend, or to tell a durable change from a fashion.
- Plan to enter a market, or to position something new.

## How it works

The procedure has seven steps. Each step names a question, the evidence it needs, and the usual failure:

1. Name the change. Give a change in cost, time, distance, rule, or tooling, with a date.
2. Name who it affects. Give a named group that you can reach.
3. Name the workaround. Find what these people do today and what they pay for it.
4. Name the formless part. Find the part of the market with no boundary and no name. Then divide it, name it, and fill it, in that order.
5. Size the ground. Count the total, reachable, and paying groups, and state a floor case.
6. Name the distribution. Give a sales channel, its cost per customer, and the growth model (mustard, which is visible and central, or leaven, which is hidden and spread out).
7. State the test. Give a threshold, a date, and a stopping rule.

The skill also applies a demand test from Genesis 3:6. The test asks how the offer answers appetite, beauty, and status. The skill treats this test as a description and a caution, not a command.

When you ask for ideas or for a verdict on an idea, the answer comes in a fixed order. It covers the change, the group, the formless part, the ground, the distribution, the demand check, the position in the cycle, the test, the smallest first step that you can reverse, and a confidence label on each claim.

## What is in this folder

- `SKILL.md`: The main skill file. It holds the Scripture cases, the conversion test, the seven-step procedure, the output format, and a table of common failure modes.
- `references/opportunity-method.md`: The full procedure. It holds the evidence standards for each step, the conversion and demand tests, the bet portfolio, market sizing, falsification, and the smallest reversible first step. It also has two worked examples with invented figures: a software product for small accounting firms and a mobile e-bike service.
- `references/reading-the-times.md`: The trend material. It covers what a trend is, seven tests to separate durable change from fashion, what to watch in the field, how long a market position lasts, and how to test a claim about the future. It ends with 25 working rules.

Both reference files end with a verification log. The log lists the verses and Strong's entries that the tools checked, and the claims that the tools cannot verify.

## Limits

The Bible gives cases, not a method for market research. The procedure is a synthesis, and the files label it as one.

The tools cannot verify a market fact. Every market number in the skill is an assumption or an invented illustration. You must count the real numbers yourself.

The Strong's data has no tags at the verse level. Each link from a verse to a Strong's number is a standard concordance association, not a tool finding.

The skill does not forecast business cycles. The skill states that experts disagree about whether anyone can forecast them. Nobody knows the error rate of the trend tests.

The skill does not decide the ethics of what you sell. For ethics, it points to `business-first-principles/references/ethics.md`.
