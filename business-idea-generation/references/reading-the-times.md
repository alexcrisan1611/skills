# Reading the Times

Trend material for opportunity work. This file covers durable change, present conditions, the
shelf life of a position, and the tests for a claim about the future.

## Method and labels

**Text states** / **Lexical** / **Inference** / **Contested**. Verses come from `skills/scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts`.
Strong's definitions come from `skills/scripture-foundations/scripts/lexicon.ts` and are copied verbatim.

**A necessary honesty.** Scripture supplies no trend detection method. It supplies cases,
warnings, and tests. Everything in this file after the quoted text is either **Lexical** or
**Inference**, and the file says which. The one place a method appears, it is a synthesis made
here, and it has no known error rate.

**The two capacities, in brief.** 1 Chronicles 12:32 names "understanding of the times" and "to
know what Israel ought to do" as two things, and both are required. The full derivation lives
in `skills/business-first-principles/references/trends-and-timing.md`, Part 1. This file treats
the first capacity: the reading. The conversion test that turns a reading into a decision sits
in `opportunity-method.md`, Part 2.

## Part 1. What a trend is

**Inference.** A trend, for business purposes, is a change in a constraint that persists long
enough for people to reorganise their lives around it.

The definition carries three parts.

1. **A constraint.** Cost, time, distance, rule, or tooling. A constraint binds behaviour. A
   preference does not.
2. **Persistence.** The change outlasts the first generation of adopters, and it outlasts the
   first cost decline.
3. **Reorganisation.** People change what they buy, where they work, what they own, or what
   they expect. The test of a trend is behaviour that has already changed, not opinion.

**Text states (Matthew 16:3).** "ye can discern the face of the sky; but can ye not discern the
signs of the times?"

**Lexical.** G4592 _semeion_ stands behind "signs". The entry reads "an indication, especially
ceremonially or supernaturally". The word names an indication, not a guarantee.

**Inference.** The passage treats the signs as present and available. The failure is
interpretive and volitional, not informational. Luke 12:56 asks "how is it that ye do not
discern this time?"

**The practical consequence.** Do not ask for a forecast when the change has already happened.
If behaviour has moved, the question changes. Ask how far the change has gone, and what it
breaks next. That question has present evidence behind it.

## Part 2. Durable change against fashion

**Contested in part.** There is no settled method here. The seven tests below are a synthesis
and a discipline. Separate durable changes from fashions in the wild, and the tests disagree
with each other at the edges.

### The seven tests

1. **Does the change bind behaviour or only attract attention?** A binding change alters a
   constraint. A fashion alters a preference. **Inference.**
2. **Who has already paid?** A durable change leaves capital behind it. Buildings, supply
   chains, training, and contracts are expensive to reverse, so people reverse slowly.
   **Inference.**
3. **Does it survive a downturn?** Ask what happens in a bad year. A change that disappears in
   a bad year was a luxury. A change that survives is closer to a constraint. **Inference.**
4. **Does it show up in more than one kind of source?** Counts, prices, wait times, hiring,
   and physical density are independent observations. A change that shows up in one kind of
   source, usually the reporting about itself, is weaker. **Inference.**
5. **Is the observation from the field or from the report?** See Part 3. The person who reads
   the market directly has evidence. The person who reads summaries has a summary.
   **Inference.**
6. **What reverses it, and who pays for the reversal?** Name the reversal condition
   in writing. A change with no named reversal is an assumption. **Inference.**
7. **Is the change old enough to have a second act?** Durable changes produce second order
   effects. Remote work moved the commute hours, and then it moved housing demand, then
   commercial property. The second order effects are often where the opportunity sits.
   **Inference.**

### The hard truth about the distinction

**Inference.** Some durable changes look like fashions at the start, and some fashions look
durable. The distinction cannot be made with certainty in real time. Two consequences follow.

1. **Make early commitments small.** Test the change with a reversible step. This is A32. A
   small commitment can be abandoned. A large one cannot.
2. **Write the reversal condition.** State the observation that shows the change is a fashion.
   A person who cannot name the reversal condition has stopped reading and started believing.

**Text states (Proverbs 27:1).** "Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a
day may bring forth." The prohibition is on the boast, not on the plan.

## Part 3. Reading how people live now

**Inference.** This part is fieldwork. It has no substitute, and it cannot be done from
summaries.

**Text states (1 Chronicles 12:32).** The men of Issachar had "understanding of the times".

**Lexical.** H3045 _yada_ stands behind "know" in the same verse. The entry reads "to know
(properly, to ascertain by seeing)". The gloss list includes "consider", "discern", "perceive",
and "look well to". The knowledge is by observation and acquaintance.

**Text states (Acts 17:23).** Paul says "as I passed by, and beheld your devotions, I found an
altar with this inscription, TO THE UNKNOWN GOD."

**Inference.** The reading comes from walking the market and handling its objects. Paul read an
altar and its inscription. He did not read a segment report.

### What to watch

1. **Where the hours go.** Ask for a calendar, not a survey. A week of real appointments shows
   the actual pattern. **Inference.**
2. **What people already pay.** Include payment in hours and in annoyance. A workaround with a
   true cost is a market. A workaround with no cost is usually not a problem. **Inference.**
3. **The unofficial workarounds.** Look for the spreadsheet nobody owns, the group chat that
   runs the operation, and the relative who does the admin. A workaround is a specification for
   the product. **Inference.**
4. **The avoided tasks.** Watch what people pay to never do again. Avoidance is a strong signal,
   because the person is already spending to keep the task away. **Inference.**
5. **The substitutes.** Ask what the buyer uses instead, including doing nothing. The
   substitute sets the price ceiling. **Inference.**
6. **The words.** Write down the market's own vocabulary. Acts 17:28 quotes the market's own
   poets. Words from your own frame get translated into the market's frame or discarded.
   **Inference.**
7. **The second order effects.** Ask what breaks when the change continues. Every durable
   change breaks a system that was built for the old condition. **Inference.**
8. **The unserved edge.** Watch the group at the edge of the change, because the edge shows the
   direction. The person who changed early and the person who cannot change at all both supply
   information. **Inference.**

### What to avoid

- **Do not read a market from its own marketing.** Press releases, brand pages, and conference
  talks are the market's presentation of itself. **Inference.**
- **Do not count opinions as behaviour.** A stated intention is not a purchase. **Inference.**
- **Do not sample only the satisfied.** The dissatisfied, the churned, and the person who
  never adopted all carry evidence. **Inference.**
- **Do not treat one conversation as a reading.** One conversation is one observation.
  **Inference.**

**Text states (Numbers 13:17-20).** The reconnaissance brief asks binary questions about the
land, the people, and the cities. It also asks for physical evidence: "bring of the fruit of the
land." The brief is a survey instrument. The full treatment sits in
`skills/business-first-principles/references/trends-and-timing.md`, Part 5.

## Part 4. The shelf life of a position

**Text (Daniel 2:20-22, KJV)**

> 20. Daniel answered and said, Blessed be the name of God for ever and ever: for wisdom and
>     might are his:
> 21. And he changeth the times and the seasons: he removeth kings, and setteth up kings: he
>     giveth wisdom unto the wise, and knowledge to them that know understanding:
> 22. He revealeth the deep and secret things: he knoweth what is in the darkness, and the
>     light dwelleth with him.

**Text states (Daniel 2:39).** "And after thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee".

**Text states (Daniel 2:41-43).** The fourth kingdom is "divided", "partly strong, and partly
broken", and its parts "shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay."

**Text states (Daniel 2:44).** A kingdom "shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms,
and it shall stand for ever."

**Text states (Daniel 7:17).** "These great beasts, which are four, are four kings, which shall
arise out of the earth."

**Text states (Daniel 7:24).** "And the ten horns out of this kingdom are ten kings that shall
arise: and another shall rise after them."

### The observations

1. **Text states.** Dominance arrives in a sequence. The first king is told that he is first,
   and that another follows.
2. **Text states.** The sequence runs gold, silver, brass, iron, then iron mixed with clay.
   **Inference.** The value of the material falls while the strength rises, until the final
   stage mixes strength with fragility.
3. **Text states.** The parts do not bond. "they shall not cleave one to another".
4. **Text states.** The four are destroyed together in one event. **Inference.** The ending is
   not gradual.
5. **Text states.** Daniel 2:21 states the mechanism. "he changeth the times and the seasons: he
   removeth kings, and setteth up kings."

### The business inference

**Inference.** Assume the category will be superseded. The question is not whether, but when
and by what.

**Inference.** The incumbent is not at the top of a progression. The metals degrade. A company
that reads its own position as the leading edge is reading the sequence backwards.

**Inference.** Watch for internal incoherence inside a large competitor. An organisation
assembled from parts that do not bond is at the iron and clay stage, and it can look strong
while it is fragile.

**Inference.** The disruption often arrives from outside the category. The stone in Daniel 2
comes from outside the image. A threat that competes in the same frame is a normal rival.

**Inference.** Long transitions and short endings. Four kingdoms run for centuries, and the
change comes in one event. A plan that assumes a gradual end will be late.

**Text states (Isaiah 28:16).** "he that believeth shall not make haste." Preparations made
against a series do not require speed. They require placement.

### The contested part

**Contested.** The identification of the four kingdoms is disputed among faithful readers.
Daniel 2 supplies the sequence and the metals. Daniel 7 supplies the beasts and the number.
The text quoted here does not name the kingdoms. Do not build a market forecast on a disputed
identification. The claims that survive the dispute are the sequence, the degradation, the
incoherence, the abrupt ending, and the governance of the succession.

## Part 5. New things declared in advance

**Text (Isaiah 42:9, KJV)**

> Behold, the former things are come to pass, and new things do I declare: before they spring
> forth I tell you of them.

**Text (Isaiah 43:19, KJV)**

> Behold, I will do a new thing; now it shall spring forth; shall ye not know it? I will even
> make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert.

**Text states.** The ground for confidence in the new declaration is the completion of the
former one. "the former things are come to pass".

**Text states.** The announcement precedes the event. "before they spring forth I tell you of
them", and "now it shall spring forth; shall ye not know it?"

**Text states.** The audience is expected to recognise the event when it arrives.

### The business inference

**Inference.** These verses describe God's declarations. They are not a human forecasting
method, and the file does not present them as one. Two features transfer as a pattern.

1. **The track record is the warrant.** A prediction is worth what the predictor's prior
   predictions are worth. Keep the record, and ask for the other party's record.
2. **Specificity is the test.** The declaration names gates, bars, and treasures. A forecast
   that names nothing checkable cannot be falsified.

**Inference.** A declaration made in advance also has a purpose: the audience will recognise
the event. A strategy that nobody inside the company can recognise when it happens is not a
strategy.

**Contested.** Readers differ on the scope of these passages. Some read them as promises about
the return from exile, some as messianic, and some as both. The business inference rests on the
pattern of declaration, track record, and recognition. It does not rest on a particular
reading of the fulfilment.

## Part 6. Disclosed and deduced

**Text (Amos 3:7, KJV)**

> Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the
> prophets.

**Text states.** The claim is about the source of knowledge. Genuine future events are
disclosed to the prophets, and they are not deduced from a model.

**Inference.** The verse does not claim that every forecast is a disclosure. It claims that
God's own acts come with a disclosure to his servants. The distinction for business use is
between disclosed information and deduced information.

| Kind      | Source                      | Error behaviour                          |
| --------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Disclosed | Given, and stated as given  | Verified after the event                 |
| Deduced   | Model, data, and assumption | Carries model error and assumption error |

**Inference.** A deduced forecast is useful and must be labelled as deduced. A person who
presents a model output as a disclosure has made a category error. The error is expensive,
because the audience drops its guard.

**Text states (Numbers 13:17-20).** The reconnaissance produced observations, not disclosures.
See Part 3.

**Working rule.** Label the source of every claim about the future. Write "model output",
"reported, unverified", or "stated directly".

## Part 7. Testing a claim about the future

**Text (Deuteronomy 18:21-22, KJV)**

> 21. And if thou say in thine heart, How shall we know the word which the LORD hath not
>     spoken?
> 22. When a prophet speaketh in the name of the LORD, if the thing follow not, nor come to
>     pass, that is the thing which the LORD hath not spoken, but the prophet hath spoken it
>     presumptuously: thou shalt not be afraid of him.

**Text (1 John 4:1, KJV)**

> Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many
> false prophets are gone out into the world.

**Text (1 Thessalonians 5:21, KJV)**

> Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.

**Text states.** The question is asked and answered. "How shall we know the word which the LORD
hath not spoken?" The answer gives an outcome test: "if the thing follow not, nor come to
pass."

**Text states.** The test is retrospective. It applies after the event.

**Text states.** The instruction includes action after the test. "thou shalt not be afraid of
him." The failed predictor loses authority.

**Text states.** 1 John 4:1 commands the test and gives the reason: "many false prophets are
gone out into the world."

**Lexical.** G1381 _dokimazo_ stands behind "try" in 1 John 4:1 and "Prove" in 1 Thessalonians
5:21. The entry reads "to test (literally or figuratively); by implication, to approve". The
KJV gloss list includes "discern", "examine", and "try". The test aims at approval, not at
scepticism.

**Text states (1 Thessalonians 5:21).** Two instructions follow each other. "Prove all things"
and "hold fast that which is good."

### The assembled test

**Inference.** The passages combine into a procedure with five steps.

| Step | Source               | The question                                                    |
| ---- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Deuteronomy 18:22    | Did this predictor's prior claims come to pass?                 |
| 2    | 1 John 4:1           | What is the source of the claim, and is it identifiable?        |
| 3    | 1 Thessalonians 5:21 | Is this testable before the event, and is the threshold stated? |
| 4    | Deuteronomy 18:22    | If it fails, does the predictor keep authority?                 |
| 5    | Amos 3:7             | Is the claim disclosed or deduced?                              |

**Text states.** The procedure has a verification half and no generation half. The passages
tell you how to test a forecast. They do not tell you how to produce a certain one.

**Inference.** The record of past predictions is the most valuable instrument available for
evaluating present ones. A business without a forecast log cannot apply the test.

**Inference.** "Many false prophets are gone out into the world" states a low base rate for
reliable forecasts. Plan with that base rate in view.

## Part 8. The honest limits of forecasting

This part states the limits plainly. The limits come from the text.

**Text (Ecclesiastes 3:11, KJV)**

> He hath made every thing beautiful in his time: also he hath set the world in their heart, so
> that no man can find out the work that God maketh from the beginning to the end.

**Text states.** "no man can find out the work that God maketh from the beginning to the end."

**Text (Ecclesiastes 9:11, KJV)**

> I returned, and saw under the sun, that the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the
> strong, neither yet bread to the wise, nor yet riches to men of understanding, nor yet favour
> to men of skill; but time and chance happeneth to them all.

**Text states.** Five qualities fail to guarantee their outcome. The reason given is "time and
chance happeneth to them all."

**Text (Daniel 2:21, KJV)**

> And he changeth the times and the seasons: he removeth kings, and setteth up kings: he giveth
> wisdom unto the wise, and knowledge to them that know understanding:

**Text states.** The times and the seasons change by an act that is not the market's.

### What follows

1. **The whole plan is not visible.** Text states, Ecclesiastes 3:11. A reading is always
   partial, so a decision made on a partial reading is the only kind available. **Inference.**
2. **Effort and outcome are separate variables.** Text states, Ecclesiastes 9:11. Excellence
   improves the odds, and it does not determine the result. **Inference.**
3. **Timing is not fully controllable.** Text states, Daniel 2:21. **Inference.**
4. **A forecast is a limited instrument.** Inference. State the confidence, the assumptions,
   the error range, and the reversal cost. Do not state a date as a fact.
5. **Reversible decisions carry the uncertainty.** Inference. When the reading is uncertain, the
   plan must be reversible. This is A32.
6. **Reserves cover the encounter.** Inference. Ecclesiastes 9:11 names "time and chance" as
   things that meet everyone. A reserve is a hedge against the encounter, not against a trend.
   See `skills/business-first-principles/references/capital-and-stewardship.md`.

### The two honest statements

**Statement one.** No verse gives a method for detecting a trend. The trend material in this
file is a synthesis, and no part of it is a divine command.

**Statement two.** Business cycle theory is contested. Economists disagree about the regularity
of cycles, about their drivers, and about whether they are forecastable at all. A plan that
depends on a cycle forecast carries that dispute inside it.

## Part 9. Working rules

Stated as inference, unless the line gives a source.

**On the reading itself**

1. Read by observation, not by inference (1 Chronicles 12:32, _yada_, "ascertain by seeing").
2. Read the times in the plural. Different parts of the market face different conditions.
3. Read artifacts and behaviours, not presentations of them (Acts 17:23).
4. Write the market's own words down, and use them (Acts 17:28).
5. Look for the second order effect. The first order change is priced in.

**On durability**

6. Ask who has already paid to make the change stick.
7. Ask what survives a bad year.
8. Ask what reverses the change, and who pays for the reversal.
9. Make the early commitment small, because the distinction between a change and a fashion
   cannot be settled in real time (A32).
10. Write the reversal condition before the commitment, not after.

**On positions**

11. Assume the category will be superseded (Daniel 2:39).
12. Do not read the incumbent as the top of a progression (Daniel 2:32-33).
13. Watch internal incoherence in a large competitor (Daniel 2:43).
14. Expect the disruption from outside the category (Daniel 2:45).
15. Expect a long transition and a short ending (Daniel 2:44).

**On claims about the future**

16. Keep the forecast log, because the test is retrospective (Deuteronomy 18:22).
17. Remove the authority of a predictor who failed (Deuteronomy 18:22).
18. Test the claim, then keep what passes (1 Thessalonians 5:21).
19. Label a claim as disclosed or deduced (Amos 3:7).
20. State the threshold and the date, so the claim can fail (Isaiah 42:9).
21. Check the track record before the content (Isaiah 42:9).

**On the limits**

22. State the confidence, the assumptions, and the error range (Ecclesiastes 3:11).
23. Do not read outcomes back into process quality (Ecclesiastes 9:11).
24. Put the reversibility where the uncertainty is (A32).
25. Reserve against the encounter, not against the trend (Ecclesiastes 9:11).
