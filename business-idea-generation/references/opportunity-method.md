# The Opportunity Method

The full procedure for finding and testing a business opportunity.

This file is a synthesis. The Bible supplies cases, not a market research method. Every step
below is an **Inference**, drawn from the passages named at each point. The two worked examples
use invented figures. They show the arithmetic of the method and are not market evidence.

## Method and labels

**Text states** / **Lexical** / **Inference** / **Contested**. Verses come from `skills/scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts`.
Strong's definitions come from `skills/scripture-foundations/scripts/lexicon.ts` and are copied verbatim.

Governing passages:

- 1 Chronicles 12:32, the two capacities
- Luke 12:54-56 and Matthew 16:2-3, the reading and the decision
- John 4:35, the harvest is now
- Acts 17:22-31, the model entry
- Genesis 2:19-20, the gap appears by doing
- Genesis 41 and 47, the cycle
- Ecclesiastes 11:1-6, many bets
- Nehemiah 2:11-16 and Numbers 13, reconnaissance
- Matthew 13:3-9 and 13:31-33, distribution
- Ecclesiastes 1:9-10, recombination
- Genesis 1:2-5, formlessness
- Proverbs 27:23 and 22:29, evidence and diligence
- 1 Samuel 17, experience
- Genesis 3:6, the demand test

Labels used below: **A** refers to the axiom numbers in `business-first-principles`. **A5** is
formation precedes filling. **A19** is that doing the work reveals the need. **A20** is that
abundance precedes restriction. **A24** is that desire runs on three channels.

## Part 1. The seven steps

Each step gives the question, the evidence standard, the failure mode, and the label.

### Step 1. Name the change

**The question.** What changed in the world?

**Text states (John 4:35).** "Say not ye, There are yet four months, and then cometh harvest?
behold, I say unto you, Lift up your eyes, and look on the fields; for they are white already to
harvest."

**The evidence standard.** A change in a constraint, with a date. The usable categories are
cost, time, distance, rule, and tooling. A change in taste is weaker evidence and needs more
support, because taste reverses without a written record.

**The failure mode.** Naming a mood as a change. "People want authenticity now" is not a
change. "A rule took effect in March that requires a digital filing for this group" is a
change.

**Inference.** The step corresponds to the reading half of 1 Chronicles 12:32. It is not the
decision half.

### Step 2. Name who it affects

**The question.** Who feels this change first, and who feels it most?

**Text states (Acts 17:23).** "For as I passed by, and beheld your devotions, I found an altar
with this inscription, TO THE UNKNOWN GOD."

**The evidence standard.** A named group you can reach. The group needs a location, a way to
contact it, and a reason to care this quarter.

**The failure mode.** "Everyone" or "small businesses". A group that large has no shared
workaround, so the message has no shared content.

**Inference.** The observation is specific in Acts. Paul names an altar and an inscription,
not a demographic.

### Step 3. Name the workaround

**The question.** What do these people do today, and what do they pay for it?

**Text states (Genesis 2:20).** "And Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the
air, and to every beast of the field; but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him."

**The evidence standard.** A named practice that already exists. The spreadsheet, the group
chat, the hired relative, the paid substitute, the task that is simply not done.

**The failure mode.** Assuming the current method is nothing. If the workaround is "nothing",
the need is usually weak, because nobody pays for a problem nobody solves.

**Inference.** The workaround is the real competitor. A new offer replaces it, so the offer
must beat it on cost, time, or standing.

### Step 4. Name the formless part

**The question.** Which part of this market has no structure and no contents?

**Text states (Genesis 1:2, 1:4-5).** "And the earth was without form, and void". Then "God
divided the light from the darkness." Then "God called the light Day, and the darkness he
called Night."

**Text states.** The order in Genesis 1 is form, then boundary, then name, then contents.

**The evidence standard.** A part with no boundary and no name. Signs: customers describe the
area with different words, no one owns the record, and the handoffs are verbal.

**The failure mode.** Filling before forming, which is A5. Features arrive before the container
exists, and the result is clutter that cannot be evaluated.

**Inference.** The three moves are division, naming, and filling. A6 says distinction precedes
order. A7 says naming is an act of authority.

### Step 5. Size the ground

**The question.** How many are in the group, and what do they already spend?

**Text states (Matthew 13:8).** "But other fell into good ground, and brought forth fruit,
some an hundredfold, some sixtyfold, some thirtyfold."

**The evidence standard.** A counted number with a stated method, an error range, and a floor
case. Count the installed base, the transaction volume, or the annual spend, and show the
arithmetic.

**The failure mode.** A number from a report with no method. A top-down percentage of a large
figure is a guess wearing a suit.

**Inference.** The same seed returns nothing or a hundredfold by ground. Segment size and
segment fit matter more than the offer's quality.

### Step 6. Name the distribution

**The question.** How does the offer reach the group, and at what cost per customer acquired?

**Text states (Matthew 13:31-33).** One model is visible and central, and becomes a tree where
"the birds of the air come and lodge in the branches thereof." The other is hidden, and works
"till the whole was leavened."

**The evidence standard.** A named channel, a named owner, and a cost per acquired customer
that the price supports. Test the channel before the product.

**The failure mode.** Building the product and leaving the channel to luck. The best product in
a channel you cannot reach is an expensive hobby.

**Inference.** Know which model you run. A mustard business measures the size of the tree. A
leaven business has no useful interim dashboard.

### Step 7. State the test

**The question.** What result, at what date, ends the attempt?

**Text states (Luke 14:28).** "For which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down
first, and counteth the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it?"

**The evidence standard.** A threshold, a date, and a stopping rule. The threshold names a
number. The date names a day. The stopping rule names what happens when the number misses.

**The failure mode.** No stopping rule. A decision with no stopping rule is a commitment to
drift, and it consumes the years A30 says are the binding constraint.

**Inference.** The test is written before the build, because a test written after the build is
a rationalisation.

## Part 2. The conversion test

**Text (Luke 12:54-56, KJV)**

> 54. And he said also to the people, When ye see a cloud rise out of the west, straightway ye
>     say, There cometh a shower; and so it is.
> 55. And when ye see the south wind blow, ye say, There will be heat; and it cometh to pass.
> 56. Ye hypocrites, ye can discern the face of the sky and of the earth; but how is it that ye
>     do not discern this time?

**Text states.** Both forecasts come true. "and so it is", and "it cometh to pass."

**Text states.** The charge is inconsistency, not incapacity. The skill works in one domain and
is not used in another.

**Lexical.** G2540 _kairos_ stands behind "time" in verse 56. The entry reads "an occasion, i.e.
set or proper time". The KJV gloss list includes "opportunity" and "(due) season". The missed
object is a window, not a schedule date.

**Lexical.** G4592 _semeion_ stands behind "signs" in Matthew 16:3. The entry reads "an
indication, especially ceremonially or supernaturally". The gloss list includes "token".

### The test

A reading has become a decision when five answers exist. **Inference.**

| Question               | A decision has                                  | A report has                   |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 1. Who owns it?        | A named person                                  | A committee or nobody          |
| 2. What is the action? | A verb with an object                           | "We are watching this space"   |
| 3. What starts it?     | A date or a trigger                             | No start condition             |
| 4. What did you stop?  | A named reallocation of money, people, or hours | No cost, because nothing moved |
| 5. What ends it?       | A stopping rule with a number                   | No exit condition              |

**The diagnosis for a missing answer.** If answer 1 is missing, the reading is entertainment.
If answer 2 is missing, the reading has not been interpreted. If answer 3 is missing, the
decision drifts. If answer 4 is missing, the decision is not funded, so it will not happen. If
answer 5 is missing, the decision cannot be evaluated and cannot end.

**A worked use.** A team reports that a regulation will change the market in eighteen months.
The report is accurate. Ask the five questions. Suppose the answers are a name, a pricing page
tweak, a trigger date, nothing, and nothing. Then the team has a reading and no decision. Write
the four missing answers or stop calling it a plan.

## Part 3. The demand test

**Text (Genesis 3:6, KJV)**

> And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes,
> and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and
> gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.

**Text states.** The verse names three properties: "good for food", "pleasant to the eyes",
"to be desired to make one wise".

**Text states.** The three appear in one sequence, and the act follows.

**Inference.** The three properties are appetite, beauty, and status. A24 states that every
purchase, hire, and commitment is decided on all three at once.

### The three questions

**1. Appetite.** What need does this remove, and what does the buyer pay for the workaround
now? Put a number on the current cost. Include the buyer's own hours at a real rate.

**2. Beauty.** What does the buyer see, hold, or show? The interface, the van, the packaging,
the invoice, and the handover all carry this answer. A product with no answer here loses to a
worse product with one.

**3. Status.** What standing does the buyer gain or protect? Buying this can raise the buyer
in front of clients, peers, or staff. It can also lower the buyer, and a purchase that lowers
standing does not happen.

**The failure mode.** Two channels answered and one left open. The buyer takes the offer that
answers the missing channel, even when the other two answers are weaker.

**The caution.** The verse describes desire ahead of judgment. It records a fall, and it
commands nothing. The test is descriptive. The ethics of what you sell are governed by
`skills/business-first-principles/references/ethics.md`.

## Part 4. The bet portfolio

**Text (Ecclesiastes 11:1-6, KJV)**

> 1. Cast thy bread upon the waters: for thou shalt find it after many days.
> 2. Give a portion to seven, and also to eight; for thou knowest not what evil shall be upon
>    the earth.
> 3. If the clouds be full of rain, they empty themselves upon the earth: and if the tree fall
>    toward the south, or toward the north, in the place where the tree falleth, there it shall
>    be.
> 4. He that observeth the wind shall not sow; and he that regardeth the clouds shall not reap.
> 5. As thou knowest not what is the way of the spirit, nor how the bones do grow in the womb
>    of her that is with child: even so thou knowest not the works of God who maketh all.
> 6. In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine hand: for thou knowest
>    not whether shall prosper, either this or that, or whether they both shall be alike good.

**Text states.** The instruction names more than one portion. "Give a portion to seven, and
also to eight."

**Text states.** The instruction names two sowings. "In the morning sow thy seed, and in the
evening withhold not thine hand."

**Text states.** The stated reason is ignorance. The text says "thou knowest not" three times
in three verses.

**Text states.** The counter-case is a person who watches conditions and never acts. "He that
observeth the wind shall not sow."

**Text states.** Three outcomes are named, and one is both bets succeeding. "or whether they
both shall be alike good."

### The rules, stated as inference

1. **Waiting for certainty is a decision.** It is a decision to have no harvest.
2. **The remedy is parallel attempts, not better forecasting.** The uncertainty is permanent.
3. **The attempts are spaced.** Morning and evening. A second attempt starts on its own
   schedule, and it does not depend on the first.
4. **Both attempts can win.** Do not assume the attempts cannibalise each other.
5. **Count the cost of the portfolio.** Money, attention, and hours divide. The text does not
   name this cost, so name it yourself.
6. **Do not turn a number into a formula.** "Seven, and also to eight" reads as a large number
   and then more. It is not an allocation rule, and no verse supplies a bet count.

**Contested.** The idiom reading in rule 6 is a reading. The tools verify the verse text and
do not verify the idiom.

## Part 5. Sizing the ground

**Text (Matthew 13:8).** The same seed yields a hundredfold, sixtyfold, or thirtyfold, and
three of four grounds yield nothing that is named.

**Inference.** Size the ground in three layers. Count each layer with its own method.

| Layer               | The question                        | The method                                          | The honest limit                                            |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| The total group     | How many exist?                     | A count from a register, a census, or a dealer list | Registers go stale, so date the source                      |
| The reachable group | How many can you contact this year? | A count from a list you can actually call or write  | A reachable count is smaller than the total, often by a lot |
| The paying group    | How many will pay at your price?    | A measured conversion from a small test             | This number is the least knowable before the test           |

**The floor case.** State the number under pessimistic assumptions and show the arithmetic. If
the floor case supports the plan, the plan is robust to the estimate. If only the ceiling case
supports the plan, the plan depends on a forecast.

**The floor case as a decision rule.** Use the floor case for the go decision, and use the
ceiling case for the capacity plan. **Inference.**

**What not to do.** Do not multiply a large population by a small percentage and call it a
market. The small percentage is the whole argument, and it carries no evidence.

**Contested.** Market sizing methods differ among practitioners. Top-down and bottom-up
approaches give different numbers, and neither is reliable for a new category. The text here
gives a discipline for stating the estimate, not a method for making it accurate.

## Part 6. Worked example A: a software product

**All figures in this example are invented.** They show the method. Do not read them as market
data. The scenario is illustrative.

### The change

Accounting firms and their clients moved to digital document exchange. Tax authorities accept
digital filings directly. Banks issue digital statements. Clients now receive and send
documents through many channels.

**The evidence standard.** A named rule or system with a date. The illustration assumes a
digital filing requirement that took effect two years ago.

### Who it affects

Small accounting firms with 5 to 20 staff. They serve individual clients and small businesses.
They feel the change hardest in the filing season, when document chasing peaks.

### The workaround

Email, shared folders, and phone calls. The staff keep a mental list of missing items. Some
firms keep a spreadsheet. The current cost is the staff hours spent chasing, at the firm's
billing rate.

### The formless part

The request list for a filing has no structure, no status, and no owner. Each preparer keeps a
private version.

**Divide.** This is not a general document manager, and it is not a client portal. It is the
list of what is missing and who owes it.

**Name.** "Filing readiness."

**Fill.** A per-client checklist with a status, an owner, and a reminder.

### The ground

Assume 42,000 firms in the target country sit in the 5 to 20 staff band. Assume 12 percent
adopt a paid tool within three years under the central case. That gives about 5,000 firms.
Assume an annual price that is a small fraction of one staff week. The floor case assumes 2
percent adoption, which gives about 840 firms.

**The count method.** Firm counts come from a professional register. The adoption rate comes
from a test with 30 firms. Until the test runs, the adoption rate is an assumption, and it must
carry that label.

### The distribution

Three channels. Professional associations carry the offer to their members. Review sites carry
it to buyers who compare. Firms share template libraries with each other.

The association channel is the mustard model, because value accrues at a visible centre. The
template sharing is the leaven model, because it works without a dashboard. Run both, and
measure each by its own evidence. Count the association signups. Measure the template sharing
by the rate at which non-customers use a shared template.

### The demand test

- **Appetite.** The firm recovers staff hours in the filing season. The number is the hours
  saved times the billing rate.
- **Beauty.** The partner shows a clean status board to the client, and the firm looks
  organised at the moment the client is anxious.
- **Status.** The firm looks modern to clients and to its own junior staff. The last point
  matters for hiring in a trade that struggles to hire.

### The cycle

The tax year is the cycle. Sell and onboard in the off-season. Serve and support in the season.
Build the feature calendar around the same cycle, because a release in the middle of the season
is a release nobody can adopt.

### The test

Ten firms run one full filing season. The threshold: four firms finish the season without
returning to email, and three of the ten pay for the next season. The date: the end of the
season. The stopping rule: if two or fewer firms finish, stop the build and keep the manual
service.

### The smallest reversible first step

Run the checklist as a manual service for three firms. Use a spreadsheet and a shared inbox. No
software, no company, no hire. This tests the demand and the workaround before any build cost.

## Part 7. Worked example B: a local service business

**All figures in this example are invented.** They show the method. Do not read them as market
data.

### The change

E-bike sales grew for several years, and the repair network did not keep pace. Batteries,
brakes, and drivetrains need periodic service. Shops are booked out in the riding season, and
owners delay service until the bike fails.

**The evidence standard.** A named change with a physical footprint. The illustration assumes
sales data from dealers and a waiting time reported by shops.

### Who it affects

Commuters who own an e-bike inside a 20 kilometre radius, plus small fleets held by building
managers and employers. They feel the change when the bike stops working, which is usually in
the season.

### The workaround

The owner takes the bike to a shop, leaves it, and returns for it, or stops riding. Some owners
use a general courier and a shop, which splits the responsibility and delays the repair. The
current cost is the trip, the wait, and the days without the bike.

### The formless part

The pickup, repair, and return loop has no owner. Nobody keeps an intake record, so nobody can
say where a bike is or what was agreed. The loop is verbal.

**Divide.** This is not a shop, and it is not a parcel service. It is a booked service with a
pickup and a return.

**Name.** "Mobile e-bike service."

**Fill.** The loop, a scheduling rule, an intake record, and an optional spare bike.

### The ground

Assume 30,000 e-bikes inside the radius. Assume 20 percent need a paid service in a year,
which gives 6,000 jobs. Assume one mobile unit completes 8 jobs a day in season and runs 200
days, which gives 1,600 jobs in a year. The floor case assumes 5,000 bikes and a 10 percent
service rate.

**The count method.** The bike count comes from dealer sales, registration data where it
exists, and a visual count at commuter parking points. The service rate comes from a small
survey at parking points and a dealer conversation. All three sources must be labelled.

### The distribution

Employers, building managers, bike brands, and dealer overflow. The leaven model fits here,
because neighbours and colleagues pass the recommendation on. The employer channel is the
mustard model, because the employer becomes a visible hub with a contract.

Run both. Count the employer contracts. Track the referral share of all bookings.

### The demand test

- **Appetite.** The bike works, and the owner makes no trip. State the days without the bike
  that the service removes.
- **Beauty.** A clean van, a tidy handover, and a clear note of what was done. In a service
  business, the visible work is the product.
- **Status.** The owner is a commuter who is not stuck. The employer is a workplace that
  supports cycling.

### The cycle

Weather drives the cycle. Demand peaks in the riding season and falls in the cold months. Sell
pre-season service plans in the shoulder months. Use the winter for fleet contracts and
overhauls.

### The test

Thirty paid pickups in six weeks. Repeat rate above 30 percent. At least one mechanic employed
full time. The stopping rule: fewer than twelve paid jobs in six weeks ends the attempt.

### The smallest reversible first step

One van lease, one mechanic, no premises. A premises lease is the irreversible decision, so it
comes after the test. This is A32 applied to the plan.

## Part 8. Falsification

**Inference.** Write the disconfirming evidence before the build. Four questions.

1. **What evidence shows the change is not real?** Name a source and a number. A
   change in a rule has a text and a date. A change in cost has a price series.
2. **What evidence shows the workaround is good enough?** If buyers already accept the
   current cost without complaint, the offer has no room.
3. **What evidence shows the channel is closed?** Name the test. Try to sell ten units
   through the channel before the product exists.
4. **What evidence shows the ground is too small?** State the floor case, and state the
   number below which the plan fails.

**Text states (Proverbs 27:23).** "Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look
well to thy herds." The verse commands the state count. It does not command optimism.

**Text states (Numbers 13:28).** "Nevertheless the people be strong that dwell in the land".
The majority report carried true facts and a false conclusion. Separate the two in your own
report.

## Part 9. The smallest reversible first step

**Inference.** Every opportunity plan ends with a first step that is small and reversible. Six
properties define the step.

1. It tests the riskiest assumption, not the easiest one.
2. It costs little enough to abandon without a funeral.
3. It produces a number, not an impression.
4. It has a date.
5. It involves a real customer who pays something, even a small amount.
6. It does not create an obligation that outlives the test.

**Text states (Isaiah 28:16).** "he that believeth shall not make haste." Speed is for
reversible decisions, and slowness is for the ones that cannot be undone. This is A32.

**The failure mode.** The first step is a website, a logo, and a business plan. None of the
three tests the assumption, and all three take time.

## Part 10. Filling the output format

**Inference.** The output format from the skill file, with the content of each line.

1. **The change.** One sentence. The constraint that moved, and the date.
2. **The affected group.** Named, reachable, and sized. Include the workaround and its cost.
3. **The formless part.** The boundary, the name, and the contents.
4. **The ground.** The three layers, the floor case, and the source of each number.
5. **The distribution.** The channel, the owner, the cost per customer, and the growth model.
6. **The demand check.** All three channels, with an answer for each.
7. **The cycle position.** The driver, the measure, and the countercyclical action.
8. **The test.** The threshold, the date, and the stopping rule.
9. **The first step.** Small, reversible, and aimed at the riskiest assumption.
10. **Confidence.** Each claim labelled. Name the observation that changes the answer.

**A note on honesty.** If a number is an assumption, write "assumption" next to it. If a claim
rests on a report you did not read, write "reported, unverified". The reader needs the labels
more than the reader needs the confidence.
