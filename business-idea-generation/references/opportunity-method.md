# The Opportunity Method

This file gives the full procedure to find and test a business opportunity.

This file is a synthesis. The Bible supplies cases. It does not supply a method for market
research. Every step below is an inference from the passages that each step names. The two
worked examples use invented figures. They show the arithmetic of the method. They are not
market evidence.

## Method and labels

The file uses four labels: "Text states:", "Lexical:", "Inference:", and "Contested:". Verses
come from `../scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts`. Strong's definitions come from
`../scripture-foundations/scripts/lexicon.ts`. The file copies them verbatim.

Governing passages:

- 1 Chronicles 12:32, the two capacities
- Luke 12:54-56 and Matthew 16:2-3, the reading and the decision
- John 4:35, the harvest is now
- Acts 17:22-31, the model entry
- Genesis 2:19-20, the gap appears when you do the work
- Genesis 41 and 47, the cycle
- Ecclesiastes 11:1-6, many bets
- Nehemiah 2:11-16 and Numbers 13, reconnaissance
- Matthew 13:3-9 and 13:31-33, distribution
- Ecclesiastes 1:9-10, recombination
- Genesis 1:2-5, formlessness
- Proverbs 27:23 and 22:29, evidence and diligence
- 1 Samuel 17, experience
- Genesis 3:6, the demand test

An ID that starts with "A" refers to an axiom in
`../business-first-principles/references/axioms.md`. The file uses these axioms most:

- A5. Formation precedes filling
- A19. Doing the work reveals the need
- A20. Abundance precedes restriction; the boundary is few and stated in advance
- A24. Desire runs on three channels

This file uses two different words for two different things. A "desire channel" is one of the
three kinds of desire in A24. A "sales channel" is a route that carries the offer to the buyer.

## Part 1. The seven steps

Each step gives the question, the evidence standard, the failure mode, and the label.

### Step 1. Name the change

The question: What changed in the world?

Text states (John 4:35): "Say not ye, There are yet four months, and then cometh harvest?
behold, I say unto you, Lift up your eyes, and look on the fields; for they are white already to
harvest."

Inference: Verse 36 makes the harvest one of souls. The reaper "gathereth fruit unto life
eternal." This file uses the verse for markets only as an analogy.

The evidence standard: A change in a constraint, with a date. The usable categories are cost,
time, distance, rule, and tooling. A change in taste is weaker evidence and needs more support.
Taste can reverse, and it leaves no written record.

The failure mode: You name a mood as a change. "People want authenticity now" is not a change.
"A rule took effect in March 2026 and requires a digital filing for this group" is a change.

Inference: This step is the reading half of 1 Chronicles 12:32. It is not the decision half.

### Step 2. Name who it affects

The question: Who feels this change first, and who feels it most?

Text states (Acts 17:23): "For as I passed by, and beheld your devotions, I found an altar
with this inscription, TO THE UNKNOWN GOD."

The evidence standard: A named group that you can reach. The group needs a location, a way to
contact it, and a reason to care this quarter.

The failure mode: You name "everyone" or "small businesses". A group that large has no shared
workaround. Your message then has no shared content.

Inference: The observation in Acts is specific. Paul names an altar and an inscription. He
does not name a demographic.

### Step 3. Name the workaround

The question: What do these people do today, and what do they pay for it?

Text states (Genesis 2:20): "And Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the
air, and to every beast of the field; but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him."

The evidence standard: A named practice that already exists. Examples are the spreadsheet,
the group chat, the hired relative, the paid substitute, and the task that nobody does.

The failure mode: You assume that the current method is nothing. If the workaround is
"nothing", the need is usually weak. Nobody pays for a problem that nobody tries to solve.

Inference: The workaround is the real competitor. A new offer replaces it. The offer must beat
the workaround on cost, time, or standing.

### Step 4. Name the formless part

The question: Which part of this market has no structure and no contents?

Text states (Genesis 1:2, 1:4-5): "And the earth was without form, and void". Then "God
divided the light from the darkness." Then "God called the light Day, and the darkness he
called Night."

Inference: The order in Genesis 1 is divide, then name, then fill. The earth starts without
form. God divides (1:4), then names (1:5), and on later days fills what he formed. The
business-idea-generation skill file states the same order.

The evidence standard: A part with no boundary and no name. Look for these signs:

- Customers describe the area with different words.
- Nobody owns the record.
- The handoffs are verbal.

The failure mode: You fill before you form. This breaks A5 (Formation precedes filling).
Features arrive before the container exists. The result is clutter that nobody can evaluate.

Inference: The three moves are divide, name, and fill. A6 (Distinction precedes order) covers
the first move. A7 (Naming is an act of authority) covers the second.

### Step 5. Size the ground

The question: How many people are in the group, and what do they already spend?

Text states (Matthew 13:8): "But other fell into good ground, and brought forth fruit,
some an hundredfold, some sixtyfold, some thirtyfold."

The evidence standard: A counted number with a stated method, an error range, and a floor
case. Count the installed base, the transaction volume, or the annual spend. Show the
arithmetic.

The failure mode: You use a number from a report that gives no method. A top-down percentage
of a large figure is a guess. It only looks like a measurement.

Inference: The same seed returns nothing or a hundredfold, and the ground decides which.
Segment size and segment fit matter more than the quality of the offer.

### Step 6. Name the distribution

The question: How does the offer reach the group, and what does each new customer cost?

Text states (Matthew 13:31-33): The mustard seed becomes a tree, "so that the birds of the
air come and lodge in the branches thereof." The leaven is hidden in meal, "till the whole
was leavened."

Inference: The parables give two growth models. The mustard model is visible and central.
The leaven model is hidden.

The evidence standard: A named sales channel, a named owner, and a cost per new customer that
the price can carry. Test the sales channel before you build the product.

The failure mode: You build the product and leave the sales channel to luck. The best product
in a sales channel that you cannot reach is an expensive hobby.

Inference: Know which model you run. A mustard business measures the size of the tree. A
leaven business has no useful dashboard while the leaven works.

### Step 7. State the test

The question: What result, at what date, ends the attempt?

Text states (Luke 14:28): "For which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down
first, and counteth the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it?"

The evidence standard: A threshold, a date, and a stopping rule. The threshold names a number.
The date names a day. The stopping rule names what happens when the result misses the number.

The failure mode: You set no stopping rule. A decision without a stopping rule is a commitment
to drift. It uses up the years that A30 (Mortality makes time the binding constraint) names as
the binding constraint.

Inference: Write the test before the build. A test that you write after the build is a
rationalization.

## Part 2. The conversion test

Text (Luke 12:54-56, KJV):

> 54 And he said also to the people, When ye see a cloud rise out of the west, straightway ye say, There cometh a shower; and so it is.
> 55 And when ye see the south wind blow, ye say, There will be heat; and it cometh to pass.
> 56 Ye hypocrites, ye can discern the face of the sky and of the earth; but how is it that ye do not discern this time?

Text states: Both forecasts come true: "and so it is", and "it cometh to pass."

Text states: The people "can discern the face of the sky and of the earth". They "do not
discern this time".

Inference: The charge is inconsistency, not incapacity. The people have the skill in one area
and do not use it in another.

Lexical: G2540 _kairos_ is the word behind "time" in verse 56. The entry reads "an occasion,
i.e. set or proper time". The KJV gloss list includes "opportunity" and "(convenient, due)
season". The people miss a window, not a date on a schedule.

Lexical: G4592 _semeion_ is the word behind "signs" in Matthew 16:3. The entry reads "an
indication, especially ceremonially or supernaturally". The gloss list includes "token".

### The test

Inference: A reading becomes a decision when it has answers to five questions.

| Question               | A decision has                                  | A report has                   |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 1. Who owns it?        | A named person                                  | A committee or nobody          |
| 2. What is the action? | A verb with an object                           | "We are watching this space"   |
| 3. What starts it?     | A date or a trigger                             | No start condition             |
| 4. What did you stop?  | A named reallocation of money, people, or hours | No cost, because nothing moved |
| 5. What ends it?       | A stopping rule with a number                   | No exit condition              |

Use this list to read a missing answer:

1. If answer 1 is missing, the reading is entertainment.
2. If answer 2 is missing, nobody interpreted the reading.
3. If answer 3 is missing, the decision drifts.
4. If answer 4 is missing, nobody funds the decision, so it will not happen.
5. If answer 5 is missing, nobody can evaluate the decision, and it cannot end.

A worked use: A team reports that a regulation will change the market in eighteen months.
The report is accurate. Ask the five questions. Suppose that the answers are a name, a small
change to the pricing page, a trigger date, nothing, and nothing. Then the team has a reading
and no decision. Write the missing answers, or stop calling it a plan.

## Part 3. The demand test

Text (Genesis 3:6, KJV):

> 6 And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.

Text states: The verse names three properties: "good for food", "pleasant to the eyes", and
"to be desired to make one wise".

Text states: The three properties appear in one sequence, and the act follows.

Inference: The three properties are appetite, beauty, and status. A24 (Desire runs on three
channels) states that a buyer decides each purchase, hire, and commitment on all three at once.

### The three questions

1. Appetite: What need does this remove, and what does the buyer pay for the workaround now?
   Put a number on the current cost. Include the hours of the buyer at a real rate.
2. Beauty: What does the buyer see, hold, or show? The interface, the van, the packaging, the
   invoice, and the handover all carry this answer. A product with no answer here loses to a
   worse product with one.
3. Status: What standing does the buyer gain or protect? A purchase can raise the buyer in
   front of clients, peers, or staff. It can also lower the buyer. A purchase that lowers
   standing does not happen.

The failure mode: Your offer answers two desire channels and leaves one open. The buyer takes
the offer that answers the missing desire channel, even when its other two answers are weaker.

The caution: The verse describes desire ahead of judgment. It records a fall, and it commands
nothing. The test is descriptive. The ethics of what you sell come from
`../business-first-principles/references/ethics.md`.

## Part 4. The bet portfolio

Text (Ecclesiastes 11:1-6, KJV):

> 1 Cast thy bread upon the waters: for thou shalt find it after many days.
> 2 Give a portion to seven, and also to eight; for thou knowest not what evil shall be upon the earth.
> 3 If the clouds be full of rain, they empty themselves upon the earth: and if the tree fall toward the south, or toward the north, in the place where the tree falleth, there it shall be.
> 4 He that observeth the wind shall not sow; and he that regardeth the clouds shall not reap.
> 5 As thou knowest not what is the way of the spirit, nor how the bones do grow in the womb of her that is with child: even so thou knowest not the works of God who maketh all.
> 6 In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine hand: for thou knowest not whether shall prosper, either this or that, or whether they both shall be alike good.

Text states: The instruction names more than one portion: "Give a portion to seven, and
also to eight."

Text states: The instruction names two sowings: "In the morning sow thy seed, and in the
evening withhold not thine hand."

Text states: The stated reason is ignorance. The phrase "thou knowest not" occurs four times in
three verses: once in verse 2, twice in verse 5, and once in verse 6.

Text states: The counter-case is a person who watches conditions and never acts: "He that
observeth the wind shall not sow."

Text states: The verse names three outcomes, and one is that both bets succeed: "or whether
they both shall be alike good."

### The rules, stated as inference

1. Waiting for certainty is a decision. It is a decision to have no harvest.
2. The remedy is parallel attempts, not better forecasts. The uncertainty is permanent.
3. Space the attempts, as the text spaces morning and evening. A second attempt starts on its
   own schedule. It does not depend on the first.
4. Both attempts can win. Do not assume that the attempts take sales from each other.
5. Count the cost of the portfolio. Money, attention, and hours divide across the attempts.
   The text does not name this cost, so name it yourself.
6. Do not turn a number into a formula. "Seven, and also to eight" reads as a large number and
   then more. It is not a rule for allocation, and no verse supplies a bet count.

Contested: The idiom reading in rule 6 is one reading. The tools verify the verse text. They do
not verify the idiom.

## Part 5. Sizing the ground

Text states (Matthew 13:3-8): The same seed yields a hundredfold, sixtyfold, or thirtyfold.
Three of the four grounds yield nothing that the text names.

Inference: Size the ground in three layers. Count each layer with its own method.

| Layer               | The question                        | The method                                          | The honest limit                                            |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| The total group     | How many exist?                     | A count from a register, a census, or a dealer list | Registers go out of date, so date the source                |
| The reachable group | How many can you contact this year? | A count from a list that you can call or write to   | A reachable count is smaller than the total, often by a lot |
| The paying group    | How many will pay at your price?    | A measured conversion from a small test             | Before the test, you know this number least of all          |

The floor case: State the number under pessimistic assumptions and show the arithmetic. If the
floor case supports the plan, an error in the estimate does not break the plan. If only the
ceiling case supports the plan, the plan depends on a forecast.

Inference: Use the floor case for the go decision. Use the ceiling case for the capacity plan.

What not to do: Do not multiply a large population by a small percentage and call the result a
market. The small percentage is the whole argument, and it carries no evidence.

Contested: Practitioners use different methods to size a market. Top-down and bottom-up methods
give different numbers. Neither method is reliable for a new category. This part gives a
discipline to state the estimate. It does not give a method to make the estimate accurate.

## Part 6. Worked example A: a software product

All figures in this example are invented. They show the method. Do not read them as market
data. The scenario is an illustration.

### The change

Accounting firms and their clients moved to digital document exchange. Tax authorities, such as
the IRS in the United States, accept digital filings directly. Banks issue digital statements.
Clients now receive and send documents through many routes.

The evidence standard: A named rule or system with a date. The illustration assumes a digital
filing requirement that took effect on January 1, 2024.

### Who it affects

The group is small accounting firms with 5 to 20 staff. They serve individual clients and
small businesses. They feel the change most in the filing season, when the work to chase
documents peaks.

### The workaround

The firms use email, shared folders, and phone calls. The staff keep a mental list of missing
items. Some firms keep a spreadsheet. The current cost is the staff hours spent to chase
documents, at the billing rate of the firm.

### The formless part

The request list for a filing has no structure, no status, and no owner. Each preparer keeps a
private version.

Divide: The product is not a general document manager, and it is not a client portal. It is
the list of what is missing and who owes it.

Name: "Filing readiness."

Fill: A checklist for each client, with a status, an owner, and a reminder.

### The ground

Assume that 42,000 firms in the United States are in the band of 5 to 20 staff. In the
central case, assume that 12 percent of them buy a paid tool within three years. That gives
about 5,000 firms. Assume an annual price that is a small fraction of the cost of one staff
week. The floor case assumes 2 percent adoption, which gives about 840 firms.

The count method: The firm counts come from a professional register. The adoption rate comes
from a test with 30 firms. Until the test runs, the adoption rate is an assumption, and it must
carry that label.

### The distribution

The plan uses three sales channels:

- Professional associations carry the offer to their members.
- Review sites carry the offer to buyers who compare products.
- Firms share template libraries with each other.

The association channel is the mustard model, because the value collects at a visible center.
The template sharing is the leaven model, because it works without a dashboard. Run both, and
measure each one by its own evidence. Count the signups that come through the associations.
Measure the template sharing by the rate at which non-customers use a shared template.

### The demand test

- Appetite: The firm gets back staff hours in the filing season. The number is the hours saved
  times the billing rate.
- Beauty: The partner shows a clean status board to the client. The firm looks organized at the
  moment when the client is anxious.
- Status: The firm looks modern to its clients and to its own junior staff. The second group
  matters for hiring, because this trade finds it hard to hire.

### The cycle

The tax year is the cycle. Sell and onboard in the off-season. Serve and support in the season.
Plan the feature calendar around the same cycle. A release in the middle of the season is a
release that nobody can adopt.

### The test

Ten firms run one full filing season. The date is the end of the season. Every result falls in
one of two bands:

- Continue: Four or more firms finish the season without a return to email, and three or more
  of the ten firms pay for the next season.
- Stop: Three or fewer firms finish, or two or fewer firms pay. Stop the build and keep the
  manual service.

### The smallest reversible first step

Run the checklist as a manual service for three firms. Use a spreadsheet and a shared inbox.
Do not build software, form a company, or hire anyone. This step tests the demand and the
workaround before you pay any build cost.

## Part 7. Worked example B: a local service business

All figures in this example are invented. They show the method. Do not read them as market
data.

### The change

E-bike sales grew for several years, and the repair network did not grow at the same rate.
Batteries, brakes, and drivetrains need periodic service. Shops are fully booked in the riding
season. Owners delay service until the bike fails.

The evidence standard: A named change that you can see and count. The illustration assumes
sales data from dealers and a waiting time that shops report.

### Who it affects

The group is commuters who own an e-bike inside a 12-mile radius. It also includes small fleets
that building managers and employers own. They feel the change when the bike stops working.
That usually happens in the riding season.

### The workaround

The owner takes the bike to a shop, leaves it, and comes back for it. Other owners stop riding.
Some owners use a general courier and a shop. This splits the responsibility and delays the
repair. The current cost is the trip, the wait, and the days without the bike.

### The formless part

The loop of pickup, repair, and return has no owner. Nobody keeps an intake record. So nobody
can say where a bike is or what the owner and the shop agreed. The loop is verbal.

Divide: The business is not a shop, and it is not a parcel service. It is a booked service with
a pickup and a return.

Name: "Mobile e-bike service."

Fill: The loop, a scheduling rule, an intake record, and an optional spare bike.

### The ground

Assume 30,000 e-bikes inside the radius. Assume that 20 percent need a paid service in a year.
That gives 6,000 jobs. Assume that one mobile unit completes 8 jobs a day in the season and
works 200 days. That gives 1,600 jobs in a year. The floor case assumes 5,000 bikes and a
service rate of 10 percent.

The count method: The bike count comes from dealer sales, from registration data where it
exists, and from a visual count at commuter parking points. The service rate comes from a
small survey at parking points and a conversation with a dealer. Label each of the three
sources.

### The distribution

The sales channels are employers, building managers, bike brands, and overflow work from
dealers. The leaven model fits here, because neighbors and colleagues pass the recommendation
on. The employer channel is the mustard model, because the employer becomes a visible hub with
a contract.

Run both. Count the employer contracts. Track the share of all bookings that come from
referrals.

### The demand test

- Appetite: The bike works, and the owner makes no trip. State the number of days without the
  bike that the service removes.
- Beauty: A clean van, a tidy handover, and a clear note of the work done. In a service
  business, the visible work is the product.
- Status: The owner is a commuter who is not stuck. The employer is a workplace that supports
  cycling.

### The cycle

Weather drives the cycle. Demand peaks in the riding season and falls in the cold months. Sell
service plans before the season, in the months between the seasons. Use the winter for fleet
contracts and overhauls.

### The test

The test runs for six weeks. The thresholds are 30 paid pickups, a repeat rate above 30
percent, and at least one mechanic employed full time. Every count of paid jobs falls in
exactly one band:

- Continue: 30 or more paid jobs, and the other two thresholds hold.
- Extend: 12 to 29 paid jobs, or 30 or more paid jobs with another threshold missed. Run one
  more six-week test with the same thresholds. If that test does not reach the Continue band,
  end the attempt.
- Stop: 11 or fewer paid jobs. End the attempt.

### The smallest reversible first step

Lease one van, employ one mechanic, and rent no premises. A lease on premises is the
irreversible decision, so it comes after the test. This applies A32 (Some states must not be
allowed to become permanent) to the plan.

## Part 8. Falsification

Inference: Write the evidence that will disprove the plan before you build. Answer four
questions.

1. What evidence shows that the change is not real? Name a source and a number. A change in a
   rule has a text and a date. A change in cost has a price series.
2. What evidence shows that the workaround is good enough? If buyers accept the current cost
   without complaint, the offer has no room.
3. What evidence shows that the sales channel is closed? Name the test. Try to sell ten units
   through the sales channel before the product exists.
4. What evidence shows that the ground is too small? State the floor case. State the number
   below which the plan fails.

Text states (Proverbs 27:23): "Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look
well to thy herds."

Inference: The verse commands a count of the real state. It does not command optimism.

Text states (Numbers 13:28): "Nevertheless the people be strong that dwell in the land".

Inference: The majority report carried true facts and a false conclusion. Keep the two
separate in your own report.

## Part 9. The smallest reversible first step

Inference: Every opportunity plan ends with a first step that is small and reversible. The
step has six properties:

1. It tests the riskiest assumption, not the easiest one.
2. It costs so little that you can abandon it without loss of face.
3. It produces a number, not an impression.
4. It has a date.
5. It involves a real customer who pays something, even a small amount.
6. It does not create an obligation that continues after the test.

Text states (Genesis 3:22): "lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and
eat, and live for ever". God acts to stop a state from becoming permanent. This verse is the
grounding text of A32 (Some states must not be allowed to become permanent).

Inference: Move fast on reversible decisions. Move slowly on decisions that you cannot undo.
This is the business form of A32.

Text states (Isaiah 28:16): "he that believeth shall not make haste."

Inference: The verse speaks of faith in a tried foundation stone. It does not speak of business
decisions. This file uses it only as an analogy: a sure foundation removes the need to hurry.
The verse does not ground A32.

The failure mode: The first step is a website, a logo, and a business plan. None of the three
tests the assumption, and all three take time.

## Part 10. Filling the output format

Inference: The skill file gives an output format. This list gives the content of each line:

1. The change: One sentence. The constraint that moved, and the date.
2. The affected group: Named, reachable, and sized. Include the workaround and its cost.
3. The formless part: The boundary, the name, and the contents.
4. The ground: The three layers, the floor case, and the source of each number.
5. The distribution: The sales channel, the owner, the cost per customer, and the growth model.
6. The demand check: All three desire channels, with an answer for each.
7. The cycle position: The driver, the measure, and the countercyclical action.
8. The test: The threshold, the date, and the stopping rule.
9. The first step: Small, reversible, and aimed at the riskiest assumption.
10. Confidence: A label on each claim. Name the observation that will change the answer.

A note on honesty: If a number is an assumption, write "assumption" next to it. If a claim
rests on a report that you did not read, write "reported, unverified". The reader needs the
labels more than the reader needs your confidence.
