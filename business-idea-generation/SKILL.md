# Business Idea Generation

## What this skill is for

An opportunity is a change in the world that makes some work newly worth doing. This skill
finds those changes, tests them, and turns them into a decision.

Two rules govern the whole file.

First, every business application here is an inference. Genesis reports what God did. A
reading of Genesis 2:19 is the reading of a fallible person. Say "this suggests". Do not say
that the Bible commands it.

Second, the text gives no method for market research. It gives cases. The procedure in
`references/opportunity-method.md` is a synthesis, and that file labels it as one.

## How to read the claims

Every claim in this skill carries one of four labels:

- Text states: the verse says it. The file quotes the verse above the claim.
- Lexical: the Strong's entry says it. The file copies the definition from `../scripture-foundations/scripts/lexicon.ts`.
- Inference: a reader drew it. The file shows the derivation.
- Contested: faithful readers or serious practitioners disagree. The file gives the range.

Two tool limits apply to the whole file. The Strong's data has no tags at the verse level. Thus
each link from a verse to a Strong's number is a standard concordance association, not a tool
finding. Also, the tools cannot verify a market fact. Every market claim here is an inference
or an invented illustration.

## When to use it, and when not

Use it when the user asks for business ideas, wants to find a gap or a niche, asks whether an
idea is any good, reads a trend, enters a market, or positions something new.

Do not use it to choose the tools or the platform for an idea. That is `technology-selection`.
Do not use it for a business decision that is not about a new opportunity, such as capital,
hiring, or org design. That is `business-first-principles`.

## The procedure

The procedure has seven steps. Each step names the question, the evidence, and the failure:

1. Name the change. Question: what changed in the world? Evidence: a change in cost, time,
   distance, rule, or tooling, with a date. Failure: you name a mood and call it a change.
2. Name who it affects. Question: who feels this change first, and who feels it most? Evidence:
   a named group that you can reach. Failure: "everyone", which means no one.
3. Name the workaround. Question: what do these people do today? Evidence: the spreadsheet, the
   group chat, the hired relative, the paid substitute. Failure: you assume that the current
   method is nothing.
4. Name the formless part. Question: which part has no structure and no contents? Evidence: a
   boundary that nobody drew and a category that nobody named. Failure: you fill without
   forming. Axiom A5 names this order.
5. Size the ground. Question: how many people are in the group, and what do they already spend?
   Evidence: a counted number with a stated error range, plus a floor case. Failure: a number
   from a report that has no method behind it.
6. Name the distribution. Question: how does the offer reach them? Evidence: a named channel
   and its cost per acquired customer. Failure: you build the product and leave the channel to
   chance.
7. State the test. Question: what result at what date ends the attempt? Evidence: a threshold,
   a date, and a stopping rule. Failure: no stopping rule. Without one, the venture drifts with
   no end point.

Inference: every step is a plan, and the plan stays under the will of God. James 4:13 speaks to
people who say, "we will go into such a city, and continue there a year, and buy and sell, and
get gain". James 4:15 answers, "ye ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this,
or that." Proverbs 16:9 says, "A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his
steps." The test and the stopping rule do not remove this. They are a way to plan while you say
"If the Lord will".

`references/opportunity-method.md` holds the full procedure, with the standards of evidence and
two worked examples.

## Output format

When the user asks for ideas or asks whether an idea is good, answer in this order:

1. The change, and when it happened.
2. The affected group, and the workaround that it uses today.
3. The formless part, and the form that you will give it, with the boundary and the name.
4. The ground, sized, with the evidence and the floor case.
5. The distribution, and the growth model: mustard or leaven.
6. The ethics check, across appetite, beauty, and status: the real need served, the beauty
   that is true to the use, and no standing sold.
7. The position in the cycle, and the action that works against the cycle.
8. The test: the threshold, the date, and the stopping rule.
9. The smallest first step that you can reverse.
10. Confidence, with each claim labeled as stated, inferred, contested, or unverified.

## Common failure modes

| Failure                                       | Source              | The cost shows up as                                        |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| A correct reading with no decision            | 1 Chronicles 12:32  | A missed window, and the same meeting next quarter          |
| A method used in one area only                | Luke 12:54-56       | A missed structural change while demand is forecast well    |
| Waiting for the scheduled harvest             | John 4:35           | The crop spoils in the field                                |
| Entering without reading the market           | Acts 17:23          | The offer ignores what the buyer already holds              |
| Planning the need without doing the work      | Genesis 2:19-20     | The real need appears late, at a higher cost                |
| Preparing only in the good phase              | Genesis 41:53-54    | No store when the turn comes                                |
| One bet, held until certainty                 | Ecclesiastes 11:4-6 | No harvest at all                                           |
| Announcing before inspecting                  | Nehemiah 2:16       | People take positions before the facts are in               |
| A report that moves from facts to self-image  | Numbers 13:33       | The team abandons the opportunity on a projection           |
| Improving the seed and ignoring the ground    | Matthew 13:3-9      | An excellent product, and no segment                        |
| Chasing novelty                               | Ecclesiastes 1:9-10 | Rework of a thing that already exists                       |
| Filling before forming                        | Genesis 1:2         | Clutter, and features that nobody can evaluate              |
| Confidence without a count of the state       | Proverbs 27:23      | The flock is smaller than the plan assumed                  |
| Borrowed equipment                            | 1 Samuel 17:39      | A tool that the team did not prove, at the decisive moment  |
| Selling standing (the third desire channel)   | Genesis 3:5-6       | Buyers pay for pride, and the team learns to build for it   |

## The Scripture cases behind the procedure

The Scripture cases that ground each step are in `references/scripture-cases.md`. Read the section that
matches the step you are on:

- [The two capacities](references/scripture-cases.md#the-two-capacities): read when a correct reading of the times has produced no decision (1 Chronicles 12:32).
- [Reading well and acting late](references/scripture-cases.md#reading-well-and-acting-late): read when a team reads one area well and misses another. It holds the conversion test (Luke 12:54-56).
- [The harvest is now](references/scripture-cases.md#the-harvest-is-now): read when the user waits for a future window that is already open (John 4:35-36).
- [The model entry](references/scripture-cases.md#the-model-entry): read before the user enters a market, to see what the buyer already holds (Acts 17:22-28).
- [The gap appears by doing](references/scripture-cases.md#the-gap-appears-by-doing): read for step 3, when the real need shows only in the work (Genesis 2:19-20).
- [Cycles create opportunity](references/scripture-cases.md#cycles-create-opportunity): read for output line 7. It holds the steps to locate a market cycle position (Genesis 41).
- [Many bets under uncertainty](references/scripture-cases.md#many-bets-under-uncertainty): read when the user wants one bet, held until certainty (Ecclesiastes 11:1-6).
- [Reconnaissance before announcement](references/scripture-cases.md#reconnaissance-before-announcement): read before the user announces a plan. It holds the failure case of Numbers 13 (Nehemiah 2:11-16).
- [Distribution determines yield](references/scripture-cases.md#distribution-determines-yield): read for step 6 and for the growth model, mustard or leaven (Matthew 13).
- [Nothing is entirely new](references/scripture-cases.md#nothing-is-entirely-new): read when an idea looks new. It holds the steps to find what has been remade (Ecclesiastes 1:9-10).
- [The opportunity is formlessness](references/scripture-cases.md#the-opportunity-is-formlessness): read for step 4, the formless part (Genesis 1:2).
- [Evidence before enthusiasm](references/scripture-cases.md#evidence-before-enthusiasm): read for step 5, when you size the ground (Proverbs 27:23).
- [Experience as the source](references/scripture-cases.md#experience-as-the-source): read when the user asks where to start, or relies on equipment that the team did not prove (1 Samuel 17).
- [The demand test](references/scripture-cases.md#the-demand-test): read for output line 6, the ethics check on appetite, beauty, and status (Genesis 3:6).
