# Business Idea Generation

## What this skill is for

An opportunity is a change in the world that makes some work newly worth doing. This skill
finds those changes, tests them, and turns them into a decision.

Two rules govern the skill:

1. Every business application of Scripture here is a reading by a fallible person. Say "this
   suggests". Do not say that the Bible commands it.
2. Scripture gives cases, not a method for market research. The procedure is a synthesis.

Label every claim with the labels defined in `scripture-foundations`: Scripture says, Word
meaning, Reading, Disputed, Guess. The scripts cannot verify a market fact, so every market
claim is a Reading, a Guess, or an invented illustration, and you say which.

## When to use it, and when not

Use it when the user asks for business ideas, wants to find a gap or a niche, asks whether an
idea is any good, reads a trend, enters a market, or positions something new.

Do not use it to choose the tools or the platform for an idea. That is out of scope. Do not use
it for a business decision that is not about a new opportunity, such as capital, hiring, or org
design. That is `business-first-principles`.

## The procedure

Seven steps. Each names the question, the evidence, and the failure:

1. Name the change. Question: what changed in the world? Evidence: a change in cost, time,
   distance, rule, or tooling, with a date. Failure: you name a mood and call it a change.
2. Name who it affects. Question: who feels this change first, and who feels it most? Evidence:
   a named group that you can reach. Failure: "everyone", which means no one.
3. Name the workaround. Question: what do these people do today? Evidence: the spreadsheet, the
   group chat, the hired relative, the paid substitute. Failure: you assume that the current
   method is nothing.
4. Name the unstructured part. Question: which part of the problem has no structure and no
   name yet? Evidence: a boundary that nobody drew and a category that nobody named. Failure:
   you add features before you define the category and its limits (A5: give a thing its
   structure before you fill it).
5. Size the market. Question: how many people are in the group, and what do they already spend?
   Evidence: a counted number with a stated error range, plus a worst case. Failure: a number
   from a report that has no method behind it.
6. Name the distribution. Question: how does the offer reach them? Evidence: a named channel
   and its cost per acquired customer. Failure: you build the product and leave the channel to
   chance.
7. State the test. Question: what result at what date ends the attempt? Evidence: a threshold,
   a date, and a stopping rule. Failure: no stopping rule, so the venture drifts.

`references/opportunity-method.md` holds the full procedure, the standards of evidence, and two
worked examples.

## Output format

When the user asks for ideas or asks whether an idea is good, answer in this order:

1. The change, and when it happened.
2. The affected group, and the workaround that it uses today.
3. The unstructured part, and the structure you will give it: its boundary and its name.
4. The market size, with the evidence and the worst case.
5. The distribution, and the growth model: growth from one small start, or spread through a
   group that already exists.
6. The ethics check on what the buyer wants: the offer serves a real need, its beauty is true
   to the use, and it does not sell status.
7. The position in the market cycle, and the action that works against the cycle.
8. The test: the threshold, the date, and the stopping rule.
9. The smallest first step that you can reverse.
10. Confidence, with each claim labeled: Scripture says, Word meaning, Reading, Disputed, or
    Guess.

Keep the answer short when the question is small. A quick "is this idea any good?" gets a few
lines on the weakest steps, not all ten sections. The format is for real decisions, not a ritual.

## Common failure modes

| Failure                                               | Source              | The cost shows up as                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| A correct reading of the market with no decision      | 1 Chronicles 12:32  | A missed window, and the same meeting next quarter        |
| A method used in one area only                        | Luke 12:54-56       | A missed structural change while demand is forecast well  |
| Waiting for a later window that is already open       | John 4:35           | The demand goes unserved                                  |
| Entering without learning what buyers already believe | Acts 17:23          | The offer ignores what the buyer already holds            |
| Planning the need without doing the work              | Genesis 2:19-20     | The real need appears late, at a higher cost              |
| Preparing only in the good phase                      | Genesis 41:53-54    | No reserves when the downturn comes                       |
| One bet, held until certainty                         | Ecclesiastes 11:4-6 | Nothing launched at all                                   |
| Announcing before inspecting                          | Nehemiah 2:16       | People take positions before the facts are in             |
| A report that moves from facts to self-image          | Numbers 13:33       | The team abandons the opportunity on a projection         |
| Improving the product and ignoring the market         | Matthew 13:3-9      | An excellent product, and no segment                      |
| Chasing novelty                                       | Ecclesiastes 1:9-10 | Rework of a thing that already exists                     |
| Adding features before defining the category          | Genesis 1:2         | Clutter, and features that nobody can evaluate            |
| Confidence without counting what you have             | Proverbs 27:23      | The customer base is smaller than the plan assumed        |
| Relying on tools the team has not proven              | 1 Samuel 17:39      | A tool that fails at the decisive moment                  |
| Selling status                                        | Genesis 3:5-6       | Buyers pay for pride, and the team learns to build for it |
