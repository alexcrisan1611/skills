# Business Idea Generation

## What this skill is for

An opportunity is a change in the world that makes some work newly worth doing. This skill
finds those changes, tests them, and turns them into a decision.

Two rules govern the whole file.

First, every business application here is an inference. Genesis reports what God did. A
reading of Genesis 2:19 is the reading of a fallible person. Say "this suggests", not "the
Bible commands".

Second, the text supplies no market research method. It supplies cases. The procedure in
`references/opportunity-method.md` is a synthesis, and the file labels it as one.

## How to read the claims

Every claim in this skill carries one of four labels.

- **Text states** means the verse says it. The verse is quoted above the claim.
- **Lexical** means the Strong's entry says it. The definition is copied from `skills/scripture-foundations/scripts/lexicon.ts`.
- **Inference** means a reader drew it. The derivation is shown.
- **Contested** means faithful readers or serious practitioners disagree. The range is given.

Two tool limits apply throughout. The supplied Strong's data has no verse-level tagging, so a
verse to Strong's mapping is a standard concordance association and not a tool finding. The
supplied tools cannot verify any market fact. Every market claim here is either an inference
or an invented illustration.

## The two capacities

**Text (1 Chronicles 12:32, KJV)**

> And of the children of Issachar, which were men that had understanding of the times, to know
> what Israel ought to do; the heads of them were two hundred; and all their brethren were at
> their commandment.

**Text states:** The verse names two things. The first is "understanding of the times". The
second is "to know what Israel ought to do". The word "to" joins the second to the first.

**Text states:** The object of the understanding is "the times", and the noun is plural.

**Lexical:** H6256 _eth_ is the standard word behind "times". The entry reads "time,
especially (adverb with preposition) now, when, etc." The gloss list includes "season" and
"(due) season".

**Lexical:** H3045 _yada_ stands behind "know" in the concordance. The entry reads "to know
(properly, to ascertain by seeing)". The gloss list includes "be diligent", "consider",
"discern", and "perceive". See the verification log in `references/reading-the-times.md`.

**Text states:** The men are an organised body. "The heads of them were two hundred; and all
their brethren were at their commandment."

The derivation:

1. **Reading and deciding are separate capacities.** Inference. The verse names both because a
   person can hold one without the other.
2. **Both are required, and the reading comes first.** Inference. The purpose clause puts the
   reading before the knowing.
3. **The reading is a function, not a gift.** Inference. Two hundred heads carry it, and the
   rest answer to them. A capacity with a staff is a capability, not an inspiration.
4. **The object is the present moment in the plural.** Inference. The word is _eth_, the
   ordinary word for time and occasion. The reading is situational awareness, not prophecy.

The failure the verse exposes: the common failure is not a bad reading. It is a good reading
with no decision attached. Inference. The verse does not say this, because it only separates
the two functions. But once the two are separated, the failure is available. The man who reads
well and acts late has done the first half of the job.

## Reading well and acting late

**Text (Luke 12:54-56, KJV)**

> 54. And he said also to the people, When ye see a cloud rise out of the west, straightway ye
>     say, There cometh a shower; and so it is.
> 55. And when ye see the south wind blow, ye say, There will be heat; and it cometh to pass.
> 56. Ye hypocrites, ye can discern the face of the sky and of the earth; but how is it that ye
>     do not discern this time?

**Text (Matthew 16:2-3, KJV)**

> 2. He answered and said unto them, When it is evening, ye say, It will be fair weather: for
>    the sky is red.
> 3. And in the morning, It will be foul weather to day: for the sky is red and lowring. O ye
>    hypocrites, ye can discern the face of the sky; but can ye not discern the signs of the
>    times?

**Text states:** The forecasts in the passage are accurate. Luke 12:54 says "and so it is".
Luke 12:55 says "and it cometh to pass".

**Text states:** The charge is not incompetence. The charge is inconsistency. The same skill
works in one domain and is not applied in another.

**Text states:** The missed object is "this time" in Luke and "the signs of the times" in
Matthew.

**Lexical:** G2540 _kairos_ stands behind "time" in Luke 12:56. The entry reads "an occasion,
i.e. set or proper time". The gloss list includes "opportunity" and "(due) season". The failure
is a missed window rather than a late date.

**Lexical:** G4592 _semeion_ stands behind "signs" in Matthew 16:3. The entry reads "an
indication, especially ceremonially or supernaturally". The gloss list includes "token".

**Inference:** the reading capability already exists in most businesses. The problem is where
it is pointed. Sales teams forecast demand accurately and do not forecast structural change in
their own market. The test is a question: where do we already forecast well, and why is that
method not applied here?

### The conversion test

A reading of the times becomes a decision when five questions have answers. Inference.

1. Who owns the decision? A reading with no owner is a report, and reports do not act.
2. What is the action, stated as a verb? If no verb is named, no decision exists.
3. What is the trigger or the date? A decision starts on a condition or a date.
4. What did you stop doing to fund it? A decision reallocates money, people, or hours.
5. What reverses it, and what does the reversal cost? A decision carries a stopping rule.
   This connects to axiom A14 in `business-first-principles`, which states that completion is
   a real state.

If any answer is missing, the reading is still a reading. Write the missing answer or stop
calling it a plan.

## The harvest is now

**Text (John 4:35, KJV)**

> Say not ye, There are yet four months, and then cometh harvest? behold, I say unto you, Lift
> up your eyes, and look on the fields; for they are white already to harvest.

**Text states:** The passage quotes the standard schedule. "There are yet four months, and then
cometh harvest."

**Text states:** The schedule is overridden. "behold, I say unto you."

**Text states:** The correction is an act of looking. "Lift up your eyes, and look on the
fields."

**Text states:** The timing word is "already".

**Lexical:** G2326 _therismos_ stands behind "harvest". The entry reads "reaping, i.e. the
crop". The word names the crop, not only the season.

**Inference:** opportunities are usually present rather than pending. The general schedule can
be correct and the present moment different. The remedy for a wrong schedule is to look, not
to recalculate.

The business consequence: a plan that waits for the market to arrive spends the interval in
which the market is already there. The cost of being early is money. The cost of being late at
a ripe harvest is the crop.

## The model entry

**Text (Acts 17:22-31, KJV, selected)**

> 22. Then Paul stood in the midst of Mars' hill, and said, Ye men of Athens, I perceive that
>     in all things ye are too superstitious.
> 23. For as I passed by, and beheld your devotions, I found an altar with this inscription, TO
>     THE UNKNOWN GOD. Whom therefore ye ignorantly worship, him declare I unto you.
> 24. For in him we live, and move, and have our being; as certain also of your own poets have
>     said, For we are also his offspring.

**Text states:** The observation comes first. Paul says "as I passed by, and beheld your
devotions". He walks the market and reads its objects.

**Text states:** He names what he found. The finding is specific: "an altar with this
inscription, TO THE UNKNOWN GOD."

**Text states:** He quotes the market's own sources as evidence. "as certain also of your own
poets have said."

**Text states:** He starts from what the market already holds. "Whom therefore ye ignorantly
worship, him declare I unto you." The offer attaches to an existing object of attention.

**Text states:** The response is mixed. Some mock, some defer, some believe (Acts 17:32-34).
**Inference:** the text does not present the mixed outcome as a failure.

The pattern for entering a market, stated as inference:

1. Walk the market and read what it has built. Read artifacts, not demographics.
2. Name the finding in the market's own words.
3. Quote the market's own sources as evidence, not as flattery.
4. Start from a need the market already acknowledges.
5. Expect a mixed response and plan for it.

## The gap appears by doing

**Text (Genesis 2:19-20, KJV)**

> 19. And out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field, and every fowl of
>     the air; and brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam
>     called every living creature, that was the name thereof.
> 20. And Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the air, and to every beast of the
>     field; but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him.

**Text states:** The animals come to Adam so that he names them. The naming is the task.

**Text states:** Verse 20 gives the result: "but for Adam there was not found an help meet for
him."

**Text states:** The lack appears after the work. Verse 18 states that it is not good for the
man to be alone. Verse 20 records the discovery of that lack in the doing.

**Inference:** execution is an instrument of discovery, not only of delivery. The real need
often sits next to the stated one, and it appears once the work is underway. This is axiom A19
in `business-first-principles`.

The business consequence: build the adjacent thing and ship it early. Name the price before
you do. An early release costs rework, support load, and reputation if the first version is
public and weak. The text does not promise that early shipping is free.

## Cycles create opportunity

**Text (Genesis 41:29-30, 33-36, KJV, selected)**

> 29. Behold, there come seven years of great plenty throughout all the land of Egypt:
> 30. And there shall arise after them seven years of famine; and all the plenty shall be
>     forgotten in the land of Egypt; and the famine shall consume the land;
> 31. Now therefore let Pharaoh look out a man discreet and wise, and set him over the land of
>     Egypt.
> 32. Let Pharaoh do this, and let him appoint officers over the land, and take up the fifth
>     part of the land of Egypt in the seven plenteous years.
> 33. And let them gather all the food of those good years that come, and lay up corn under the
>     hand of Pharaoh, and let them keep food in the cities.
> 34. And that food shall be for store to the land against the seven years of famine, which
>     shall be in the land of Egypt, that the land perish not through the famine.

**Text (Genesis 47:15-17, 23-24, KJV, selected)**

> 15. And when money failed in the land of Egypt, and in the land of Canaan, all the Egyptians
>     came unto Joseph, and said, Give us bread: for why should we die in thy presence? for the
>     money faileth.
> 16. And Joseph said, Give your cattle; and I will give you for your cattle, if money fail.
> 17. And they brought their cattle unto Joseph: and Joseph gave them bread in exchange for
>     horses, and for the flocks, and for the cattle of the herds, and for the asses: and he fed
>     them with bread for all their cattle for that year.
> 18. Then Joseph said unto the people, Behold, I have bought you this day and your land for
>     Pharaoh: lo, here is seed for you, and ye shall sow the land.
> 19. And it shall come to pass in the increase, that ye shall give the fifth part unto
>     Pharaoh, and four parts shall be your own, for seed of the field, and for your food, and for
>     them of your households, and for food for your little ones.

**Text states:** The cycle is named and bounded in advance. Seven years of plenty, then seven
of famine.

**Text states:** The plan is countercyclical in both phases. Joseph accumulates during the
plenty and distributes during the famine.

**Text states:** The transition has no described ramp. Genesis 41:53-54 says the years of
plenteousness "were ended" and then the dearth "began to come".

**Text states:** The opportunity sits in both halves. The store is built in the first half. In
the second half the store becomes a market. Bread buys money, then cattle, then land, then seed
on a fifth-part share.

**Contested:** The text reports the famine administration and the people's response ("Thou
hast saved our lives", Genesis 47:25). The text states no verdict on the policy, and readers
differ on whether the account shows prudent stewardship or a transfer of property under
distress. Do not treat Joseph's administrative acts as a command for a business.

**Inference:** the passage supplies a shape, not a forecasting instrument. Joseph did not
detect the cycle. God disclosed it. Using the passage as a method for locating a market cycle
is a further inference, and one the tools cannot verify.

### Locating a market cycle position

Five steps, stated as inference.

1. Name the cycle and its driver. Grain and weather in the text. Credit, capacity, inventory,
   or adoption in a market.
2. Find a quantity you can measure. Use the store level, the inventory count, or the default
   rate. Do not use a mood.
3. State the countercyclical action for each phase, and price it.
4. Fix the policy in advance, because the good phase is self justifying. See axiom A20.
5. Name the error case. If the cycle does not turn, what does the policy cost?

**Contested:** the modern literature on business cycles disagrees about their regularity,
their drivers, and whether they are forecastable. Nothing here settles that dispute.

## Many bets under uncertainty

**Text (Ecclesiastes 11:1-6, KJV)**

> 1. Cast thy bread upon the waters: for thou shalt find it after many days.
> 2. Give a portion to seven, and also to eight; for thou knowest not what evil shall be upon
>    the earth.
> 3. If the clouds be full of rain, they empty themselves upon the earth: and if the tree fall
>    toward the south, or toward the north, in the place where the tree falleth, there it shall
>    be.
> 4. He that observeth the wind shall not sow; and he that regardeth the clouds shall not reap.
> 5. As thou knowest not what is the way of the spirit, nor how the bones do grow in the womb
>    of her that is with child: even so thou knowest not the works of God who maketh all.
> 6. In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine hand: for thou knowest
>    not whether shall prosper, either this or that, or whether they both shall be alike good.

**Text states:** The number is more than one. "Give a portion to seven, and also to eight".

**Text states:** The timing is two sowings. "In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening
withhold not thine hand."

**Text states:** The reason is stated three times. "thou knowest not what evil shall be upon
the earth", "thou knowest not the works of God", "thou knowest not whether shall prosper,
either this or that".

**Text states:** The counter-case is named. "He that observeth the wind shall not sow".

**Text states:** Three outcomes are available, and one of them is both bets succeeding. "or
whether they both shall be alike good."

**Inference:** the remedy for uncertainty is portfolio structure rather than better
forecasting. The uncertainty is stated as permanent.

**Inference:** "seven, and also to eight" is a large number and then more. The idiom reads as
abundance rather than an allocation formula. Do not derive a bet count from the verse.

**Inference:** the cost of parallel bets is real. Money, attention, and the founder's hours
divide across the portfolio. The text does not name that cost. Name it yourself.

> 13. And I went out by night by the gate of the valley, even before the dragon well, and to
>     the dung port, and viewed the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down, and the gates

> 14. Then I went on to the gate of the fountain, and to the king's pool: but there was no
>     place for the beast that was under me to pass.
> 15. Then went I up in the night by the brook, and viewed the wall, and turned back, and

> 16. And the rulers knew not whither I went, or what I did; neither had I as yet told it to

**Text states:** The inspection comes before the announcement. Nehemiah inspects by night and

**Text states:** The inspection is physical and specific. He sees broken walls, burned gates,

**Text states:** The finding is held. "neither had I as yet told it" (verse 16).

**Inference:** reconnaissance is private by design. A public announcement invites positions
before the facts are in.

**Inference:** the announcement that follows (Nehemiah 2:17-18) carries the finding, the call,
and the evidence. Build the report first.

### The failure case

**Text (Numbers 13:27-33, KJV, selected)**

> 27. And they told him, and said, We came unto the land whither thou sentest us, and surely it
>     floweth with milk and honey; and this is the fruit of it.
> 28. Nevertheless the people be strong that dwell in the land, and the cities are walled, and
>     very great: and moreover we saw the children of Anak there.
> 29. And Caleb stilled the people before Moses, and said, Let us go up at once, and possess
>     it; for we are well able to overcome it.
> 30. But the men that went up with him said, We be not able to go up against the people; for
>     they are stronger than we.
> 31. And there we saw the giants, the sons of Anak, which come of the giants: and we were in
>     our own sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight.

**Text states:** The majority report opens with the truth. The land flows with milk and honey,
and the fruit is in hand.

**Text states:** The pivot is one word. "Nevertheless."

**Text states:** Caleb does not dispute the facts. He disputes the conclusion.

**Text states:** The self assessment appears at the end. "we were in our own sight as
grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight."

**Inference:** the failure enters at the interpretation, not at the data collection. The facts
were gathered as briefed. The reading of the self is what moved.

**Inference:** the second clause of verse 33 is a claim about the enemy's mind. The spies had
no recorded contact with the Canaanites. The claim is a projection.

**Text states:** The cost is stated. The opportunity is lost for a generation (Numbers
14:30-34).

The business consequence: write the reconnaissance brief as binary questions, separate the
facts from the recommendation, and treat any self-assessment as a separate section. When a
report contains claims the brief did not ask for, stop and re-read the source.

## Distribution determines yield

**Text (Matthew 13:3-9, KJV)**

> 3. And he spake many things unto them in parables, saying, Behold, a sower went forth to sow;
> 4. And when he sowed, some seeds fell by the way side, and the fowls came and devoured them
>    up:
> 5. Some fell upon stony places, where they had not much earth: and forthwith they sprung up,
>    because they had no deepness of earth:
> 6. And when the sun was up, they were scorched; and because they had no root, they withered
>    away.
> 7. And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprung up, and choked them:
> 8. But other fell into good ground, and brought forth fruit, some an hundredfold, some
>    sixtyfold, some thirtyfold.
> 9. Who hath ears to hear, let him hear.

**Text (Matthew 13:31-33, KJV)**

> 31. Another parable put he forth unto them, saying, The kingdom of heaven is like to a grain
>     of mustard seed, which a man took, and sowed in his field:
> 32. Which indeed is the least of all seeds: but when it is grown, it is the greatest among
>     herbs, and becometh a tree, so that the birds of the air come and lodge in the branches
>     thereof.
> 33. Another parable spake he unto them; The kingdom of heaven is like unto leaven, which a
>     woman took, and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened.

**Text states:** The seed is constant across the four grounds. The ground is the variable.

**Text states:** The yields in the good ground vary. "some an hundredfold, some sixtyfold, some
thirtyfold."

**Text states:** One growth model is visible and central. The mustard seed becomes a tree, and
the birds come to it.

**Text states:** The other is hidden and distributed. The leaven is hidden in the meal and
works until the whole is leavened.

**Inference:** segment choice is the dominant variable in a new venture. The same effort
returns nothing or a hundredfold depending on the ground.

**Inference:** fast early growth is not evidence of good ground. The stony places sprung up
"forthwith".

**Inference:** the thorns are competing growth, not a static obstacle. Name the competitor
that grows alongside you.

**Inference:** know which growth model you run. A leaven business has no useful interim
dashboard, and a team that builds one will manage the wrong number.

## Nothing is entirely new

**Text (Ecclesiastes 1:9-10, KJV)**

> 9. The thing that hath been, it is that which shall be; and that which is done is that which
>    shall be done: and there is no new thing under the sun.
> 10. Is there any thing whereof it may be said, See, this is new? it hath been already of old
>     time, which was before us.

**Text states:** The claim is general. "there is no new thing under the sun."

**Text states:** The test question is quoted and answered. "Is there any thing whereof it may
be said, See, this is new? it hath been already of old time."

**Inference:** the context is the cycle of the created order (sun, wind, rivers in verses
5-7). The claim is about novelty as a category, not a ban on improvement.

**Inference:** most new ventures recombine existing parts. The work is to find what has been
remade.

### Finding what has been remade

Five steps, stated as inference.

1. Name the offer in plain terms, without the brand language.
2. Name the older thing that does the same job.
3. Name what changed. Look for a change in cost, channel, regulation, or tooling.
4. Find who did it before, and why they stopped or stayed small.
5. State the recombination in one sentence. If the sentence contains a part that is genuinely
   new, say which part, and why it is new now.

## The opportunity is formlessness

**Text (Genesis 1:2, 1:4-5, KJV, selected)**

> 2. And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And
>    the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.
> 3. And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.
> 4. And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the
>    morning were the first day.

**Text states:** The starting condition is "without form, and void."

**Text states:** The first acts of ordering are division and naming. Verse 4 divides. Verse 5
names.

**Lexical:** H8414 _tohu_ is the standard word behind "without form". The entry reads "a
desolation (of surface), i.e. desert; figuratively, a worthless thing; adverbially, in vain".
The gloss list includes "confusion", "empty place", "waste", and "wilderness".

**Lexical:** H922 _bohu_ is the standard word behind "void". The entry reads "a vacuity, i.e.
(superficially) an undistinguishable ruin". The gloss list gives "emptiness, void".

**Lexical:** H914 _badal_ is the standard word behind "divided". The entry reads "to divide (in
variation senses literally or figuratively, separate, distinguish, differ, select, etc.)".

**Lexical:** H7121 _qara_ is the standard word behind "called". The entry reads "to call out to
(i.e. properly, address by name, but used in a wide variety of applications)". The gloss list
includes "(give) name".

**Inference:** an opportunity is usually a formless part of a market. The method is the
creation pattern.

1. Find what is formless and empty. Look for the place with no structure and no contents.
2. Divide. State what the thing is not. Draw the boundary. This is axiom A6.
3. Name it. Give the category a name, because the name sets what people believe is true. This
   is axiom A7.
4. Fill it. Put the contents into the container you formed.

This connects to axiom A5, which states that formation precedes filling, and to axiom A2,
which states that the default state is formless and empty. The method inverts the default by
giving form, then filling.

## Experience as the source

**Text (1 Samuel 17:34-37, KJV, selected)**

> 34. And David said unto Saul, Thy servant kept his father's sheep, and there came a lion, and
>     a bear, and took a lamb out of the flock:
> 35. And I went out after him, and smote him, and delivered it out of his mouth: and when he
>     arose against me, I caught him by his beard, and smote him, and slew him.
> 36. Thy servant slew both the lion and the bear: and this uncircumcised Philistine shall be

> 37. David said moreover, The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of
>     the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine.

**Text states:** The qualification is prior work. David answers the objection with the lion and
the bear.

**Text states:** He names the analogy himself. "this uncircumcised Philistine shall be as one
of them."

**Text states:** The prior work was private. The lion and the bear were fought while he kept

**Text states:** He tests the incumbent's equipment and refuses it. 1 Samuel 17:39 says "he

**Inference:** ideas usually come from the work the founder has already performed. The
founder's lion and bear are the raw material.

**Inference:** the transfer from the small work to the large one needs an argument, not an
assumption. Name why the past work applies here, in the same market, with the same mechanism,
or with the same capability. Otherwise the confidence is unfounded.

**Inference:** the private track record is the evidence, and it is invisible. Look for it in
the unglamorous history rather than in the pitch.

## The demand test

**Text (Genesis 3:6, KJV)**

> And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes,
> and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and
> gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.

**Text states:** The verse names three properties. "good for food", "pleasant to the eyes", and
"to be desired to make one wise".

**Text states:** The three appear in one sequence, and the act follows.

**Inference:** the three properties are appetite, beauty, and status. This is axiom A24 in
`business-first-principles`. Every purchase is decided on all three at once.

The demand test, stated as inference. For the offer, answer three questions.

1. Appetite: what need does this remove or satisfy, and what does the buyer already pay for
   the workaround today?
2. Beauty: what does the buyer see, hold, or show to others? A product with no answer here
   loses to one that has an answer.
3. Status: what standing does the buyer gain or protect? A purchase that lowers standing will
   not happen, even when the first two answers are strong.

**Inference:** a missing channel is a lost sale to whoever fills it. Satisfy need and beauty
and leave status open, and the buyer takes the offer that gives standing.

**Contested and cautionary:** the verse describes a desire that ran ahead of judgment. It does
not command an appeal to appetite. The test is descriptive. The ethics of what you sell are
governed by `skills/business-first-principles/references/ethics.md`.

## The procedure

Seven steps. Each step names the question, the evidence, and the failure.

1. **Name the change.** The question: what changed in the world? Evidence: a change in cost,
   time, distance, rule, or tooling, with a date. Failure: naming a mood and calling it a
   change.
2. **Name who it affects.** The question: who feels this change first, and who feels it most?
   Evidence: a named group you can reach. Failure: "everyone", which is no one.
3. **Name the workaround.** The question: what do these people do today? Evidence: the
   spreadsheet, the group chat, the hired relative, the paid substitute. Failure: assuming the
   current method is nothing.
4. **Name the formless part.** The question: which part has no structure and no contents?
   Evidence: a boundary that is not drawn and a category that is not named. Failure: filling
   without forming, which is axiom A5.
5. **Size the ground.** The question: how many are in the group, and what do they already
   spend? Evidence: a counted number with a stated error range, plus a floor case. Failure: a
   number from a report with no method behind it.
6. **Name the distribution.** The question: how does the offer reach them? Evidence: a named
   channel and its cost per acquired customer. Failure: building the product and leaving the
   channel to chance.
7. **State the test.** The question: what result at what date ends the attempt? Evidence: a
   threshold, a date, and a stopping rule. Failure: no stopping rule, which is a commitment to
   drift.

The full procedure, with evidence standards and two worked examples, sits in
`references/opportunity-method.md`.

## Output format

When the user asks for ideas or asks whether an idea is good, answer in this shape.

1. **The change** and when it happened.
2. **The affected group** and the workaround in use today.
3. **The formless part** and the form you will give it, with the boundary and the name.
4. **The ground**, sized, with the evidence and the floor case.
5. **The distribution** and the growth model, mustard or leaven.
6. **The demand check**, across appetite, beauty, and status.
7. **The cycle position** and the countercyclical action.
8. **The test**, the threshold, the date, and the stopping rule.
9. **The smallest reversible first step**.
10. **Confidence**, with each claim labelled as stated, inferred, contested, or unverified.
