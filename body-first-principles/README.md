# Body First Principles

This skill uses the healthy human body, from the whole organism down to the cell, as a second layer of evidence for business and organizational questions, with Scripture as the final authority.

## What it does

The skill compares an organization to a body. The body has structure, supply, control, growth, repair, waste removal, and a fixed lifespan. The skill uses these working parts to find principles for a firm, a team, or a system.

The skill holds 24 principles, B1 to B24, in five groups:

1. One organism, many members (B1 to B7).
2. Control and feedback (B8 to B12).
3. Supply and flow (B13 to B16).
4. Growth and adaptation (B17 to B20).
5. Limits and mortality (B21 to B24).

Each principle gives a body fact, a principle, a business consequence, and the Scripture that governs the function. The principle and the business consequence are always marked as inferences.

The skill also lists eight places, M1 to M8, where the logic of the body runs against Scripture. Examples are self-preservation against self-sacrifice, appetite as a guide, and the instinct to hide weakness. In each case, Scripture wins and the skill says so.

## When to use it

Use this skill when a business, technical, or organizational question is about one of these topics:

- Structure, systems, feedback, or flow.
- Growth, maintenance, waste, rest, limits, or resilience.
- How an organization is like a body or a living system.

You do not have to mention the body. These questions also fit:

- Why does something keep breaking?
- Why did growth stall?
- Why is the team exhausted?
- What does a healthy system look like?

## How it works

The skill follows three rules:

1. Scripture governs the body. The body is evidence, not authority. Explicit commands of Scripture and direct descriptions of God come first. Inferences from the body come last and never override them.
2. The analogy has a limit. A body cannot choose its members, change its species, or be dissolved by agreement. A firm can do all three. Where the analogy breaks, the skill says that it breaks.
3. The skill does not give health advice. If you ask about your own health, the skill tells you to speak to a qualified physician.

For each question, the skill uses six steps:

1. Name the function in plain terms.
2. Find the body system that performs the function, and state the body fact.
3. Find the Scripture that governs the function, and read it in context.
4. Compare the two. If they conflict, follow Scripture and name the conflict.
5. Derive the practice for the actual situation.
6. Name the failure mode and the early warning sign.

If the Scripture is not clear on a conflict, the skill says so. It does not use the body to settle the question.

The answer has seven parts: the function, the body fact, the governing Scripture, where the analogy holds and where it breaks, the practice, the warning sign, and a confidence note. The confidence note marks what the text states and what is inferred.

## What is in this folder

- `SKILL.md`: The skill itself. It holds the three rules, the principles B1 to B24, the conflicts M1 to M8, the procedure, and the output format.
- `references/body-systems.md`: The body facts behind each principle, the limit of each analogy, and a closing section on why a firm is not an organism. Each claim carries a label: Body fact, Text states, Lexical, Inference, Contested, or Speculation.
- `references/body-scripture-map.md`: A table that maps each body fact to the Scripture that governs it, with longer notes on the rows that carry the most weight.
- `references/where-the-body-misleads.md`: The eight conflicts M1 to M8 in full. Each one gives the instinct, its effect on a firm, the Scripture that overrides it, the counter-practice, and the case where the instinct is most likely to win.

The reference files take all Scripture quotes from the KJV search tool in the `scripture-foundations` skill (`scripts/scripture.ts`), not from memory.

## Limits

- The skill is not medical guidance. For a health question, speak to a qualified physician.
- The skill reasons by analogy. A firm is an agreement among persons, not an organism. The analogy describes coordination, supply, feedback, growth, and decay. It does not describe what a person is worth.
- The body is not a moral guide. Where the body and Scripture disagree, the skill follows Scripture.
- Some points are open. For example, the skill marks the question of whether aging is part of the design or a result of the curse as contested. The body cannot settle it.
