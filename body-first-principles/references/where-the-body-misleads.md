# Where the Body Misleads

Reference file for the `body-first-principles` skill.

This file expands the eight conflicts in section VI of the skill file. Each one has the
same shape.

1. The body's instinct, stated as a body fact.
2. What the instinct produces when a firm follows it without correction.
3. The Scripture that overrides the instinct.
4. The counter-practice that follows.
5. The business case where the instinct is most likely to win.

All Scripture quotes come from `../scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts`. The
interpretation rules come from the `scripture-foundations` skill, and they bind this file.
The KJV is 1611 English, so this file checks the words that carry an argument.

Plain-text labels mark the status of each claim:

- "Body fact" marks physiology.
- "Text states" marks what Scripture says.
- "Lexical" marks a claim about the Hebrew or Greek.
- "Inference" marks reasoning that goes past the body fact and the text.
- "Contested" marks a disputed point.

This file does not give health advice.

## M1. Self preservation against self sacrifice

The body's instinct: The body protects itself first. A threat triggers a stress
response that redirects blood flow, raises glucose, and narrows attention to the danger.
(Body fact) Withdrawal from harm is a reflex, and the reflex runs before any
deliberation. (Body fact) Deliberation arrives after the body has already moved.

What it produces if followed: A firm that follows the instinct will not accept a loss
for the sake of another. Every obligation becomes conditional on the firm's own survival.
Cash gets hoarded, the supplier in difficulty gets dropped, the staff get cut first, and
the long-term commitment gets postponed. (Inference) The end state is a firm whose
mission has quietly become survival. (Inference) Survival is a condition of the
mission, and it is not the mission. (Inference)

The Scripture that overrides it: "Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay
down his life for his friends." (John 15:13) (Text states) "Look not every man on his
own things, but every man also on the things of others." (Philippians 2:4) (Text
states) The passage around that verse supplies the pattern rather than a sentiment. "Let
nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem
other better than themselves." (Philippians 2:3) (Text states) The example that follows
is not a survival strategy. "But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form
of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: And being found in fashion as a man, he
humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross."
(Philippians 2:7-8) (Text states) The text does not abolish self preservation. "For no man
ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church:"
(Ephesians 5:29) (Text states) "A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the
simple pass on, and are punished." (Proverbs 22:3) (Text states) Scripture subordinates self
preservation to love. It does not abolish it. Self preservation is a good that a person can
give up for a higher one. It is not the highest good. (Inference)

The counter-practice: Write the limits before the crisis arrives. Name in advance what
the firm will not do to survive, and who has the authority to invoke the limit.
(Inference) Practice the small surrenders while the firm is strong, because the habit
is what shows up under pressure. (Inference) When the large choice arrives, the
decision will be made by the person the firm has already become. (Inference)

Where the instinct wins: A liquidity crisis with a payroll date. The threat is
immediate, the sacrifice is dated, and the person deciding is often the person who bears
the loss. (Inference) The instinct also wins in negotiation, where the counterparty's
need is visible and the firm's own need is felt. (Inference)

## M2. Strength as the measure of worth

The body's instinct: Bodies read strength, health, and vigor, and people attend to
those signals in one another. (Body fact) The signals carry real information about
capability in work that requires the body. (Body fact) The weight of biology against
culture in the ranking that follows is disputed. (Contested)

What it produces if followed: Hiring by energy, promotion by presence, and exclusion
of the disabled, the older, the ill, and the quiet. (Inference) A preference gets
relabeled as a standard, and the label hides the move. (Inference) The firm loses the
people whose contribution does not show up in a first impression, which is where the
maintenance and reliability functions usually live. (Inference)

The Scripture that overrides it: The weaker members are the necessary ones. "Nay,
much more those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are necessary: And
those members of the body, which we think to be less honourable, upon these we bestow more
abundant honour; and our uncomely parts have more abundant comeliness. For our comely
parts have no need: but God hath tempered the body together, having given more abundant
honour to that part which lacked:" (1 Corinthians 12:22-24) (Text states) The standard
for service to the weak is the standard for service to Christ himself. "And the King shall
answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of
the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me." (Matthew 25:40) (Text states)
Note what the text does not say. It does not call strength evil. It denies that strength is
the measure of worth. (Inference)

The counter-practice: State the requirements of a role in terms of the work and not in
terms of the person. (Inference) Test for the work itself, and check who never gets
asked. (Inference) Examine the criteria that carry no test, such as presence,
polish, and confidence, because those are the criteria that hide preference. (Inference)

Where the instinct wins: An early-stage firm with no track record and no money, where
the whole evaluation rests on an impression in a room. (Inference) It also wins in
promotion rounds, where "executive presence" enters the criteria and leaves no trace.
(Inference)

## M3. Competition for resources

The body's instinct: Cells in a developing tissue compete for survival factors, and
less fit cells get eliminated. The process is documented and it is a normal part of
development. (Body fact. Contested in the details of the mechanism) Competition for
nutrients and signals is built into how tissue is assembled.

What it produces if followed: Budgets become contests, and teams hoard information as
a weapon. Each team optimizes its own metric at the expense of the whole.
(Inference) The contest can turn into predation, where the strongest unit consumes the
others and the firm celebrates the winner. (Inference) A firm that runs on internal
competition will produce internal competitors, and it will get exactly that. (Inference)

The Scripture that overrides it: "But so shall it not be among you: but whosoever will
be great among you, shall be your minister: And whosoever of you will be the chiefest,
shall be servant of all. For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to
minister, and to give his life a ransom for many." (Mark 10:43-45) (Text states) The
text does not abolish rank. It defines greatness as service, which reverses the measure
that competition produces. (Inference) The warning about internal division belongs
beside it. "And Jesus knew their thoughts, and said unto them, Every kingdom divided
against itself is brought to desolation; and every city or house divided against itself
shall not stand:" (Matthew 12:25) (Text states)

The counter-practice: Fund the work from the mission, not from the contest. Make the internal
exchange explicit, so that a transfer between teams is a decision and not a raid. (Inference) Reward the unit that gives up a resource for a better use.
(Inference) Measure the whole, and report the whole, because a metric that only ever
measures a part will produce competition for that part. (Inference)

Where the instinct wins: The annual planning cycle, where each unit writes its own
case and the totals must fit a number. (Inference) It also wins wherever a forced
ranking distributes a fixed pool of reward, because the system pays for the contest
directly. (Inference)

## M4. Appetite as a guide

The body's instinct: Hunger, thirst, craving, and desire drive behavior, and they
carry real information about need. (Body fact) They do not carry authority over what
is good. An appetite reports a state, and it does not rank the options. (Inference)
The appetites also respond to what is in front of them rather than to what is good over
time. (Body fact)

What it produces if followed: A firm that treats demand as a mandate will build
whatever sells. The customer's appetite becomes the roadmap, and the roadmap has no
standard in it. (Inference) The end state is a product that is wanted and harmful, and
a team that has no language for the difference. (Inference)

The Scripture that overrides it: Genesis 3:6 shows desire running ahead of judgment.
"And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the
eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did
eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat." (Genesis 3:6) (Text
states) The verse stacks three appeals and no evaluation. (Inference) Proverbs warns
about the ruler's table in the same terms. "When thou sittest to eat with a ruler, consider
diligently what is before thee: And put a knife to thy throat, if thou be a man given to
appetite. Be not desirous of his dainties: for they are deceitful meat." (Proverbs 23:1-3)
(Text states) The word "meat" there is 1611 English for food of any kind, so the
warning covers a table and not only a plate. (Lexical)

The counter-practice: Separate the demand question from the good question, and ask
them in that order. (Inference) Ask whether the product is good for the buyer before
asking whether the buyer wants it. (Inference) Name the appetite the product feeds,
because an unnamed appetite cannot be judged. (Inference)

Where the instinct wins: Product decisions under revenue pressure, where the shortfall
is real and the harm is distant. (Inference) It wins most completely in a business
where the attention of the user is the product. There, the appetite is the supply and the
measure at the same time. (Inference)

## M5. The instinct to hide weakness

The body's instinct: A body conceals injury. Animals hide a limp from predators, and
the concealment is protective. (Body fact) The body also hides the injury from its own
owner in some cases, because pain can be suppressed under stress. (Body fact)

What it produces if followed: Bad news travels slowly and arrives distorted. The
customer finds the defect before the firm does. The person who knows about the problem
decides that reporting it costs more than carrying it. (Inference) The concealment
compounds, because the fix gets more expensive with every week it stays hidden.
(Inference)

The Scripture that overrides it: "He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso
confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy." (Proverbs 28:13) (Text states) Two more
passages speak of light and confession. "But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light,
we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us
from all sin." (1 John 1:7) (Text states) "Confess your faults one to another, and pray one
for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth
much." (James 5:16) (Text states) These two verses are about fellowship, cleansing, and the
confession of faults. Their use for the reporting of problems in a firm is an extension.
(Inference)
Genesis shows the pattern and its cost. "And he said, I heard thy voice in the garden, and
I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself." (Genesis 3:10) (Text states) Note
the order in that verse. Fear comes first, and the hiding follows. (Inference)

The counter-practice: Build the reporting channel before the incident that needs it.
(Inference) Give the first person who reports bad news a good experience, because the
second reporter is watching. (Inference) Separate the report from the consequence, and
say plainly which failures are learning and which are not. (Inference)

Where the instinct wins: Any firm where the numbers determine the standing of the
person who reports the numbers. (Inference) It wins in quarterly reporting, in project
status meetings near a deadline, and under any leader with a history of punishing the
messenger. (Inference)

## M6. Pain avoidance

The body's instinct: The body withdraws from pain. The withdrawal reflex pulls a hand
off a hot surface before the brain registers the heat. (Body fact) The avoidance is
protective, and it is not a defect in itself. (Body fact)

What it produces if followed: Every difficulty gets optimized away. The hard
conversation does not happen, the audit gets deferred, the rewrite gets postponed, and the
debt gets refinanced instead of repaid. (Inference) The firm becomes skilled at
removing discomfort and unskilled at removing causes. (Inference)

The Scripture that overrides it: "Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be
joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of
righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby." (Hebrews 12:11) (Text states)
The verse names the immediate cost and the later yield in one sentence. It ties the
yield to the one who underwent the training. (Inference) The same pattern appears in
James. "My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this,
that the trying of your faith worketh patience." (James 1:2-3) (Text states)

Note the difference between this conflict and B9. B9 says the alarm carries information
and must not be silenced. M6 says the alarm does not order a retreat. (Inference) The
two rules work together. Keep the sensor, and read the signal rather than obeying it.
(Inference)

The counter-practice: Distinguish the difficulty that is the mechanism from the
difficulty that is only waste. (Inference) Name the hardships the firm accepts as
training, and the ones it removes as debris. (Inference) Then spend the money on the
debris and not on the training. (Inference)

Where the instinct wins: Wherever a leader can pay to remove a discomfort without
removing its cause. (Inference) It wins in performance conversations, in exit
decisions, and in any review where the reviewer and the reviewed will work together again
next week. (Inference)

## M7. Short horizons

The body's instinct: Hunger and thirst return within hours, and they do not wait for a
better plan. (Body fact) The body also plans ahead in a limited way. It stores fuel as
glycogen and fat, and it runs cycles that last weeks and years. (Body fact) The appetites
still pull toward the near term. (Inference)
People discount future rewards, and the discount is steep over short horizons. (Body
fact) The neural account of that discounting is an active research area. (Contested)

What it produces if followed: Quarterly thinking. Deferred maintenance. The asset that
produces the long yield gets sold for the quarter that needs a number. (Inference)
Training gets cut first, because its return lands after the person who cut it has moved.
(Inference)

The Scripture that overrides it: The command is generational. "Hear, O Israel: The
LORD our God is one LORD: And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and
with all thy soul, and with all thy might. And these words, which I command thee this day,
shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt
talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when
thou liest down, and when thou risest up. And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine
hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes. And thou shalt write them upon the
posts of thy house, and on thy gates." (Deuteronomy 6:4-9) (Text states) Psalm 78
states the purpose of the transfer. "We will not hide them from their children, shewing to
the generation to come the praises of the LORD, and his strength, and his wonderful works
that he hath done. For he established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel,
which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children: That
the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should
arise and declare them to their children: That they might set their hope in God, and not
forget the works of God, but keep his commandments:" (Psalms 78:4-7) (Text states) The
horizon in both passages runs past the life of the person receiving the command.
(Inference)

The counter-practice: Write the long horizon down and put it in the decision record,
because an unwritten horizon loses to a written number. (Inference) Give the long
horizon a name, a budget, and a dated review, so that it has an owner and a calendar.
(Inference) Teach the next generation on purpose, since the transfer is the mechanism
the text names. (Inference)

Where the instinct wins: Any bonus period, and any fund with a fixed life.
(Inference) It wins whenever the person making the decision will not be present at the
outcome, because the cost lands on someone else. (Inference)

## M8. Adaptation to the wrong thing

The body's instinct: A body adapts to whatever load it is given, good or bad. Bone
remodels around the posture it is held in, and repetitive strain produces an adaptation to
the strain. (Body fact) The body has no mechanism for judging whether the load is
worth adapting to. (Body fact)

What it produces if followed: Teams adapt to dysfunction and then call it culture.
(Inference) The workaround becomes the process, the exception becomes the rule, and the
newcomer is taught the adaptation as if it were the design. (Inference) The firm
mistakes its own history for a standard, and the adaptation gets defended because it was
survived. (Inference)

The Scripture that overrides it: "And be not conformed to this world: but be ye
transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and
acceptable, and perfect, will of God." (Romans 12:2) (Text states) The verse sets
conformity against transformation, and it locates the change in the mind. (Inference)
It also supplies a test. The renewed mind proves what the will of God is. (Inference)
Adaptation without a test produces conformity by default. (Inference)

The counter-practice: Measure what the team has adapted to, and name the adaptation
out loud. (Inference) Ask whether the adaptation serves the mission, and answer the
question with evidence rather than with tenure. (Inference) Change the load, then let
the team adapt again, because adaptation follows the load and not the announcement.
(Inference)

Where the instinct wins: Any long-tenured team with strong informal norms and no
external reference point. (Inference) It wins hardest after a crisis, when whatever got
the firm through becomes the template, and the template then outlives the crisis.
(Inference)

## Why the body's logic is fallen and not merely limited

The body is not evil. The created order is good, and the text says so before the fall.
"And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening
and the morning were the sixth day." (Genesis 1:31) (Text states) The body is part of
that verdict. Its design is not a mistake and not a punishment in itself. (Inference)

The fall changed the operating conditions of every system, and the text names the change
in physical terms. "And unto Adam he said, Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of
thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree, of which I commanded thee, saying, Thou shalt not eat
of it: cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of
thy life; Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the
herb of the field; In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the
ground; for out of it wast thou taken: for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou
return." (Genesis 3:17-19) (Text states) The ground now resists, work now costs, and
the body now returns to dust. (Text states)

Romans extends the damage past the human body to the whole creation. "For the creature was
made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by reason of him who hath subjected the same in
hope, Because the creature itself also shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption
into the glorious liberty of the children of God. For we know that the whole creation
groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now." (Romans 8:20-22) (Text states)
Three things in that passage matter here. The subjection was not willing. It came with
hope attached. And the present state is called a bondage, which means it is not the
intended permanent condition. (Inference)

That is what makes the body's logic fallen rather than merely limited. A limit is a
boundary of a good design. A fallen logic is a good design operating against its own
purpose. (Inference) The distinction has two sides, and both must be kept.

Limits that are by design: The body is creaturely and dependent. It is formed from
dust, and God remembers that. "For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust."
(Psalms 103:14) (Text states) Dependence on other members is by design, and B2 states
it. The need for rest is by design, and Genesis 2:2-3 states the pattern. The need for the
other person is by design, and Genesis 2:18 calls solitude not good. Mortality as
creaturely finitude belongs on this side, and B21 treats it as part of creaturely life.
(Inference)

Distortions that came with the fall: Fear driving concealment belongs on this side, and
Genesis 3:10 gives the sequence. "And he said, I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was
afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself." (Genesis 3:10) (Text states) Blame
follows immediately. "And the man said, The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave
me of the tree, and I did eat." (Genesis 3:12) (Text states) The ground resisting work
belongs here. Corruption and death as an enemy belong here. "Wherefore, as by one man sin
entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all
have sinned:" (Romans 5:12) (Text states) The end of the story is the removal of that
enemy. "And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more
death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former
things are passed away." (Revelation 21:4) (Text states)

One point in the distinction is genuinely contested, and honesty requires naming it.
Mortality sits on both sides at once. The body is creaturely and finite by design. Scripture
also treats death as the penalty of sin and as an enemy that God will destroy. (Contested)
Genesis 2:17 and Romans 5:12 tie death to sin, and Genesis 3:19 is part of the curse. The
skill file treats finitude as creaturely in B21. Romans 8 and Revelation 21 treat death as a
condition that will end. Both readings have textual support,
and the body itself cannot settle the question. (Contested) What the body shows is
that aging and death are normal processes in its design. What the text adds is that the
present state is a bondage under hope, and not the final state. (Inference)

Three consequences follow for the use of this skill.

1. The body remains evidence of design. Its systems show what coordination, supply,
   feedback, and rest look like when they work. (Inference)
2. The body's instincts are not a moral guide. They are signals from a system whose
   operating logic now runs against its purpose in specific, nameable ways. (Inference)
3. The body's instincts still carry information. The instinct is not noise. It is a true
   report of a state, and it becomes an error when it is treated as a rule. (Inference)

The rule from the skill file settles the procedure. Explicit commands of Scripture govern.
Direct descriptions of God's character and action govern. Inferences from the body inform
and illustrate. When the body suggests one thing and Scripture says another, follow
Scripture and name the conflict. (Inference)
