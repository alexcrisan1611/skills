# Body Systems

Reference file for the `body-first-principles` skill.

This file holds the body facts behind B1 to B24, from the whole organism down to the cell.
It gives the fact, the analogy it supports, and the limit of that analogy.

Three rules govern the file.

1. The body is evidence, not authority. Scripture governs. The file
   `references/where-the-body-misleads.md` holds the eight places where the body's instinct
   runs against the text.
2. This file reasons about organizations by analogy. It is not medical guidance. A reader
   with a health question must speak to a qualified physician.
3. Every substantive claim carries a label. **Body fact** marks physiology a reader can
   check in a standard text. **Text states** marks what Scripture says. **Lexical** marks a
   claim about the Hebrew or Greek. **Inference** marks reasoning that goes past either
   one. **Contested** marks a point where the evidence or the mechanism is in dispute.

Numeric figures are approximate. Where a figure is a range, the range is given. Where a
figure is disputed, the dispute is named.

Scripture is quoted from `skills/scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts`. The
interpretation rules come from the `scripture-foundations` skill, and they bind this file.

## I. One organism, many members

### B1. One body, tens of trillions of cells

The body is one organism made of many cells. Estimates of the human cell count run from
roughly 20 trillion to roughly 40 trillion. A common working figure is about 37 trillion.
**(Body fact, approximate)** The count depends on what is counted. Red blood cells alone
make up a large share of the total, and they carry no nucleus. **(Body fact)** The number
of distinct cell types is commonly given as more than 200. **(Body fact, approximate)**

Nearly every cell carries the same genome. Real exceptions exist. Red blood cells lose
their nucleus as they mature, and immune cells rearrange their receptor genes on purpose.
**(Body fact)**

The unity is not a slogan. It is enforced. A body cell that stops obeying the controls on
division and place becomes a cancer cell, and the body treats it as a threat. **(Body
fact)** The one interest of the organism is the condition under which every member lives.
**(Inference)**

Scripture states the structure directly. "For as the body is one, and hath many members,
and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body: so also is Christ."
(1 Corinthians 12:12) **(Text states)** The next two verses press the point. "For the body
is not one member, but many." (1 Corinthians 12:14) **(Text states)**

The psalmist states the value of the individual member from the other direction. "I will
praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my
soul knoweth right well." (Psalms 139:14) **(Text states)**

The principle is unity of purpose with diversity of function. **(Inference)**

### B2. Dependence between organs: the brain and interrupted flow

No organ is self-sufficient. The brain needs oxygen and glucose delivered by the blood.
The heart muscle needs its own coronary arteries. The liver makes the proteins that carry
material through the blood. The kidneys clear the waste that the rest of the body
produces. **(Body fact)**

The brain is the sharp example, because its tolerance for interrupted flow is short and
measurable. Loss of consciousness follows within about 10 seconds after blood flow to the
brain stops. Permanent damage begins after roughly 4 to 6 minutes at normal body
temperature. **(Body fact, approximate)** The exact thresholds move with temperature,
blood sugar, and the age of the person, so the figures describe a typical case and not a
fixed rule. **(Contested in detail)**

The dependency is not a defect. It is the design. A system with independent organs and no
shared supply is not a stronger body. It is a collection of parts. **(Inference)**

Scripture names the dependency as a rebuke to contempt. "And the eye cannot say unto the
hand, I have no need of thee: nor again the head to the feet, I have no need of you."
(1 Corinthians 12:21) **(Text states)** Romans repeats the structure. "For as we have many
members in one body, and all members have not the same office: So we, being many, are one
body in Christ, and every one members one of another." (Romans 12:4-5) **(Text states)**

### B3. Criticality against visibility: the parathyroid and the capillary bed

Some of the most important parts of the body are the least visible.

The parathyroid glands sit behind the thyroid gland in the neck. A person normally has
four. Each is about the size of a grain of rice. **(Body fact, approximate)** They control
the level of calcium in the blood. Their failure causes calcium to fall, and a severe fall
produces muscle spasm and can kill. **(Body fact)** Removal of the glands without
replacement is a recognized cause of that outcome. **(Body fact)**

The capillary bed is smaller still. A capillary is the smallest blood vessel, with a
diameter of roughly 5 to 10 micrometers. A human hair is roughly 70 micrometers across,
so a capillary is far thinner than a hair. **(Body fact, approximate)** A blockage in one
capillary starves the tissue it feeds. The tissue dies even though the tissue itself was
never injured. **(Body fact)**

The two cases teach the same lesson. Criticality is not proportional to visibility. The
gland that no one can see from outside decides whether the body survives. **(Inference)**

Scripture makes the weaker member the necessary one. "Nay, much more those members of the
body, which seem to be more feeble, are necessary:" (1 Corinthians 12:22) **(Text states)**

### B4. Honor has to be allocated on purpose

Attention flows to what shows. Left alone, honor in a group will follow visibility rather
than necessity. **(Inference)**

The text describes the body doing the opposite. "And those members of the body, which we
think to be less honourable, upon these we bestow more abundant honour; and our uncomely
parts have more abundant comeliness." (1 Corinthians 12:23) **(Text states)** The next
verse gives the reason. "For our comely parts have no need: but God hath tempered the body
together, having given more abundant honour to that part which lacked:" (1 Corinthians
12:24) **(Text states)**

Note what the text says and what it does not say. It says God tempered the body together
and gave the extra honor to the part that lacked. **(Text states)** It does not say the
parts organized a committee and reached the same result. A body does this by design. A
firm must do it by decision, because the default in a firm runs the other way. **(Inference)**

The practical consequence is a reward rule. The maintainers, the testers, the support
desk, and the people who answer the phone carry functions that do not show. A firm that
honors only what can be shown will lose what cannot be shown. **(Inference)**

### B5. Role placement

Cell function is set by position and by the signals a cell receives. A cell does not
choose its own job. **(Body fact)** Differentiation runs through signal pathways that tell
a cell what to become, and the same cell placed in a different environment behaves
differently. **(Body fact)**

Scripture states the placement as an act of God. "But now hath God set the members every
one of them in the body, as it hath pleased him." (1 Corinthians 12:18) **(Text states)**

The placement is a decision, and it belongs to whoever holds the authority to place.
**(Inference)** The person who wants the visible role is not automatically the right person
for it. A firm that fills roles by preference rather than by fit will place strong
ambition in the wrong seat and call the result motivation. **(Inference)**

### B6. A local infection has a systemic effect

A local infection raises the temperature of the whole body. Immune signals act on the
hypothalamus, which is the temperature control center, and the set point rises. **(Body
fact)** A core temperature above roughly 38.0 degrees Celsius is commonly called a fever.
**(Body fact, approximate)** The cause sits in one place. The effect runs through
everything.

Pain behaves the same way. Pain captures attention, and a persistent pain in one toe
consumes the whole mind. **(Body fact)**

Scripture gives the mutual form of the same rule. "And whether one member suffer, all the
members suffer with it; or one member be honoured, all the members rejoice with it."
(1 Corinthians 12:26) **(Text states)**

Local failure carries global cost. One bad hire, one broken process, or one unpaid supplier
changes the state of the whole firm. **(Inference)** The budget for prevention belongs to
the whole, not to the part that failed. **(Inference)**

### B7. Growth runs through connective tissue

Growth in the body is triggered by central signals and carried out through connective
tissue. The pituitary gland releases growth hormone. The liver answers with insulin-like
growth factor 1, which acts on the growth plates of the long bones and on other tissues.
**(Body fact)** The growth plate is cartilage. Cartilage is connective tissue. The bone
lengthens at that plate until the plate closes in late adolescence. **(Body fact)**

The signaling and the structure are two different jobs. Remove the signal and growth
stops. Remove or injure the plate and that bone stops lengthening, even with the signal
present. **(Body fact)**

Scripture uses the same structure for the growth of the church. "But speaking the truth in
love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ: From whom the
whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth,
according to the effectual working in the measure of every part, maketh increase of the
body unto the edifying of itself in love." (Ephesians 4:15-16) **(Text states)** The same
image appears again in Colossians. "And not holding the Head, from which all the body by
joints and bands having nourishment ministered, and knit together, increaseth with the
increase of God." (Colossians 2:19) **(Text states)**

Growth is directed centrally and delivered distributively. **(Inference)** Direction is a
leadership task. Delivery is an interface task. If growth has stalled, inspect the joints,
which are the handoffs between teams. **(Inference)**

## II. Control and feedback

### B8. Homeostasis: four set points

The body defends set points without conscious thought. Four of them are named in B8.

Blood temperature sits in a narrow range around 36.5 to 37.5 degrees Celsius. The
hypothalamus compares the current value against the set point and triggers sweating,
shivering, and changes in blood flow to the skin. **(Body fact, approximate)**

Blood pH sits between about 7.35 and 7.45. Chemical buffers act first. The lungs adjust
carbon dioxide within minutes, and the kidneys adjust bicarbonate over hours and days.
**(Body fact, approximate)**

Blood glucose in a fasting adult commonly sits between about 70 and 99 milligrams per
deciliter. Insulin lowers it. Glucagon raises it. The liver stores and releases glycogen.
**(Body fact, approximate)**

Blood pressure is held by a control loop with several parts. Stretch receptors in the
carotid sinus and the aortic arch report pressure. The autonomic nervous system adjusts
heart rate, heart contractility, and vessel width within seconds. The kidneys and the
renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system adjust blood volume over longer periods. **(Body
fact)** A resting figure near 120 over 80 millimeters of mercury is often quoted as a
reference. The normal range is wide, it moves with age and activity, and the clinical
thresholds for treatment are a matter of ongoing debate. **(Body fact, approximate.
Contested in the clinical thresholds)**

One property of these loops deserves care. The set point is not a single number. Each
system holds a range, and the range itself changes with activity, time of day, and age.
**(Body fact)**

Correction has a cost. Regulation consumes energy and material, and a body under load
defends its set points with less margin. **(Body fact)**

Scripture commands the measurement, not only the wish. "Be thou diligent to know the state
of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds." (Proverbs 27:23) **(Text states)**

A target with no correction loop is a wish. Name the sensor, the threshold, and the
response. **(Inference)**

### B9. Pain: the signal matters more than the sensation

A small number of people are born unable to feel pain. The condition is called congenital
insensitivity to pain. It is rare, and it is caused by variants in genes that build the
pain signaling pathway, most often SCN9A. **(Body fact)** These children injure themselves
repeatedly. They bite their tongues, break bones, and fail to notice infections. Many die
young from injuries and from infections that went unnoticed. **(Body fact)** The pain is
not the disease.

Related conditions exist, including hereditary sensory and autonomic neuropathy, and
acquired loss of pain sensation in leprosy. The mechanisms differ. The outcome is the
same pattern, which is damage that accumulates because the alarm is silent. **(Body fact)**

Scripture treats the honest wound as a kindness. "Open rebuke is better than secret love.
Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful." (Proverbs
27:5-6) **(Text states)**

Complaints, churn, error rates, and staff departures are pain receptors. **(Inference)**
Fix the cause. Do not disable the sensor. The warning sign is a fall in reports while the
underlying condition is unchanged. **(Inference)**

### B10. Acute inflammation and chronic inflammation

Inflammation is the repair response. The classic signs are redness, heat, swelling, pain,
and loss of function. Blood flow rises, vessel walls become permeable, and immune cells
move into the tissue. When the cause is cleared, the response resolves and the tissue
heals. **(Body fact)**

When the response never resolves, it damages the tissue it was sent to protect. **(Body
fact)** The term covers many different processes, and the boundary between a helpful and
a harmful response is an active research question. **(Contested in the details)**

Scripture states that friction is permanent after the fall. "And unto Adam he said,
Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree, of
which I commanded thee, saying, Thou shalt not eat of it: cursed is the ground for thy
sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life;" (Genesis 3:17) **(Text
states)** The curse names a condition that does not end. It does not command an emergency
that does not end.

A crisis that ends is a cost. A crisis that never ends becomes the operating disease.
**(Inference)** Set an end date on every emergency. **(Inference)**

### B11. Self against non-self

The immune system must attack what is foreign and spare what is self. It learns the
distinction in part during its development, when strongly self-reactive immune cells are
removed. Regulatory cells restrain many of the self-reactive cells that remain. **(Body
fact)**

Autoimmune disease happens when the defense system attacks the body's own tissue. Type 1
diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, and multiple sclerosis are examples. More than 80
conditions carry the label. **(Body fact, approximate)** The response is correct in kind
and wrong in target.

The simple self versus non-self model is a good first description. Immunology also uses a
danger model, in which the trigger is tissue damage rather than foreignness alone, and the
resident microbiome complicates the boundary. **(Contested in the model's details)**

Scripture states the consequence of a divided house. "And Jesus knew their thoughts, and
said unto them, Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation; and every
city or house divided against itself shall not stand:" (Matthew 12:25) **(Text states)**

A defense system needs an accurate target list. Competition is external. Keep the
distinction between competitor and colleague. **(Inference)**

### B12. Sustained cortisol

Cortisol is the main human glucocorticoid hormone. The adrenal glands release it on a
daily rhythm, with a peak shortly after waking. **(Body fact)** In the short term it
mobilizes glucose and supports the response to a threat. **(Body fact)**

Sustained cortisol acts on memory, immunity, and judgement.

Memory. Long-term exposure to high cortisol is associated with changes in the hippocampus
and with impaired memory for facts and events. The clearest human evidence comes from
conditions that raise cortisol for medical reasons and from treatment with steroid drugs.
Evidence from ordinary chronic stress in humans is mixed in size and not uniform across
studies. **(Body fact. Contested in the size of the effect in ordinary stress)**

Immunity. Glucocorticoids suppress the movement and function of several kinds of immune
cell. Steroid drugs are used for exactly that reason. The link between psychological
stress and infection in humans is documented, and the effect size varies. **(Body fact.
Contested in the size of the effect)**

Judgement. Acute stress shifts control of behavior toward habit and away from flexible,
goal-directed choice. Uncontrollable stress impairs working memory in animal models. The
shift is real and measurable, and its size depends on the task and the person. **(Body
fact)**

Scripture addresses the state that drives the hormone.

- "In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul." (Psalms 94:19)
  **(Text states)**
- "Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving
  let your requests be made known unto God." (Philippians 4:6) **(Text states)** The word
  "careful" here is 1611 English for anxious, not attentive. **(Lexical)** The Greek
  behind it is G5431 _phrontizo_, "to exercise thought, i.e. be anxious".
- "Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you." (1 Peter 5:7) **(Text states)**

A frightened team hides information, and hidden information is the expensive kind.
**(Inference)** Lower the cost of telling the truth. **(Inference)**

## III. Supply and flow

### B13. Circulation: the clot kills downstream

Blood carries oxygen, fuel, heat, signals, and immune cells to every tissue. An adult
body holds roughly 4.5 to 5.5 liters of blood. The heart moves roughly 5 liters per
minute at rest. **(Body fact, approximate)**

A clot inside a vessel is a thrombus. It narrows or blocks the vessel. The tissue beyond
the blockage loses its supply and dies. **(Body fact)** The damage lands downstream of the
blockage, not at the blockage.

Three familiar cases show the pattern. A clot in a coronary artery causes a heart attack,
and the damaged muscle sits past the clot. A clot in a cerebral artery causes a stroke,
and the injured brain sits past the clot. A clot that travels to the lungs causes a
pulmonary embolism, and the starved lung tissue sits past the clot. **(Body fact)**

Scripture commends scattering over hoarding. "Cast thy bread upon the waters: for thou
shalt find it after many days. Give a portion to seven, and also to eight; for thou
knowest not what evil shall be upon the earth." (Ecclesiastes 11:1-2) **(Text states)**
"There is that scattereth, and yet increaseth; and there is that withholdeth more than is
meet, but it tendeth to poverty." (Proverbs 11:24) **(Text states)** The word "meet" is
1611 English for fitting or proper. **(Lexical)**

Cash that does not move is a clot. Inventory that does not move is a clot. **(Inference)**
Find the blockages, not the shortage. **(Inference)**

### B14. Selective permeability

The cell membrane is a double layer of lipid with proteins embedded in it. It is roughly
5 to 10 nanometers thick. **(Body fact, approximate)** Some material crosses by simple
diffusion.
Some crosses through channels. Some is carried across by pumps that spend energy. The
membrane admits nutrients and refuses most large molecules and charged particles.
**(Body fact)**

The blood brain barrier is the strict version of the same design. The endothelial cells
of the brain capillaries are joined by tight junctions. Astrocytes and pericytes support
the structure. The barrier admits oxygen, carbon dioxide, glucose, and some drugs, and it
refuses most large molecules, many toxins, and most pathogens. **(Body fact)**

A wall starves the tissue. An open door poisons it. **(Inference)** The designed answer
is a controlled crossing, with rules about what passes.

Scripture describes the garden as a guarded space, not an open one. "And the LORD God took
the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it." (Genesis 2:15)
**(Text states)** Two different Hebrew verbs carry the two jobs. **(Lexical)** The word
rendered "dress" is H5647 `abad`, "to work (in any sense); by implication, to serve, till,
(causatively) enslave". The word rendered "keep" is H8104 `shamar`, "properly, to hedge
about (as with thorns), i.e. guard; generally, to protect, attend to". The second verb is
the watchman's word, and the dictionary image is a hedge.

The guarded way back appears again after the fall. "So he drove out the man; and he placed
at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way,
to keep the way of the tree of life." (Genesis 3:24) **(Text states)** The same verb
`shamar` carries the guarding there. **(Lexical)**

Hiring, security, and partnership need filters, not walls and not open doors. Decide what
crosses. **(Inference)**

### B15. Waste removal

Several organs remove waste, and the work never stops. The kidneys filter blood, the liver
converts nitrogen waste into urea and clears many drugs and toxins, the lungs remove carbon
dioxide, the colon and skin remove some material, and the lymph system drains fluid and
debris from the tissues. **(Body fact)**

The kidney numbers give the scale. The kidneys filter roughly 180 liters of fluid per day.
Almost all of it is reabsorbed, and urine output is roughly 1 to 2 liters per day. **(Body
fact, approximate)** When the kidneys stop working, waste products accumulate in the
blood. That state is called uremia. Without replacement therapy, it kills in roughly 1 to
2 weeks. **(Body fact, approximate)** The comparison in B15 holds, because a person who
has water but no food commonly survives for weeks to months, and retained waste kills
sooner. **(Body fact, approximate)** The exact times vary with the person and the
situation. **(Contested in the specific figures)**

Scripture treats removal as a continuing function, not a one-time event. "Every branch in
me that beareth not fruit he taketh away: and every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth
it, that it may bring forth more fruit." (John 15:2) **(Text states)** "And he shall sit
as a refiner and purifier of silver: and he shall purify the sons of Levi, and purge them
as gold and silver, that they may offer unto the LORD an offering in righteousness."
(Malachi 3:3) **(Text states)**

Removal is as important as intake, and it never stops. **(Inference)** Kill dead products,
dead processes, dead inventory, and roles that no longer fit. Pruning is a function with
an owner. **(Inference)**

### B16. Input quality

The body is built from what it absorbs. The raw material arrives through the gut, the
lungs, and the skin. Nine amino acids, several fatty acids, and a set of vitamins and
minerals must come from outside, because the body cannot make them. **(Body fact)**

The body then synthesizes most of its own molecules from that raw material. The claim in
B16 is about the supply, not about assembly. Nothing reaches the tissues that did not
first cross a barrier from outside. **(Inference)** A short supply of one raw material
limits the whole system, whatever else is present in abundance.

Scripture directs the diet of the mind. "Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true,
whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure,
whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any
virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things." (Philippians 4:8) **(Text
states)** "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."
(Proverbs 4:23) **(Text states)**

The information diet of leadership shapes every decision. Curate it on purpose, because it
will not curate itself. **(Inference)**

## IV. Growth and adaptation

### B17. Wolff's law, hypertrophy, and atrophy

Bone remodels in response to the load it carries. The principle carries the name Wolff's
law, after the nineteenth century surgeon Julius Wolff. Load-bearing bone adds mass, and
unloaded bone loses it. Cells inside the bone sense strain and direct the rebuilding.
**(Body fact)** The mechanism is well established in outline, and the details of the
sensing pathway remain an active research area. **(Contested in the mechanism)**

Muscle grows under demand and shrinks without it. Resistance training raises muscle
protein synthesis, and the fiber cross-section grows over weeks. **(Body fact)** Whether
adult human muscle adds new fibers as well as enlarging existing ones is disputed. The
evidence for new fibers is stronger in some animal models than in humans. **(Contested)**

Unused systems atrophy. Immobilization in a cast, prolonged bed rest, and spaceflight all
reduce muscle mass and bone density. **(Body fact)** The loss is measurable within weeks.

Scripture treats the load as the mechanism rather than the obstacle. "My brethren, count
it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your
faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and
entire, wanting nothing." (James 1:2-4) **(Text states)** "And not only so, but we glory
in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience; And patience, experience;
and experience, hope:" (Romans 5:3-4) **(Text states)**

Capability follows demand, not intention. **(Inference)** A plan does not build capacity.
A carried load does, and the load must be real and measured. **(Inference)**

### B18. The training pair

A training stimulus damages tissue and triggers an adaptation signal. Recovery rebuilds
the tissue and adds capacity. **(Body fact)** Both halves are required.

Stimulus without recovery causes decline. The condition is called overtraining syndrome.
It is a recognized sports medicine condition with symptoms that include persistent fatigue
and a drop in performance. It has no single diagnostic marker, and clinicians diagnose it
by excluding other causes. Recovery takes weeks to months. **(Body fact. Contested in the
definition and the mechanism)** Recovery without stimulus causes atrophy, as the previous
section states. **(Body fact)**

Scripture gives the rhythm of six and one. "And on the seventh day God ended his work
which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.
And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from
all his work which God created and made." (Genesis 2:2-3) **(Text states)** The command to
Israel repeats the pattern. "Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work: But the
seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work"
(Exodus 20:9-10) **(Text states)**

Challenge and restoration must be paired. **(Inference)** Push without rest breaks people.
Rest without push makes them stale. Design the pair, not the halves. **(Inference)**

### B19. Sleep and repair

Tissue rebuilds during sleep. Growth hormone is released mostly during the early part of
the night, in deep slow-wave sleep. **(Body fact)** The peak is tied to the sleep stage,
not to the clock hour, so a shifted sleep schedule moves the release.

Sleep also consolidates memory. Newly formed memories are stabilized and reorganized
during sleep, and both slow-wave sleep and REM sleep contribute. **(Body fact)** Immune
function interacts with sleep in both directions. **(Body fact)** Healthy adults commonly
sleep about 7 to 9 hours a night, and the requirement varies by person. **(Body fact,
approximate)**

Scripture names rest as a gift rather than a failure. "It is vain for you to rise up early,
to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep." (Psalms
127:2) **(Text states)** Jesus commands the pause directly. "And he said unto them, Come ye
yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and
going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat." (Mark 6:31) **(Text states)**

The gain is in the recovery, not only in the work. **(Inference)** Retrospectives, quiet
planning time, and the deliberate offsite produce improvements that execution cannot.
**(Inference)**

### B20. Oscillation

The body runs on rhythms at several scales.

The heart beats and rests in a cycle. A resting adult heart rate commonly falls between
about 60 and 100 beats per minute, and the beat is a contraction followed by a filling
phase. **(Body fact, approximate)**

Breathing runs a cycle of inhalation and exhalation. A resting adult rate commonly falls
between about 12 and 20 breaths per minute. **(Body fact, approximate)**

The sleep cycle alternates between non-REM and REM stages across the night, with a period
of roughly 90 minutes per cycle. **(Body fact, approximate)**

The circadian system runs an internal period close to 24 hours. The suprachiasmatic
nucleus in the hypothalamus keeps the rhythm, and light resets it each day. **(Body fact)**

Digestion runs a fasting cycle of motility that repeats about every 90 to 120 minutes, and
many hormones are released in pulses rather than a steady stream. **(Body fact,
approximate)**

A body without oscillation is a body in arrest. A heart with no rhythm is not beating.
**(Body fact)**

Scripture builds rhythm into the creation account. "And God called the light Day, and the
darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day." (Genesis
1:5) **(Text states)** The pattern repeats through the chapter, and the sixth day closes
the same way. "And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.
And the evening and the morning were the sixth day." (Genesis 1:31) **(Text states)**
Ecclesiastes states the principle for every activity. "To every thing there is a season,
and a time to every purpose under the heaven:" (Ecclesiastes 3:1) **(Text states)**

Rhythm is the operating mode, not a deviation from it. **(Inference)** Set the cadence.
Review rhythm, release rhythm, and planning horizon are design choices. **(Inference)**

## V. Limits and mortality

### B21. Replication limits and designed aging

Most human cells divide a limited number of times in culture. The limit is called the
Hayflick limit, after Leonard Hayflick, who described it. Human fibroblasts commonly
complete about 50 to 70 divisions before they stop. **(Body fact, approximate)** The limit
is tied to the ends of the chromosomes. Those ends, the telomeres, shorten with each
division, and a cell with critically short telomeres stops dividing. **(Body fact)**

The relationship between telomere shortening and aging in the whole body is real and
partial. Telomere length is one of several recognized hallmarks of aging, and long-lived
animals do not all follow the same pattern. **(Contested in how far the mechanism drives
whole-body aging)**

Aging is a normal process, not a single disease. It is not one fault. It is a set of
processes that appear in every multicellular body. **(Body fact)** The claim that aging is
designed rather than accidental follows from the pattern, and it is an inference from the
design rather than a measurement. **(Inference)**

Scripture states the limit in the curse. "In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread,
till thou return unto the ground; for out of it wast thou taken: for dust thou art, and
unto dust shalt thou return." (Genesis 3:19) **(Text states)** The prayer asks for the
same accounting as wisdom. "So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts
unto wisdom." (Psalms 90:12) **(Text states)** "And as it is appointed unto men once to
die, but after this the judgment:" (Hebrews 9:27) **(Text states)** The outward decay and
the inward renewal run at the same time. "For which cause we faint not; but though our
outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day." (2 Corinthians 4:16)
**(Text states)**

Finitude is design. **(Inference)** A firm has a lifespan. Plan for succession, sale, or
closure, and do it while the firm is strong. A founder who cannot imagine the end will be
surprised by it. **(Inference)**

### B22. Redundancy in critical systems

Some critical systems are paired. A person normally has two kidneys, two lungs, two
carotid arteries, and paired nerves along the limbs and the spine. **(Body fact)** One
kidney and one lung can carry the load when the pair loses a member, and people live
normal lives after donating a kidney. **(Body fact)**

The pattern is not universal, and honesty requires the qualification. The heart, the
liver, and the pancreas are single organs. The body does not have a spare for everything.
**(Body fact)** Redundancy is present where it is present, and the design does not read
as a general rule of duplication.

Scripture commends the pair and the cord. "Two are better than one; because they have a
good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe
to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up. Again, if
two lie together, then they have heat: but how can one be warm alone? And if one prevail
against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken."
(Ecclesiastes 4:9-12) **(Text states)** The first "not good" in Scripture is a statement
about solitude. "And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I
will make him an help meet for him." (Genesis 2:18) **(Text states)** The word "help" is
H5828 `ezer`, defined as "aid". **(Lexical)** The KJV applies the same English word to God
in Psalms 121:2, so the word does not settle rank. **(Lexical)**

Protect the irreplaceable. **(Inference)** Single points of failure belong at the top of
the risk register. A spare is cheaper than the failure. **(Inference)**

### B23. Failure at the smallest scale

Failure propagates upward. A blocked capillary kills a patch of tissue. Damaged tissue
impairs the organ. A failed organ destabilizes the body. **(Body fact)** Sepsis is the
clearest clinical picture of the chain. An infection at one site provokes a body-wide
response, and the response can progress to organ failure and death. **(Body fact)**

The chain runs in the other direction too. A small leak in one system consumes resources
that every other system depends on. **(Inference)**

Scripture names small agents with large effects. "Take us the foxes, the little foxes,
that spoil the vines: for our vines have tender grapes." (Song of Solomon 2:15) **(Text
states)** The damage comes from something small enough to be overlooked.

Micro defects propagate upward. **(Inference)** Small leaks compound. Fraud, latency,
waste, and one unprofitable customer segment are capillaries. **(Inference)**

### B24. Breath

The first act of a newborn is to breathe. The first breath is triggered by a rise in
carbon dioxide and a fall in oxygen, together with cooling and mechanical changes in the
chest. **(Body fact)** The details of the trigger are still studied, and the outline is
not in dispute. **(Contested in the details)**

Breathing then runs automatically. A network of neurons in the brainstem generates the
rhythm. Sensors report carbon dioxide, oxygen, and pH, and the system adjusts rate and
depth without conscious thought. **(Body fact)**

The same process can be brought under voluntary control. The cortex can drive the
breathing muscles directly, which is how a person holds a breath or speaks a long
sentence. **(Body fact)** Voluntary control has a ceiling. The automatic drive overrides
it, and a person who loses consciousness starts breathing again. **(Body fact)**

Scripture treats breath as the gift that starts and continues the life. "And the LORD God
formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life;
and man became a living soul." (Genesis 2:7) **(Text states)** "The spirit of God hath
made me, and the breath of the Almighty hath given me life." (Job 33:4) **(Text states)**
"In whose hand is the soul of every living thing, and the breath of all mankind." (Job
12:10) **(Text states)** "Thou hidest thy face, they are troubled: thou takest away their
breath, they die, and return to their dust." (Psalms 104:29) **(Text states)** The New
Testament states the same dependence. "Neither is worshipped with men's hands, as though
he needed any thing, seeing he giveth to all life, and breath, and all things;" (Acts
17:25) **(Text states)**

The vital process starts first, then runs on its own, with deliberate control available.
**(Inference)** Find the breath of the firm. If it needs conscious effort every hour, the
firm is dying. If nobody can describe it, nobody can protect it. **(Inference)**

## VI. Where the analogy breaks

The body is a living thing. A firm is not. Each of the six sections above rests on a
comparison that stops somewhere. This section names where each one stops.

### One organism, many members

A body cannot choose its members. Cells are assigned by development, and a cell that
leaves its place becomes a problem. A firm can hire, fire, and reassign on purpose, and
those decisions are its main lever. **(Inference)** A body also cannot change its species.
A firm can change what it is, enter a new market, or rebuild its operating model. The
analogy from biology describes structure and coordination, and it does not describe
choice. **(Inference)**

### Control and feedback

A body's control loops are automatic and fast. A firm's control loops run through people
who read reports, hold opinions, and have interests in the reading. **(Inference)** A firm
can also decide to abandon a set point. A business can accept a wider margin, a different
quality level, or a slower response time as a deliberate trade. A body cannot renegotiate
its blood pH. The comparison to homeostasis is useful for the shape of the loop, and it
does not transfer the automatic part. **(Inference)**

### Supply and flow

A body's supply chain is fixed at birth. It cannot open a second route or find a new
vendor. A firm can build redundancy into supply, qualify a second source, or change the
product to fit the material. **(Inference)** The firm also can decide what it is for, and
supply decisions follow that. The body's circulation shows what flow does. It does not
tell a firm what to move or why. **(Inference)**

### Growth and adaptation

A body's growth is bounded by genetics and by the growth plates. A firm can grow past any
planned size, and it can shrink on purpose. **(Inference)** A body cannot select what it
adapts to. A firm can choose its training, its market, and its exposure. That choice
carries moral weight, and the body carries none. **(Inference)**

### Limits and mortality

A body cannot be dissolved by agreement. It dies on its own terms. A firm can be closed
deliberately, sold, merged, or wound down, and the people inside it go on living.
**(Inference)** The body's mortality teaches that an end is normal. It does not teach that
a firm's end must arrive the way a body's does. **(Inference)**

### Where the body misleads

This is the largest break of the six. A body is not a moral agent. It has no conscience,
no standing, and no accountability. A firm is made of persons who are moral agents, and
the firm is answerable for what it does. **(Inference)** A body's appetites carry no
authority, and neither does a firm's. The section that follows the instincts is covered in
`references/where-the-body-misleads.md`.

## The hard limit of this analogy

A firm is not an organism.

A body has one interest and one life. A firm is an agreement among persons, and those
persons have interests that do not collapse into the firm's interest. **(Inference)** The
body analogy describes coordination, supply, feedback, growth, and decay with unusual
precision. It does not describe what a person is worth.

That distinction is not a courtesy. It is stated in the text. "So God created man in his
own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them." (Genesis
1:27) **(Text states)** The image of God is carried by the person, not by the firm and not
by the role. "Whoso sheddeth man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image
of God made he man." (Genesis 9:6) **(Text states)** The value of the person stands before
any use the firm has for the person.

Scripture applies the same status to the body of the person. "What? know ye not that your
body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not
your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your
spirit, which are God's." (1 Corinthians 6:19-20) **(Text states)** A person is not a
component, and the person's body is not an asset of the firm.

The analogy must never be used to treat a person as a cell. **(Inference)** Three
applications of that rule follow.

1. A cell has no standing. A person does. A person can object, refuse, and appeal, and
   those acts are not dysfunction.
2. A cell exists for the body. A person exists before God, and the firm exists for
   purposes that are judged by a higher standard than survival.
3. A cell that the body discards has no claim. A person who leaves a firm, or whom a firm
   releases, keeps every claim that Scripture gives a person.

The order of authority in the skill file settles the question. Explicit commands of
Scripture govern. Direct descriptions of God's character and action govern. Inferences
from the body inform and illustrate. They never override the first two. **(Inference)**
