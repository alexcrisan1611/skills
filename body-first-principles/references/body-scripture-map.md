# Body Scripture Map

Reference file for the `body-first-principles` skill.

This file maps the body facts of B1 to B24 to the Scripture that governs each function. The
table is the map. The prose sections that follow expand the rows that carry the most
weight.

Scripture is quoted from `skills/scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts`, and every
quote below was read from that tool rather than recalled. The interpretation rules come
from the `scripture-foundations` skill. Two of those rules do most of the work here.

- Applications are inferences. A principle drawn from the text is not itself the text.
- Read in context. Check the speaker, the audience, and whether the passage describes what
  is or prescribes what must be.

Labels mark the status of each claim. **Body fact** marks physiology. **Text states**
marks what Scripture says. **Lexical** marks a claim about the Hebrew or Greek.
**Inference** marks reasoning that goes past either one. **Contested** marks a disputed
point.

## How to read the map

The fourth column does the real work. It answers one question. What does the text supply
that the body cannot supply at all?

The body supplies mechanism. It shows how a system coordinates, corrects, supplies,
grows, and fails. It never supplies purpose, standing, moral direction, or accountability.
**(Inference)** The body of a dead man runs none of these systems, and nothing in the
physiology says the man was worth anything. That question is answered somewhere else.
**(Inference)**

So the fourth column names what only the text can give. Where the column reads as a
command, the body cannot match it, because a body has no will to command. Where the column
reads as a promise, the body cannot match it, because a body has no future beyond its own
decay. Where the column reads as a statement about God, the body cannot match it, because
a body is not God. **(Inference)**

One caution applies to the whole table. Numeric figures in the body fact column are
approximate, and a range is given where one exists. The body language of 1 Corinthians and
Ephesians belongs first to the church, which the text calls the body of Christ. **(Text
states)** Applying those arguments to a commercial firm is an inference, and the inference
holds only where the comparison holds. **(Inference)**

## The rows that carry the most weight

### B1 and B2: the passage that argues from the body

Most passages that mention the body use it as a picture. 1 Corinthians 12 does something
stronger. Paul reasons from the structure of a body to the structure of a congregation,
and he draws commands out of the comparison. **(Inference)**

The argument opens with the structure. "For as the body is one, and hath many members,
and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body: so also is Christ."
(1 Corinthians 12:12) **(Text states)** The next verse supplies the mechanism of unity.
"For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles,
whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit."
(1 Corinthians 12:13) **(Text states)** The many members are not a problem to be solved.
"For the body is not one member, but many." (1 Corinthians 12:14) **(Text states)**

Then the argument turns to exclusion. The foot and the ear both raise the same objection
against themselves, and the answer is a question. "If the foot shall say, Because I am not
the hand, I am not of the body; is it therefore not of the body?" (1 Corinthians 12:15)
**(Text states)** The belonging is settled by the body, not by the member's own estimate
of itself. **(Inference)**

The placement is then assigned to God. "But now hath God set the members every one of them
in the body, as it hath pleased him." (1 Corinthians 12:18) **(Text states)** The verse
does two jobs. It states that placement is a decision, and it names who decides.
**(Inference)** Verse 19 draws the consequence. "And if they were all one member, where
were the body?" (1 Corinthians 12:19) **(Text states)** A group of identical members is
not a smaller body. It is not a body at all. **(Inference)**

Verse 21 is where the argument becomes a command. "And the eye cannot say unto the hand, I
have no need of thee: nor again the head to the feet, I have no need of you."
(1 Corinthians 12:21) **(Text states)** Read the sentence as an argument and not as a
scolding. If the parts depend on each other, then the claim of independence is false, and
a false claim about a system produces wrong decisions inside it. **(Inference)**

Verses 22 to 24 reverse the ranking that appearance produces. "Nay, much more those
members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are necessary: And those members of the
body, which we think to be less honourable, upon these we bestow more abundant honour; and
our uncomely parts have more abundant comeliness. For our comely parts have no need: but
God hath tempered the body together, having given more abundant honour to that part which
lacked:" (1 Corinthians 12:22-24) **(Text states)**

Two words in that passage repay attention.

- The text says "those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble." The word behind
  "seem" in the standard concordance is G1380 _dokeo_, "to think; by implication, to seem
  (truthfully or uncertainly)". **(Lexical)** The tools here carry no verse-level tagging,
  so the mapping of a word to a verse is the conventional reading and not a tool result.
  The ranking is a judgment made by an observer, and the text says the judgment is wrong.
- The text says God "tempered the body together." The word rendered "tempered" is G4786
  _synkerannymi_, "to commingle, i.e. (figuratively) to combine or assimilate". **(Lexical)**
  The honor is not an afterthought. It is bound into the mixture.

This is where the analogy to a firm has to be handled with care. A body's weakness is
compensated by design, without deliberation. A firm's compensation for unglamorous work is
a budget decision and a culture decision, and it can be refused. **(Inference)** The
command in verse 25 is the goal. "That there should be no schism in the body; but that the
members should have the same care one for another." (1 Corinthians 12:25) **(Text states)**
Schism is the failure the passage is built to prevent, and the passage prevents it by
stating the body's structure accurately.

Verse 26 states the shared state. "And whether one member suffer, all the members suffer
with it; or one member be honoured, all the members rejoice with it." (1 Corinthians
12:26) **(Text states)** Note that the verse includes honor as well as suffering. A firm
that shares only the pain has read half the verse. **(Inference)**

The conclusion names the body. "Now ye are the body of Christ, and members in
particular." (1 Corinthians 12:27) **(Text states)** That identification governs the
application. The passage is about the church first. Extending it to a firm is an inference,
and the inference holds only where the firm actually shares one interest and one mission.
**(Inference)**

### B7: growth from the head, through every joint

The body fact is that growth is directed centrally and delivered distributively. The text
states the same shape and then adds two things the body cannot supply, which are the head
and the goal. **(Inference)**

"But speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head,
even Christ: From whom the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which
every joint supplieth, according to the effectual working in the measure of every part,
maketh increase of the body unto the edifying of itself in love." (Ephesians 4:15-16)
**(Text states)**

Four elements of that sentence carry operating weight.

1. The source is named. Growth comes "from whom," which is Christ, the head. The direction
   of growth is not self-generated.
2. The delivery is distributed. The increase arrives "by that which every joint
   supplieth." A joint is a handoff, and every one of them supplies something.
3. The measure is specific to the part. The working is "in the measure of every part,"
   which means no single measure applies to every member.
4. The goal is stated. The increase is "unto the edifying of itself in love."

The growth is measurable in the text, and the measurement is not size. **(Inference)** The
body grows toward its head and builds itself up in love, and both of those are outside what
physiology can state. **(Inference)**

Colossians states the same mechanism with the same emphasis on the head. "And not holding
the Head, from which all the body by joints and bands having nourishment ministered, and
knit together, increaseth with the increase of God." (Colossians 2:19) **(Text states)**
Here the joints and bands minister nourishment. The connective tissue is not decorative.
It is the channel, and the text treats it as the channel.

The business reading follows. If growth has stalled, inspect the joints rather than the
headcount. **(Inference)** The body fact behind that move is that cutting the connections
stops the signal regardless of how strong the signal is. The text adds that the head is
Christ, so the direction of growth is not the firm's own ambition. **(Inference)**

### B13: scattering against hoarding

The body fact is that flow is life and stagnation kills at a distance from the blockage.
The text goes further. It attaches a promise to the outward move and a warning to the
inward one. **(Inference)**

"There is that scattereth, and yet increaseth; and there is that withholdeth more than is
meet, but it tendeth to poverty." (Proverbs 11:24) **(Text states)** The word "meet" is
1611 English for fitting or proper. **(Lexical)** The verse does not condemn withholding
as such. It condemns withholding past the fitting point, and it says the excess runs to
poverty. That is a claim about outcomes, not only about motives. **(Inference)**

The next two verses widen the claim. "The liberal soul shall be made fat: and he that
watereth shall be watered also himself. He that withholdeth corn, the people shall curse
him: but blessing shall be upon the head of him that selleth it." (Proverbs 11:25-26)
**(Text states)** The withholding in verse 26 is a supply decision in a shortage. The
people curse it, and the text puts the curse in the record.

Ecclesiastes gives the same instruction with a reason attached. "Cast thy bread upon the
waters: for thou shalt find it after many days. Give a portion to seven, and also to
eight; for thou knowest not what evil shall be upon the earth." (Ecclesiastes 11:1-2)
**(Text states)** The reason is ignorance of the future. The giver does not know which
portion will return, so the giver spreads the portion. **(Inference)**

The body fact and the text agree on the direction of the motion. They differ in what they
supply. The body shows that a blockage damages tissue far from the blockage. The text
supplies the judgment on the person who blocks, and a promise to the person who does not.
**(Inference)** A circulation diagram cannot make either of those claims.

### B17 and B18: the load and the recovery

These two rows belong together because the text treats them as one pattern.

James states the load and its product. "My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into
divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let
patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing."
(James 1:2-4) **(Text states)** The command to count it joy rests on a stated mechanism.
The trying works patience. The verse does not command joy at the damage. It commands joy
at the product. **(Inference)** That distinction is the same one the body shows, because
the stimulus is not the adaptation and the adaptation arrives later. **(Inference)**

Hebrews states the same sequence with the time gap made explicit. "Now no chastening for
the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the
peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby." (Hebrews 12:11)
**(Text states)** Three elements match the physiology closely.

1. "For the present" names the immediate cost. The text does not soften it.
2. "Afterward" names the delay. The yield arrives later.
3. "Unto them which are exercised thereby" names the condition. The fruit comes to the
   one who underwent the training, and not to an observer.

Romans supplies the chain. "And not only so, but we glory in tribulations also: knowing
that tribulation worketh patience; And patience, experience; and experience, hope: And
hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy
Ghost which is given unto us." (Romans 5:3-5) **(Text states)** The chain ends outside the
person. It ends in hope and in the love of God, which no tissue produces. **(Inference)**

Genesis supplies the rhythm that pairs the load with the pause. "And on the seventh day
God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work
which he had made. And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it
he had rested from all his work which God created and made." (Genesis 2:2-3) **(Text
states)** The command to Israel makes the rhythm a rule rather than a preference. "Six
days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work: But the seventh day is the sabbath of the
LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work" (Exodus 20:9-10) **(Text states)**

The physiology states that recovery without stimulus causes atrophy, and that stimulus
without recovery causes decline. The text supplies the reason the pair is a pattern rather
than a technique. The pattern is older than the body. It is in the creation week.
**(Inference)**

### B22: the two and the threefold cord

The body fact is that the body provisions some critical systems twice, and that a single
member of a pair can carry the load. The text reasons in the same direction and grounds
the need in a verdict rather than in a mechanism. **(Inference)**

"Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they
fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for
he hath not another to help him up. Again, if two lie together, then they have heat: but
how can one be warm alone? And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a
threefold cord is not quickly broken." (Ecclesiastes 4:9-12) **(Text states)**

The passage argues by cases, and each case names a specific failure.

1. A fall needs a lifter. The danger is not the fall. The danger is the fall without
   another person present.
2. Cold needs shared heat. The benefit is physical and immediate.
3. Attack needs a second defender. Two withstand what one cannot.
4. The cord closes the list. Three strands hold longer than the sum of three separate
   strands.

The last item is a small argument in itself. A cord is not three cords. It is one object
made of three, and its strength is a property of the whole. **(Inference)**

Genesis supplies the ground of the pattern. "And the LORD God said, It is not good that
the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him." (Genesis 2:18) **(Text
states)** The verse is the first place in Scripture where God calls something not good.
That placement gives the verdict weight. **(Inference)** The word rendered "help" is H5828
`ezer`, defined as "aid". **(Lexical)**

Two cautions follow from that word.

- The KJV phrase "help meet" is not one Hebrew idea. "Meet" is a separate English word
  meaning suitable. Do not build on the two-word English phrase.
- The same English word applies to God. "My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven
  and earth." (Psalms 121:2) **(Text states)** The word says nothing about rank.

The body fact and the text agree that a single point of failure is dangerous. The text
adds that the need for the other person is a verdict from God and not an accident of
physiology. **(Inference)**

## Where Scripture uses the body as an argument rather than an image

An image illustrates a point that stands on other grounds. Remove the image and the
argument survives. An argument uses a fact about the thing as a premise, and removing the
premise breaks the conclusion. **(Inference)**

The difference matters for how the passage binds. An image can be swapped for another
image, because the point travels with the meaning and not with the picture. An argument
cannot be swapped, because the conclusion depends on the fact. **(Inference)**

Four passages in this map argue from the body rather than illustrating with it.

1. **1 Corinthians 12:12-27.** Paul reasons from the structure of a body to the structure
   of the congregation, and he draws commands out of the comparison. The claim that the
   eye cannot despise the hand is a claim about how bodies work, and the command not to
   despise the member follows from it. The passage is the clearest case in Scripture of
   reasoning from anatomy. **(Inference)**
2. **Romans 12:4-5.** The same argument in miniature. "For as we have many members in one
   body, and all members have not the same office: So we, being many, are one body in
   Christ, and every one members one of another." (Romans 12:4-5) **(Text states)** The
   structure is a premise, and the conclusion follows with "so." **(Inference)**
3. **Ephesians 4:15-16.** The mechanism of growth is stated as a fact about the body, and
   the conduct of the congregation is measured against it. A body grows through every
   joint, and the working is measured per part. Remove those premises and the instructions
   about how the members deal with one another lose their stated basis. **(Inference)**
4. **Colossians 2:19.** The same argument with the same premise. Nourishment comes through
   the joints from the head, and the increase follows from holding the head.
   **(Inference)**

The rest of the map divides into four groups, and none of them is a body argument.

- **Commands that use no body image.** Genesis 2:15 places the man to dress and to keep
  the garden. It is a command about work and guard. Proverbs 27:23 commands diligence in
  knowing the state of the flock. Philippians 4:8 sets the standard for thought. Proverbs
  4:23 commands the guarding of the heart. James 1:2 commands joy under trial.
- **Statements about God's action.** Genesis 2:7 and Job 33:4 state that God formed man
  and gave him breath. Psalm 104:29 and Acts 17:25 state the dependence that follows.
  These are statements about the giver, not arguments about organization.
- **Wisdom observations.** Proverbs 11:24, Proverbs 27:5-6, Ecclesiastes 4:9-12,
  Ecclesiastes 11:1-2, and Song of Solomon 2:15 reason from experience and from the
  ordinary order of things. Ecclesiastes 4:9-12 argues, but it argues from cases a reader
  can check, not from the anatomy of a body.
- **Figures of speech.** John 15:2 uses the vine and the branches. Malachi 3:3 uses the
  refiner and the fire. Song of Solomon 2:15 uses the foxes and the vines. These are
  images. Each one carries real force in its own passage, and none of them is an anatomy
  lesson.

Two further cautions belong with this section.

First, the four argument passages belong to the church before they belong to any firm.
The text calls the congregants the body of Christ. **(Text states)** A claim about a
commercial firm drawn from them is an inference about an institution that the text does
not name. **(Inference)** The inference is worth making in this skill, and the reader must
know that it is one.

Second, an argument from the body still leaves the body under Scripture. The body supplies
the structure in these four passages because the text chose to use it. The body does not
supply the goal, the standard, or the judge. Those come from the same text, and the
`references/where-the-body-misleads.md` file lists the eight places where the body's own
instinct runs against them. **(Inference)**
