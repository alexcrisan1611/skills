# Body First Principles

## What this layer is for

Genesis 1-3 gives the axioms of business. This skill adds a second layer of evidence: the
human body.

The body is a working system with a known design. It has structure, supply, control, growth,
repair, waste removal, and a fixed lifespan. It runs at three scales at once. The whole
organism coordinates, the organ systems trade with each other, and the cell does the work.
You can observe all of it, and you can measure much of it.

Scripture uses the body as its own model for a group of people. For that reason, this layer
belongs in the framework and not beside it. 1 Corinthians 12 does not offer the body as a
poetic image. It argues from the structure of the body to how a group of people must work.
The passage is about the church, which 1 Corinthians 12:27 calls "the body of Christ". The
use of the passage for a firm is an inference.

## Rule one: Scripture governs the body

The body is evidence, not authority. It shows how a system works. It does not tell you what
the system is for.

The order of authority in this skill is fixed:

1. Explicit commands of Scripture govern.
2. Direct descriptions of the character and action of God govern.
3. Inferences from the body inform and illustrate.
4. Inferences from the body never override 1, 2, or 3.

When the body suggests one thing and Scripture says another, follow Scripture. Section VI of
this file lists the eight places where the logic of the body runs against Scripture. The
reader then does not have to find them by accident.

Each entry below gives a body fact, a principle, a business consequence, and the Scripture.
The principle and the business consequence are always inferences. The Scripture line gives
the text that governs the function. It does not claim that the text states the business
consequence.

## Rule two: analogy has a limit

The body is a living thing. A business is not. Do not stretch the comparison past what it can
carry. A body cannot choose its members, cannot change its species, and cannot be dissolved by
agreement. A firm can do all three. Where the analogy breaks, say that it breaks.

## Rule three: this skill does not give health advice

This skill reasons about organizations by analogy. It is not medical guidance. If the user asks
about their own health or the health of another person, say plainly that this skill is not for
that. Tell them that they must speak to a qualified physician.

## I. One organism, many members

### B1. The body is one and has many members.

The body fact: a human body holds tens of trillions of cells. They are one organism with one
interest and many functions.
The principle: unity of purpose with diversity of function.
The business consequence: a firm needs one mission and many specialties. A group that shares
functions but not purpose is not a body. It is a crowd.
Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:12 and 12:14 state this structure for the church. Verse 27 names
that body as the body of Christ. The application to a firm is an inference. It holds only
where the firm shares one interest and one mission.

### B2. No member is self-sufficient.

The body fact: every organ depends on the others. Without blood from the heart, the brain
loses consciousness in seconds and dies in minutes.
The principle: specialization creates mutual dependency by design.
The business consequence: every function needs every other function. Contempt between
departments is a design error, not a personality clash.
Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:21, "the eye cannot say unto the hand, I have no need of thee".

### B3. The weaker members are necessary.

The body fact: a person has four parathyroid glands, each about the size of a grain of rice.
Loss of all of them kills. A blocked arteriole, or a bed of failed capillaries, kills the
tissue it feeds.
The principle: criticality is not proportional to visibility.
The business consequence: the unglamorous functions carry the highest risk. Find the small
thing whose failure ends the firm, and protect it.
Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:22, "Nay, much more those members of the body, which seem to be
more feeble, are necessary".

### B4. Honor must flow to the unpresentable parts.

The principle: the allocation of honor is a decision. Left alone, honor flows to whatever is
visible.
The business consequence: reward the maintainers, the testers, the support desk, and the
people who answer the phone. A firm that honors only what it can show will lose what it
cannot show.
Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:23-24.

### B5. Membership is by assignment, not by preference.

The principle: role fit is a placement decision.
The business consequence: put people where they function, not where they want to be seen. The
person who wants the visible role is not always the right person for it.
Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:18, "God set the members every one of them in the body, as it hath
pleased him."

### B6. Suffering is shared.

The body fact: a local infection raises the temperature of the whole body. Pain in one toe
takes the whole attention.
The principle: local failure carries global cost.
The business consequence: one bad hire, one broken process, or one unpaid supplier changes the
state of the whole firm. Budget for the whole, not for the part.
Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:26.

### B7. Growth comes from the head, through the joints.

The body fact: central signals trigger growth, and connective tissue carries it out. If you
cut the connections, the signal stops arriving.
The principle: the center directs growth, and the parts deliver it.
The business consequence: direction is a leadership task. Delivery is an interface task. If
growth stalled, inspect the joints, which are the handoffs between teams.
Scripture: Ephesians 4:15-16, growth "by that which every joint supplieth".

## II. Control and feedback

### B8. The body defends a set point.

The body fact: temperature, blood pH, blood glucose, and blood pressure stay inside narrow
ranges. A deviation starts a correction, and the correction runs without conscious thought.
The principle: a healthy system corrects itself automatically.
The business consequence: build feedback loops, not only targets. A target with no
correction loop is a wish. Name the sensor, the threshold, and the response.
Scripture: Proverbs 27:23, "Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks". The command is
to measure, not only to want.

### B9. Pain is a signal, and the signal must be preserved.

The body fact: a small number of people are born unable to feel pain. They injure themselves
again and again, and many die young. The pain is not the disease.
The principle: the alarm carries information. If you silence the alarm, you remove the
information.
The business consequence: complaints, churn, error rates, and staff departures are pain
receptors. Fix the cause. Do not disable the sensor.
Scripture: Proverbs 27:5-6, "Faithful are the wounds of a friend".

### B10. Acute inflammation heals. Chronic inflammation destroys.

The body fact: inflammation is the repair response. When it resolves, the tissue heals. When it
never resolves, it damages the tissue that it came to protect.
The principle: urgency must have a time limit.
The business consequence: a crisis that ends is a cost. A crisis that never ends becomes the
operating disease. Set an end date on every emergency.
Scripture: Genesis 3:17-19 makes friction permanent. The response to permanent friction must be
a steady practice, not a permanent emergency.

### B11. The immune system must tell self from non-self.

The body fact: autoimmune disease occurs when the defense system attacks the tissue of the
body itself. The response is correct in kind and wrong in target.
The principle: a defense system needs an accurate target list.
The business consequence: do not attack your own people. Competition is external. Keep the
distinction between competitor and colleague.
Scripture: Matthew 12:25, "every city or house divided against itself shall not stand".

### B12. Fear degrades the instrument.

The body fact: sustained high cortisol is associated with impaired memory, immunity, and
judgment. The size of the effect in ordinary stress is contested. A frightened person usually
decides worse than a calm one.
The principle: anxiety damages the capacity to decide.
The business consequence: a fearful team hides information, and hidden information is the
expensive kind. Lower the cost of telling the truth.
Scripture: Psalm 94:19, Philippians 4:6-8, 1 Peter 5:7.

## III. Supply and flow

### B13. Fluid must circulate and must not pool.

The body fact: blood carries everything. A clot stops the flow, and the tissue downstream dies.
The tissue itself had no injury.
The principle: flow is life. Stagnation kills at a distance from the blockage.
The business consequence: cash withheld past what is fitting is a clot. Reserves are not.
Inventory that does not move is a clot. Find the blockages, not the shortage.
Scripture: Proverbs 11:24, "There is that scattereth, and yet increaseth". The same verse warns
against the one who "withholdeth more than is meet", which means more than is fitting.
Proverbs 21:20 commends reserves: "There is treasure to be desired and oil in the dwelling of
the wise". Ecclesiastes 11:1-2 commends generosity and spreading the portion, because the
future is unknown.

### B14. The barrier is selective, not absolute.

The body fact: the cell membrane and the blood brain barrier admit some things and refuse
others. A wall will starve the tissue. An open door will poison it.
The principle: a boundary must let things through in a controlled way.
The business consequence: hiring, security, and partnership need filters, not walls and not
open doors. Decide what crosses.
Scripture: Genesis 2:15, where the word rendered "keep" (H8104 shamar) means to guard or keep.
Inference: the garden is a guarded space. Genesis 3:24 uses the same verb for the guarded way
back.

### B15. Waste removal is a permanent function.

The body fact: the kidneys, liver, lymph system, and colon remove waste all the time. Retained
waste kills faster than starvation.
The principle: removal is as important as intake, and it never stops.
The business consequence: kill dead products, dead processes, dead inventory, and roles that
no longer fit. Pruning is a function with an owner.
Scripture: John 15:2, "Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away". Malachi 3:3
describes the refiner's fire.

### B16. Input quality sets output quality.

The body fact: the body builds itself from what it absorbs and from nothing else. Nine amino
acids, two fatty acids (linoleic and alpha-linolenic acid), and a set of vitamins and minerals
must come from outside.
The principle: the diet determines the result.
The business consequence: the information diet of leadership shapes every decision. Choose it
on purpose, because it will not choose itself.
Scripture: Philippians 4:8, and Proverbs 4:23, "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it
are the issues of life."

## IV. Growth and adaptation

### B17. The body adapts to the load it bears.

The body fact: bone remodels in response to load. Muscle grows only under demand. Unused
systems atrophy.
The principle: capability follows demand, not intention.
The business consequence: you cannot plan your way to capacity. Carry load to build it. The
load must also be real, and you must measure it.
Scripture: James 1:2-4, where trials produce patience. Hebrews 12:11. Romans 5:3-5.

### B18. Growth needs load and recovery together.

The body fact: training stimulus without recovery causes overtraining and decline. Recovery
without stimulus causes atrophy. The body needs both halves, and neither works alone.
The principle: challenge and restoration must be paired.
The business consequence: push without rest breaks people. Rest without push makes them stale.
Design the pair, not the halves.
Scripture: Genesis 2:2-3 gives the rhythm of six and one.

### B19. Repair happens at rest, and repair is what makes you stronger.

The body fact: tissue rebuilds during sleep. Growth hormone peaks in deep sleep. The
improvement arrives while the person does nothing.
The principle: the gain is in the recovery, not only in the work.
The business consequence: retrospectives, quiet planning time, and the deliberate offsite
produce the improvements that execution cannot produce.
Scripture: Psalm 127:2, "he giveth his beloved sleep". Mark 6:31, "Come ye yourselves apart
into a desert place, and rest a while".

### B20. The body oscillates.

The body fact: the heartbeat, the breath, the sleep cycle, digestion, and the hormonal cycles
all run as rhythms. A body without oscillation is a body in arrest.
The principle: rhythm is the operating mode, not a deviation from it.
The business consequence: set the cadence. The review rhythm, the release rhythm, and the
planning horizon are design choices.
Scripture: Genesis 1 repeats "the evening and the morning". Ecclesiastes 3:1-8.

## V. Limits and mortality

### B21. The body has a design limit.

The body fact: most human cells stop dividing after about 40 to 60 divisions in culture. Every
human body ages and dies.
Inference: aging is built into the design of the human body, and it is not a defect.
Mainstream biology does not treat aging as programmed, so this is not a body fact.
The principle (Inference): a creature is finite and dependent (Psalm 103:14).
Contested: Genesis 3:19 is part of the curse, and Genesis 2:17 and Romans 5:12 tie death to sin.
Scripture treats death both as creaturely limit and as an enemy. The body cannot settle the
question. The reference file `references/where-the-body-misleads.md` names this dispute.
The business consequence: a firm has a lifespan. Plan for succession, sale, or closure, and do
it while the firm is strong. A founder who cannot imagine the end will be surprised by it.
Scripture: Genesis 3:19. Psalm 90:12, "So teach us to number our days".

### B22. Redundancy in critical systems is design, not waste.

The body fact: the body has two kidneys, two lungs, two carotid arteries, and paired nerves.
The body has spares for some systems that must not fail. It has one heart, one liver, and one
pancreas, so the body does not duplicate everything.
The principle: protect the irreplaceable.
The business consequence: single points of failure belong at the top of the risk register. A
spare is cheaper than the failure.
Scripture: Ecclesiastes 4:9-12, "Two are better than one", and the threefold cord. Genesis 2:18.

### B23. Small failures kill the whole.

The body fact: the failure of a capillary bed leads to tissue death, then organ failure, then
death. The smallest unit is decisive.
The principle: small defects travel upward.
The business consequence: small leaks compound. Fraud, latency, waste, and one unprofitable
customer segment are capillaries.
Scripture: Song of Solomon 2:15, "the little foxes, that spoil the vines".

### B24. Breath is the first act and it continues without attention.

The body fact: the first thing a newborn does is breathe. Breathing then runs automatically,
and a person can take control of it at will.
The principle: the vital process starts first, then runs on its own. Deliberate control is
available.
The business consequence: find the breath of the firm. If it needs conscious effort every
hour, the firm is dying. If nobody can describe it, nobody can protect it.
Scripture: Genesis 2:7, "breathed into his nostrils the breath of life". Job 33:4.

## VI. Where the body misleads

The body is a fallen system, like everything else in the created order. Its logic is not a
moral guide. These eight cases are the ones most likely to lead a business astray. In each
one, Scripture clearly overrides the instinct of the body.

### M1. Self preservation against self sacrifice.

The body protects itself first. Scripture subordinates self-preservation to love. It does not
abolish it. John 15:13, "Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for
his friends." Philippians 2:4, "Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on
the things of others." Ephesians 5:29 and Proverbs 22:3 show that care for the self and
prudence are not sins.
The business consequence: do not let self preservation set strategy. The instinct is real, but
it is not the rule.

### M2. Strength as the measure of worth.

The body values vigor and fitness. Scripture honors the weak: 1 Corinthians 12:22-24, and
Matthew 25:40, "Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have
done it unto me."
The business consequence: do not let energy, youth, or physical presence become the test for
hiring or promotion.

### M3. Competition for resources.

Cells compete for nutrients. Scripture commands service and love, and it defines greatness as
service: Mark 10:43-45, "whosoever will be great among you, shall be your minister".
The business consequence: competition between teams for budget is not automatically healthy. It
must not become predation.

### M4. Appetite as a guide.

The appetites of the body are not moral guides. Genesis 3:6 shows desire that runs ahead of
judgment: "good for food", "pleasant to the eyes", and "to be desired to make one wise".
Proverbs 23:1-3 warns about the table of the ruler.
The business consequence: demand is not a mandate. What sells is not automatically good.

### M5. The instinct to hide weakness.

The body conceals injury. Scripture says the opposite. Text states: Proverbs 28:13, "He that
covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have
mercy." Genesis 3:10 shows the pattern and its cost: "I was afraid, because I was naked; and I
hid myself." Inference: 1 John 1:7 ("if we walk in the light") and James 5:16 ("Confess your
faults one to another") speak of fellowship and confession, and they support openness in a
firm by extension.
The business consequence: concealment is the default setting, and it is fatal. Build the
reporting channel before you need it.

### M6. Pain avoidance.

The body withdraws from pain. Scripture treats hardship as training. Hebrews 12:11, "Now no
chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it
yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness".
The business consequence: do not remove every difficulty. Some difficulty is the mechanism,
not an obstacle in front of the mechanism.

### M7. Short horizons.

Body fact: hunger and thirst return within hours, and people discount future rewards. The body
also stores fuel as glycogen and fat, and it runs long cycles. Inference: the appetites pull
toward the near term. Scripture commands generational thinking: Deuteronomy 6:4-9, and Psalm
78:4-7, "That they might set their hope in God".
The business consequence: the pull of the appetites is short by nature. Choose long horizons
against that pull, and defend them in writing.

### M8. The body adapts to whatever it is given, including the wrong thing.

A body adapts to a bad posture as readily as to a good one. Scripture says, "And be not
conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind" (Romans 12:2).
The business consequence: teams adapt to dysfunction and then call it culture. Adaptation is
not evidence that the adaptation is good.

## The procedure

Use these six steps when a question fits this layer.

1. Name the function in plain terms. What has to happen here?
2. Find the body system that performs that function. State the body fact.
3. Find the Scripture that governs the function, and read it in context.
4. Compare. If the body and Scripture conflict, follow Scripture and name the conflict.
5. Derive the practice for the actual situation.
6. Name the failure mode and the early warning sign, so the reader can watch for it.

If step 4 produces a conflict and the Scripture is not clear, say that it is not clear. Do not
resolve it by an appeal to the body.

## Output format

When you answer a question from this layer, use this shape:

1. The function, named.
2. The body fact, with the system named.
3. The governing Scripture, quoted from the text.
4. The point where the analogy holds, and the point where it breaks.
5. The practice that follows.
6. The warning sign of failure.
7. Confidence. Mark what the text states and what you infer.
