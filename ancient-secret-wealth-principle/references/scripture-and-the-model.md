## 1. What Scripture says about seeking wealth

This skill helps people earn more, so these texts govern every step of it.

Scripture says: "Labour not to be rich: cease from thine own wisdom." (Proverbs 23:4)

Scripture says: "But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition. For the love of money is the root of all evil" (1 Timothy 6:9-10).

Scripture says: "he that maketh haste to be rich shall not be innocent." (Proverbs 28:20)

Scripture says: "Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth." (Luke 12:15)

Scripture says: "But godliness with contentment is great gain." (1 Timothy 6:6) "be content with such things as ye have" (Hebrews 13:5).

Scripture says: the charge to the rich is "That they do good, that they be rich in good works, ready to distribute, willing to communicate" (1 Timothy 6:18).

Reading: Scripture does not forbid more income or more reach. It forbids the love of money, the haste to be rich, and trust in riches. To provide for a household is a duty: "if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith" (1 Timothy 5:8). To give is a purpose of work: "that he may have to give to him that needeth" (Ephesians 4:28). A wish for more is not by itself a reason. More income is a larger stewardship under 1 Timothy 6:17-19, not the goal.

## 2. The definition of wealth

Golden defines wealth as your ability to create value for someone other than yourself, or for many people, in exchange for pay.

Scripture says: "And thou say in thine heart, My power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth. But thou shalt remember the LORD thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth" (Deuteronomy 8:17-18).

Reading: in Scripture the ability to create value is itself a gift. Scripture also names riches that money does not measure: "rich toward God" (Luke 12:21) and "rich in good works" (1 Timothy 6:18). Golden's definition covers only the first kind.

## 3. The ethics check on the three sources of value

Reading: each of Golden's three sources of value can serve the buyer or exploit the buyer.

- Past voids. Name a real need honestly. Do not enlarge the pain to raise the price. Scripture says: "Thou shalt not curse the deaf, nor put a stumblingblock before the blind, but shalt fear thy God" (Leviticus 19:14).
- Present virtues. Appeal to real goods, such as quality, beauty and use. Do not sell status. Scripture says: "that which is highly esteemed among men is abomination in the sight of God" (Luke 16:15). Axiom A24 in `business-first-principles` gives the same warning from Genesis 3:6 and 1 John 2:16.
- Future visions. Promise only what the offer can deliver. Scripture says: "Whoso boasteth himself of a false gift is like clouds and wind without rain." (Proverbs 25:14) "And through covetousness shall they with feigned words make merchandise of you" (2 Peter 2:3).

For the full ethics of selling, read `../../business-first-principles/references/ethics.md`, Part 2 (Leviticus 19:9-18), Part 3 (measurement integrity) and Part 7 (speech).

## 4. Levels measure reach, not worth

Scripture says: "In all labour there is profit: but the talk of the lips tendeth only to penury." (Proverbs 14:23) This verse is in tension with any model that ranks words above labor. Talk without work leads to poverty.

Reading: the verse contrasts labor with empty talk, not with honest selling. Selling, writing and speaking are labor when they deliver the value that they promise. They become "the talk of the lips" when they promise more than the work delivers. So the high income figures for Level 3 do not override this verse. Persuasion has worth only when the thing it sells is true and worth the price.

Scripture says: God put the man in the garden "to dress it and to keep it" (Genesis 2:15). Work came before the fall. The axioms in `business-first-principles` make the same point in "A16. Work precedes the fall" and "A17. Building and keeping are equal partners". The body layer of `business-first-principles` (`../../business-first-principles/references/body.md`) makes it in B4, "Honor must flow to the unpresentable parts".

Scripture says: Scripture commends work done by hand. "work with your own hands, as we commanded you" (1 Thessalonians 4:11). "let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth" (Ephesians 4:28). "For thou shalt eat the labour of thine hands: happy shalt thou be" (Psalm 128:2). Jesus was called "the carpenter" (Mark 6:3), and Paul worked as a tentmaker (Acts 18:3).

Reading: hard work at the wrong level often produces exhaustion and not wealth. The fault is the leverage of the work, not the worth of the worker.

## 5. Golden's order of creation

Golden claims that the order of creation mirrors his value levels. His order is the image or idea, the spoken word, the unified purpose, and the physical forming. Reading: this is his reading of Genesis 1-2. The text does not state a value ranking, so do not present the mirror as Scripture.

The text also does not state his order. Genesis 1:1 says "God created" before Genesis 1:3 says "God said". The "image" of Genesis 1:26-27 is man, made in the image of God. Word meaning: the Hebrew word is H6754 _tselem_, and Strong's defines it as "resemblance; hence, a representative figure". It is not God's mental picture of a plan. Do not read the modern word "imagination" into "image". That is the 1611-English error that Rule two of `scripture-foundations` warns against.

## 6. A warning about Level 4

Scripture says: in the KJV, the words "imagine" and "imagination" occur in 36 verses. Almost all of them describe the evil or the vanity of the human heart. "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" (Genesis 6:5). "became vain in their imaginations" (Romans 1:21).

Scripture says: at Babel, "the LORD said, Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do" (Genesis 11:6). Their motive was "let us make us a name" (11:4). "So the LORD scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth: and they left off to build the city." (11:8)

Reading: Babel joined a unified people, one language, and a shared imagination for the sake of a name, and God stopped it. Reach is not a good in itself. Test Level 4 work by its motive and submit its plans to God. Scripture says: "A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps." (Proverbs 16:9) "For that ye ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that." (James 4:15)

Reading: the positive model for Level 4 is Bezaleel. God says, "I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship, To devise cunning works" (Exodus 31:3-4). His design work came from a gift, and it served the house of God.

## 7. The conscience and resistance to selling

Rule one in `scripture-foundations` forbids "the overriding of the conscience of another person". Scripture says: "for whatsoever is not of faith is sin" (Romans 14:23). Reading: if the user resists selling because the offer is not true or not worth the price, the conscience is right. Fix the offer, not the conscience.
