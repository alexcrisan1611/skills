# Simplifier and Multiplier

## What this skill does

A business needs two roles:

- The simplifier takes what is messy and makes it plain: a method, a rule, a template, a design, an offer. The test of the work is that someone else can act on it alone.
- The multiplier takes what is plain and repeats it at scale: through people, AI agents, software or social media. It copies faithfully, so that as many people as possible are served and drawn in by a product that works for them perfectly, completely and immediately.

Each needs the other. A multiplier with nothing plain to copy spreads confusion faster. A simplifier with no multiplier stays the bottleneck. This skill tests whether an idea is worth multiplying, designs the hand-over from one role to the other, and sets up the feedback that keeps the copy true.

Assume the user is a Christian. The skill reads one pattern from Scripture, Exodus 18:13-26, and uses it as the structure for every step.

## Rules

1. Quote the proof. Every time you cite a verse in your answer, quote its text as proof, word for word, from the lookup script. Never quote from memory. Never name the translation in your answer: give the reference and the words.

   ```
   bun run <skill-dir>/../scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.ts lookup "Exodus 18:22"
   ```

   `<skill-dir>` is the folder that holds this `SKILL.md`. Without Bun, use `python <skill-dir>/../scripture-foundations/scripts/scripture.py` with the same arguments.

2. Label every claim with the five labels of `scripture-foundations`: Scripture says, Word meaning, Reading, Disputed, Guess. Every business application is a Reading.

3. God gives the increase. The simplifier plants and the multiplier waters, but "God gave the increase" (1 Corinthians 3:6). Moses was told to "bring the causes unto God" (Exodus 18:19), and the counsel held only "If thou shalt do this thing, and God command thee so" (18:23). Never present this pattern as a technique that guarantees growth, and never promise a result.

4. The miracles are Christ's. In the feeding of the five thousand, Christ multiplied the bread. Nobody can copy that. Read only the order around it (seat the people in companies, give thanks, distribute through the disciples, gather the fragments) and say that it is an analogy.

5. Read the parables in their own setting first. Luke 14 is a parable of the kingdom: the feast is God's, and the guests are the poor who cannot repay. State that meaning before you draw any business analogy from it.

6. Ask why before you multiply. Growth is permitted, not a duty, and what you multiply reproduces after its kind (axiom A11 in `business-first-principles`). Ask what the added reach is for. For the rules on seeking wealth, read `../ancient-secret-wealth-principle/references/scripture-and-the-model.md`.

## The pattern: Exodus 18:13-26

Quote these verses as the proof for the step that uses them. `references/scripture.md` holds the full text and the reading of each one.

| Verses | What happens | The role |
|---|---|---|
| 18:13-18 | Moses judges alone from morning to evening. "Thou wilt surely wear away... thou art not able to perform it thyself alone." | The bottleneck: a simplifier with no multiplier |
| 18:19 | "Be thou for the people to God-ward, that thou mayest bring the causes unto God" | The source above the simplifier |
| 18:20 | "teach them ordinances and laws, and shalt shew them the way wherein they must walk, and the work that they must do" | The simplifier: make the way plain |
| 18:21 | "provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness" | The multipliers: character before capacity |
| 18:22, 26 | "every great matter they shall bring unto thee, but every small matter they shall judge" | The threshold: what returns to the source |
| 18:23 | "If thou shalt do this thing, and God command thee so, then thou shalt be able to endure" | The result, under God |

The threshold in 18:22 is the part most people leave out. A multiplier with no threshold either swamps the simplifier with every question or drifts away from what the simplifier meant. (Reading)

## The procedure

Run the steps in order. Quote the verse for each step as you use it.

1. Find the bottleneck (18:13-18). Find the first point where the work waits on the simplifier personally being there. Use `problem-solving-first-principles` for the diagnosis: location before source, source before act. Do not design the multiplier until you know which step it must take over first.

2. Name the source and the motive (18:19, 18:23; Psalm 127:1). Ask what the multiplication is for, and invite the user to bring the plan to God (James 1:5). The simplifier's clarity is received before it is given (A1).

3. Test the idea at the gate (A11; Luke 14:28). Before you multiply anything, test the idea against six required criteria: first-hand need, purpose beyond the market, existing tools fail, seen whole, lasting, and not a feature in waiting. Push back at the first sign of a mismatch, even when the user assumes the idea is fine, and ask questions one at a time until each criterion is shown or plainly fails. Give a verdict of pass, refine or no. The criteria, the pushback rules and the verdicts are in `references/idea-gate.md`. Read it before you run this step.

4. Write the way (18:20; Habakkuk 2:2). Turn the simplifier's judgment into three things: the rules ("ordinances and laws"), the path ("the way wherein they must walk") and the task ("the work that they must do"). The test is Habakkuk 2:2: "make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it." If the reader cannot act on it without asking the simplifier, it is not simple yet. For AI agents, the way is skills, templates and decision rules that they can load. The path from idea to approved, the idea file, the spec format and the done ladder are in `references/the-way.md`. Read it before you write the path or a spec.

5. Choose the multipliers (18:21; Proverbs 25:13). Choose for character before capacity: "able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness." For people, that means honesty and faithfulness. For AI agents and software, it means three things: they stay inside the written way, they report honestly what they did and what they could not do, and they never fake a result. A "faithful messenger" refreshes the one who sends him (Proverbs 25:13).

6. Set the threshold (18:22, 18:26). List the great matters that always return to the simplifier and the small matters the multipliers decide alone. Start with a tight threshold. Move a class of matters to the multipliers only after their record on it is faithful: "He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much" (Luke 16:10). State the record that earns each move, for example a number of approvals in a row with no edits. Keep a ledger of what has moved and when.

7. Order before you multiply (Mark 6:39-40; John 6:11). Before the bread was distributed, the people sat "in ranks, by hundreds, and by fifties," and the bread went out through the disciples. Give the multiplier its structure (stages, roles, queues) before you give it volume. (Reading, by analogy; the multiplying was Christ's.)

8. Check the copy (Mark 6:42; John 6:12). The standard is that "they did all eat, and were filled," and that nothing is lost. In product terms:
   - Immediately: it works on first use, with no help.
   - Completely: it does the whole job, with no workaround.
   - Perfectly: it passes layered checks. The multiplier checks its own work, an independent reviewer checks it again, and the threshold catches the rest. Layered checks multiply each other, the way the cell's copying is checked three times (`references/nature.md`).
   Do not multiply a copy that fails any of the three. A multiplier copies errors as faithfully as truth.

9. Draw people in (Luke 14:17, 14:23). The servant compelled the guests because the feast was ready: "Come; for all things are now ready." The product is the feast, and its working is what compels. Never compel through status, fear or a promise the product cannot keep (A24). See "Social media" below.

10. Close the loop (18:22; John 6:12). Feed three kinds of feedback back to the simplifier: what spreads and what dies (selection), the settings that change per context (configuration), and the great matters that need the way rewritten (escalation). "Gather up the fragments that remain, that nothing be lost": every failure, edit and question is material for the next version of the way.

11. Name the test and the limit. State the event that would show the multiplier fails, for example that the simplifier still redoes more than a stated share of its output after a month. If so, the bottleneck is the simplification, not the multiplier. Then name what this cannot settle: the increase is God's (1 Corinthians 3:6-7).

## Social media

Social media is one of the largest multipliers of reach, and it also copies after its kind: it spreads what people share, and people share what worked for them. Build it in this order, and explain the order to the user:

1. Demonstrations first. Show the product doing its job, before and after, in seconds. A demonstration is the most faithful copy, because it shows the work and not a claim about it. It is also a test: if a demonstration cannot show the product working at once, the product is not ready to multiply, and that is a great matter for the simplifier.
2. Built-in sharing next. Design each product so that customers share their own results. This is the largest multiplier, because every customer becomes one ("faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also", 2 Timothy 2:2). It comes second because it multiplies whatever the product does, including its failures, so the copy must pass step 8 first. The demonstrations also show which moments people respond to, and those are the moments to make shareable.
3. The simplifier's own voice, alongside. The simplifier teaches the way in public (18:20). It does not wait on product readiness, but it costs the simplifier's time, the resource the whole pattern protects (18:18). Keep it low in volume and high in signal: agents draft from the written way, and the simplifier approves.

Feed algorithms reward attention, not truth. Post the working product, never hype.

## Output format

1. The bottleneck: the first point where the work waits on the simplifier.
2. The motive: what the multiplication is for.
3. The gate: the verdict on each of the six criteria, and the questions still open.
4. The way: the rules, the path and the task, and whether they pass the Habakkuk 2:2 test.
5. The multipliers: who or what, and the character tests they must pass.
6. The threshold: the great matters, the small matters, and the record that moves each class.
7. The structure, the checks and the reach plan.
8. The feedback loops.
9. The test, the limit, and the confidence. Label each claim, and quote every verse you cite as proof.

Keep the answer short when the question is small.
