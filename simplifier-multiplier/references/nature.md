# The parallel in nature: the seed and its code

Use this when the user asks for the parallel in nature, or when a step turns on checking the copy or on feedback. It is an analogy, so name the point where it breaks.

## The parallel

"God said, Let the earth bring forth... the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself" (Genesis 1:11). Quote the verse from the script when you use it.

- The simplifier is the genetic code. A whole tree is written in an alphabet of four letters, small enough to fit in a seed, and the seed grows without the parent tree being there. That is the Habakkuk 2:2 test passed: the reader runs without the writer.
- The multiplier is the cell's copying machinery. It is fast and faithful, and it is blind. It copies an error as exactly as it copies the truth.

## Checking the copy: three layers

The cell checks its copying three times, and each layer multiplies the effect of the others:

| Layer | What it does | Errors per letter copied |
|---|---|---|
| Base selection | the copying enzyme picks the matching letter | about 1 in 10,000 to 100,000 |
| Proofreading | the same enzyme checks and removes its own mistakes | about 100 times fewer |
| Mismatch repair | a separate system checks the finished copy | about 1,000 times fewer again |
| All three | | about 1 in a billion to 1 in ten billion |

The figures vary by organism and by method of measurement. Present them as approximate.

The lesson for a multiplier: a self-check by the multiplier and an independent review multiply each other. One check, however careful, does not reach the accuracy of layered checks.

## Feedback: what nature does and does not do

Nature has two feedback loops:

1. Selection. Seeds whose trees survive leave more seeds, so the code changes across generations. It is slow and blind, and it is paid for in losses. Nobody reads a report.
2. Epigenetic memory. The letters of the code stay the same, but chemical marks on them change which genes switch on. In Norway spruce, the temperature while the seed forms sets when the offspring tree buds and goes dormant. A warmer seed gives later timing, and the tree keeps the setting for more than 20 years. The parent's conditions reach the child without a change to the code.

Neither loop is a source that understands what went wrong and rewrites the code on purpose. Selection pays in deaths, and epigenetic memory changes settings, not the design.

| Nature | Business | In the multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| Selection | the market drops what does not fit, slowly and at a cost | measure what spreads and what dies |
| Epigenetic memory | the same product set up for each context | settings per customer or audience, with no change to the way |
| (no equivalent) | Exodus 18:22: great matters return to the source | escalate to the simplifier, who rewrites the way |

## Where the parallel breaks

- The code does not choose, and the simplifier does. A business can learn from one failure, on purpose, in a day. Nature learns only across generations.
- The cell copies whatever it holds. People and customers who carry the copy have wills of their own, and the copy can be refused, changed or improved by them.
- Some changes in the code are improvements, and nature keeps them by selection. In a business, a change made by a multiplier is a great matter: bring it to the simplifier, do not let it spread unreviewed.
- The seed's power is God's word, "and it was so" (Genesis 1:11). The analogy describes a mechanism. It does not explain where life comes from.
