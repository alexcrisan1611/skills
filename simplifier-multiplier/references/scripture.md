## Exodus 18:13-26: the pattern

Scripture says:

> 18:13 And it came to pass on the morrow, that Moses sat to judge the people: and the people stood by Moses from the morning unto the evening.
>
> 18:14 And when Moses’ father in law saw all that he did to the people, he said, What is this thing that thou doest to the people? why sittest thou thyself alone, and all the people stand by thee from morning unto even?
>
> 18:17 And Moses’ father in law said unto him, The thing that thou doest is not good.
>
> 18:18 Thou wilt surely wear away, both thou, and this people that is with thee: for this thing is too heavy for thee; thou art not able to perform it thyself alone.
>
> 18:19 Hearken now unto my voice, I will give thee counsel, and God shall be with thee: Be thou for the people to God-ward, that thou mayest bring the causes unto God:
>
> 18:20 And thou shalt teach them ordinances and laws, and shalt shew them the way wherein they must walk, and the work that they must do.
>
> 18:21 Moreover thou shalt provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness; and place such over them, to be rulers of thousands, and rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens:
>
> 18:22 And let them judge the people at all seasons: and it shall be, that every great matter they shall bring unto thee, but every small matter they shall judge: so shall it be easier for thyself, and they shall bear the burden with thee.
>
> 18:23 If thou shalt do this thing, and God command thee so, then thou shalt be able to endure, and all this people shall also go to their place in peace.
>
> 18:26 And they judged the people at all seasons: the hard causes they brought unto Moses, but every small matter they judged themselves.

Reading:

- The bottleneck harms both sides. "Thou wilt surely wear away, both thou, and this people" (18:18). The people wait all day, and the one who serves them wears out. A business whose delivery waits on one person fails its customers and its founder at once.
- The simplifier is not the top of the chain. Moses stands "to God-ward" and brings the causes "unto God" (18:19). The counsel is conditional: "If thou shalt do this thing, and God command thee so" (18:23). The simplifier's clarity is received before it is given.
- 18:20 has three parts: the rules ("ordinances and laws"), the path ("the way wherein they must walk") and the task ("the work that they must do"). A written way that has only rules, with no path and no task, cannot be acted on.
- 18:21 sets character before capacity: "able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness." For agents and software, the nearest equivalent is staying inside the written way and reporting honestly. This is an analogy: an agent does not fear God.
- 18:22 and 18:26 name the threshold. The text's own terms are "great matter" and "hard causes" against "small matter." Who decides which is which was set in advance, not argued case by case.
- The result is endurance and peace, "able to endure... go to their place in peace" (18:23). The stated goal is not growth.

## Habakkuk 2:2: the test of a plain way

Scripture says:

> 2:2 And the LORD answered me, and said, Write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it.
>
> 2:3 For the vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie: though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry.

Reading: the vision here is God's, given to the prophet, and it concerns God's judgment and timing. The business use is an analogy: a written way is plain enough when the reader can run with it. The vision "shall speak, and not lie" (2:3), so the way must also be true, not only clear.

## Mark 6:39-43 and John 6:11-12: order, distribution and the fragments

Scripture says:

> Mark 6:39 And he commanded them to make all sit down by companies upon the green grass.
>
> 6:40 And they sat down in ranks, by hundreds, and by fifties.
>
> 6:41 And when he had taken the five loaves and the two fishes, he looked up to heaven, and blessed, and brake the loaves, and gave them to his disciples to set before them; and the two fishes divided he among them all.
>
> 6:42 And they did all eat, and were filled.
>
> 6:43 And they took up twelve baskets full of the fragments, and of the fishes.

> John 6:11 And Jesus took the loaves; and when he had given thanks, he distributed to the disciples, and the disciples to them that were set down; and likewise of the fishes as much as they would.
>
> 6:12 When they were filled, he said unto his disciples, Gather up the fragments that remain, that nothing be lost.

Reading:

- The multiplying is Christ's miracle. No method reproduces it. The analogy uses only the order around it.
- Order came first: the people sat "by companies," "by hundreds, and by fifties." Then thanks was given ("he looked up to heaven, and blessed"). Then the bread went out through the disciples. Then the fragments were gathered.
- The standard: "they did all eat, and were filled." All of them, and filled. Nothing was lost.

## Luke 14:16-23: the feast that compels

Scripture says:

> 14:16 Then said he unto him, A certain man made a great supper, and bade many:
>
> 14:17 And sent his servant at supper time to say to them that were bidden, Come; for all things are now ready.
>
> 14:21 So that servant came, and shewed his lord these things. Then the master of the house being angry said to his servant, Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city, and bring in hither the poor, and the maimed, and the halt, and the blind.
>
> 14:23 And the lord said unto the servant, Go out into the highways and hedges, and compel them to come in, that my house may be filled.

Word meaning: "compel" is G315 anankázō, "to necessitate", rendered elsewhere as "compel" and "constrain."

Reading: this is a parable of the kingdom of God. The feast is God's, the invitation is the gospel, and the guests brought in are the poor, the maimed, the halt and the blind (14:21). State that first. The business analogy is narrow: the servant could compel because the supper was ready ("all things are now ready"). The draw is the feast, not pressure on the guest. A product compels in the right way only when it already works for the person it is offered to.

## 2 Timothy 2:2: multipliers who multiply

Scripture says:

> 2:2 And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.

Reading: the text is about passing on the apostolic teaching. It shows four links: Paul, Timothy, faithful men, others also. Two conditions carry it: "the same" (the content is passed on unchanged) and "faithful men" (character). By analogy, a customer who shares their result is a multiplier of the multiplier, and the copy holds only if the content passes on unchanged.

## Acts 6:2-7: the source keeps the word

Scripture says:

> 6:2 Then the twelve called the multitude of the disciples unto them, and said, It is not reason that we should leave the word of God, and serve tables.
>
> 6:3 Wherefore, brethren, look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business.
>
> 6:4 But we will give ourselves continually to prayer, and to the ministry of the word.
>
> 6:7 And the word of God increased; and the number of the disciples multiplied in Jerusalem greatly; and a great company of the priests were obedient to the faith.

Reading: the apostles delegated serving tables and kept prayer and the word. The text then reports that the word of God increased and the disciples multiplied. The text does not say the delegation caused the increase; the increase is God's. The analogy: the simplifier hands over the work that others can do and keeps the work that only the source can do.

## Luke 16:10: the record that moves the threshold

Scripture says:

> 16:10 He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much.

Reading: faithfulness in small things is evidence for larger trust. The threshold starts tight and moves one class of matters at a time, on a record of faithfulness in that class.

## Proverbs 25:13: the faithful messenger

Scripture says:

> 25:13 As the cold of snow in the time of harvest, so is a faithful messenger to them that send him: for he refresheth the soul of his masters.

Reading: a multiplier that delivers the message as sent relieves the one who sent it. A multiplier that changes the message adds work instead of removing it.

## 1 Corinthians 3:6-7 and Psalm 127:1: the increase is God's

Scripture says:

> 1 Corinthians 3:6 I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase.
>
> 3:7 So then neither is he that planteth any thing, neither he that watereth; but God that giveth the increase.

> Psalm 127:1 Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain.

Reading: Paul is writing about ministry, not business. The principle the text states is that the planter and the waterer are nothing in comparison with God who gives the increase. The simplifier plants and the multiplier waters. Neither one owns the result.

## Genesis 1:11-12: the seed is in itself

Scripture says:

> 1:11 And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth: and it was so.
>
> 1:12 And the earth brought forth grass, and herb yielding seed after his kind, and the tree yielding fruit, whose seed was in itself, after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

Reading: the seed carries what it needs to reproduce, "whose seed is in itself," and it reproduces "after his kind." The power is God's word: "God said... and it was so." See `nature.md` for the parallel in nature and where it breaks.
