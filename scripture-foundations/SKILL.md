# Scripture Foundations

## What this skill is for

Most reasoning fails at the foundation, not the conclusion. This skill supplies three things:

1. **The text** — a complete, local, searchable KJV, so verses are quoted accurately rather than recalled.
2. **The map** — the chapters Scripture itself treats as load-bearing, and why.
3. **The method** — how to move from a text to a principle without proof-texting.

The rest of the skill family (`first-principles-thinking`, `business-first-principles`,
`business-idea-generation`, `problem-solving-first-principles`, `technology-decisions`)
builds on this layer. Load this one first when a question is foundational.

---

## Rule one: applications are inferences

This matters more than any technique here, and it cuts both ways.

The Bible is the source. A business principle drawn from Genesis is **not** Scripture — it is
an inference, made by a fallible reader, about how a revealed order applies to a commercial
situation. Say so. "Genesis 1 suggests…" is honest; "the Bible commands you to…" is not,
unless the text actually commands it.

Concretely:

- **Read in context.** Before quoting, read the surrounding verses. Check the speaker, the
  audience, and whether the passage describes what _is_ or prescribes what _ought_ to be.
  Many bad doctrines come from turning narrative into command.
- **Distinguish description from prescription.** Genesis 3 describes the curse. It does not
  command thorns. It tells you to expect them.
- **Do not manufacture certainty on disputed matters.** Where faithful readers differ,
  represent the range and let the user weigh it. False confidence here is a form of lying.
- **Never use Scripture to justify harm, exploitation, or the overriding of someone's
  conscience.** The keystone governs every application: love is the first principle of motive
  (1 Corinthians 13:1-3), and a reading that produces cruelty has misread something.
- **Label your confidence.** "This is stated plainly," "this follows from the text,"
  "this is my inference," and "this is speculation" are four different claims.

A foundation you cannot question is not a foundation. It is an assumption wearing a costume.

## Rule two: the KJV is 1611 English, and English moves

The King James Version was translated in 1611 and revised toward its familiar form in 1769.
Its English is not modern English. Several of its most theologically loaded words have drifted
since — some into near-opposites — and a great deal of bad reasoning rests on reading a modern
sense back into a 1611 word.

Two checks are mandatory before any inference is drawn from the text.

### Check the underlying Hebrew or Greek

Every KJV word is a translation choice, and the choice is not always the nearest one. Use
`scripts/lexicon.ts` over Strong's. Watch especially the words where the traditional rendering
now understates or redirects the original:

| KJV       | Reference        | Underlying word             | What it carries                                                               |
| --------- | ---------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| charity   | 1 Corinthians 13 | G26 ἀγάπη (_agapē_)         | love. In 1611 "charity" meant benevolent love, not almsgiving                 |
| faith     | Hebrews 11:1     | G4102 πίστις (_pistis_)     | persuasion, conviction, **constancy** — not bare belief                       |
| dominion  | Genesis 1:26, 28 | H7287 רָדָה (_radah_)       | "to tread down, i.e. subjugate" — stronger than custodianship                 |
| subdue    | Genesis 1:28     | H3533 כָּבַשׁ (_kabash_)    | to bring into bondage, force into service                                     |
| dress     | Genesis 2:15     | H5647 עָבַד (_abad_)        | to work, **to serve**, to till — the same verb used of worship                |
| keep      | Genesis 2:15     | H8104 שָׁמַר (_shamar_)     | "to hedge about (as with thorns), i.e. guard" — protective                    |
| beginning | Genesis 1:1      | H7225 רֵאשִׁית (_reshith_)  | "the first, in place, time, order or rank (specifically, a firstfruit)"       |
| help meet | Genesis 2:18     | H5828 עֵזֶר (_ezer_)        | "aid" — a word also used of God as Israel's help, not a subordinate assistant |
| talent    | Matthew 25:15    | G5007 τάλαντον (_talanton_) | a weight of money (~6,000 denarii, ~20 years' wages), **not** an ability      |

That last row deserves dwelling on. The popular reading of the parable of the talents as "use
your God-given abilities" is an anachronism: in 1611 "talent" was a unit of weight, and the
sense "natural aptitude" is later. The parable is about **capital entrusted, allocated
unequally, and accounted for** — a sharper and more demanding point than the one usually drawn
from it.

### Check the 1611 sense of the English word

Some KJV words have drifted into something close to their opposite:

| KJV word     | 1611 sense                   | Modern sense        | Example                          |
| ------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| let          | to hinder, restrain          | to permit           | 2 Thessalonians 2:7              |
| prevent      | to go before                 | to stop             | 1 Thessalonians 4:15             |
| conversation | conduct, manner of life      | spoken exchange     | Philippians 3:20; 1 Peter 3:1-2  |
| careful      | full of care, anxious        | attentive, cautious | Philippians 4:6; Luke 10:41      |
| suffer       | to permit                    | to endure pain      | Matthew 19:14                    |
| quick        | alive                        | fast                | Hebrews 4:12; 2 Timothy 4:1      |
| meat         | food, any provision          | flesh               | Genesis 1:29                     |
| bowels       | the inner person; compassion | intestines          | Colossians 3:12; Philippians 1:8 |
| by and by    | immediately                  | eventually          | Matthew 13:21; Luke 21:9         |
| peculiar     | one's own possession         | odd                 | 1 Peter 2:9; Titus 2:14          |
| want         | to lack                      | to desire           | Psalm 23:1                       |
| ghost        | spirit                       | apparition          | "Holy Ghost," throughout         |

The working rule: **whenever a verse is doing argumentative work in your reasoning, verify the
word before you use it.** Never build an inferred practice on an unverified modern reading of a
1611 word. If the underlying term will not support the inference, the inference is wrong, and
no amount of rhetorical force repairs it.

### Two failure modes in the other direction

- **Etymology is not meaning.** A word's root does not control its usage. The fact that _ezer_
  is related to words for aid does not by itself license a doctrine of marriage; the usage
  across the canon does. Lexical data constrains interpretation; it does not produce it.
- **Do not overclaim precision.** Translation is not mechanical, and the KJV translators
  sometimes rendered loosely. "The Hebrew is stronger than 'dominion' suggests" is a fair claim
  with the dictionary open. "The Hebrew proves Genesis 1 commands aggressive resource
  extraction" is not.

## The text: how to look things up

The KJV lives as one JSON file per book. Use the bundled script rather than memory.

```
bun run <skill-dir>/scripts/scripture.ts lookup "Genesis 1:1-5"
bun run <skill-dir>/scripts/scripture.ts lookup "Gen 1:26-28" "Ps 24:1"
bun run <skill-dir>/scripts/scripture.ts search "dominion" --limit 20
bun run <skill-dir>/scripts/scripture.ts search "foundation" --book Psalms --book Isaiah
bun run <skill-dir>/scripts/scripture.ts search "lov(e|eth) the Lord" --regex
bun run <skill-dir>/scripts/scripture.ts stats
```

Notes that will save you time:

- Book names are flexible: `Gen`, `1Cor`, `Song`, `ecc`, `Ps` all resolve. Ambiguous or
  unknown names produce a clear error rather than a wrong answer.
- Search is **literal and case-insensitive by default**; pass `--regex` for patterns.
- The source data contains curly apostrophes. The script folds them, so `Lord's` matches.
  If you read the JSON directly, fold them yourself — this breaks naive string matching.
- Data directory resolves from `--dir`, then `$KJV_DIR`, then `~/bible-kjv`.
- The whole Bible is ~31,102 verses. Search across all books is fast enough to run freely.

**Quote from the script output, not from memory.** Recalled wording is usually close and
occasionally wrong in the ways that matter most — "charity" for _agape_ in 1 Corinthians 13,
for instance, which changes how the passage reads.

## The method: text → observation → axiom → application

Four steps. Skipping the second is where most "biblical business" content goes wrong.

**1. Text.** Read the passage in full, in order, before extracting anything.

**2. Observation.** What is actually happening? Attend to:

- **Structure** — Genesis 1 has a two-pass shape (days 1-3 form domains; days 4-6 fill them).
  Structure is often the argument.
- **Repetition** — "and God said" (10×), "and God saw… that it was good" (7×),
  "and the evening and the morning were the Nth day." Repetition marks what the author
  wants you to notice.
- **Sequence** — what comes first, what depends on what.
- **Vocabulary** — the verbs and nouns the text chooses. In Genesis 2:15 the man is placed
  to _dress_ and _keep_ the garden, two different verbs doing two different jobs.
- **The negative space** — what is conspicuously absent, or named as "not good."

**3. Axiom.** State the underlying principle as a general claim, at the level where it stops
needing justification. Test it: is it derived from the observation, or smuggled in? Would it
still hold in a different situation? Does it contradict another axiom you hold?

**4. Application.** Derive the consequence for the actual situation, then price the friction
(Genesis 3:17-19 applies to every application: the ground resists). Name what could make the
derivation wrong.

**Worked example:**

| Step        | Content                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Text        | Genesis 2:15 — "And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it."                                                                                                   |
| Observation | Work is assigned before the fall. Two verbs, not one: _dress_ (cultivate, serve, bring forth) and _keep_ (guard, preserve, hold). Both are given as the job.                                                      |
| Axiom       | Creation and maintenance are co-equal duties, not a hierarchy. Building is not more holy than keeping.                                                                                                            |
| Application | An organization that rewards only shipping will accumulate maintenance debt until the thing it built cannot be kept. Budget for "keep" explicitly — it needs owners, calendar time, and status equal to new work. |
| Friction    | Maintenance is invisible and therefore underfunded in every incentive system. Naming it does not automatically fix the incentive; someone must hold the line.                                                     |

## The foundation map

Scripture names the category and develops it from beginning to end. Three tests select a
foundation chapter: **(1)** explicit foundation language ("first," "beginning," "foundation,"
"cornerstone"); **(2)** chapters later Scripture quotes as foundations — a chapter cited by
Romans, Hebrews, or 1 Peter is load-bearing by definition; **(3)** direct windows into God's
own mind and ways.

**The chapter that names the topic.** Hebrews 5:11-6:2 — the only New Testament passage that
literally says "first principles" (_archē_, "beginnings"): repentance from dead works, faith
toward God, baptisms, laying on of hands, resurrection of the dead, eternal judgment.

## The vocabulary of foundations

Where the text itself says "foundation," it is talking about what cannot be replaced without
destroying the structure:

- **Matthew 7:24-27** — the house on the rock. Foundations are tested by storms, not by plans.
- **1 Corinthians 3:10-15** — Christ the only foundation; every builder's work tried by fire.
- **1 Corinthians 3:11** — "other foundation can no man lay than that is laid."
- **Ephesians 2:19-22** — apostles and prophets, "Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone."
- **Isaiah 28:16** — the sure foundation in Zion; "he that believeth shall not make haste."
- **Hebrews 11:10** — the city "which hath foundations."
- **2 Timothy 2:19** — "nevertheless the foundation of God standeth sure."
- **Revelation 21:14** — the foundations of the New Jerusalem.

The nearest thing to a definition is **Hebrews 11:1**: "Now faith is the substance of things
hoped for, the evidence of things not seen." The Greek _hypostasis_ means "that which stands
under" — the same word used for foundation. A foundation is what you stand on before you can
see the outcome. That is the working definition for everything in this skill family.

## When a question is foundational

Not every question is. First-principles work is expensive — it takes longer than applying a
known pattern, and it is only worth it when the pattern is actually broken. Reach for this
skill when:

- The decision is hard to reverse, or the cost of being wrong is structural.
- The conventional answer has failed, or everyone agrees too quickly.
- The question is "why are we doing this at all?" rather than "how do we do this better?"
- Something is being justified by authority, fashion, or analogy rather than by principle.
- The user explicitly asks for foundations, Genesis, or first principles.

For everything else, use the pattern. Foundations are for building on, not for admiring.
