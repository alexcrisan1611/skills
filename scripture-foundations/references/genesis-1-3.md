# Genesis 1 to 3: A Close Read

Exegetical foundation for the `scripture-foundations` skill and for
`business-first-principles`. This file works through Genesis 1 to 3 in verse groups and draws
out the observations the rest of the skill family depends on.

Every verse below came from the local KJV through `scripture.ts`. Every Hebrew definition came
from `lexicon.ts` and is copied verbatim. Reproduction commands sit at the end of the file.

Labels used in this file:

- _Text states_: the verse itself makes the claim.
- _Lexical_: the claim rests on the Hebrew entry.
- _Inference_: this document draws the claim from the text. The text does not state it.
- _Contested_: faithful readers disagree, or the bundled tools cannot settle the point.

One limit applies throughout. The KJV text files are not tagged with Strong's numbers at the
word level. The tools can verify that a Hebrew entry exists and what it says, and that a verse
exists and what it says. The tools cannot verify that a specific word in a specific verse is the
number named. Where a Hebrew word is attached to a verse below, treat the link as the standard
concordance link and not as a tool result.

Where an observation carries an axiom number, the derivation lives in
`../business-first-principles/references/axioms.md`. The numbers run A1 to A34.

## 1. How to read this file

Genesis 1 to 3 is the account of a first venture. It has raw material, a maker, a method, a
domain, workers, a boundary, a failure, and a recovery. The skill family reads it that way. The reading
is an _Inference_. The text itself is a narrative about God and the world.

Two habits keep the reading honest.

First, separate description from prescription. The chapters describe what God did and what the
people did. They command very little. Genesis 3 describes thorns. It does not command thorns.

Second, separate the text from the tradition around it. Several popular readings of these
chapters came from later sources. Section 16 lists five of them.

## 2. Genesis 1:1-2, the source and the substrate

_Text states:_

- Genesis 1:1: "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth."
- Genesis 1:2: "And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters."

The opening clause puts God before the material. Nothing in the chapter explains where God came
from, and nothing explains where the raw material came from. The text treats both as given.
A1 follows from this: all value is derived.

### The verb

_Lexical:_ H1254 `bara`, _"(absolutely) to create; (qualified) to cut down (a wood), select,
feed (as formative processes)"_, from a primitive root, glossed "choose, create (creator), cut
down, dispatch, do, make (fat)".

The common claim is that this verb takes only God as its subject. The bundled tools cannot
verify the claim. The KJV files carry no word-level tagging, so no search here can find the
subject of a Hebrew verb. Treat the claim as a concordance tradition rather than a tool result.

_Inference:_ the claim matters for one reason. If the verb is reserved for God, then human
making is a different category from divine making. The skill family uses this distinction. It
does not make human work less real. It makes human work derivative. A4 follows: making is by
word, and the pattern is God's.

### The nouns

_Lexical:_ H7225 `reshith`, _"the first, in place, time, order or rank (specifically, a
firstfruit)"_, from the same as H7218, glossed "beginning, chief(-est), first(-fruits, part,
time), principal thing".

The word carries rank as well as sequence. A beginning is the first item and the governing item.
A1 and A14 both rest on that double sense.

_Lexical:_ H8414 `tohu`, _"a desolation (of surface), i.e. desert; figuratively, a worthless
thing; adverbially, in vain"_, from an unused root meaning to lie waste, glossed "confusion,
empty place, without form, nothing, (thing of) nought, vain, vanity, waste, wilderness".

_Lexical:_ H922 `bohu`, _"a vacuity, i.e. (superficially) an undistinguishable ruin"_, from an
unused root meaning to be empty, glossed "emptiness, void".

The pair describes a state with no order and no content. A2 follows: the default state is
formless and empty. Order is imposed work.

_Lexical:_ H2822 `choshek`, _"the dark; hence (literally) darkness; figuratively, misery,
destruction, death, ignorance, sorrow, wickedness"_, from H2821, glossed "dark(-ness), night,
obscurity".

_Lexical:_ H8415 `tehom`, _"an abyss (as a surging mass of water), especially the deep (the
main sea or the subterranean watersupply)"_, from H1949, glossed "deep (place), depth".

The darkness of verse 2 is not yet moral. The word gains the moral sense elsewhere. _Inference:_
reading verse 2 as a description of evil reads a later sense into the first occurrence.

### The brooding

_Lexical:_ H7363 `rachaph`, _"to brood; by implication, to be relaxed"_, from a primitive root,
glossed "flutter, move, shake".

_Text states:_ the Spirit of God moves on the face of the waters before anything is said. A3
follows: presence precedes production. Attention comes before output.

### Where the text speaks and where readers divide

_Text states:_ the earth was without form, and void, and darkness was on the face of the deep.

_Contested:_ readers disagree about whether that state was original or whether it resulted from
an earlier judgment. The text of Genesis 1:1-2 does not describe an earlier catastrophe, and it
does not describe a prior populated world. The Hebrew verb behind "was" is H1961 `hayah`,
_"to exist, i.e. be or become, come to pass (always emphatic, and not a mere copula or
auxiliary)"_. The word can carry either "was" or "became". That ambiguity is real. It is not
enough on its own to establish a gap between the two verses. A reader who holds the gap reading
holds it on other grounds. A reader who rejects it does the same.

## 3. Genesis 1:3-5, the first act

_Text states:_

- Genesis 1:3: "And God said, Let there be light: and there was light."
- Genesis 1:4: "And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness."
- Genesis 1:5: "And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day."

### Creation by speech

The chapter repeats one formula. The phrase "and God said" appears in ten verses of Genesis 1:
verses 3, 6, 9, 11, 14, 20, 24, 26, 28, and 29. _Text states:_

- Genesis 1:6: "And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters."
- Genesis 1:9: "And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so."
- Genesis 1:11: "And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth: and it was so."
- Genesis 1:14: "And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:"
- Genesis 1:20: "And God said, Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl that may fly above the earth in the open firmament of heaven."
- Genesis 1:24: "And God said, Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind: and it was so."
- Genesis 1:26: "And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth."
- Genesis 1:28: "And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth."
- Genesis 1:29: "And God said, Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat."

_Inference:_ the repetition is the argument of the chapter. Specification precedes manifestation.
What is said first becomes so. A4 follows: the quality of the output is bounded by the clarity
of the specification.

A note on the 1611 word "let." The word is on the false-friend list because it can mean
"hinder." That sense does not apply in Genesis 1. The "Let there be" form marks a command. The
standard grammar reads each one as a jussive of H1961 `hayah`, _"to exist, i.e. be or become,
come to pass (always emphatic, and not a mere copula or auxiliary)"_. The tools cannot verify a
verb form, so treat the grammar as the standard reading and not as a tool result.

### The noun

_Lexical:_ H216 `or`, _"illumination or (concrete) luminary (in every sense, including
lightning, happiness, etc.)"_, from H215, glossed "bright, clear, [phrase] day, light (-ning),
morning, sun".

### Division

_Lexical:_ H914 `badal`, _"to divide (in variation senses literally or figuratively, separate,
distinguish, differ, select, etc.)"_, from a primitive root, glossed "(make, put) difference,
divide (asunder), (make) separate (self, -ation), sever (out), [idiom] utterly".

_Text states:_ God divides the light from the darkness before he names either one. A6 follows:
distinction precedes order. A thing becomes definable when it is separated from what it is not.

_Inference:_ division is the first ordering act in the chapter, and it happens on day one. Scope,
segment, and boundary work is therefore not a later refinement. It is the beginning of making.

### Naming

_Text states:_ God calls the light Day and the darkness Night (verse 5). Later, God brings the
animals to the man to see what he will call them (2:19), and the man names the woman (2:23).

_Inference:_ A7 follows. Naming is an act of authority, not a label. The maker establishes what
a thing is by naming it. The text delegates animal naming to the man and keeps the naming of
Day, Night, Heaven, Earth, and Seas with God. Authority to name is real and it is granted.

## 4. Genesis 1:6-13, forming the domains

_Text states:_

- Genesis 1:6-7: "And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters. And God made the firmament, and divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament: and it was so."
- Genesis 1:8: "And God called the firmament Heaven. And the evening and the morning were the second day."
- Genesis 1:9-10: "And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so. And God called the dry land Earth; and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas: and God saw that it was good."
- Genesis 1:11-12: "And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth: and it was so. And the earth brought forth grass, and herb yielding seed after his kind, and the tree yielding fruit, whose seed was in itself, after his kind: and God saw that it was good."

### The word

_Lexical:_ H7549 `raqia`, _"properly, an expanse, i.e. the firmament or (apparently) visible
arch of the sky"_, from H7554, glossed "firmament" alone.

The English word "firmament" comes from the Latin `firmamentum`, meaning a support. The solidity
in the English word belongs to the translation chain, not to the Hebrew. The Hebrew word
describes an expanse. See `references/kjv-1611.md`.

### The two passes

_Inference:_ the chapter has a two-pass shape. Days 1 to 3 form domains. Days 4 to 6 fill them.
The text does not state this structure. It is a reading, and a widely held one. The
correspondence runs:

| Forming day    | What is formed                         | Filling day     | What fills it                                   |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Day 1 (1:3-5)  | Light and dark divided                 | Day 4 (1:14-19) | Lights placed to rule the day and the night     |
| Day 2 (1:6-8)  | Waters divided by the expanse          | Day 5 (1:20-23) | Fish fill the waters, fowl fly above the earth  |
| Day 3 (1:9-13) | Dry land separated, vegetation brought | Day 6 (1:24-31) | Land animals and man, given the plants for food |

_Inference:_ A5 follows. Structure comes first and contents come second. Filling before forming
does not produce a full thing. It produces clutter.

_Text states:_ the vegetation carries its own seed, described as "whose seed is in itself"
(Genesis 1:11). The domain is formed with its own means of continuation built in.

## 5. Genesis 1:14-19, time as an instrument

_Text states:_

- Genesis 1:14: "And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:"
- Genesis 1:15: "And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so."
- Genesis 1:16: "And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also."
- Genesis 1:17-18: "And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth, And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that it was good."

_Lexical:_ H226 `owth`, _"a signal (literally or figuratively), as aflag, beacon, monument,
omen, prodigy, evidence, etc."_, glossed "mark, miracle, (en-) sign, token".

_Lexical:_ H4150 `moed`, _"properly, an appointment, i.e. a fixed time or season; specifically,
a festival; conventionally ayear; by implication, an assembly (as convened for a definite
purpose); technically the congregation; by extension, the place of meeting; also a signal (as
appointed beforehand)"_, glossed "appointed (sign, time), (place of, solemn) assembly,
congregation, (set, solemn) feast, (appointed, due) season, solemn(-ity), synogogue, (set) time
(appointed)".

_Lexical:_ H3117 `yom`, _"a day (as the warm hours), whether literal (from sunrise to sunset,
or from one sunset to the next), or figurative (a space of time defined by an associated term),
(often used adverb)"_, with a long gloss list that begins "age, [phrase] always, [phrase]
chronicals, continually(-ance), daily" and continues through the year forms.

_Inference:_ A8 follows. Time keeping is a created function, and the instruments for it are
made things with stated purposes. Cadence is a design choice. It is not a natural fact that a
business inherits.

_Inference:_ the chapter does not use the ordinary names for the sun and the moon. It calls them
the greater light and the lesser light. The narrative reserves the names. A reader can see the
restraint. The reason for the restraint is not stated in the chapter.

_Contested:_ the length of the days. The text numbers them and uses the phrase "the evening and the morning". It
does not state a duration in hours, and it does not state that the days are ages. The word `yom`
carries both a literal and a figurative range. The instruments in verse 14 are for days and
years, which suggests ordinary time. The chapter does not settle the mechanism. See section 16.

## 6. Genesis 1:20-25, filling the domains

_Text states:_

- Genesis 1:20: "And God said, Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl that may fly above the earth in the open firmament of heaven."
- Genesis 1:21: "And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind: and God saw that it was good."
- Genesis 1:22: "And God blessed them, saying, Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let fowl multiply in the earth."
- Genesis 1:24: "And God said, Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind: and it was so."
- Genesis 1:25: "And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that it was good."

_Lexical:_ H5315 `nephesh`, _"properly, a breathing creature, i.e. animal of (abstractly)
vitality; used very widely in a literal, accommodated or figurative sense (bodily or mental)"_,
from H5314, with a long gloss list that includes "creature", "life", "soul", "person", and
"mind".

_Text states:_ the same word describes the animals here and the man in Genesis 2:7, where "man
became a living soul." The text gives the animals life and gives the man something more. The
difference appears in Genesis 1:26-27, which is the image. It does not appear in the word
`nephesh`.

### After its kind

_Text states:_ the reproduction principle appears ten times across five verses of Genesis 1
(verses 11, 12, 21, 24, and 25). The phrase is "after his kind" or "after their kind".

_Inference:_ A11 follows. Growth is intrinsic to the design, and seeds yield the species that
were planted. Culture, standards, and character replicate in hires and subsidiaries with high
fidelity. A person cannot scale a thing they do not want more of.

_Text states:_ verse 22 blesses the sea creatures and the birds with the same command that the
man receives in verse 28. Fruitfulness is a blessing before it is a duty.

## 7. Genesis 1:26-28, the image and the mandate

_Text states:_

- Genesis 1:26: "And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth."
- Genesis 1:27: "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them."
- Genesis 1:28: "And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth."

### The image

_Lexical:_ H6754 `tselem`, _"a phantom, i.e. (figuratively) illusion, resemblance; hence, a
representative figure, especially an idol"_, from an unused root meaning to shade, glossed
"image, vain shew".

_Lexical:_ H1823 `demuth`, _"resemblance; concretely, model, shape; adverbially, like"_, from
H1819, glossed "fashion, like (-ness, as), manner, similitude".

_Inference:_ the two words describe representation. A representative figure stands for the one
it represents. The word `tselem` also describes an idol, which is a substitute that claims the
place of the one represented. The man is the first kind of figure and not the second.

_Contested:_ the plural "Let us." Readers divide over whether it addresses the divine council,
the Trinity, or a plural of deliberation. The text does not explain it.

_Text states:_ verse 27 makes the image plural in its bearers. "Male and female created he
them." The image is not carried by one sex alone.

### The verbs

_Lexical:_ H7287 `radah`, _"to tread down, i.e. subjugate; specifically, to crumble off"_, from
a primitive root, glossed "(come to, make to) have dominion, prevail against, reign, (bear,
make to) rule,(-r, over), take".

_Lexical:_ H3533 `kabash`, _"to tread down; hence, negatively, to disregard; positively, to
conquer, subjugate, violate"_, from a primitive root, glossed "bring into bondage, force, keep
under, subdue, bring into subjection".

These are not gentle words. Both come from the language of a foot on a neck. The KJV choice of
"dominion" and "subdue" is weaker than the Hebrew, not stronger.

_Inference:_ a stronger verb does not license aggressive extraction. Three facts in the text
constrain the reading.

1. _Text states:_ the mandate is bounded. It names the fish, the fowl, the cattle, the earth,
   and the creeping things. It is a domain, not a blank check.
2. _Text states:_ the authority is granted to a steward. The text says "let them have dominion".
   It does not say that they took it. The verb is given before it is exercised.
3. _Text states:_ the owner keeps the title. Leviticus 25:23: "The land shall not be sold for
   ever: for the land is mine; for ye are strangers and sojourners with me." Psalms 24:1: "The
   earth is the LORD's, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein."

_Text states:_ Genesis 2:15 puts the man in the garden "to dress it and to keep it." The same
account that grants dominion assigns custodial work. A12 follows: dominion is delegated and
bounded.

The logical shape is a delegated authority under a retained ownership. A tenant with a strong
lease still has a landlord. Reading `radah` as a permission to extract is a reading of one word
against the rest of the account.

### The blessing

_Lexical:_ H6509 `parah`, _"to bear fruit (literally or figuratively)"_, from a primitive
root, glossed "bear, bring forth (fruit), (be, cause to be, make) fruitful, grow, increase".

_Lexical:_ H7235 `rabah`, _"to increase (in whatever respect)"_, from a primitive root, with a
very long gloss list that includes "multiply", "increase", "abundance", and "exceedingly".

_Lexical:_ the KJV word "replenish" is H4390 `male`, _"to fill or (intransitively) be full of,
in a wide application (literally and figuratively)"_, glossed with "replenish" and "fill" in the
same list. In 1611 "replenish" meant "fill." It does not imply a prior emptying.

_Text states:_ the command is given to both, "male and female," and it is a blessing before it
is an assignment.

## 8. Genesis 1:29-31, provision and evaluation

_Text states:_

- Genesis 1:29: "And God said, Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat."
- Genesis 1:30: "And to every beast of the earth, and to every fowl of the air, and to every thing that creepeth upon the earth, wherein there is life, I have given every green herb for meat: and it was so."
- Genesis 1:31: "And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day."

### Provision

_Text states:_ the food is given before the work begins. The man is placed in the garden to
dress and keep it in Genesis 2:15, after the provision of Genesis 1:29. A13 follows: provision
precedes assignment.

_Inference:_ the provision covers the animals as well as the people. The chapter states one
food system for both. A model that requires output before inputs is not disciplined. It is
starving.

A note on the 1611 word "meat." In Genesis 1:29 "meat" means food of any kind. It does not mean
animal flesh. The same verse gives "every herb bearing seed" as meat.

### Evaluation

_Text states:_ the phrase "God saw" appears seven times in Genesis 1, at verses 4, 10, 12, 18,
21, 25, and 31. Six of the seven say "it was good". The seventh says "it was very good", and it
covers "every thing that he had made".

_Inference:_ A9 follows. Judgment is part of production, not a separate phase. The maker
assesses the work against an intention, and he does it repeatedly.

_Inference:_ A10 follows, and this is the sharper point. Each day is called good. Only the
whole is called very good. Local correctness can compose into a global failure. Seven good parts
can make one bad product. Review the assembly, not only the parts.

## 9. Genesis 2:1-3, completion and rest

_Text states:_

- Genesis 2:1: "Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them."
- Genesis 2:2: "And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made."
- Genesis 2:3: "And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made."

_Lexical:_ H7673 `shabath`, _"to repose, i.e. desist from exertion; used in many implied
relations (causative, figurative or specific)"_, from a primitive root, glossed "(cause to, let,
make to) cease, celebrate, cause (make) to fail, keep (sabbath), suffer to be lacking, leave,
put away (down), (make to) rest, rid, still, take away".

_Text states:_ the work is finished, and the finished state is declared before the rest. The
seventh day is blessed and sanctified. Exodus 20:11 states the same pattern as a reason for the
command.

_Inference:_ A14 follows. There is a finish line, and it is declared. A thing that is never
finished cannot be evaluated, shipped, celebrated, or built upon.

_Inference:_ A15 follows. Six units of work and one of cessation. The ratio is given, not
earned. A plan that only works at full throttle forever is not a plan.

## 10. Genesis 2:4-17, cultivation and boundary

_Text states:_

- Genesis 2:4-5: "These are the generations of the heavens and of the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens, And every plant of the field before it was in the earth, and every herb of the field before it grew: for the LORD God had not caused it to rain upon the earth, and there was not a man to till the ground."
- Genesis 2:7: "And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul."
- Genesis 2:8-9: "And the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden; and there he put the man whom he had formed. And out of the ground made the LORD God to grow every tree that is pleasant to the sight, and good for food; the tree of life also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of knowledge of good and evil."
- Genesis 2:15: "And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it."
- Genesis 2:16-17: "And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die."

### The garden

_Lexical:_ H1588 `gan`, _"a garden (as fenced)"_, from H1598, glossed "garden" alone.

_Inference:_ the garden is a fenced place. A boundary is part of the design of the first
dwelling, and it is drawn before the fall. Access control is not a response to evil. A33
follows.

### The two verbs

_Lexical:_ H5647 `abad`, rendered "dress", _"to work (in any sense); by implication, to serve,
till, (causatively) enslave, etc."_, from a primitive root, with a gloss list that includes
"dress", "serve", "till", "labour", and "worshipper".

_Lexical:_ H8104 `shamar`, rendered "keep", _"properly, to hedge about (as with thorns), i.e.
guard; generally, to protect, attend to, etc."_, from a primitive root, glossed "beward, be
circumspect, take heed (to self), keep(-er, self), mark, look narrowly, observe, preserve,
regard, reserve, save (self), sure, (that lay) wait (for), watch(-man)". The dictionary entry
reads "beward", which is a typo in the source document.

_Inference:_ A16 follows. Work is assigned before the fall, in the garden, as part of the
design. Work is not the punishment and not merely the price of survival.

_Inference:_ A17 follows. Two verbs, two duties, given together as the job. Creation and
maintenance are co-equal. Most organizations die of a keep failure while congratulating
themselves on a dress success. The word `shamar` is a guarding word, and it carries the sense
of hedging a thing about.

### The permission and the prohibition

_Text states:_ the permission is wide and the prohibition is single. The command is given before
the pressure arrives and before the other party appears in the narrative. A20 follows: default
to generous permission, and keep prohibitions few, explicit, and unambiguous.

_Inference:_ a rule invented after the temptation arrives is not a rule. It is a negotiation.
The text gives the boundary first.

_Text states:_ Genesis 2:5 links the absence of a man to the absence of cultivation. The
sentence is a description of the state before the man. It is not a command. _Inference:_ the
link suggests that the ground reaches its intended state through work.

## 11. Genesis 2:18-25, the first "not good"

_Text states:_

- Genesis 2:18: "And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him."
- Genesis 2:19-20: "And out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field, and every fowl of the air; and brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof. And Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the air, and to every beast of the field; but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him."
- Genesis 2:21-22: "And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and he slept: and he took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh instead thereof; And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman, and brought her unto the man."
- Genesis 2:23: "And Adam said, This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man."
- Genesis 2:24: "Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh."
- Genesis 2:25: "And they were both naked, the man and his wife, and were not ashamed."

### The word

_Lexical:_ H5828 `ezer`, _"aid"_, from H5826, glossed "help" alone.

_Inference:_ A18 follows. In a chapter that calls everything else good, the first "not good" is
isolation. The correction is not a servant. The word is "aid."

The skill states that the same word describes God as Israel's help. The bundled tools cannot
verify that claim. They have no verse-level tagging, so they cannot show which Hebrew word
stands in which verse. The reverse map does confirm that the KJV renders H5828 as "help" and
nothing else. The claim about God as Israel's help rests on the standard concordance and on the
existing `SKILL.md` table. Treat it as the skill's reading.

A note on the 1611 phrase "help meet". The word "meet" is an adjective meaning suitable. The
phrase "an help meet for him" means a helper suitable for him. The phrase is not a job title and "meet" is
not a noun. See `references/kjv-1611.md`.

### The work that reveals the need

_Text states:_ God brings the animals to the man to see what he will call them. In the naming,
the man finds no counterpart.

_Inference:_ A19 follows. The gap was not visible from outside the work. It surfaced by doing
it. Execution is an instrument of discovery, not only of delivery.

### The union

_Text states:_ the man leaves and cleaves, and the two become one flesh. Verse 24 states the
pattern as a general rule, and Jesus quotes it in Matthew 19.

_Inference:_ A21 follows. A new union requires a real exit and a binding attachment. A venture
that keeps every prior option open is not a venture yet.

## 12. Genesis 3:1-7, the fracture

_Text states:_

- Genesis 3:1: "Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made. And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?"
- Genesis 3:2-3: "And the woman said unto the serpent, We may eat of the fruit of the trees of the garden: But of the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, God hath said, Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die."
- Genesis 3:4-5: "And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die: For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil."
- Genesis 3:6: "And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat."
- Genesis 3:7: "And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together, and made themselves aprons."

### The nouns

_Lexical:_ H5175 `nachash`, _"a snake (from its hiss)"_, from H5172, glossed "serpent" alone.

_Lexical:_ H6175 `arum`, _"cunning (usually in a bad sense)"_, a passive participle of H6191,
glossed "crafty, prudent, subtil".

The gloss list holds three words. The modern sense of "prudent" sits in the list, and the
definition puts the word in a bad sense. Do not build on the favorable gloss.

_Text states:_ the chapter calls the figure a snake. It does not call him Satan. Revelation 12:9
makes that identification later: "And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called
the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world". A reader who brings the identification
into Genesis 3 brings it from the last book.

### The attack

_Text states:_ the first move attacks the stated word. The question misquotes the command.
God gave the man free access to every tree except one. The question asks whether God said
they shall not eat of every tree.

_Text states:_ the woman's reply adds a prohibition. God said not to eat. She says not to eat
and not to touch. The addition is not in the command as recorded in Genesis 2:17.

_Inference:_ A22 follows. The attack opens on the reliability of what was said. Reputation is
attacked with questions, not with facts.

### The two levers

_Text states:_ the serpent denies the consequence and promises autonomy. The two lines are "Ye
shall not surely die" and "ye shall be as gods".

_Inference:_ A23 follows. Both levers appear every time. There is no downside, and you will be
free. An opportunity presented with no downside is being sold.

### The three channels

_Lexical:_ H2530 `chamad`, _"to delight in"_, from a primitive root, glossed "beauty, greatly
beloved, covet, delectable thing, ([idiom] great) delight, desire, goodly, lust, (be) pleasant (thing),
precious (thing)".

_Text states:_ verse 6 names three things. The tree was good for food, pleasant to the eyes, and
desired to make one wise. First John 2:16 names the same three: "the lust of the flesh, and the
lust of the eyes, and the pride of life".

_Inference:_ A24 follows. Appetite, beauty, and status. Every commitment is decided on all three
channels at once.

_Text states:_ the fruit is called "the fruit thereof." The text does not name the species.

## 13. Genesis 3:8-13, the interrogation

_Text states:_

- Genesis 3:8: "And they heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day: and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amongst the trees of the garden."
- Genesis 3:9-11: "And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou? And he said, I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself. And he said, Who told thee that thou wast naked? Hast thou eaten of the tree, whereof I commanded thee that thou shouldest not eat?"
- Genesis 3:12-13: "And the man said, The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat. And the LORD God said unto the woman, What is this that thou hast done? And the woman said, The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat."

### The sequence of questions

_Text states:_ the questions come in an order, and each one establishes something.

| Question                                                                             | Verse | What it establishes                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----- | ----------------------------------------- |
| "Where art thou?"                                                                    | 3:9   | Location, and the fact of hiding          |
| "Who told thee that thou wast naked?"                                                | 3:11  | The source of the new knowledge           |
| "Hast thou eaten of the tree, whereof I commanded thee that thou shouldest not eat?" | 3:11  | The act, against a command already stated |
| "What is this that thou hast done?"                                                  | 3:13  | The woman's account of her own part       |

_Inference:_ A26 follows. The first response to failure is diagnosis. Location, source, and act
come before any verdict. Judgment that outruns diagnosis produces the wrong verdict and teaches
people to hide.

_Inference:_ the questions are not requests for information. The text presents God as knowing.
The questions bring the person to speech. That reading is an inference from the narrative
shape.

### Hiding and blame

_Text states:_ the man hides, then explains the hiding as fear. He then blames the woman and,
in the same sentence, God who gave her: "The woman whom thou gavest to be with me". The woman
blames the serpent.

_Inference:_ A25 follows. Undisclosed failure produces hiding and blame. Shame leads to
concealment, concealment to blame, and blame destroys the ability to learn.

_Text states:_ the man and the woman both admit the act in the same breath. The admission is
present and the responsibility is redistributed.

## 14. Genesis 3:14-19, the consequences

_Text states:_

- Genesis 3:14: "And the LORD God said unto the serpent, Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field; upon thy belly shalt thou go, and dust shalt thou eat all the days of thy life:"
- Genesis 3:15: "And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel."
- Genesis 3:16: "Unto the woman he said, I will greatly multiply thy sorrow and thy conception; in sorrow thou shalt bring forth children; and thy desire shall be to thy husband, and he shall rule over thee."
- Genesis 3:17: "And unto Adam he said, Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree, of which I commanded thee, saying, Thou shalt not eat of it: cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life;"
- Genesis 3:18-19: "Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the herb of the field; In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the ground; for out of it wast thou taken: for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return."

### The serpent

_Text states:_ the serpent is cursed above the cattle and the beasts, moves on its belly, and
eats dust. The text does not say the serpent had legs before this verse. That detail is an
import.

### The woman

_Lexical:_ H6093 `itsabon`, rendered "sorrow", _"worrisomeness, i.e. labor or pain"_, from
H6087, glossed "sorrow, toil".

_Lexical:_ H8669 `teshuqah`, rendered "desire", _"a longing"_, from H7783 in the original sense
of stretching out after, glossed "desire" alone.

_Lexical:_ H4910 `mashal`, rendered "rule", _"to rule"_, from a primitive root, glossed
"(have, make to have) dominion, governor, [idiom] indeed, reign, (bear, cause to, have) rule(-ing, -r),
have power".

_Text states:_ the conception continues and the sorrow multiplies. Fruitfulness is not removed.
Its delivery cost is increased. A28 follows.

_Contested:_ the sense of `teshuqah` in this verse. The same word appears in Genesis 4:7, where
it describes sin's posture toward Cain. Some readers hear a strong longing in verse 16, and
others hear a turning that creates conflict. The tools supply the definition and cannot settle
the usage.

_Inference:_ A29 follows from `mashal`. Hierarchy after the fall becomes a site of struggle
rather than a smooth order. The contest is stated as a consequence, and the rest of Scripture
answers it with service rather than domination.

### The man and the ground

_Lexical:_ the word behind "sorrow" in verse 17 is the same H6093 `itsabon` that appears in
verse 16. The woman's pain and the man's toil are named with one word.

_Lexical:_ H6975 `qots`, _"a thorn"_, from H6972 in the sense of pricking, glossed "thorn".

_Lexical:_ H1863 `dardar`, _"a thorn"_, of uncertain derivation, glossed "thistle".

_Lexical:_ H2188 `zeah`, _"perspiration"_, from H2111, glossed "sweat".

_Text states:_ the ground is cursed, not the man. "Cursed is the ground for thy sake". The man
still eats. The bread still comes. The ground resists and the yield costs more.

_Inference:_ A27 follows. Friction becomes structural. Resistance is permanent, "all the days of
thy life." Every plan must price the thorns.

_Inference:_ A30 follows from "till thou return unto the ground". Finite time is the base
constraint under every plan.

### The first promise

_Text states:_ verse 15 places enmity between the serpent and the woman, and between their
seeds. It states a bruising on both sides.

_Inference:_ the victory is asymmetric. The promise is a head wound against a heel wound. A34
follows. Conflict is not a phase, and the long game is the only game.

_Text states:_ the verse is the first promise of recovery in the canon, and it is spoken to the
serpent rather than to the people.

### The asymmetry, stated plainly

| Line                         | Wound              | Result                                |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| "it shall bruise thy head"   | the serpent's head | a killing blow                        |
| "thou shalt bruise his heel" | the woman's seed   | a wound that does not end the contest |

## 15. Genesis 3:20-24, the covering and the guard

_Text states:_

_Text states:_ God makes the covering and clothes them. The fig leaves of Genesis 3:7 came from

_Inference:_ A31 follows. Failure is covered, and the covering required a life. The text does

than the person pays for it.

_Text states:_ the man names his wife Eve after the sentence is spoken. He acts on the promise

_Text states:_ the state of living forever in a broken condition is prevented. The man is sent

## 16. What this text does not say

Readers import a great deal into Genesis 1 to 3. These five readings are common and the text
does not support them.

### 1. That "dominion" and "subdue" authorize aggressive extraction

_Text states:_ the words are strong. `radah` is "to tread down, i.e. subjugate" and `kabash` is
"to tread down; hence, negatively, to disregard; positively, to conquer, subjugate, violate".
The reading that turns them into a license ignores three things in the same account. The
mandate names a bounded domain. The authority is granted rather than seized. The text says "let
them have dominion". The owner keeps the title, "The land shall not be sold for ever: for the land is
mine" (Leviticus 25:23), and "The earth is the LORD's, and the fulness thereof" (Psalms 24:1).
A steward with strong authority is still a steward. A reader who takes the verbs seriously must
also take the ownership seriously.

### 2. That work began at the fall

_Text states:_ the man is placed in the garden "to dress it and to keep it" in Genesis 2:15.
That verse comes before Genesis 3. The fall changes the conditions of work. It does not
introduce work. The curse reaches the ground and the yield. It does not create the assignment.

### 3. That the six days are 24-hour days, or that they are long ages

_Text states:_ the days are numbered, and each closes with "the evening and the morning". The
word `yom` carries a literal sense and a figurative sense. The chapter gives the sequence and
does not give a mechanism or a duration. This is a _Contested_ point, and the chapter does not
settle it. Both views are open to a reader. The reader must not claim the chapter settles it.

### 4. That Genesis 3 names Satan

_Text states:_ the chapter calls the figure a snake. The identification with Satan comes in
Revelation 12:9, "that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan". Reading the identification
back into Genesis 3 is a reading of the whole canon, and it is a reasonable one. It is not what
the chapter says.

### 5. That the forbidden fruit was an apple, and that the tree itself was evil

_Text states:_ verse 6 says "the fruit thereof" and names no species. The apple comes from a
Latin wordplay that does not exist in the Hebrew. The tree is also not called evil. It is called
"the tree of the knowledge of good and evil," and the prohibition concerns eating from it. The
text says the tree was good for food. The problem in the narrative is the command and the act,
not a poison in the fruit.

A sixth candidate belongs with these, and it is treated in section 2. The text does not say the
formless and empty state of Genesis 1:2 resulted from an earlier judgment. That reading is
_Contested_ and the two verses do not settle it.
