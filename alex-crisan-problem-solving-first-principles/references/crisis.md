# Crisis: first hour and after

First hour or later? If delay adds harm, decide now. Otherwise it waits for the diagnosis.

- Protect people first; accept the loss of the asset (Acts 27:22, 42-44).
- Keep a log: time with observation, headcount, the raw record before any interpretation.
- Write the stopping rule at the start: the end condition, a review date, and the person who declares the end. An emergency does not end by itself.
- Pray and set a watch, both (Nehemiah 4:9). Plan for exhaustion (4:10).
- Raise an ignored warning once, to earn trust for the next instruction, then move on (Acts 27:21).

After: close the losses, debrief the warning, diagnose, write the permanent change, name the next test.

Where readings go wrong:
- Paul's word in Acts 27:22-26 is a disclosure from God, not a forecast. No method transfers.
- His first warning was right about ship and cargo, not about lives (27:10, 24).
- The rescue is credited to God's promise and the centurion's will (27:24, 43).
- 2 Chronicles 20:12-17 is not "do nothing": a promise for one battle, and it still ordered movement.
