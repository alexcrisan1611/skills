# Business First Principles

## How to use this skill

Most business writing offers patterns: what worked somewhere else, generalized into advice. Patterns are cheap and often fine. They fail in three situations: a new market, a changed constraint, and a business that is dying for reasons nobody can name. In those situations you need the axioms under the patterns.

This skill supplies them. They come from Genesis 1-3, the account of the first venture. It has raw material, a maker, a method, a domain, workers, a boundary, a failure, and a recovery. Everything after it in Scripture assumes it.

Keep two things in mind when you use this skill:

1. These are inferences, not Scripture. Genesis states what God did. A fallible reader derives the business consequence. Say "Genesis 1 suggests," not "the Bible commands." Read `../scripture-foundations/SKILL.md` for the method and the guardrails.
2. Axioms are for deriving, not for reciting. A quote of an axiom is not an argument. The work is to show that the axiom governs this decision, and what follows from it here.

In each axiom below, "Inference:" marks the business consequence. The label is there because the consequence is a reading, not a statement of the text.

## The axioms

The axioms follow the groups of Genesis 1-3: the source, the method, the rhythm, the fracture. Each entry gives the axiom, its ground in the text, and the consequence that it forces.

### I. The source and the substrate (Genesis 1:1-2)

#### 1. All value is derived

Genesis 1:1: "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth."

Nothing is self-made. You receive the material, the mind, the market, and the moment.

Inference: ownership is stewardship. The correct posture toward an asset is responsibility for it, not possession of it. Entitlement is a factual error, and it is expensive. It produces founders who cannot learn, because they believe that they earned what they were given.

#### 2. The default state is formless and empty

Genesis 1:2: "And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep."

Chaos, emptiness, and darkness are the starting condition, not the anomaly. Order is work that someone imposes. Disorder is the resting state of everything.

Inference: decay is the baseline. Every system, relationship, and margin drifts toward disorder without continuous attention. A plan that assumes stability assumes a state that does not exist. Budget for entropy. That is not pessimism. It is physics with a precedent.

#### 3. Presence precedes production

Genesis 1:2: "And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters."

Something broods over the chaos before anything is said. Attention comes before output.

Inference: the cheapest hour in any project is the hour that you spend to understand the situation before you act. The maker who speaks first and examines later pays for it in rework. A rush to visible output is the most common way to be slow.

### II. The method of making (Genesis 1:3-2:1)

#### 4. Making is by word

Genesis 1:3: "And God said, Let there be light: and there was light." The phrase "And God said" occurs ten times in the chapter.

Specification comes before manifestation. The word is the main instrument of creation.

Inference: the clarity of the specification sets the upper limit on the quality of the output. Briefs, specs, contracts, promises, and names are production tools, not paperwork. An unclear word is not a soft problem. It guarantees an unclear result.

#### 5. Formation precedes filling

Genesis 1:3-31: days 1-3 create domains (light and dark, waters, land). Days 4-6 fill them (lights, fish and fowl, animals and man).

Structure comes first and contents second, in two passes, not one.

Inference: build the container before the contents. Build the platform before the features, the roles before the people, the chart of accounts before the spending, and the outline before the prose. If you fill before you form, you do not get a full thing. You get clutter, and clutter is harder to fix than emptiness.

Proverbs 24:3-4 states the same shape: "Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established: And by knowledge shall the chambers be filled".

#### 6. Distinction precedes order

Genesis 1:4: "And God divided the light from the darkness."

Meaning requires boundaries. A thing becomes definable when you separate it from what it is not.

Inference: you must be able to say what something is not. Scope, segment, and positioning are acts of division. A category that nobody divided has no meaning. A business that will not say "no" did not say "yes" to anything.

#### 7. Naming is an act of authority

Genesis 1:5: "And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night."

Naming is how the maker sets what a thing is.

Inference: names are decisions, not labels. Whoever names the category defines the terms of competition in it. Product names, role titles, and metric definitions each quietly set what people believe is true. Renaming is expensive because it means a new founding.

#### 8. Time is a created instrument

Genesis 1:14: "let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years".

Calendars, cycles, and seasons are made things, given for a purpose.

Inference: cadence is a design choice, not a natural fact. Review rhythm, release rhythm, planning horizon, and the fiscal year are all instruments that you can shape. A business with no cadence has no memory and no rhythm of correction.

#### 9. The maker evaluates the work

Genesis 1:4: "And God saw the light, that it was good". The pattern occurs seven times and ends at "very good".

Evaluation is not a separate phase. It is inside the making. The maker does it again and again, against an intention.

Inference: judgment is part of production. Taste is a deliverable. A person who builds without assessing is not building. That person accumulates. Build the assessment into the act.

#### 10. Whole-system review is not the sum of part-reviews

Genesis 1:31: "And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good."

God calls the work of each day good. He calls only the whole very good. Parts that are each correct can combine into a failure of the whole.

Inference: review the assembly, not only the components. Examples are end-to-end testing, an integrated P&L (profit and loss statement), review at the level of the portfolio, and the whole customer journey. Seven good parts can make one bad product.

#### 11. Multiplication is commanded, and it reproduces after its kind

Genesis 1:28: "Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth". See also "whose seed is in itself... after his kind" in verses 11-12, 21, and 24-25.

Growth is part of the design, and seeds yield the species that was planted.

Inference, by analogy: Genesis 1:28 speaks to humans, not to businesses. By analogy, a business that never reproduces lacks something that the design of living things includes. And what you plant is what you get. Culture, standards, and character replicate in hires and subsidiaries with high fidelity. You cannot scale a thing that you do not want more of.

#### 12. Dominion is delegated and bounded

Genesis 1:26: "let them have dominion over the fish of the sea... and over all the earth". See also 1:28.

Authority is real. It is real over a domain and under a law. It is granted, not seized.

Inference: delegate real authority with real limits, and state both. Authority without limits becomes tyranny. Limits without authority become paralysis. In a growing company, the most expensive misunderstanding is about which of the two the company grants.

#### 13. Provision precedes assignment

Genesis 1:29: "Behold, I have given you every herb... to you it shall be for meat."

God grants the resources before he requires the work of the worker.

Inference: fund the work before you operate. Fund the work before you demand from it. Deuteronomy 25:4 says the same: do not muzzle the ox that treads out the corn. Paul applies this law directly to the pay of workers (1 Corinthians 9:9-10, 1 Timothy 5:18). A model that requires output before inputs is not disciplined. It is starving.

#### 14. Completion is a real state

Genesis 2:1: "Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them."

There is a finish line, and God declares it. The work ends.

Inference: define "done" before you start, and mean it. Scope creep is not ambition. It is a refusal to accept limits that were designed. You cannot evaluate, ship, celebrate, or build on a thing that is never finished.

### III. The rhythm (Genesis 2:1-3, 2:15-25)

#### 15. Work is bounded; rest is structural

Genesis 2:2: "And on the seventh day God ended his work... and he rested".

There are six units of work and one unit of cessation. The ratio is given, not earned.

Inference: capacity planning must include rest, or the plan is wrong. This shows up as the sabbath and as the fallow year and the jubilee, which limit accumulation (Leviticus 25). It also shows up in a plain fact: burnout is a design violation, not a character flaw. A plan that works only at full throttle forever is not a plan.

#### 16. Work precedes the fall

Genesis 2:15: "And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it."

God assigns work before the curse, in the garden, as part of the design.

Inference: work is not the punishment, and it is not only the price of survival. It is where meaning is produced. A business that only pays, and makes nothing that anyone can love, will hold people with money and lose them anyway.

#### 17. Building and keeping are equal partners

Genesis 2:15, the same verse: to "dress" it (cultivate, serve, bring forth) and to "keep" it (guard, preserve, hold).

The verse gives two verbs and two duties together as the job.

Inference: creation and maintenance are equal. Maintenance is not junior work. Most businesses die of a failure to keep while they congratulate themselves on how they dress. The wall falls down while the new wing goes up. Give "keep" named owners, calendar time, and equal status.

Nehemiah 4:17 is the image. The builders worked "with one of his hands... in the work, and with the other hand held a weapon." They build and defend at once. Proverbs 24:30-34 is the warning. The field is grown over with thorns, the stone wall is broken down, and the diagnosis is: "Yet a little sleep, a little slumber".

#### 18. Aloneness is not good

Genesis 2:18: "And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone".

After a chapter that calls everything good, the first "not good" is isolation.

Inference: the earliest lack that the design names is a person without a counterpart. Partnership and counsel are not luxuries for the insecure. They correct a stated defect. "An help meet for him" is a corresponding strength, not an assistant.

Proverbs 15:22: "Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established."

#### 19. Doing the work reveals the need

Genesis 2:19-20: Adam names the animals, and in the naming he finds that no counterpart exists for him.

God states the need in 2:18, before the naming. But Adam finds it by doing the work.

Inference: execution is an instrument of discovery, not only of delivery. The real problem is usually next to the one you set out to solve, and it appears only once you are in motion. This is the case for prototypes and for shipping a smaller version sooner.

#### 20. Abundance precedes restriction; the boundary is few and stated in advance

Genesis 2:16-17: "Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it".

A wide yes comes first, then one clear no, given before the pressure arrives.

Inference: make generous permission the default, and keep prohibitions few, explicit, and clear. A rule invented after the temptation arrives is not a rule. It is a negotiation.

Daniel 1:8 is the pattern. He "purposed in his heart" before the request about the meat of the king.

#### 21. Commitment requires leaving and cleaving

Genesis 2:24: "Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh."

A new union requires a real exit and a binding attachment.

Inference: new ventures fail when founders try to keep every prior option open. A covenant structure is a real, costly, stated commitment. It produces behavior that a contract cannot produce. If you are not willing to leave something, you did not start anything.

### IV. The fracture and how to operate in it (Genesis 3)

#### 22. Trust in the stated word is the first target

Genesis 3:1: "Now the serpent was more subtil... And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?"

The attack opens on whether what was said is reliable, not on anything material.

Inference: the integrity of your promises is the asset most worth defending, and it is the easiest to damage. People attack a reputation with questions, not with facts. So you cannot defend it with facts alone. Say what you will do, do what you said, and correct the record plainly when you cannot.

#### 23. Deception works by denying consequence and promising autonomy

Genesis 3:4-5: "Ye shall not surely die... ye shall be as gods".

Both levers appear every time: there is no downside, and you will be free.

Inference: if someone presents an opportunity with no downside, someone is selling it. An offer of pure autonomy always hides a new master. These two signals are the fastest available test for a bad deal, a bad hire, or a bad partner.

#### 24. Desire runs on three channels

Genesis 3:6: "good for food... pleasant to the eyes... a tree to be desired to make one wise".

The three channels are appetite, beauty, and status. Inference: 1 John 2:16 ("the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life") lines up with the same three.

Inference: people decide every purchase, hire, and commitment on all three channels at once. If you appeal only to need, you lose to whoever appeals to beauty. If you satisfy need and beauty but ignore status, you lose to whoever offers standing. It works in both directions. It is the diagnostic for what you sell, and for how someone sells a bad decision to you.

#### 25. Undisclosed failure produces hiding and blame

Genesis 3:10-12: "I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself... The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree".

Shame leads to concealment, concealment leads to blame, and blame destroys the ability to learn.

Inference: the report of a failure must be cheap, and the person must survive it. A culture that punishes the messenger pays for the silence later. Blame is not accountability. Accountability is owning your part without pushing it onto others.

#### 26. Diagnose before you judge

Genesis 3:9-13: "Where art thou?... Who told thee that thou wast naked?... What is this that thou hast done?"

The first response to failure is a sequence of questions that establish location, source, and act.

Inference: establish the facts before you assign responsibility, and keep the two questions separate. A judgment that runs ahead of the diagnosis produces the wrong verdict and teaches people to hide. Ask, then weigh.

#### 27. Friction is structural

Genesis 3:17-19: "cursed is the ground for thy sake... Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee... In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread".

The environment now resists. Effort yields less. The resistance is permanent: "all the days of thy life".

Inference: every plan must price the thorns. Name the specific friction as a line item, not as a surprise. Examples are churn, compliance cost, the drag on recruiting, and technical debt. The ROI (return on investment) must cover the weeds. A model that works only in ground with no curse is not a model.

#### 28. Fruitfulness continues, but delivery costs

Genesis 3:16: "I will greatly multiply thy sorrow and thy conception; in sorrow thou shalt bring forth children".

The output still comes. The pain is in the bringing forth.

Inference: pain is not evidence that a course is wrong, because every real thing has a hard delivery. But pain is also not a license to ignore the signal. Tell the pain of producing apart from the pain of a broken thing. The first is the cost of the work. The second is information.

#### 29. Authority gets contested

Genesis 3:16: "thy desire shall be to thy husband, and he shall rule over thee."

After the fall, hierarchy becomes a place of struggle, not a smooth order.

Inference: expect the contest and design for it. Authority that service does not make legitimate becomes domination. Domination produces resistance, not followers.

Mark 10:43: "whosoever will be great among you, shall be your minister". 1 Peter 5:2-3: not for money, "Neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being ensamples to the flock."

#### 30. Mortality makes time the binding constraint

Genesis 3:19: "till thou return unto the ground... for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return."

Finite time is the base constraint under every plan.

Inference: opportunity cost is the real cost of everything. The correct question is rarely "is this good?" It is "is this the best use of the years it will consume?" Luke 12:16-21 is the failure mode: the man who plans barns with no account of his own end.

#### 31. Recovery is provided, and it costs something that is not you

Genesis 3:21: "Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them."

God covers the failure, and the covering required a life.

Inference: failure is survivable, and recovery is never free. Something or someone pays for it. Cash reserves exist to be the thing that pays. Build them before you need them. Treat them as the cost of permission to fail, not as idle capital.

#### 32. Some states must not be allowed to become permanent

Genesis 3:22-23: "and now, lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever: Therefore the LORD God sent him forth".

God prevented a bad condition from becoming irreversible.

Inference: identify the actions that you cannot undo, and put a gate on them. Examples are irreversible deletions, obligations that grow out of control, contracts with no exit, and architectures that you can never migrate off. Slow these down on purpose. Speed is for reversible decisions.

#### 33. Access is guarded, and the guard is a feature

Genesis 3:24: "he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword... to keep the way of the tree of life."

God sets the guard after the fall. The cherubim are part of the order that follows the fall, not the order of Genesis 1-2. God himself sets this access control, so it is not only a human reaction to evil.

Inference: permissions, locks, and limits belong in the design. The real, permanent cost of failure is lost access: to the garden, the market, the account, the trust. Guard the way back in.

#### 34. There is a permanent adversary, and the victory is asymmetric

Genesis 3:15: "it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel."

Conflict is not a phase. The promised win is to the head, and the cost is to the heel.

Inference: competition and opposition last. They are not a passing stage. Expect the wound to the heel as the price. Inference, by analogy only: the verse is a promise about the seed of the woman, not an instruction about competitors. The analogy is about where to aim. Aim at the head of the problem, its root cause, not at a competitor. A competitor is not the enemy. The long game is the only game.

## The procedure

For a decision that deserves real work, run these ten steps. They come from the axioms above. Each step is cheap compared with the failure that it prevents.

1. Name the decision and its real deadline (A30). Tell the deadline that exists apart from the one that feels urgent. The poor planning of someone else makes most urgency.
2. Separate what is given from what is chosen (A1). Make an inventory of the substrate: the market, the capital, the team, the constraints, the moment. You cannot reason from a starting point that you describe wrongly.
3. Find the governing axiom. Which one or two axioms really control this decision? Say why. If none applies, this is not a foundational decision. Use a pattern and save the effort.
4. Derive the consequence. Work forward from the axiom to what it forces here. Write the derivation out. If you cannot state a step, it is an assumption dressed as the axiom.
5. Price the thorns (A27). Name the specific friction, with numbers where you can. What will resist this? What will it cost each month? What is the plan for it?
6. Name the keeper and the cost of maintenance (A17). Who owns this after it ships? What does "keep" cost each year? If there is no keeper, the answer is that it will decay.
7. State the boundary (A6, A20). What is this not? What will you not do? How few can the rules be? Are they stated before the pressure arrives?
8. Define "finished" (A14). What condition that you can observe means done? Who declares it?
9. Assign the evaluator and the standard (A9, A10). Who judges this, against what, and at which level: component or whole? Make sure that someone reviews the assembly and not only the parts.
10. Set the cadence and the stopping rule (A8, A14). When is the next review? What result will make you stop, and what does it cost to stop? A decision with no stopping rule is not a decision. It is a commitment to drift.

### The short form

For decisions that do not justify the ten steps, these seven questions carry most of the load:

1. What here is given, and did I not make it? (A1)
2. What is the formless thing that I give form to? (A2, A5)
3. Who keeps this after it ships, and at what cost? (A17)
4. What are the thorns, specifically? (A27)
5. What is the boundary, and is it stated before the pressure? (A6, A20)
6. What does "finished" mean, and who declares it? (A14)
7. What will this make permanent that must not be permanent? (A32)

## Anti-patterns

Each of these is a direct violation with a predictable cost:

| Anti-pattern | Violates | The bill comes as |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Shipping with no owner for maintenance | A17 | Rot, then a rewrite (unsourced estimate: 5-10 times the cost of keeping it) |
| Filling before forming | A5 | Clutter, and features that cannot be evaluated |
| A plan with no stopping rule | A8, A14 | Endless projects, no learning, no exit |
| Growth with no stated boundary | A6, A20 | Scope explosion. You can no longer say what you are. |
| Assuming a stable baseline | A2, A27 | "Unexpected" churn, cost, and delay every quarter |
| Punishing the bearer of bad news | A25 | Late discovery, and the discovery is worse |
| Specifying by implication | A4 | Rework (inference: often the most expensive form of labor, because you pay twice for one result) |
| Judging without diagnosing | A26 | Wrong verdicts, and a team that hides |
| Avoiding commitment to keep options open | A21 | No venture, only a permanent cost of holding options |
| Full-throttle plans with no rest | A15 | Burnout, attrition, and a cliff |
| Growth in a thing you do not want more of     | A11 | Culture rot, copied faithfully at scale |
| Decision by urgency rather than by deadline | A30 | You spend your life on the fires of other people |

## Output format

When you answer a foundational business question, give the response this structure:

1. The decision: restated precisely, with the real deadline.
2. The substrate: what is given and not chosen.
3. Governing axioms: named, with the derivation to this situation.
4. The conclusion: what follows, stated plainly.
5. The thorns: the specific friction and its cost.
6. The keeper and the boundary: who maintains it, and what it is not.
7. Confidence: what the text states and what is inferred, and what will change the answer.

If the user asks a factual or tactical question, do not force this structure. The axioms are for foundations. The rest of the work is ordinary work.
