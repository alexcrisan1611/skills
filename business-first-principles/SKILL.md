# Business First Principles

## How to use this skill

Business writing mostly offers patterns: what worked somewhere else, generalised into advice.
Patterns are cheap and often fine. They fail in three situations — a new market, a changed
constraint, and a business that is dying for reasons nobody can name. In those situations you
need the axioms underneath the patterns.

This skill supplies them. They are drawn from Genesis 1-3, which is the account of the first
venture: raw material, a maker, a method, a domain, workers, a boundary, a failure, and a
recovery. Everything after it in Scripture assumes it.

Two things to hold onto while using this:

**These are inferences, not Scripture.** Genesis states what God did. The business consequence
is derived by a fallible reader. Say "Genesis 1 suggests," not "the Bible commands." Read
`scripture-foundations` for the method and the guardrails.

**Axioms are for deriving, not for reciting.** Quoting an axiom is not an argument. The work is
showing that the axiom governs _this_ decision, and what follows from it _here_.

## The axioms

Grouped as Genesis 1-3 is grouped: the source, the method, the rhythm, the fracture. Each line
gives the axiom, its grounding, and the consequence it forces.

### I. The source and the substrate (Genesis 1:1-2)

**1. All value is derived.**
_"In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth."_ — Genesis 1:1
Nothing is self-made. The material, the mind, the market, and the moment are all received.
_So:_ ownership is stewardship. The correct posture toward an asset is responsibility for it,
not possession of it. Entitlement is a factual error, and it is expensive — it produces
founders who cannot learn, because they believe they earned what they were given.

**2. The default state is formless and empty.**
_"And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep."_ — Genesis 1:2
Chaos, emptiness, and darkness are the starting condition, not the anomaly. Order is imposed
work; disorder is the resting state of everything.
_So:_ decay is the baseline. Every system, relationship, and margin drifts toward disorder
without continuous attention. Plans that assume stability are assuming a state that does not
exist. Budget for entropy — it is not pessimism, it is physics with a precedent.

**3. Presence precedes production.**
_"And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters."_ — Genesis 1:2
Something broods over the chaos before anything is said. Attention comes before output.
_So:_ the cheapest hour in any project is the one spent understanding the situation before
acting. The maker who speaks first and examines later pays for it in rework. Rushing to
visible output is the most common way to be slow.

### II. The method of making (Genesis 1:3-31)

**4. Making is by word.**
_"And God said, Let there be light: and there was light."_ — Genesis 1:3 ("and God said" appears ten times in the chapter)
Specification precedes manifestation. The word is the primary instrument of creation.
_So:_ the quality of the output is bounded by the clarity of the specification. Briefs,
specs, contracts, promises, and names are production tools, not paperwork. An unclear word
is not a soft problem — it guarantees an unclear result.

**5. Formation precedes filling.**
_Days 1-3 create domains (light and dark, waters, land). Days 4-6 fill them (lights, fish and fowl, animals and man)._ — Genesis 1:3-31
Structure first, contents second. Two passes, not one.
_So:_ build the container before the contents. Platform before features. Roles before people.
Chart of accounts before spending. Outline before prose. Filling before forming does not
produce a full thing — it produces clutter, and clutter is harder to fix than emptiness.
_Proverbs 24:3-4 states the same shape: "Through wisdom is an house builded; and by
understanding it is established: and by knowledge shall the chambers be filled."_

**6. Distinction precedes order.**
_"And God divided the light from the darkness."_ — Genesis 1:4
Meaning requires boundaries. Things become definable when they are separated from what they
are not.
_So:_ you must be able to say what something is _not_. Scope, segment, and positioning are
acts of division. A category that has not been divided has no meaning, and a business that
will not say "no" has not said "yes" to anything.

**7. Naming is an act of authority.**
_"And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night."_ — Genesis 1:5
Naming is how the maker establishes what a thing is.
_So:_ names are decisions, not labels. Whoever names the category defines the terms of
competition within it. Product names, role titles, metric definitions — each one silently
sets what people believe is true. Renaming is expensive because it means re-founding.

**8. Time is a created instrument.**
_"Let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and for years."_ — Genesis 1:14
Calendars, cycles, and seasons are made things, given for a purpose.
_So:_ cadence is a design choice, not a natural fact. Review rhythm, release rhythm, planning
horizon, and the fiscal year are all instruments you get to shape. A business with no cadence
has no memory and no rhythm of correction.

**9. The maker evaluates the work.**
_"And God saw the light, that it was good."_ — Genesis 1:4 (the pattern recurs seven times, ending at "very good")
Evaluation is not a separate phase. It is interior to making, repeated, and performed by the
maker against an intention.
_So:_ judgment is part of production. Taste is a deliverable. The person who builds without
assessing is not building — they are accumulating. Build the assessment into the act.

**10. Whole-system review is not the sum of part-reviews.**
_"And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good."_ — Genesis 1:31
Each day's work is called good; only the whole is called _very_ good. Local correctness can
compose into a global failure.
_So:_ review the assembly, not just the components. End-to-end testing, integrated P&L,
portfolio-level review, the whole customer journey. Seven good parts can make one bad product.

**11. Multiplication is commanded, and it reproduces after its kind.**
_"Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth."_ — Genesis 1:28 (with "whose seed is in itself… after his kind," verses 11-12, 21, 24-25)
Growth is intrinsic to the design — and seeds yield the species that was planted.
_So:_ a business that does not reproduce is not being prudent, it is failing a mandate.
And what you plant is what you get: culture, standards, and character replicate in hires and
subsidiaries with high fidelity. You cannot scale a thing you would not want more of.

**12. Dominion is delegated and bounded.**
_"Let them have dominion over the fish of the sea… and over all the earth."_ — Genesis 1:26, 28
Authority is real, and it is real _over a domain_ and _under a law_. It is granted, not seized.
_So:_ delegate actual authority, with actual limits, and state both. Authority without limits
becomes tyranny; limits without authority becomes paralysis. The most expensive
misunderstanding in a growing company is which of the two is being granted.

**13. Provision precedes assignment.**
_"Behold, I have given you every herb… to you it shall be for meat."_ — Genesis 1:29
The resources are granted before the work is required of the worker.
_So:_ capitalise before you operate. Fund the work before you demand from it. This is also
Deuteronomy 25:4 — do not muzzle the ox that treads the corn — which Paul applies directly to
paying workers (1 Corinthians 9:9-10, 1 Timothy 5:18). A model that requires output before
inputs is not disciplined, it is starving.

**14. Completion is a real state.**
_"Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them."_ — Genesis 2:1
There is a finish line, and it is declared. The work ends.
_So:_ define "done" before you start, and mean it. Scope creep is not ambition; it is a refusal
to accept designed limits. A thing that is never finished cannot be evaluated, shipped,
celebrated, or built upon.

### III. The rhythm (Genesis 2:1-3, 2:15-25)

**15. Work is bounded; rest is structural.**
_"And on the seventh day God ended his work… and he rested."_ — Genesis 2:2-3
Six units of work, one of cessation. The ratio is given, not earned.
_So:_ capacity planning must include rest, or the plan is wrong. This shows up as sabbath, as
the fallow year and jubilee limits on accumulation (Leviticus 25), and as the plain fact that
burnout is a design violation rather than a character flaw. A plan that only works at full
throttle forever is not a plan.

**16. Work precedes the fall.**
_"And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it."_ — Genesis 2:15
Work is assigned before the curse, in the garden, as part of the design.
_So:_ work is not the punishment and not merely the price of survival. It is where meaning is
produced. A business that only pays — that makes nothing anyone could love — will hold people
with money and lose them anyway.

**17. Building and keeping are equal partners.**
_Same verse: to "dress" it (cultivate, serve, bring forth) and to "keep" it (guard, preserve, hold)._ — Genesis 2:15
Two verbs, two duties, given together as the job.
_So:_ creation and maintenance are co-equal. Maintenance is not junior work. Most businesses
die of _keep_ failure while congratulating themselves on _dress_ — the wall falls down while
the new wing goes up. Give "keep" named owners, calendar time, and equal status.
_Nehemiah 4:17 is the image: the builders worked "with one of his hands… in the work, and with
the other held a weapon." Build and defend at once. Proverbs 24:30-34 is the warning — the
field grown over with thorns, the stone wall broken down, and the diagnosis: "yet a little
sleep, a little slumber."_

**18. Aloneness is not good.**
_"And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone."_ — Genesis 2:18
In a chapter that calls everything else good, the first "not good" is isolation.
_So:_ the earliest deficiency identified in the design is a person without a counterpart.
Partnership and counsel are not luxuries for the insecure — they are the correction of a
stated defect. "An help meet for him" is a corresponding strength, not an assistant.
_Proverbs 15:22: "Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of
counsellors they are established."_

**19. Doing the work reveals the need.**
_Adam names the animals, and in the naming discovers there is no counterpart for him._ — Genesis 2:19-20
The gap was not visible from outside the work. It surfaced by doing it.
_So:_ execution is an instrument of discovery, not merely of delivery. The real problem is
usually adjacent to the one you set out to solve, and it appears only once you are in
motion. This is the case for prototypes and for shipping a smaller version sooner.

**20. Abundance precedes restriction; the boundary is few and stated in advance.**
_"Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it."_ — Genesis 2:16-17
A wide yes, then one clear no — given before the pressure arrives.
_So:_ default to generous permission and keep prohibitions few, explicit, and unambiguous.
A rule invented after the temptation arrives is not a rule; it is a negotiation.
_Daniel 1:8 is the pattern: he "purposed in his heart" before the king's meat was set before him._

**21. Commitment requires leaving and cleaving.**
_"Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh."_ — Genesis 2:24
A new union requires a real exit and a binding attachment.
_So:_ new ventures fail when founders try to keep every prior option open. Covenant structure —
a real, costly, stated commitment — produces behaviour that a contract cannot. If you are not
willing to leave something, you have not started anything.

### IV. The fracture and how to operate in it (Genesis 3)

**22. Trust in the stated word is the first target.**
_"Now the serpent was more subtil… And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?"_ — Genesis 3:1
The attack opens on the reliability of what was said, not on anything material.
_So:_ the integrity of your promises is the asset most worth defending and the easiest to
damage. Reputation is attacked with questions, not with facts, which means it cannot be
defended with facts alone. Say what you will do, do what you said, and correct the record
plainly when you cannot.

**23. Deception works by denying consequence and promising autonomy.**
_"Ye shall not surely die… ye shall be as gods."_ — Genesis 3:4-5
Both levers, every time: there is no downside, and you will be free.
_So:_ an opportunity presented with no downside is being sold. An offer of pure autonomy
always conceals a new master. These two signals are the fastest available test for a bad
deal, a bad hire, or a bad partner.

**24. Desire runs on three channels.**
_"Good for food… pleasant to the eyes… a tree to be desired to make one wise."_ — Genesis 3:6
Appetite, beauty, and status. (1 John 2:16 names the same three.)
_So:_ every purchase, hire, and commitment is being decided on all three channels at once.
Appeal only to need and you lose to whoever appeals to beauty; satisfy need and beauty but
ignore status and you lose to whoever offers standing. This cuts both ways — it is the
diagnostic for what you sell _and_ for how a bad decision is being sold to you.

**25. Undisclosed failure produces hiding and blame.**
_"I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself… The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree."_ — Genesis 3:10-12
Shame leads to concealment, concealment to blame, and blame destroys the ability to learn.
_So:_ the reporting of failure must be cheap and the person must survive it. A culture that
punishes the messenger pays for the silence later. Blame is not accountability; accountability
is owning your part without redistributing it.

**26. Diagnose before you judge.**
_"Where art thou?… Who told thee that thou wast naked?… What is this that thou hast done?"_ — Genesis 3:9-13
The first response to failure is a sequence of questions establishing location, source, and act.
_So:_ establish facts before assigning responsibility, and separate the two questions. Judgment
that outruns diagnosis produces the wrong verdict and teaches people to hide. Ask, then weigh.

**27. Friction is structural.**
_"Cursed is the ground for thy sake… thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee… In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread."_ — Genesis 3:17-19
The environment resists now. Effort yields diminishing returns. Resistance is permanent —
"all the days of thy life."
_So:_ every plan must price the thorns. Name the specific friction — the churn, the compliance
cost, the recruiting drag, the technical debt — as a line item, not a surprise. ROI must cover
the weeds. A model that only works in un cursed ground is not a model.

**28. Fruitfulness continues, but delivery costs.**
_"I will greatly multiply thy sorrow and thy conception; in sorrow thou shalt bring forth children."_ — Genesis 3:16
The output still comes. The pain is in the bringing forth.
_So:_ pain is not evidence that a course is wrong — every real thing has a hard delivery. But
neither is pain a licence to ignore the signal. Distinguish the pain of producing from the pain
of a broken thing. The first is the cost of the work; the second is information.

**29. Authority gets contested.**
_"Thy desire shall be to thy husband, and he shall rule over thee."_ — Genesis 3:16
Hierarchy after the fall becomes a site of struggle rather than a smooth order.
_So:_ expect the contest and design for it. Authority that is not legitimated by service
becomes domination, and domination produces resistance rather than followership.
_Mark 10:43-45: "whosoever will be great among you, shall be your minister."_
_1 Peter 5:2-3: not for money, "neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being ensamples."_

**30. Mortality makes time the binding constraint.**
*"Till thou return unto the ground… for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return." — Genesis 3:19
Finite time is the base constraint under every plan.
_So:_ opportunity cost is the real cost of everything. The correct question is rarely "is this
good?" but "is this the best use of the years it will consume?" _Luke 12:16-21 is the failure
mode — the man who plans barns with no account of his own end._

**31. Recovery is provided, and it costs something that is not you.**
*"Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them." — Genesis 3:21
Failure is covered — and covering required a life.
_So:_ failure is survivable, and recovery is never free; something or someone pays for it.
Reserves exist to be the thing that pays. Build them before you need them, and treat them as
the cost of being allowed to fail, not as idle capital.

**32. Some states must not be allowed to become permanent.**
*"And now, lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever: therefore the LORD God sent him forth." — Genesis 3:22-23
A bad condition was prevented from becoming irreversible.
_So:_ identify the actions that cannot be undone and gate them — irreversible deletions,
runaway obligations, contracts without exit, architectures you can never migrate off. Slow
these down on purpose. Speed is for reversible decisions.

**33. Access is guarded, and the guard is a feature.**
*"He placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword… to keep the way of the tree of life." — Genesis 3:24
Security and access control are established in the good order, not only against evil.
_So:_ permissions, locks, and limits belong in the design. The real, permanent cost of failure
is lost access — to the garden, the market, the account, the trust. Guard the way back in.

**34. There is a permanent adversary, and the victory is asymmetric.**
*"It shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." — Genesis 3:15
Conflict is not a phase. The promised win is to the head; the cost is to the heel.
_So:_ competition and opposition are enduring, not transitional. Expect the heel-wound as the
price of the head-crush — and aim at the head. The long game is the only game.

## The procedure

For a decision that warrants real work, run these ten steps. They are derived from the axioms
above, and each one is cheap compared to the failure it prevents.

1. **Name the decision and its actual deadline.** (A30) Distinguish the deadline that exists
   from the one that feels urgent. Most urgency is manufactured by someone else's poor planning.
2. **Separate what is given from what is chosen.** (A1) Inventory the substrate: the market,
   the capital, the team, the constraints, the moment. You cannot reason from a starting point
   you have misdescribed.
3. **Locate the governing axiom.** Which one or two actually control this decision? Say why.
   If none applies, this is not a foundational decision — use a pattern and save the effort.
4. **Derive the consequence.** Work forward from the axiom to what it forces _here_. Write the
   derivation out. If a step cannot be stated, it is an assumption wearing the axiom's clothes.
5. **Price the thorns.** (A27) Name the specific friction, numerically if possible. What will
   resist this, what will it cost per month, and what is the plan for it?
6. **Name the keeper and the maintenance cost.** (A17) Who owns this after it ships? What does
   "keep" cost annually? If there is no keeper, the answer is that it will decay.
7. **State the boundary.** (A6, A20) What is this not? What will you not do? How few rules can
   it be, and are they stated before the pressure arrives?
8. **Define "finished."** (A14) What observable condition means done? Who declares it?
9. **Assign the evaluator and the standard.** (A9, A10) Who judges this, against what, and at
   which level — component or whole? Make sure someone is reviewing the assembly and not only
   the parts.
10. **Set the cadence and the stopping rule.** (A8, A15) When is the next review? What result
    would make you stop, and what is the cost of stopping? A decision with no stopping rule is
    not a decision, it is a commitment to drift.

### The short form

For decisions that do not justify the ten steps, these seven questions carry most of the load:

1. What here is given, and did I not make it? (A1)
2. What is the formless thing I am giving form to? (A2, A5)
3. Who keeps this after it ships, and at what cost? (A17)
4. What are the thorns, specifically? (A27)
5. What is the boundary, and is it stated before the pressure? (A6, A20)
6. What does "finished" mean, and who declares it? (A14)
7. What would this make permanent that should not be? (A32)

## Anti-patterns

Each of these is a direct violation with a predictable cost:

| Anti-pattern                                 | Violates | The bill arrives as                                 |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Shipping with no owner for maintenance       | A17      | Rot, then a rewrite at 5-10× cost                   |
| Filling before forming                       | A5       | Clutter; features that cannot be evaluated          |
| A plan with no stopping rule                 | A10, A14 | Endless projects, no learning, no exit              |
| Growth with no stated boundary               | A6, A20  | Scope explosion; you can no longer say what you are |
| Assuming a stable baseline                   | A2, A27  | "Unexpected" churn, cost, and delay every quarter   |
| Punishing the bearer of bad news             | A25      | Late discovery, and the discovery is worse          |
| Specifying by implication                    | A4       | Rework — the most expensive form of labour          |
| Judging without diagnosing                   | A26      | Wrong verdicts, and a team that hides               |
| Avoiding commitment to keep options open     | A21      | No venture, only a permanent option-holding cost    |
| Full-throttle plans with no rest             | A15      | Burnout, attrition, and a cliff                     |
| Growth in a thing you would not want more of | A11      | Culture rot, replicated faithfully at scale         |
| Decision by urgency rather than by deadline  | A30      | You spend your life on other people's fires         |

## Output format

When answering a foundational business question, structure the response as:

1. **The decision** — restated precisely, with the real deadline.
2. **The substrate** — what is given and not chosen.
3. **Governing axioms** — named, with the derivation to this situation.
4. **The conclusion** — what follows, stated plainly.
5. **The thorns** — specific friction and its cost.
6. **The keeper and the boundary** — who maintains it; what it is not.
7. **Confidence** — plainly stated versus inferred, and what would change the answer.

If the user is asking a factual or tactical question, do not force this structure. The axioms
are for foundations; the rest of the work is just work.
