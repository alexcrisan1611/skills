# Org and Labour

Structure, delegation, hiring, pay, and succession. What Scripture states about each, what I
infer, and where the inference is strained by the distance between an agrarian covenant community
and a modern firm.

## Method and labels

**Text states** / **Lexical** / **Inference** / **Speculation**. Verses from `scripture.ts`,
Strong's definitions verbatim from `lexicon.ts`.

The governing axioms for this file are **A12** (dominion is delegated and bounded), **A17**
(building and keeping are equal partners), **A18** (aloneness is not good), **A13** (provision
precedes assignment), **A29** (authority gets contested), and **A11** (multiplication reproduces
after its kind).

## Part 1. Exodus 18: the first management fix

**Text (Exodus 18:13–27, KJV)**

>     by Moses from the morning unto the evening.
> 14. And when Moses' father in law saw all that he did to the people, he said, What is this thing
>     that thou doest to the people? why sittest thou thyself alone, and all the people stand by thee
>     from morning unto even?
> 15. And Moses said unto his father in law, Because the people come unto me to enquire of God:
> 16. When they have a matter, they come unto me; and I judge between one and another, and I do
>     make them know the statutes of God, and his laws.
> 17. And Moses' father in law said unto him, The thing that thou doest is not good.
> 18. Thou wilt surely wear away, both thou, and this people that is with thee: for this thing is
>     too heavy for thee; thou art not able to perform it thyself alone.
> 19. Hearken now unto my voice, I will give thee counsel, and God shall be with thee: Be thou for
>     the people to God-ward, that thou mayest bring the causes unto God:
> 20. And thou shalt teach them ordinances and laws, and shalt shew them the way wherein they must
>     walk, and the work that they must do.
> 21. Moreover thou shalt provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God, men of truth,
>     hating covetousness; and place such over them, to be rulers of thousands, and rulers of
>     hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens:
> 22. And let them judge the people at all seasons: and it shall be, that every great matter they
>     shall bring unto thee, but every small matter they shall judge: so shall it be easier for
>     thyself, and they shall bear the burden with thee.
> 23. If thou shalt do this thing, and God command thee so, then thou shalt be able to endure, and
>     all this people shall also go to their place in peace.
> 24. So Moses hearkened to the voice of his father in law, and did all that he had said.
> 25. And Moses chose able men out of all Israel, and made them heads over the people, rulers of
>     thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens.
> 26. And they judged the people at all seasons: the hard causes they brought unto Moses, but every
>     small matter they judged themselves.
> 27. And Moses let his father in law depart; and he went his way into his own land.

### The diagnosis: "the thing is too heavy for thee"

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The problem is framed in terms of _duration and volume_: "from the morning unto
   the evening" (v. 13), and the same phrase in Jethro's question — "from morning unto even"
   (v. 14).
2. **Text states:** Jethro's first question is diagnostic, not advisory: "What is this thing that
   thou doest to the people?" (v. 14). He asks what is happening before saying what to do.
3. **Text states:** The second question identifies the structural defect: "why sittest thou thyself
   alone, and all the people stand by thee?" (v. 14). **Inference:** the word _alone_ is the
   diagnosis, and it is the same word used in Genesis 2:18 ("It is not good that the man should be
   alone"). Jethro's verdict echoes it: "The thing that thou doest is not good" (v. 17).
4. **Text states:** The cost is stated in two directions: "Thou wilt surely wear away, **both thou,
   and this people that is with thee**" (v. 18). The overload damages the judge _and_ the judged.
5. **Text states:** The cause is stated: "for this thing is too heavy for thee; thou art not able
   to perform it thyself alone" (v. 18). **Inference:** this is not a claim that Moses is
   incompetent. It is a claim that the _thing_ exceeds one person's capacity, which is a fact about
   the work, not the worker.
6. **Text states:** Moses' own account of what he does (vv. 15–16) contains two distinct functions:
   _enquiry of God_ (v. 15) and _judging between one and another_ plus _teaching the statutes_
   (v. 16). He describes three jobs as one.
7. **Text states:** Jethro's advice is conditional on divine approval: "If thou shalt do this
   thing, and God command thee so" (v. 23). **Inference:** the advice is offered as counsel, not
   as command, and it is explicitly submitted for a higher check. This is A18's "multitude of
   counsellors" in practice — the counsel is heard and tested.
8. **Text states:** Moses "hearkened" (v. 24) — and the text gives no record of a divine objection.
   The counsel was adopted.

### The four criteria for the delegated tier

**Text states:** "Moreover thou shalt provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God,
men of truth, hating covetousness" (v. 21).

Four criteria, in order:

**Observations on the list:**

1. **Text states:** The criteria are _moral and characterological_, not technical. There is no
   requirement for legal expertise, eloquence, or seniority. The four criteria are: capacity,
   orientation, honesty, and incorruptibility.
2. **Text states:** Criterion 4 is stated as _hatred_, not mere avoidance: "hating covetousness."
   **Inference:** the standard is an active disposition against the specific temptation of the
   role, not a neutral absence of vice. The role is judging disputes, which is precisely where
   bribes arrive.
3. **Text states:** The pool is "out of all the people" (v. 21). The selection is from the whole
   population, not from an existing elite or from Moses' family.
4. **Text states:** The word for the appointees is **H8269 שַׂר (_sar_)** — "a head person (of any
   rank or class)." KJV: "captain (that had rule), chief (captain), general, governor, keeper,
   lord,(-task-)master, prince(-ipal), ruler, steward." **Inference:** the term is generic — a
   _head person_ — and it is applied at four different scales in the same verse.
5. **Text states:** The criteria are _checkable_. Fear of God, truth-telling, and hatred of gain
   are all observable in a person's prior conduct. Nothing in the list requires an interview.

### The exception rule

**Text states:** "and it shall be, that every great matter they shall bring unto thee, but every
small matter they shall judge" (v. 22). And the execution: "the hard causes they brought unto
Moses, but every small matter they judged themselves" (v. 26).

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The rule has exactly two categories: _great_ and _small_. Not three, not a
   matrix.
2. **Text states:** The escalation is _upward and specific_: great matters go to Moses. Everything
   else stays.
3. **Text states:** The default is delegation: "every small matter they shall judge." The
   exception is escalation, not delegation. **Inference:** the burden of proof is on escalating.
4. **Text states:** The purpose is stated: "so shall it be easier for thyself, and they shall bear
   the burden with thee" (v. 22). Two effects: the principal's load decreases, and the load is
   _shared_. **Inference:** sharing the burden is stated as an outcome in itself, not only as a
   means to the principal's relief.
5. **Text states:** The rule is _undefined_. The text does not say what makes a matter "great."
   **Inference:** the definition is left to the parties, which means it will be negotiated in
   practice. The text supplies the structure, not the threshold.

**Business consequences:**

- **A two-category escalation rule is a design choice worth copying.** Most organisations have
  elaborate escalation paths. The text has one threshold.
- **The default must be "judge it yourself."** Observation 3. A rule where the default is
  escalation produces a queue.
- **The threshold will be contested, so name it.** The text leaves "great" undefined; a business
  cannot. Name the categories in advance (A20) — monetary size, irreversibility, cross-team
  impact, external commitment.
- **Escalation should be cheap and non-punitive.** If escalating is read as weakness, the tiers
  will not be used and the principal will be overloaded again.

### What Moses keeps

**Text states:** "Be thou for the people to God-ward, that thou mayest bring the causes unto God:
And thou shalt teach them ordinances and laws, and shalt shew them the way wherein they must walk,
and the work that they must do" (vv. 19–20).

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Three retained functions: (a) _representing the people to God_ — "for the
   people to God-ward"; (b) _teaching_ — "thou shalt teach them ordinances and laws"; (c)
   _showing the way and the work_ — "the way wherein they must walk, and the work that they must
   do."
2. **Text states:** What is delegated is _judging between one and another_ (v. 16) — the dispute
   resolution. What is retained is the _teaching_ and the _direction_.
3. **Text states:** The retained functions are all _upstream_ of the delegated ones. Teaching the
   statutes is what makes judging possible; showing the way is what makes the work possible.
4. **Text states:** Nothing in the passage says the retained functions are more important. It says
   they are the ones that cannot be distributed.

**Inference — and this is the passage's sharpest organisational claim:** the founder's job after
delegation is **teaching and direction**, not execution. What Moses hands off is the caseload.
What he keeps is the standard and the teaching of it.

**Business consequences:**

- **Delegate the caseload; retain the standard.** A founder who delegates the standard and retains
  the caseload has it exactly backwards.
- **Teaching is a retained function, not a delegation.** Observation 3. The teaching is what makes
  the tiers able to judge.
- **"The work that they must do" is a management output.** Observation 1(c). Telling people what
  the work is — not just what the goal is — is part of the retained job.
- **Note what is _not_ retained: the authority to decide individual cases.** Observation 2. The
  authority is genuinely transferred, which is A12's "delegate actual authority, with actual
  limits."

### The tier structure

**Text states:** Four tiers: "rulers of thousands, and rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and
rulers of tens" (v. 21).

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The ratios are 10 : 5 : 2 : 1 between tiers. Each tier spans ten of the one
   below.
2. **Text states:** The tiers are named by _span_, not by function. There is no "chief judge" or
   "senior judge." There is a ruler of tens and a ruler of thousands.
3. **Text states:** The four levels are stated twice, identically (vv. 21 and 25). The repetition
   confirms the structure was implemented as designed.
4. **Text states:** The escalation runs from tens to thousands to Moses — four tiers of judgment
   plus the top. **Inference:** the design is deliberately shallow relative to the population
   (which Numbers 1:46 puts at over 600,000 men of fighting age, implying thousands of rulers of
   tens).

**Business consequences:**

- **Span is the unit of structure, not title.** Observation 2. Naming tiers by span makes the
  structure legible and prevents title inflation.
- **Shallow hierarchies need a defined escalation.** Observation 4. With four tiers, most matters
  are resolved two or three levels down.
- **The structure scales by multiplication, not by adding layers.** Observation 1. To double the
  population, you double the number of rulers of tens; you do not add a tier.
- **Note the scale of the delegation.** Thousands of appointees, selected on four character
  criteria, in a single administrative act. The text presents this as normal management, not as a
  crisis response.

## Part 2. Acts 6: the first org-design decision

**Text (Acts 6:1–7, KJV)**

> 1. And in those days, when the number of the disciples was multiplied, there arose a murmuring
>    of the Grecians against the Hebrews, because their widows were neglected in the daily
>    ministration.
> 2. Then the twelve called the multitude of the disciples unto them, and said, It is not reason
>    that we should leave the word of God, and serve tables.
> 3. Wherefore, brethren, look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost
>    and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business.
> 4. But we will give ourselves continually to prayer, and to the ministry of the word.
> 5. And the saying pleased the whole multitude: and they chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of
>    the Holy Ghost, and Philip, and Prochorus, and Nicanor, and Timon, and Parmenas, and Nicolas a
>    proselyte of Antioch:
> 6. Whom they set before the apostles: and when they had prayed, they laid their hands on them.
> 7. And the word of God increased; and the number of the disciples multiplied in Jerusalem
>    greatly; and a great company of the priests were obedient to the faith.

### The trigger: a service failure, not a doctrinal one

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The problem is stated precisely: "their widows were neglected in the daily
   ministration" (v. 1). A distribution failure, affecting a specific group.
2. **Text states:** The affected group is identified by _ethnic and linguistic_ difference:
   "Grecians against the Hebrews." **Inference:** the failure fell along an existing fault line,
   which is why it produced a complaint rather than a quiet fix.
3. **Text states:** The growth preceded the failure: "when the number of the disciples was
   multiplied" (v. 1). **Inference:** the failure was a _scaling_ failure — a process that worked
   at one size did not work at another.
4. **Text states:** The response is _organisational_, not exhortational. The twelve do not preach
   about the duty of care; they create a role and fill it.
5. **Text states:** The decision is made _publicly_: "Then the twelve called the multitude of the
   disciples unto them" (v. 2). The whole body is convened, and the reasoning is stated aloud.

### The explicit focus trade

**Text states:** "It is not reason that we should leave the word of God, and serve tables… But we
will give ourselves continually to prayer, and to the ministry of the word" (vv. 2, 4).

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The trade is stated as a _judgment_: "It is not reason." Not "it is beneath
   us," not "we are too important." The claim is that it is not fitting — a statement about the
   right allocation of a scarce resource, not about status.
2. **Text states:** The verb is _leave_: "we should leave the word of God, and serve tables." The
   apostles are describing a _substitution_, not an addition. Serving tables would displace
   something.
3. **Text states:** The alternative is stated positively: "we will give ourselves continually to
   prayer, and to the ministry of the word" (v. 4). Two activities, named.
4. **Text states:** The word "continually" is used. The commitment is to _persistence_ in the
   chosen activity, which implies that the temptation to drift into the other activity is ongoing.
5. **Text states:** The apostles do not say the table service is unimportant. They say they are
   not the ones to do it. **Inference:** the trade is about _who_, not _what_.

**Inference — the axiom this yields:** a leadership role has a defined scope, and defending that
scope against legitimate competing demands is part of the role. The competing demand here was
_legitimate_ — widows were actually being neglected. The apostles still declined it, because the
cost of accepting it was higher than the cost of the neglect.

**Business consequences:**

- **The strongest argument for focus is a real competing need.** Observation 5. If the thing you
  are declining is trivial, no trade-off exists and no discipline is required. The discipline is
  needed precisely when the competing demand is important.
- **State the trade explicitly and publicly.** Observation: verse 2 is spoken to the whole
  multitude. A focus decision that is not stated will be re-litigated.
- **Name what you will do instead.** Verse 4 names two activities. A refusal without a stated
  alternative is a dodge.
- **"It is not reason" is the right register.** Not a claim of superiority — a claim of fitness.
  Founders who justify focus by rank rather than by allocation invite resentment.
- **The failure was a scaling failure.** Observation 3. This is A5: the container (a process that
  worked at one size) did not fit the contents.

### The selection criteria

**Text states:** "seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom" (v. 3).

| #   | KJV                    | Lexical                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | What it selects for                              |
| --- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | of honest report       | **G3140 μαρτυρέω (_martyreo_)** — "to be a witness, i.e. testify (literally or figuratively)." KJV: "charge, give (evidence), bear record, have (obtain, of) good (honest) report, be well reported of, testify, give (have) testimony, (be, bear, give, obtain) witness." | Reputation — a track record _attested by others_ |
| 2   | full of the Holy Ghost | (G4151 πνεῦμα, _pneuma_)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Orientation                                      |
| 3   | wisdom                 | **G4678 σοφία (_sophia_)** — "wisdom (higher or lower, worldly or spiritual)."                                                                                                                                                                                             | Judgment                                         |

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The first criterion is _reported_ reputation: "of honest report." **Lexical:**
   the word is from the witness/testimony family — the candidate is one about whom others have
   testified. **Inference:** the criterion is external, not self-assessed. It is a reference
   check.
2. **Text states:** The criteria are, again, characterological rather than technical. There is no
   requirement for administrative experience or accounting skill, for a role that is
   fundamentally administrative and financial.
3. **Text states:** The word _wisdom_ is unqualified — "higher or lower, worldly or spiritual."
   **Inference:** practical judgment is in view, not religious insight specifically.
4. **Text states:** There are _seven_ men, a specific number. **Inference:** the number is
   large enough to distribute the work and small enough to be accountable.
5. **Text states:** The named seven include "Nicolas a proselyte of Antioch" (v. 5). **Inference:**
   the selection crosses the fault line that caused the complaint. The Grecian widows' grievance
   is addressed by including a Grecian.

**Business consequences:**

- **Hire on attested reputation, not on interview performance.** Observation 1. The Greek is
  explicitly testimonial.
- **The criteria are transferable across role types.** Observation 2. Characterological criteria
  were used for a finance-and-logistics role. This is not because the technical requirements were
  absent; it is because they were assumed and the character was the differentiator.
- **Address the fault line in the composition.** Observation 5. If the failure fell along a
  group boundary, the remedy should be visible in the group that fixes it.

### Who chooses, and who appoints

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The multitude selects: "look ye out among you seven men" (v. 3), and "they
   chose" (v. 5).
2. **Text states:** The apostles appoint: "whom we may appoint over this business" (v. 3), and
   "Whom they set before the apostles: and when they had prayed, they laid their hands on them"
   (v. 6).
3. **Text states:** Two distinct acts: _selection_ by the community, _appointment_ by the leaders,
   with prayer and the laying on of hands.
4. **Text states:** The appointment is _public and ceremonial_. It is not a private decision
   communicated by memo.
5. **Text states:** The result was accepted: "And the saying pleased the whole multitude" (v. 5).
   **Inference:** the two-stage process is what produced the acceptance.

**Business consequences:**

- **Separate selection from appointment.** Observation 3. A hiring process in which the team
  selects and the leader appoints gives the leader a genuine veto and the team genuine ownership.
  A process where the leader does both produces compliance; a process where the team does both
  produces no accountability.
- **Make the appointment public.** Observation 4. The ceremony is where the authority is conferred
  in front of witnesses. This is A12 in practice.
- **The two-stage process buys legitimacy.** Observation 5. The complaint was resolved without
  further murmuring.

### The outcome

**Text states:** "And the word of God increased; and the number of the disciples multiplied in
Jerusalem greatly; and a great company of the priests were obedient to the faith" (v. 7).

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Three outcomes, in order: the word increased, the disciples multiplied
   greatly, and a new group (priests) was reached.
2. **Text states:** The outcomes are _growth_ outcomes. **Inference:** the organisational fix
   preceded the growth, and the text presents them in sequence.
3. **Text states:** Nothing in the passage records the apostles doing more work. **Inference:**
   the growth followed a _reduction_ in the leaders' workload, not an increase.

**Business consequences:**

- **Fixing the org can precede the growth.** Observation 2. The sequence in the text is: problem,
  structural fix, growth.
- **Reducing the leader's load is a growth intervention.** Observation 3. This inverts the common
  assumption that growth requires the founder to do more.

## Part 3. Nehemiah: build and defend

### Reconnaissance by night (2:11–16)

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Nehemiah waits three days before acting: "I came to Jerusalem, and was there
   three days" (v. 11). **Inference:** the waiting is deliberate and precedes the reconnaissance.
2. **Text states:** He inspects _at night_, with a few men: "I arose in the night, I and some few
   men with me" (v. 12).
3. **Text states:** He does not announce the purpose: "neither told I any man what my God had put
   in my heart to do at Jerusalem" (v. 12). And again in verse 16: "the rulers knew not whither I
   went, or what I did."
4. **Text states:** The inspection is _physical and specific_: he goes gate by gate — the gate of
   the valley, the dragon well, the dung port, the gate of the fountain, the king's pool, the
   brook (vv. 13–15). He examines "the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down, and the gates
   thereof were consumed with fire."
5. **Text states:** He encounters a constraint: "there was no place for the beast that was under
   me to pass" (v. 14). **Inference:** the reconnaissance produced an operational finding — the
   route was blocked — which is A19 (doing the work reveals the need).
6. **Text states:** The inspection is _exhaustive_: "Then went I up in the night by the brook, and
   viewed the wall, and turned back, and entered by the gate of the valley, and so returned"
   (v. 15). He covers the circuit.
7. **Text states:** He is alone except for a few, and the purpose is undisclosed until he has seen
   the whole situation himself.

**Inference:** the sequence is _see it yourself, in private, before you announce anything._ The
founder's own eyes on the actual condition precede both the vision and the plan.

**Business consequences:**

- **Do the reconnaissance yourself, before the announcement.** Observation 3. A vision cast
  before the leader has personally seen the situation will not survive contact with it.
- **Go at night, or at least quietly.** Observation 2. A reconnaissance conducted publicly is a
  performance, and it will produce different information.
- **Inspect the specific assets, not the general condition.** Observation 4. Gate by gate, not
  "the walls are broken."
- **Expect the reconnaissance to find an operational constraint.** Observation 5. The blocked
  route is the kind of finding that only appears on site.

### Vision casting (2:17–18)

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The appeal opens with the _shared_ problem: "Ye see the distress that we are
   in, how Jerusalem lieth waste, and the gates thereof are burned with fire" (v. 17). **Text
   states:** "Ye see" — he appeals to what they can already observe.
2. **Text states:** The call is stated as an invitation: "come, and let us build up the wall of
   Jerusalem" (v. 17). First person plural — _let us_.
3. **Text states:** The motive is stated: "that we be no more a reproach." **Inference:** the
   appeal includes a reputational motive, not only a practical one.
4. **Text states:** The _basis_ is given second: "Then I told them of the hand of my God which was
   good upon me; as also the king's words that he had spoken unto me" (v. 18). Two grounds:
   divine favour and royal authorisation.
5. **Text states:** The response is immediate: "Let us rise up and build" (v. 18). **Text states:**
   And they act: "So they strengthened their hands for this good work."
6. **Text states:** The order in the appeal is: _problem_ (v. 17a), _call_ (v. 17b), _motive_
   (v. 17c), _basis_ (v. 18a), _response_ (v. 18b).

**Inference:** the vision is cast in five moves and the _authorisation_ comes last, after the
problem has been named and the call issued. The audience is invited to see the problem before
being told why it is possible.

**Business consequences:**

- **Lead with the observable problem.** Observation 1. "Ye see" — the appeal starts from shared
  perception.
- **Use "let us," not "you will."** Observation 2.
- **State the basis last.** Observation 6. Authorisation and divine favour are the _reasons it can
  be done_, which only matter once the problem is accepted.
- **Reputational motive is legitimate.** Observation 3. "That we be no more a reproach" is a
  stated reason for the work.

### Opposition, stated and answered (2:19–20)

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The opposition arrives immediately after the announcement: "when Sanballat…
   heard it, they laughed us to scorn, and despised us" (v. 19).
2. **Text states:** Three specific opponents are named, with their affiliations: Sanballat the
   Horonite, Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, and Geshem the Arabian. **Inference:** the
   opposition is identified, not vague.
3. **Text states:** The attack has two components: _mockery_ ("laughed us to scorn, and despised
   us") and a _legal threat_ ("will ye rebel against the king?").
4. **Text states:** The answer is brief and has three parts: a statement of confidence ("The God of
   heaven, he will prosper us"), a statement of intent ("therefore we his servants will arise and
   build"), and a statement of exclusion ("but ye have no portion, nor right, nor memorial, in
   Jerusalem") (v. 20).
5. **Text states:** Nehemiah does not argue about the rebellion charge. He answers it by asserting
   standing. **Inference:** the charge is answered by _who he is_, not by _what the charge is_.

**Business consequences:**

- **Name the opponents.** Observation 2. An unnamed opposition cannot be tracked.
- **Answer briefly and once.** Observation 4. Three short clauses.
- **Answer the standing question, not the accusation.** Observation 5. The rebellion charge was a
  standing question in disguise, and it was answered as one.
- **Assert exclusion where it applies.** "Ye have no portion, nor right, nor memorial" — this is
  A6 (distinction precedes order) and A33 (access is guarded) in the same sentence.

### Work assigned by family and group (chapter 3)

**Text (Nehemiah 3 — selected, KJV)**

> 1. Then Eliashib the high priest rose up with his brethren the priests, and they builded the
>    sheep gate; they sanctified it, and set up the doors of it…
> 2. And next unto him builded the men of Jericho. And next to them builded Zaccur the son of Imri.
> 3. But the fish gate did the sons of Hassenaah build, who also laid the beams thereof, and set up
>    the doors thereof, the locks thereof, and the bars thereof.
> 4. Next unto him repaired Uzziel the son of Harhaiah, of the goldsmiths. Next unto him also
>    repaired Hananiah the son of one of the apothecaries, and they fortified Jerusalem unto the broad
>    wall.
> 5. And next unto them repaired Jedaiah the son of Harumaph, even over against his house. And next
>    unto him repaired Hattush the son of Hashabniah.
> 6. And next unto him repaired Shallum the son of Halohesh, the ruler of the half part of
>    Jerusalem, he and his daughters.
> 7. After him repaired Benjamin and Hashub over against their house. After him repaired Azariah
>    the son of Maaseiah the son of Ananiah by his house.
> 8. From above the horse gate repaired the priests, every one over against his house.
> 9. And between the going up of the corner unto the sheep gate repaired the goldsmiths and the
>    merchants.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (structure):** The chapter is a list of _segments_, and the formula is "next unto
   him" / "after him" — a continuous chain. **Inference:** the wall is divided into named pieces,
   each with an owner, and the list runs around the circuit.
2. **Text states (assignment by family):** Assignments are given to families and households: "the
   sons of Hassenaah" (v. 3), "he and his daughters" (v. 12).
3. **Text states (assignment by trade):** Assignments are given to occupational groups: "the
   goldsmiths" (v. 8, v. 31–32), "the apothecaries" (v. 8), "the merchants" (v. 32).
4. **Text states (assignment by locality):** "the men of Jericho" (v. 2), "the ruler of the half
   part of Jerusalem" (v. 12), "the ruler of part of Mizpah" (v. 15).
5. **Text states (assignment by proximity):** Multiple entries are "over against his house"
   (vv. 10, 23, 28, 29, 30) or "by his house" (v. 23). **Inference:** people repaired the section
   nearest their own property. The incentive is aligned by construction — the wall they built is
   the wall that protects their house.
6. **Text states:** The work includes the gates and their fittings: "the doors thereof, the locks
   thereof, and the bars thereof" (vv. 3, 6, 13, 14, 15). **Inference:** the definition of done
   includes the security hardware, not just the masonry.
7. **Text states:** Some did more than one section: "the Tekoites repaired another piece" (v. 27),
   and Baruch "earnestly repaired the other piece" (v. 20). **Inference:** effort above the
   assignment is recorded and named.
8. **Text states (the failure):** "And next unto them the Tekoites repaired; but their nobles put
   not their necks to the work of their LORD" (v. 5). **Text states:** the non-participation of a
   specific group is recorded permanently in the record.

**Business consequences:**

- **Divide the work into named segments with a single owner each.** Observation 1. The list is
  the plan.
- **Assign by existing structure — family, trade, locality.** Observation 2–4. The organisation
  uses the groups that already exist rather than creating new ones.
- **Align the incentive with proximity where possible.** Observation 5 is the sharpest management
  idea in the chapter: let people build the part that protects their own house.
- **Define done to include the fittings.** Observation 6. A gate without locks and bars is not a
  gate.
- **Record over-delivery and non-delivery by name.** Observations 7 and 8. The chapter is a public
  record, and it names both.
- **Note that the nobles' refusal is stated as a refusal toward God, not toward Nehemiah.** "The
  work of their LORD." **Inference:** the accountability is vertical, which is what makes it
  permanent.

### They prayed AND set a watch (4:9)

**Text (Nehemiah 4:9, KJV)**

> Nevertheless we made our prayer unto our God, and set a watch against them day and night, because
> of them.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Two acts, joined by "and": _prayer_ and _a watch_. Not one or the other.
2. **Text states:** The watch is _scheduled_: "day and night." It is a standing arrangement, not a
   reaction.
3. **Text states:** The reason is stated: "because of them" — a specific, identified threat.
4. **Text states (context):** Verse 8 states the threat: "And conspired all of them together to
   come and to fight against Jerusalem, and to hinder it." **Inference:** the watch is a response
   to _intelligence_, not to a general anxiety.

**Inference — and this is the axiom:** prayer and preparation are not alternatives, and the text
gives them in a single clause. An organisation that prays and does not prepare has not followed
the pattern; neither has one that prepares and does not pray.

**Business consequences:**

- **Security is scheduled, not reactive.** Observation 2. "Day and night" is a rota.
- **Threat assessment precedes the control.** Observation 4. The watch follows the intelligence.
- **The two-act structure is the model for risk management.** Observation 1. The spiritual and the
  operational responses are both required and neither substitutes for the other.

### One hand on the work, one on the weapon (4:17–18)

**Text (Nehemiah 4:16–18, 21–23, KJV)**

> 16. And it came to pass from that time forth, that the half of my servants wrought in the work,
>     and the other half of them held both the spears, the shields, and the bows, and the habergeons;
>     and the rulers were behind all the house of Judah.
> 17. They which builded on the wall, and they that bare burdens, with those that laded, every one
>     with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other hand held a weapon.
> 18. For the builders, every one had his sword girded by his side, and so builded. And he that
>     sounded the trumpet was by me.
> 19. So we laboured in the work: and half of them held the spears from the rising of the morning
>     till the stars appeared.
> 20. Likewise at the same time said I unto the people, Let every one with his servant lodge within
>     Jerusalem, that in the night they may be a guard to us, and labour on the day.
> 21. So neither I, nor my brethren, nor my servants, nor the men of the guard which followed me,
>     none of us put off our clothes, saving that every one put them off for washing.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (two models, both used):** (a) _Split the force_ — "the half of my servants
   wrought in the work, and the other half of them held both the spears" (v. 16). (b) _Every person
   does both_ — "every one with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other hand held
   a weapon" (v. 17).
2. **Text states:** The two models coexist in the same passage. Verse 16 is a division of labour;
   verse 17 is a dual-tasking of each individual.
3. **Text states:** The dual-tasking applies to the whole chain: "They which builded on the wall,
   and they that bare burdens, with those that laded" (v. 17). Everyone, not just the front line.
4. **Text states:** The weapon is carried _on the body_: "every one had his sword girded by his
   side, and so builded" (v. 18). **Inference:** the defence is not stored somewhere; it is
   immediately available.
5. **Text states (the coordination mechanism):** "And he that sounded the trumpet was by me"
   (v. 18), and the instruction: "In what place therefore ye hear the sound of the trumpet, resort
   ye thither unto us" (v. 20). **Inference:** the workers are dispersed along the wall — "we are
   separated upon the wall, one far from another" (v. 19) — and the trumpet is the concentration
   signal.
6. **Text states:** The hours are long: "from the rising of the morning till the stars appeared"
   (v. 21).
7. **Text states (the sustainability measure):** "Let every one with his servant lodge within
   Jerusalem, that in the night they may be a guard to us, and labour on the day" (v. 22).
   **Inference:** the arrangement is a _rotation_ — guard at night, build by day — not
   round-the-clock work by the same people.
8. **Text states (the leader's own participation):** "neither I, nor my brethren, nor my servants,
   nor the men of the guard which followed me, none of us put off our clothes" (v. 23).

**Business consequences:**

- **Build and defend at once.** The axiom (A17) stated as an operational plan.
- **Two staffing models are available: split the force, or dual-task everyone.** Observation 1.
  The choice depends on whether the work can be paused; if not, dual-task; if it can, split.
- **Defence must be carried, not stored.** Observation 4. Security that requires fetching is
  security that arrives late.
- **Dispersion requires a concentration signal.** Observation 5. A distributed team needs one
  agreed signal that means "come here now."
- **The rotation is what makes it sustainable.** Observation 7. Guard at night, build by day —
  the same people do not do both simultaneously around the clock. Note that verse 21 says half held
  spears "from the rising of the morning till the stars appeared," which is a _shift_, and verse 22
  gives the other half the night watch.
- **The leader is inside the arrangement.** Observation 8. Nehemiah and his household are subject
  to the same conditions as everyone else.

### The usury crisis and the restitution demanded (5:1–13)

**Text (Nehemiah 5:1–13, KJV)**

> 1. And there was a great cry of the people and of their wives against their brethren the Jews.
> 2. For there were that said, We, our sons, and our daughters, are many: therefore we take up corn
>    for them, that we may eat, and live.
> 3. Some also there were that said, We have mortgaged our lands, vineyards, and houses, that we
>    might buy corn, because of the dearth.
> 4. There were also that said, We have borrowed money for the king's tribute, and that upon our
>    lands and vineyards.
> 5. Yet now our flesh is as the flesh of our brethren, our children as their children: and, lo, we
>    bring into bondage our sons and our daughters to be servants, and some of our daughters are
>    brought unto bondage already: neither is it in our power to redeem them; for other men have our
>    lands and vineyards.
> 6. And I was very angry when I heard their cry and these words.
> 7. Then I consulted with myself, and I rebuked the nobles, and the rulers, and said unto them, Ye
>    exact usury, every one of his brother. And I set a great assembly against them.
> 8. And I said unto them, We after our ability have redeemed our brethren the Jews, which were sold
>    unto the heathen; and will ye even sell your brethren? or shall they be sold unto us? Then held
>    they their peace, and found nothing to answer.
> 9. Also I said, It is not good that ye do: ought ye not to walk in the fear of our God because of
>    the reproach of the heathen our enemies?
> 10. I likewise, and my brethren, and my servants, might exact of them money and corn: I pray you,
>     let us leave off this usury.
> 11. Restore, I pray you, to them, even this day, their lands, their vineyards, their oliveyards,
>     and their houses, also the hundredth part of the money, and of the corn, the wine, and the oil,
>     that ye exact of them.
> 12. Then said they, We will restore them, and will require nothing of them; so will we do as thou
>     sayest. Then I called the priests, and took an oath of them, that they should do according to this
>     promise.
> 13. Also I shook my lap, and said, So God shake out every man from his house, and from his labour,
>     that performeth not this promise, even thus be he shaken out, and emptied. And all the congregation
>     said, Amen, and praised the LORD. And the people did according to this promise.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The crisis has three causes, stated by three groups: (a) large families needing
   food (v. 2), (b) mortgaged property to buy corn during a dearth (v. 3), (c) borrowed money to
   pay the king's tribute (v. 4). **Inference:** the causes are a mix of household scale, food
   prices, and taxation. The crisis is systemic, not caused by improvidence.
2. **Text states:** The consequence is the enslavement of children: "we bring into bondage our sons
   and our daughters to be servants, and some of our daughters are brought unto bondage already"
   (v. 5).
3. **Text states:** The moral argument is stated by the sufferers: "our flesh is as the flesh of our
   brethren, our children as their children" (v. 5). **Inference:** the appeal is to shared
   identity.
4. **Text states:** Nehemiah's response is anger: "And I was very angry when I heard their cry"
   (v. 6). **Text states:** The anger is recorded without apology.
5. **Text states:** He deliberates before acting: "Then I consulted with myself" (v. 7). **Text
   states:** Then he rebukes, and then he convenes: "And I set a great assembly against them."
   Deliberation → private rebuke → public assembly.
6. **Text states:** The charge is stated plainly: "Ye exact usury, every one of his brother" (v. 7).
   **Lexical:** "usury" here is H5383/H4855 family — the same vocabulary as Exodus 22:25 and
   Leviticus 25:36–37. See `capital-and-stewardship.md`, Part 4.
7. **Text states:** The argument is _consistency_: "We after our ability have redeemed our
   brethren the Jews, which were sold unto the heathen; and will ye even sell your brethren?" (v. 8).
   **Text states:** The accused have no answer: "Then held they their peace, and found nothing to
   answer."
8. **Text states:** Nehemiah includes himself in the rebuke: "I likewise, and my brethren, and my
   servants, might exact of them money and corn: I pray you, let us leave off this usury" (v. 10).
   **Inference:** he names his own standing to have done the same thing and declines it.
9. **Text states (the restitution, and it is specific):** "Restore, I pray you, to them, even this
   day, their lands, their vineyards, their oliveyards, and their houses, also the hundredth part
   of the money, and of the corn, the wine, and the oil, that ye exact of them" (v. 11). Four
   categories of property, plus the interest ("the hundredth part" — 1%), plus the produce.
10. **Text states:** The timing is immediate: "even this day."
11. **Text states:** The agreement is _sworn_: "Then I called the priests, and took an oath of them,
    that they should do according to this promise" (v. 12).
12. **Text states:** The enforcement is _public and symbolic_: Nehemiah shakes his lap and pronounces
    the corresponding judgment, and the congregation says "Amen" (v. 13).
13. **Text states:** The compliance is recorded: "And the people did according to this promise."

**Inference — the axiom this yields:** restitution is _specific, immediate, and includes the
interest_, not merely the principal. The restitution is not "do better"; it is "give it back, and
give back the gain, today."

**Business consequences:**

- **Restitution should restore the gain, not just the principal.** Observation 9. The hundredth
  part — the interest — is included in the demand. A remediation that returns the principal and
  keeps the profit has not restored.
- **Restitution should be immediate.** Observation 10. "Even this day."
- **Name the categories.** Observation 9. Lands, vineyards, oliveyards, houses, money, corn, wine,
  oil. The specificity is what makes the demand enforceable.
- **The leader must be inside the rebuke.** Observation 8. Nehemiah names his own standing to have
  exploited the same position.
- **The commitment should be sworn and public.** Observations 11–12. An oath before witnesses, and
  a public sign.
- **Anger is a legitimate response to exploitation.** Observation 4. The text records it without
  qualification. **Inference:** the failure mode is not anger; it is anger that does not proceed to
  deliberation (observation 5) and then to remedy.
- **The crisis was not caused by the victims' improvidence.** Observation 1. The causes were
  household size, food prices, and tax. A remediation premised on the victims' fault would have
  missed.
- **Note the systemic interaction.** The same chapter that demands restitution also records
  Nehemiah's own refusal to take the governor's bread (5:14–18): "I and my brethren have not eaten
  the bread of the governor… because the bondage was heavy upon this people" (5:14, 18). The leader
  reduces his own claim while demanding that others release theirs.

### "I cannot come down" (6:1–4)

**Text (Nehemiah 6:1–4, KJV)**

> 1. Now it came to pass, when Sanballat, and Tobiah, and Geshem the Arabian, and the rest of our
>    enemies, heard that I had builded the wall, and that there was no breach left therein; (though at
>    that time I had not set up the doors upon the gates;)
> 2. That Sanballat and Geshem sent unto me, saying, Come, let us meet together in some one of the
>    villages in the plain of Ono. But they thought to do me mischief.
> 3. And I sent messengers unto them, saying, I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down:
>    why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?
> 4. Yet they sent unto me four times after this sort; and I answered them after the same manner.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The invitation is _reasonable on its face_: "Come, let us meet together in some
   one of the villages in the plain of Ono" (v. 2). A meeting request, not a threat.
2. **Text states:** The motive is stated to the reader and not to Nehemiah's face: "But they
   thought to do me mischief" (v. 2).
3. **Text states:** The refusal states the _cost of attending_: "why should the work cease, whilst
   I leave it, and come down to you?" (v. 3). **Inference:** the objection is not to the meeting's
   purpose but to its price — the work stops while he attends.
4. **Text states:** The refusal includes the _value of the work_: "I am doing a great work."
   **Inference:** the refusal is grounded in a positive claim about what the work is, not only a
   negative claim about the requesters.
5. **Text states:** The request is repeated _four times_, and the answer is identical each time:
   "and I answered them after the same manner" (v. 4). **Inference:** the discipline is in the
   repetition. A refusal given once is an opening bid; a refusal given four times identically is a
   boundary.
6. **Text states (the escalation that follows):** The fifth approach is an open letter with a
   slander (vv. 5–7), then a hired prophet urging him to hide in the temple (vv. 10–13).
   **Inference:** the requests escalate after the refusals.
7. **Text states (the second refusal):** "And I said, Should such a man as I flee? and who is
   there, that, being as I am, would go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in"
   (v. 11). **Text states:** And he identifies the manipulation: "And, lo, I perceived that God had
   not sent him" (v. 12).

**Inference — the axiom:** a leader's availability is a finite resource, and the cost of attending
is the work that stops. The refusal is legitimate and must be repeated identically.

**Business consequences:**

- **Price the meeting.** Observation 3. The question is not "is this a bad meeting?" but "what
  stops while I am in it?"
- **Refuse with a positive claim.** Observation 4. "I am doing a great work" — a statement about
  the work, not an accusation about the requester.
- **Repeat the identical refusal.** Observation 5. Four times, same answer. Varying the wording
  invites another attempt.
- **Expect escalation.** Observation 6. The refusals are followed by slander and by a false
  counsellor. A boundary that holds will be tested at a higher level.
- **Watch for counsel that serves the counsellor.** Observation 7. Shemaiah's advice was hired.
  The test Nehemiah applies is whether the counsel is consistent with his own standing and role:
  "Should such a man as I flee?"

### Fifty-two days (6:15)

**Text (Nehemiah 6:15–16, KJV)**

> 15. So the wall was finished in the twenty and fifth day of the month Elul, in fifty and two days.
> 16. And it came to pass, that when all our enemies heard thereof, and all the heathen that were
>     about us saw these things, they were much cast down in their own eyes: for they perceived that
>     this work was wrought of our God.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The duration is _recorded precisely_: a specific date and a specific count —
   "in fifty and two days."
2. **Text states:** The completion is _verified by third parties_: the enemies heard and saw, and
   "were much cast down in their own eyes."
3. **Text states:** The effect is attributed to God: "for they perceived that this work was wrought
   of our God."
4. **Text states:** The wall was completed _before_ the gates were hung (6:1, "though at that time
   I had not set up the doors upon the gates"). **Inference:** the fifty-two days refers to the
   wall, and the fittings were a subsequent phase. The completion claim is scoped.
5. **Text states (the friction inside the fifty-two days):** During that period there was an
   external conspiracy (4:7–8), an internal morale collapse (4:10, "The strength of the bearers of
   burdens is decayed, and there is much rubbish"), threats from multiple directions (4:12), an
   economic crisis requiring a major intervention (chapter 5), and repeated distraction attempts
   (6:1–14). **Inference:** the fifty-two days included all of it.

**Business consequences:**

- **Record the duration precisely.** Observation 1. A stated elapsed time is a claim that can be
  checked, and it becomes a benchmark.

### The founder does not always finish

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** David's desire was to build, and it was refused: "As for me, I had in mine heart
   to build an house of rest for the ark of the covenant of the LORD… But God said unto me, Thou
   shalt not build an house for my name, because thou hast been a man of war, and hast shed blood"
   (28:2–3).
2. **Text states:** The reason is stated and it is _personal to David_: "thou hast been a man of
   war." **Inference:** the disqualification is not a judgment on David's competence or devotion;
   it is a fact about what he is.
3. **Text states:** David's response is not to abandon the project but to _change his role in it_:
   "I will therefore now make preparation for it. So David prepared abundantly before his death"
   (22:5).
4. **Text states:** The preparation is named as substantial: "an hundred thousand talents of gold,
   and a thousand thousand talents of silver" (22:14). **Text states:** And the numbers are given.
5. **Text states:** The successor is _named and designated_: "And of all my sons, (for the LORD hath
   given me many sons,) he hath chosen Solomon my son to sit upon the throne" (28:5).
6. **Text states:** David hands over a _pattern_, not just a mandate: "Then David gave to Solomon his
   son the pattern" (28:11), and the pattern is detailed — porch, houses, treasuries, chambers,
   parlours, the place of the mercy seat, the courts, the vessels by weight.
7. **Text states:** The pattern's provenance is stated: "All this, said David, the LORD made me
   understand in writing by his hand upon me, even all the works of this pattern" (28:19).
   **Inference:** the specification is not David's invention; it is transmitted.

**Inference — the axiom:** the founder does not always finish, and the founder's job at the end is
to convert their intent into a _transferable specification_. The pattern in 28:11–19 is the
mechanism.

**Business consequences:**

- **The founder's final deliverable is a specification, not a result.** Observation 6. The pattern
  is what transfers.
- **Preparation is a legitimate and named role.** Observation 3. David "prepared abundantly before
  his death." The founder who cannot build the thing can fund, specify, and staff it.
- **Disqualification can be about what you are, not what you have done wrong.** Observation 2. This
  is worth holding carefully: a founder may be the wrong person for the next phase without having
  failed at the last one.
- **The successor must be named.** Observation 5. Not "the best person will emerge."
- **The specification should be documented in writing.** Observation 7. "Made me understand in

### Do not muzzle the ox

**Text (Deuteronomy 25:4, KJV)**

> Thou shalt not muzzle the ox when he treadeth out the corn.

**Text (1 Corinthians 9:9–10, KJV)**

> 9. For it is written in the law of Moses, Thou shalt not muzzle the mouth of the ox that treadeth
>    out the corn. Doth God take care for oxen?
> 10. Or saith he it altogether for our sakes? For our sakes, no doubt, this is written: that he that
>     ploweth should plow in hope; and that he that thresheth in hope should be partaker of his hope.

**Text (1 Timothy 5:18, KJV)**

> For the scripture saith, Thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn. And, The
> labourer is worthy of his reward.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The law is stated in the imperative: "Thou shalt not muzzle the ox."
2. **Text states:** The ox is _treading out the corn_ — that is, it is working on the food supply.
   **Inference:** the ox has access to the product it is producing, while producing it.
3. **Text states:** Paul reads the law as being written "altogether for our sakes" (v. 10). **Text
   states:** He states it as a rhetorical question and then answers it himself: "Doth God take care
   for oxen? Or saith he it altogether for our sakes? For our sakes, no doubt, this is written."
4. **Text states:** Paul's application is about the _worker's expectation_: "that he that ploweth
   should plow in hope; and that he that thresheth in hope should be partaker of his hope" (v. 10).
   **Text states:** The word "hope" appears three times in one sentence.
5. **Text states:** 1 Timothy 5:18 pairs the ox law with a second saying: "The labourer is worthy of
   his reward." **Inference:** the two are treated as the same principle from two sources.
6. **Text states:** The law is from Deuteronomy, in a section of miscellaneous ordinances (Deut
   25:1–19), sitting between the rules for corporal punishment (25:1–3) and the levirate marriage
   (25:5–10). **Inference:** it is stated as a _legal obligation_, not a suggestion, and it is
   placed among enforceable duties.

**Inference.** Three things follow:

- **Payment during work, not only after it.** Observation 2. The ox eats while treading. The
  application is not a bonus at the end of the harvest; it is access to the product during the
  work.
- **The worker's expectation is part of the design.** Observation 4. "Plow in hope." The
  expectation is what makes the work possible; a worker with no hope does not plow.
- **The obligation is legal, not charitable.** Observation 6. The law is stated as a prohibition.

**Business consequences:**

- **Pay during the work.** Observation 2. Deferred compensation structures are in tension with the
  pattern; the ox does not wait for the harvest.
- **The worker must be able to see and reach the product.** Observation 2. The ox is not merely
  fed; it is fed _from the corn it is treading_. **Inference:** the connection between effort and
  reward should be visible in the work itself.
- **"In hope" is a design requirement.** Observation 4. A compensation plan in which the worker
  cannot form an expectation of return is a plan that will not produce plowing.
- **The obligation is not discretionary.** Observation 6. It is stated as a law, and Paul treats it
  as a scriptural obligation.

### Wages withheld cry out

**Observations:**

   fraud and robbery: "Thou shalt not defraud thy neighbour, neither rob him: the wages of him that

5. **Text states (the woe, Jeremiah):** "Woe unto him that buildeth his house by unrighteousness,
   and his chambers by wrong; that useth his neighbour's service without wages, and giveth him not
   for his work" (22:13). **Text states:** The building is _by_ the unrighteousness — the house
   itself is the product of the withheld wages.
6. **Text states:** Jeremiah 22:14 continues the description: the man says "I will build me a wide
   house and large chambers," and ceils it with cedar and paints it with vermilion. **Inference:**
   the scale of the house is the measure of the withheld wages.
7. **Text states:** The audience in James is "ye rich men" (5:1), and the indictment is specific to
   agricultural labour: "the labourers who have reaped down your fields."

**Inference.** The passages together state: withheld wages are robbery (Leviticus), the withholding
has a deadline of the same day (Leviticus), the withheld wage is a witness that cries out (James),
and a house built on withheld wages is under a woe (Jeremiah).

**Business consequences:**

- **Payment terms are an ethics issue, not a treasury issue.** Observation 1. Leviticus puts the
  withheld wage in the same verse as robbery.
- **The deadline is stated: the same day.** Observation 2. Modern payment terms of 30, 60, or 90
  days are in direct tension with the statute. Whether the statute's _specific_ deadline binds a
  modern firm is a question about the covenant community's law, not a business question; but the
  direction of the requirement is unambiguous, and a business that can pay sooner and chooses not
  to should be able to say why.
- **Deliberate retention is fraud.** Observation 4. The distinction between "we cannot pay" and
  "we are holding it" is the distinction between hardship and a James 5 violation.
- **The wage is a witness.** Observation 3. This is a distinctive claim: the wrong is not merely
  recorded by God; the _unpaid amount itself_ is presented as crying out.
- **Watch the build.** Observation 6. Jeremiah's diagnostic is that the scale of the building
  measures the scale of the withholding. A company that is expanding while its payment terms
  stretch should expect the comparison to be made.
- **The woe is attached to the house.** Observation 5. The consequence is not a fine; it is that
  the thing built on the wrong is under judgment.

### The agreed wage and the eleventh-hour worker

**Text (Matthew 20:1–16, KJV)**

> 1. For the kingdom of heaven is like unto a man that is an householder, which went out early in the
>    morning to hire labourers into his vineyard.
> 2. And when he had agreed with the labourers for a penny a day, he sent them into his vineyard.
> 3. And he went out about the third hour, and saw others standing idle in the marketplace,
> 4. And said unto them; Go ye also into the vineyard, and whatsoever is right I will give you. And
>    they went their way.
> 5. Again he went out about the sixth and ninth hour, and did likewise.
> 6. And about the eleventh hour he went out, and found others standing idle, and saith unto them,
>    Why stand ye here all the day idle?
> 7. They say unto him, Because no man hath hired us. He saith unto them, Go ye also into the
>    vineyard; and whatsoever is right, that shall ye receive.
> 8. So when even was come, the lord of the vineyard saith unto his steward, Call the labourers, and
>    give them their hire, beginning from the last unto the first.
> 9. And when they came that were hired about the eleventh hour, they received every man a penny.
> 10. But when the first came, they supposed that they should have received more; and they likewise
>     received every man a penny.
> 11. And when they had received it, they murmured against the goodman of the house,
> 12. Saying, These last have wrought but one hour, and thou hast made them equal unto us, which have
>     borne the burden and heat of the day.
> 13. But he answered one of them, and said, Friend, I do thee no wrong: didst not thou agree with me
>     for a penny?
> 14. Take that thine is, and go thy way: I will give unto this last, even as unto thee.
> 15. Is it not lawful for me to do what I will with mine own? Is thine eye evil, because I am good?
> 16. So the last shall be first, and the first last: for many be called, but few chosen.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (two different pay arrangements, both present):** The first group has an _agreed_
   rate: "when he had agreed with the labourers for a penny a day" (v. 2). The later groups have an
   _open_ term: "whatsoever is right I will give you" (v. 4) and "whatsoever is right, that shall
   ye receive" (v. 7).
2. **Text states:** The first group's agreement is the basis of the master's defence: "didst not
   thou agree with me for a penny?" (v. 13). **Inference:** the agreed rate is what makes the
   payment just, regardless of what others receive.
3. **Text states:** The complaint is _comparative_, not absolute: "thou hast made them equal unto
   us" (v. 12). **Text states:** The workers do not claim they were underpaid; they claim others
   were overpaid relative to them.
4. **Text states:** The master's answer has three parts: (a) no wrong was done — the agreement was
   honoured (v. 13); (b) the payment is theirs to take (v. 14); (c) the owner's right to dispose of
   his own is asserted (v. 15, "Is it not lawful for me to do what I will with mine own?").
5. **Text states:** The master names the _motive_ of the complaint: "Is thine eye evil, because I am
   good?" (v. 15). **Inference:** the complaint is characterised as envy, not as a claim of
   injustice.
6. **Text states:** The order of payment is deliberate: "beginning from the last unto the first"
   (v. 8). **Inference:** the master structured the payment to surface the comparison.
7. **Text states:** The eleventh-hour workers were not idle by choice: "Because no man hath hired
   us" (v. 7). **Text states:** And they were hired after the question "Why stand ye here all the
   day idle?" was answered.
8. **Text states:** The later groups are paid "whatsoever is right" — an undefined term that the
   master then defines by paying a full day's wage.

**Inference — the axioms this yields:**

- **An agreed wage, honoured, is just, regardless of what others receive.** Observation 2. The
  master's defence is the agreement, not the market rate.
- **The comparison between workers is the source of the complaint, not the wage.** Observations 3
  and 6. This is why payment transparency is a management decision with real consequences.
- **The owner's right to dispose of his own is asserted.** Observation 4(c). This is A1: the goods
  are received, and the holder has genuine authority over them.
- **Generosity to one party is not injustice to another, provided the agreements are kept.**
  Observation 4(a).

**Business consequences:**

- **Honour the agreement.** Observation 2. The entire defence rests on it.
- **Decide your transparency policy deliberately.** Observations 3 and 6. The master's order of
  payment guaranteed the comparison would be made. If you do not want the comparison, do not
  structure the process to produce it; if you are willing to defend the differential, the
  agreement is your defence.
- **"Whatsoever is right" is a real contract term.** Observation 8. It requires the payer to have
  a defensible standard for "right," which the master had.
- **Expect the envy framing.** Observation 5. This is a hard saying, and it should be used
  carefully: the text labels the complaint as envy in this specific case, where the agreements
  were kept. It is not a general licence to dismiss pay complaints.
- **The eleventh-hour hiring is an act of provision.** Observation 7. The workers had been
  available all day. The master's late hiring gave them a wage they would not otherwise have had.
  This connects to A13 (provision precedes assignment) and to the Deuteronomic care for the poor.

### Masters: give that which is just and equal

**Text (Ephesians 6:5–9, KJV)**

> 5. Servants, be obedient to them that are your masters according to the flesh, with fear and
>    trembling, in singleness of your heart, as unto Christ;
> 6. Not with eyeservice, as menpleasers; but as the servants of Christ, doing the will of God from
>    the heart;
> 7. With good will doing service, as to the Lord, and not to men:
> 8. Knowing that whatsoever good thing any man doeth, the same shall he receive of the Lord, whether
>    he be bond or free.
> 9. And, ye masters, do the same things unto them, forbearing threatening: knowing that your Master
>    also is in heaven; neither is there respect of persons with him.

**Text (Colossians 4:1, KJV)**

> Masters, give unto your servants that which is just and equal; knowing that ye also have a Master
> in heaven.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the symmetry):** Both passages address both parties. Ephesians 6:5–8 addresses
   servants; 6:9 addresses masters. Colossians 4:1 addresses masters directly.
2. **Text states:** The instruction to masters is stated in the same imperative mode as the
   instruction to servants: "do the same things unto them" (Eph 6:9).
3. **Text states (the specific prohibition):** "forbearing threatening" (Eph 6:9). **Inference:**
   the named failure mode of a master is the use of threats, and it is the only behaviour
   specifically forbidden.
4. **Text states (the standard):** "that which is just and equal" (Col 4:1). **Text states:** Two
   criteria: _just_ (what is owed) and _equal_ (fairness in the treatment). See `ethics.md` for the
   Micah 6:8 test, which has a similar two-part structure.
5. **Text states (the ground):** Both passages give the same reason: "knowing that your Master also
   is in heaven" (Eph 6:9); "knowing that ye also have a Master in heaven" (Col 4:1). **Text
   states:** The master is under a master.
6. **Text states (the impartiality):** "neither is there respect of persons with him" (Eph 6:9).
   **Inference:** the heavenly Master does not favour the earthly master in a dispute.
7. **Text states (the servant's motivation, which is also redefined):** "not with eyeservice, as
   menpleasers; but as the servants of Christ" (Eph 6:6). **Inference:** work is redirected to a
   different audience, which removes the master's ability to command by approval.

**Business consequences:**

- **"Just and equal" is a two-part standard.** Observation 4. Just is the contract; equal is the
  treatment. A company can be just and unequal (paying exactly what was agreed, while treating
  people differently).
- **Threatening is the named failure mode.** Observation 3. This is unusually specific and worth
  taking literally: the management behaviour that is singled out for prohibition is the threat.
- **The accountability is vertical.** Observation 5. The manager's own master is named, which is
  what makes the standard non-negotiable by the organisation.
- **Impartiality is stated explicitly.** Observation 6. The heavenly master does not take the
  master's side.
- **Eyeservice is a structural problem, not a character flaw.** Observation 7. If the only
  observer is the manager, the work will be shaped by the observation. The remedy in the text is a
  change of audience, not more supervision.

## Part 7. Initiative, diligence, and the ant

### The ant: initiative without a supervisor

**Text (Proverbs 6:6–8, KJV)**

> 6. Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise:
> 7. Which having no guide, overseer, or ruler,
> 8. Provideth her meat in the summer, and gathereth her food in the harvest.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Three levels of supervision are explicitly absent: "no guide, overseer, or
   ruler" (v. 7). **Inference:** the three terms correspond to increasing authority — a guide
   (leader), an overseer (supervisor), and a ruler (executive).
2. **Text states:** The ant's activity is _seasonal_: "Provideth her meat in the summer, and
   gathereth her food in the harvest" (v. 8). **Inference:** the initiative is in service of a
   future need, and it is timed to the window in which the work is possible.
3. **Text states:** The instruction is to _study_ the ant: "Go to the ant… consider her ways, and
   be wise." **Inference:** the ant is presented as a model to be observed, not merely cited.
4. **Text states:** The addressee is the "sluggard" — and the remedy offered is not exhortation but
   observation. **Inference:** the failure being corrected is not laziness in the abstract but the
   inability to act without direction.

**Inference — the axiom:** the desirable state is productive activity that does not require
supervision. The ant is not described as hardworking in the abstract; it is described as
_unsupervised_ and _effective_.

**Business consequences:**

- **Design for the absence of the guide, the overseer, and the ruler.** Observation 1. A role that
  only functions under supervision is not a role; it is an extension of the supervisor.
- **Time the work to the window.** Observation 2. The ant's activity is seasonal, and the season
  is the harvest. This is A8 (cadence) applied to individual initiative.
- **Autonomy and effectiveness are linked, not opposed.** Observation 4. The ant is the model for
  both.
- **The absence of supervision is the test.** An organisation that measures its people by
  observed activity will not produce ants.

### Diligence in business (Proverbs 22:29)

**Text (Proverbs 22:29, KJV)**

> Seest thou a man diligent in his business? he shall stand before kings; he shall not stand before
> mean men.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The subject is "a man diligent in his business." **Inference:** the diligence is
   in _his own_ business — the thing he is responsible for — not in diligence generally.
2. **Text states:** The reward is stated as _access_: "he shall stand before kings; he shall not
   stand before mean men." **Inference:** the reward for diligence is a change in _who you deal
   with_, not in what you own.
3. **Text states:** The contrast is between kings and "mean men" — the ordinary or obscure.
   **Inference:** the movement is from the general to the specific, from a broad market to a
   narrower and higher one.
4. **Text states:** The form is observational: "Seest thou a man…?" **Inference:** the proverb
   presents this as something observable, not as a promise to a specific person.

**Business consequences:**

- **Diligence is a route to better counterparties, not merely to more output.** Observation 2.
  This reframes what diligence is for.
- **"His business" is the object.** Observation 1. Diligence about other people's business is
  busyness.
- **The reward compounds through access.** Observation 3. Better counterparties produce better
  opportunities, which produce better counterparties. This is A11 (multiplication) in the
  reputational domain.
- **The proverb is observable, which means it can be used as a hiring signal.** Observation 4. A
  track record of diligence in a prior responsibility is visible.

### If any would not work, neither should he eat

**Text (2 Thessalonians 3:10–12, KJV)**

> 10. For even when we were with you, this we commanded you, that if any would not work, neither
>     should he eat.
> 11. For we hear that there are some which walk among you disorderly, working not at all, but are
>     busybodies.
> 12. Now them that are such we command and exhort by our Lord Jesus Christ, that with quietness they
>     work, and eat their own bread.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The condition is _volitional_: "if any **would not** work." Not "cannot work."
   **Inference:** the rule addresses refusal, not incapacity.
2. **Text states:** The problem named is not idleness as such but _disorderly_ behaviour: "there are
   some which walk among them disorderly, working not at all, but are busybodies" (v. 11).
   **Inference:** the two clauses are connected — not working is what frees the person to meddle.
3. **Text states:** The remedy is stated as a positive instruction: "that with quietness they work,
   and eat their own bread" (v. 12). **Text states:** Three elements: quietness, work, and eating
   one's own bread.
4. **Text states:** The instruction is given with authority: "we command and exhort by our Lord
   Jesus Christ."
5. **Text states (the surrounding context):** Paul's own example is cited: 2 Thessalonians 3:8 —
   "Neither did we eat any man's bread for nought; but wrought with labour and travail night and
   day, that we might not be chargeable to any of you." **Inference:** the rule is grounded in the
   teacher's own practice.

**Business consequences:**

- **The rule is about refusal, not incapacity.** Observation 1. A policy that reads this as a
  statement about the disabled has misread the condition clause.
- **The failure mode is not idleness but meddling.** Observation 2. "Busybodies" is the stated
  symptom, and it is a real organisational pathology: people with no assigned work create
  problems elsewhere.
- **The remedy is "their own bread."** Observation 3. The goal is not punishment but a return to
  self-sufficiency.
- **The rule is grounded in the leader's own practice.** Observation 5. Paul had the standing to
  say it because he had done it.

### Study to be quiet and do your own business

**Text (1 Thessalonians 4:11–12, KJV)**

> 11. And that ye study to be quiet, and to do your own business, and to work with your own hands, as
>     we commanded you;
> 12. That ye may walk honestly toward them that are without, and that ye may have lack of nothing.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** "Study to be quiet" — quietness is the object of study, i.e. it is deliberately
   cultivated. **Inference:** quietness is a skill, not a temperament.
2. **Text states:** "Do your own business" — the scope of the work is defined by ownership.
3. **Text states:** "Work with your own hands" — the work is manual and direct.
4. **Text states:** The purpose is two-fold: "that ye may walk honestly toward them that are
   without" (external reputation), "and that ye may have lack of nothing" (self-sufficiency).
5. **Text states:** The instruction is a reminder of prior teaching: "as we commanded you."

**Business consequences:**

- **Quietness is cultivated, not innate.** Observation 1. This is a trainable attribute and can be
  selected for.
- **"Your own business" is a scope instruction.** Observation 2. The counterpart to the busybody
  of 2 Thessalonians 3:11.
- **The two purposes are external credibility and internal sufficiency.** Observation 4. Both are
  named, and the first is reputation.

## Part 8. Authority as service

### Mark 10:42–45: the pagan model, named and forbidden

**Text (Mark 10:42–45, KJV)**

> 42. But Jesus called them to him, and saith unto them, Ye know that they which are accounted to
>     rule over the Gentiles exercise lordship over them; and their great ones exercise authority upon
>     them.
> 43. But so shall it not be among you: but whosoever will be great among you, shall be your
>     minister:
> 44. And whosoever of you will be the chiefest, shall be servant of all.
> 45. For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a
>     ransom for many.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The pagan model is described with two verbs: "exercise lordship over them" and
   "exercise authority upon them" (v. 42). **Inference:** both verbs describe authority _directed
   at_ people rather than _exercised for_ them.
2. **Text states:** The prohibition is explicit: "But so shall it not be among you" (v. 43).
   **Inference:** this is a command, not a suggestion.
3. **Text states:** The replacement is stated as a _condition of greatness_: "whosoever will be
   great among you, shall be your minister" (v. 43), and "whosoever of you will be the chiefest,
   shall be servant of all" (v. 44). **Inference:** the desire for greatness is not condemned; the
   _route_ to it is redefined.
4. **Text states:** The escalation: "great" → "minister"; "chiefest" → "servant of all." The
   higher the ambition, the lower the service.
5. **Text states:** The ground is Christ's own pattern: "For even the Son of man came not to be
   ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many" (v. 45).
6. **Text states:** The passage is addressed to the twelve in response to a specific dispute about
   position (Mark 10:35–41, the request of James and John and the indignation of the ten).
   **Inference:** the teaching is a response to a real org-chart conflict.

**Business consequences:**

- **The prohibition is on the direction of authority, not its existence.** Observation 1. The
  problem is authority _over_ rather than authority _for_.
- **Ambition is redirected, not forbidden.** Observation 3. The text assumes people will want to be
  great; it specifies the route.
- **The route is counterintuitive and stated in degrees.** Observation 4. The higher the position,
  the greater the service — which means the org chart and the service chart run in opposite
  directions.
- **The pattern is grounded in the leader's own example.** Observation 5. This is what makes it
  enforceable: the standard is not an abstraction.
- **Expect the dispute.** Observation 6. The teaching arose from a status conflict among the
  leadership team. This is A29 (authority gets contested).

### 1 Peter 5:1–4: not for money, not as lords

**Text (1 Peter 5:1–4, KJV)**

> 1. The elders which are among you I exhort, who am also an elder, and a witness of the sufferings
>    of Christ, and also a partaker of the glory that shall be revealed:
> 2. Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but
>    willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind;
> 3. Neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being ensamples to the flock.
> 4. And when the chief Shepherd shall appear, ye shall receive a crown of glory that fadeth not
>    away.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The primary task is stated first: "Feed the flock of God which is among you"
   (v. 2). **Inference:** the leader's job is provision, not command.
2. **Text states:** Three pairs of contrasts, each naming a wrong motive and a right one:
   - "not by constraint, but willingly" — compulsion vs. willingness
   - "not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind" — money vs. eagerness
   - "neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being ensamples" — domination vs. example
3. **Text states:** The third contrast gives the alternative mechanism: **"being ensamples."**
   **Inference:** the leader's influence is to operate by _example_ rather than by command.
4. **Text states:** The reward is deferred: "And when the chief Shepherd shall appear, ye shall
   receive a crown of glory that fadeth not away" (v. 4). **Inference:** the immediate reward is
   not the point, which is why the money motive is specifically excluded.
5. **Text states:** Peter establishes his standing first: "who am also an elder, and a witness of
   the sufferings of Christ" (v. 1). **Inference:** he speaks as a peer and as a witness, not from
   above.

**Business consequences:**

- **"Being ensamples" is the mechanism, and it is specific.** Observation 3. An example is
  something people can see and copy. The leader's actual behaviour is therefore the primary
  management instrument — not the policy, not the memo.
- **The money motive is named and excluded for leaders.** Observation 2. This is a specific
  prohibition on a specific motive for a specific role.
- **Feeding is the job description.** Observation 1. Not commanding, not representing, not
  deciding. Feeding.
- **The deferred reward is what makes the exclusions bearable.** Observation 4. A leader who
  expects immediate reward for the exclusions will not sustain them.

### John 13:1–17: the footwashing

**Text (John 13:1–17, KJV — selected)**

> 1. Now before the feast of the passover, when Jesus knew that his hour was come that he should
>    depart out of this world unto the Father, having loved his own which were in the world, he loved
>    them unto the end.
> 2. Jesus knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he was come from
>    God, and went to God;
> 3. He riseth from supper, and laid aside his garments; and took a towel, and girded himself.
> 4. After that he poureth water into a bason, and began to wash the disciples' feet, and to wipe
>    them with the towel wherewith he was girded.
> 5. Then cometh he to Simon Peter: and Peter saith unto him, Lord, dost thou wash my feet?
> 6. Jesus answered and said unto him, What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter.
> 7. So after he had washed their feet, and had taken his garments, and was set down again, he said
>    unto them, Know ye what I have done to you?
> 8. Ye call me Master and Lord: and ye say well; for so I am.
> 9. If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's
>    feet.
> 10. For I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you.
> 11. Verily, verily, I say unto you, The servant is not greater than his lord; neither he that is
>     sent greater than he that sent him.
> 12. If ye know these things, happy are ye if ye do them.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the sequence in verse 3):** The act is preceded by a statement of _security_:
   "Jesus knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he was come from
   God, and went to God." **Inference:** the footwashing follows the knowledge of full authority.
   The service is performed _from_ a position of security, not in spite of one.
2. **Text states (the physical detail):** Four verbs: "riseth from supper," "laid aside his
   garments," "took a towel," "girded himself" (v. 4). Then he pours water and washes (v. 5).
   **Inference:** the act is described in detail — this is a physical, menial, unhurried
   performance.
3. **Text states:** The act is _unexplained at the time_: "What I do thou knowest not now; but thou
   shalt know hereafter" (v. 7). **Inference:** the service precedes the explanation, and the
   explanation is not a condition of the service.
4. **Text states:** The identity claim is _maintained_, not abandoned: "Ye call me Master and Lord:
   and ye say well; for so I am" (v. 13). **Inference:** the footwashing does not abolish the
   hierarchy. The Lord washes feet and remains the Lord.
5. **Text states:** The obligation is drawn directly: "If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed
   your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet" (v. 14). **Text states:** "ought" — an
   obligation, not an imitation.
6. **Text states:** The example is stated as the mechanism: "For I have given you an example, that
   ye should do as I have done to you" (v. 15). **Inference:** this is the same word as 1 Peter
   5:3's "ensamples."
7. **Text states:** The saying about the servant follows: "The servant is not greater than his lord;
   neither he that is sent greater than he that sent him" (v. 16).
8. **Text states:** The conclusion is conditional on practice: "If ye know these things, happy are
   ye if ye do them" (v. 17). **Inference:** knowing is not the point.

**Business consequences:**

- **Service is performed from security, not toward it.** Observation 1. The footwashing happened
  after the knowledge of "all things given into his hands." A leader who serves in order to secure
  their position is doing something different from a leader who serves from a secured position.
- **The hierarchy is not abolished.** Observation 4. This is important because it prevents the
  misreading where "servant leadership" means no authority. The text says: Master and Lord, _and_
  footwasher.
- **The act precedes the explanation.** Observation 3. A leader who explains before serving has
  turned the service into a communication.
- **The obligation is stated as "ought."** Observation 5. Not "may," not "might consider."
- **The example is the mechanism.** Observations 6 and 7, and it is the same term used in 1 Peter
  5:3.
- **Knowing is not doing.** Observation 8. The conclusion is conditional on the doing.

## Part 9. The body: diverse members, one body

### 1 Corinthians 12: many members, unequal honour

**Text (1 Corinthians 12:4–7, 12–27, KJV — selected)**

> 4. Now there are diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit.
> 5. And there are differences of administrations, but the same Lord.
> 6. And there are diversities of operations, but it is the same God which worketh all in all.

> 14. Nay, much more those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are necessary:
> 15. And those members of the body, which we think to be less honourable, upon these we bestow more
>     abundant honour; and our uncomely parts have more abundant comeliness.
> 16. For our comely parts have no need: but God hath tempered the body together, having given more
>     abundant honour to that part which lacked:
> 17. That there should be no schism in the body; but that the members should have the same care one
>     for another.
> 18. And whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it; or one member be honoured, all
>     the members rejoice with it.
> 19. Now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (three diversities, three unities):** Gifts/Spirit, administrations/Lord,
   operations/God (vv. 4–6). **Inference:** the differentiation is as structured as the unity. Both
   are stated as facts.
2. **Text states:** The purpose is stated: "the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man
   **to profit withal**" (v. 7). **Inference:** the gifts are for the body's benefit, not the
   individual's.
3. **Text states:** The self-exclusion argument is presented and rejected: "If the foot shall say,
   Because I am not the hand, I am not of the body; is it therefore not of the body?" (v. 15).
   **Inference:** a member's own judgment about their belonging is not determinative.
4. **Text states:** The single-member reductio: "If the whole body were an eye, where were the
   hearing?" (v. 17). **Inference:** a homogeneous organisation cannot perform the functions it has
   excluded.
5. **Text states:** Placement is _assigned_, not chosen: "God hath set the members every one of them
   in the body, as it hath pleased him" (v. 18). **Inference:** the structure is designed, not
   emergent.
6. **Text states:** The mutual-need rule runs both ways: the eye cannot dismiss the hand, and the
   head cannot dismiss the feet (v. 21).
7. **Text states (the honour rule):** "those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are
   necessary" (v. 22), and "those members of the body, which we think to be less honourable, upon
   these we bestow more abundant honour" (v. 23).
8. **Text states:** The purpose of the unequal honour is stated: "That there should be no schism in
   the body; but that the members should have the same care one for another" (v. 25). **Inference:**
   the honour differential is _compensatory_, and it exists to prevent division.
9. **Text states:** The sympathy rule: "whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it;
   or one member be honoured, all the members rejoice with it" (v. 26).

**Inference — the axiom:** the organisation's weaker and less visible parts require _more_
deliberate recognition, not less, and the purpose of the extra recognition is to prevent the
organisation from splitting.

**Business consequences:**

- **The "less honourable" members get more honour by design.** Observation 7. This is a
  counter-intuitive allocation rule: recognition should be inversely weighted to visibility.
- **The purpose is structural cohesion, not kindness.** Observation 8. The reason for the extra
  honour is "that there should be no schism."
- **Homogeneity is a functional failure, not just a cultural one.** Observation 4. A team of
  identical people cannot do the things the absent types do.
- **Membership is not self-assessed.** Observation 3. The foot's opinion about its own membership
  is wrong, and the text says so.
- **Placement is designed.** Observation 5. Someone decides who is in which role, and the text
  attributes it to God. **Inference:** the org design is an act of placement, not a market
  outcome.
- **Sympathy runs in both directions.** Observation 9. Suffering _and_ honour are shared.

### Romans 12:3–8: the measure of faith, and the list of functions

**Text (Romans 12:3–8, KJV)**

> 3. For I say, through the grace given unto me, to every man that is among you, not to think of
>    himself more highly than he ought to think; but to think soberly, according as God hath dealt to
>    every man the measure of faith.
> 4. For as we have many members in one body, and all members have not the same office:
> 5. So we, being many, are one body in Christ, and every one members one of another.
> 6. Having then gifts differing according to the grace that is given to us, whether prophecy, let us
>    prophesy according to the proportion of faith;
> 7. Or ministry, let us wait on our ministering: or he that teacheth, on teaching;
> 8. Or he that exhorteth, on exhortation: he that giveth, let him do it with simplicity; he that
>    ruleth, with diligence; he that sheweth mercy, with cheerfulness.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Self-assessment is commanded to be _sober_: "not to think of himself more highly
   than he ought to think; but to think soberly" (v. 3). **Inference:** the standard is accuracy,
   not modesty. Both over- and under-estimation would be inaccurate.
2. **Text states:** The measure is given: "according as God hath dealt to every man the measure of
   faith" (v. 3). **Inference:** the assessment is relative to what was _given_, which is A1 and
   connects to Matthew 25's differentiated allocation.
3. **Text states:** "all members have not the same office" (v. 4). **Text states:** The difference
   is stated as a fact, not a problem.
4. **Text states:** The function list (vv. 6–8) pairs each gift with a _manner_:
   - prophecy → "according to the proportion of faith"
   - ministry → "let us wait on our ministering"
   - teaching → "on teaching"
   - exhortation → "on exhortation"
   - giving → "with simplicity"
   - ruling → "**with diligence**"
   - mercy → "with cheerfulness"
5. **Text states:** The manner for ruling is _diligence_. **Inference:** the failure mode of the
   ruler is not harshness here (as in Mark 10) but neglect.
6. **Text states:** The manner for giving is _simplicity_. **Inference:** giving without complexity,
   i.e. without a hidden agenda.
7. **Text states:** The list mixes "up-front" functions (prophecy, teaching, exhortation, ruling)
   with behind-the-scenes functions (ministry, giving, mercy), and gives them all the same
   grammatical weight.

**Business consequences:**

- **Sober self-assessment means accuracy, not humility theatre.** Observation 1. Under-claiming
  your capability is also a failure to think soberly.
- **The measure is what was given.** Observation 2. A manager's assessment of their own performance
  should be relative to the resources allocated, which is exactly Matthew 25's logic.
- **Each function has a characteristic failure mode, and the text names the manner.** Observation 4. This is the most operationally useful thing in the passage: the pairing of a function with
  the quality that makes it work.
- **The ruler's named quality is diligence.** Observation 5. Not vision, not charisma. This is
  worth noting against most leadership literature.
- **The list is flat.** Observation 7. No function is ranked.

### The weaker members are necessary

Stated separately because it is the passage's sharpest organisational claim.

**Text states:** "Nay, much more those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are
necessary" (1 Corinthians 12:22).

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The claim is _comparative_: "much more… are necessary." **Inference:** the
   weaker members are not merely included; they are described as more necessary.
2. **Text states:** The qualifier is "which **seem** to be more feeble." **Inference:** the
   assessment of feebleness is the observer's, and the text marks it as appearance.
3. **Text states:** The honour rule that follows is compensatory: "upon these we bestow more
   abundant honour" (v. 23).
4. **Text states:** The reason is stated: "For our comely parts have no need" (v. 24). **Inference:**
   the visible parts do not require the additional honour because they already receive it.

**Business consequences:**

- **The apparent weakness is an assessment, not a fact.** Observation 2. The Greek qualifier
  "seem" is doing work: the observer's judgment about which parts are weak is unreliable.
- **Necessity and visibility are inversely related in this passage.** Observation 1. The parts that
  look least essential are stated to be the most necessary.
- **Honour allocation should be compensatory by design.** Observation 3. Not "equal honour to all,"
  but _more_ to the parts that receive less by default.
- **The mechanism is the prevention of schism.** From verse 25. The purpose of the compensation is
  cohesion, which means the honour policy is a management instrument with a measurable objective.

## Part 10. Counsel and the load not borne alone

### Numbers 11:16–17: the seventy elders

**Text (Numbers 11:16–17, KJV)**

> 16. And the LORD said unto Moses, Gather unto me seventy men of the elders of Israel, whom thou
>     knowest to be the elders of the people, and officers over them; and bring them unto the tabernacle
>     of the congregation, that they may stand there with thee.
> 17. And I will come down and talk with thee there: and I will take of the spirit which is upon
>     thee, and will put it upon them; and they shall bear the burden of the people with thee, that thou
>     bear it not thyself alone.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The selection criterion is _prior knowledge_: "whom thou knowest to be the
   elders of the people, and officers over them" (v. 16). **Inference:** the candidates are already
   known to be leaders; the selection is not a search.
2. **Text states:** The number is specific: seventy.
3. **Text states:** The mechanism is _transfer of the same spirit_: "I will take of the spirit which
   is upon thee, and will put it upon them" (v. 17). **Inference:** the capability is distributed,
   not merely the workload. The new leaders receive what the existing leader has.
4. **Text states:** The purpose is stated in the exact language of Genesis 2:18 and Exodus 18:18:
   "that thou bear it not thyself alone." **Text states:** The phrase "thyself alone" appears here
   and in Exodus 18:18.
5. **Text states:** The elders are brought to a specific place: "bring them unto the tabernacle of
   the congregation, that they may stand there with thee" (v. 16). **Inference:** the appointment
   includes a _public standing with_ the leader, not just a task assignment.
6. **Text states (the occasion):** Numbers 11:14 gives Moses' own statement of the problem: "I am
   not able to bear all this people alone, because it is too heavy for me." **Inference:** Moses
   states the same diagnosis Jethro had given.

**Inference — the axiom:** the load is not to be borne alone, and the remedy is _distributed
capacity_, not merely delegated tasks. The spirit is transferred, not just the work.

**Business consequences:**

- **Distribute the capability, not just the tasks.** Observation 3. A leader who delegates work
  without transferring the capacity to do it has created a bottleneck with extra steps.
- **The purpose clause is the same in three places.** Observation 4: Genesis 2:18, Exodus 18:18,
  Numbers 11:17 all use "alone." **Inference:** this is a stated design principle, repeated.
- **Select people already known to be leaders.** Observation 1. The criteria are prior, observable
  leadership.
- **Public standing is part of the appointment.** Observation 5. The elders stand _with_ Moses.
- **Moses diagnosed it himself.** Observation 6. The overloaded leader usually knows.

### Two are better than one (Ecclesiastes 4:9–12)

**Text (Ecclesiastes 4:9–12, KJV)**

> 9. Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour.
> 10. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he
>     falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.
> 11. Again, if two lie together, then they have heat: but how can one be warm alone?
> 12. And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly
>     broken.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (four benefits, in order):** (a) a better reward for labour (v. 9); (b) recovery
   from a fall (v. 10); (c) warmth (v. 11); (d) defence against an attacker (v. 12).
2. **Text states:** The first benefit is _economic_: "they have a good reward for their labour."
   **Inference:** the partnership is justified on returns, not only on support.
3. **Text states:** The second is _recovery_: "if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow"
   (v. 10). And the woe: "woe to him that is alone when he falleth."
4. **Text states:** The third is _survival_: warmth (v. 11). **Inference:** the benefit is basic,
   not strategic.
5. **Text states:** The fourth is _defence_: "if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him"
   (v. 12).
6. **Text states (the escalation):** The passage moves from two to three: "a threefold cord is not
   quickly broken" (v. 12). **Inference:** the text does not stop at the pair. The threefold cord is
   the stronger structure.

**Business consequences:**

- **Partnership has an economic justification, not only a moral one.** Observation 2. "A good
  reward for their labour."
- **The recovery benefit is stated as a woe when absent.** Observation 3. "Woe to him that is
  alone when he falleth." **Inference:** the inability to be lifted up is a stated condition of
  grief, not merely an inefficiency.
- **The threefold cord is the design target.** Observation 6. A pair can be broken by a third
  party; three is more resilient. This is an argument for boards of three rather than two, and for
  three-way partnerships over pairs.
- **The benefits escalate in severity.** Observations 2–5: returns, recovery, survival, defence.
  The text presents partnership as covering the full range.

### Counsel (Proverbs 15:22; 20:18)

**Text (Proverbs 15:22, KJV)**

> Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are
> established.

**Text (Proverbs 20:18, KJV)**

> Every purpose is established by counsel: and with good advice make war.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the antithesis in 15:22):** "Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in
   the multitude of counsellors they are established." **Text states:** The contrast is
   _disappointed_ versus _established_.
2. **Text states:** The quantity is specified: "the multitude of counsellors." **Inference:** the
   benefit is stated to scale with the number, not merely to require one adviser.
3. **Text states (20:18):** The claim is _universal_: "Every purpose is established by counsel."
   Not "some purposes."
4. **Text states:** The second half is specific: "and with good advice make war." **Inference:** the
   proverb applies the general principle to the highest-stakes decision available — going to war.
   The application to consequential decisions is the point.
5. **Text states:** The word for "purpose" is the same in both verses. **Inference:** the two
   proverbs are the same claim stated twice, once with the antithesis and once with the extension
   to war.

**Business consequences:**

- **The scale of the benefit is stated.** Observation 2. "A multitude of counsellors," not one.
- **The principle applies most to the highest-stakes decisions.** Observation 4. The proverb
  attaches it to war.
- **"Disappointed" is the failure mode.** Observation 1. The purpose does not fail loudly; it is
  disappointed, which suggests it is quietly not achieved.
- **Counsel must be sought before the decision, not after.** Observation 4. "With good advice make
  war" — the advice precedes the engagement.
- **This connects to A18.** The same claim, grounded in the first "not good" in Scripture, and
  reinforced by the "multitude" language here.

## Part 11. Working rules

Inferences, stated as working rules.

**On structure**

1. **Diagnose before you restructure** (Exodus 18:14). Jethro asked what was happening first.
2. **If one person is the bottleneck, the fault is the design, not the person** (Exodus 18:18).
   "The thing is too heavy for thee."
3. **Delegate the caseload; retain the teaching and the direction** (Exodus 18:19–20).
4. **Use a two-category escalation rule with delegation as the default** (Exodus 18:22).
5. **Name the escalation threshold in advance** (A20). The text leaves "great" undefined; a
   business cannot.
6. **Separate selection from appointment** (Acts 6:3, 5–6). The team selects; the leader appoints;
   the appointment is public.
7. **State the focus trade publicly, and name what you will do instead** (Acts 6:2, 4).
8. **Hire on attested reputation, not interview performance** (Acts 6:3, "of honest report").
9. **Divide the work into named segments with a single owner each** (Nehemiah 3).
10. **Assign by existing structure — family, trade, locality — and align by proximity where
    possible** (Nehemiah 3:10, 23, 28).
11. **Define "done" to include the fittings** (Nehemiah 3:3, 6, 13–15).
12. **Record over-delivery and non-delivery by name** (Nehemiah 3:5, 20, 27).

**On risk and defence**

13. **Do the reconnaissance yourself, quietly, before announcing anything** (Nehemiah 2:12–16).
14. **Inspect specific assets, not general conditions** (Nehemiah 2:13–15).
15. **Pray and set a watch** (Nehemiah 4:9). Both acts, scheduled.
16. **Carry the defence; do not store it** (Nehemiah 4:18).
17. **Give a dispersed team one concentration signal** (Nehemiah 4:18–20).
18. **Rotate rather than double-shift** (Nehemiah 4:22). Guard at night, build by day.
19. **Put the leader inside the arrangement** (Nehemiah 4:23).
20. **Price the meeting: what stops while you attend?** (Nehemiah 6:3).
21. **Refuse with a positive claim about the work, and repeat the refusal identically**
    (Nehemiah 6:3–4).
22. **Expect escalation after a refusal, and test counsel against your own standing**
    (Nehemiah 6:10–12).

**On money and exploitation**

23. **Restitution restores the gain, not just the principal, and it is immediate** (Nehemiah
    5:11–12).
24. **Name the categories of restitution specifically** (Nehemiah 5:11).
25. **The leader must be inside the rebuke** (Nehemiah 5:10).
26. **Pay during the work** (Deuteronomy 25:4). The ox eats while treading.
27. **The worker must be able to form an expectation of return** (1 Corinthians 9:10, "in hope").
28. **Withheld wages are robbery, not a treasury decision** (Leviticus 19:13; James 5:4).
29. **Honour the agreement, and decide your pay-transparency policy deliberately**
    (Matthew 20:13, 8).
30. **"Just and equal" is a two-part standard** (Colossians 4:1). Just is the contract; equal is
    the treatment.
31. **Stop taking resources when the work is funded** (Exodus 36:5–7). "Too much" is a state the
    text names.

**On people and capability**

32. **Craftsmanship is a gifting** (Exodus 31:3). Treat skilled people accordingly.
33. **Teaching is part of the craft** (Exodus 35:34). The master craftsman produces craftspeople.
34. **Distribute the capability, not just the tasks** (Numbers 11:17).
35. **Select people already known to be leaders** (Numbers 11:16).
36. **Design for the absence of the guide, the overseer, and the ruler** (Proverbs 6:7).
37. **Quietness and doing your own business are trainable and selectable** (1 Thessalonians
    4:11).
38. **Self-assessment must be sober — accurate, not modest** (Romans 12:3).
39. **Pair each function with its characteristic manner** (Romans 12:6–8). The ruler's quality is
    diligence.
40. **Weight recognition inversely to visibility** (1 Corinthians 12:23). The purpose is to prevent
    schism.
41. **The weaker parts are the necessary parts** (1 Corinthians 12:22).

**On authority**

42. **Authority exercised over people is forbidden; authority exercised for them is required**
    (Mark 10:42–43).
43. **Ambition is redirected, not forbidden** (Mark 10:43–44).
44. **Lead by example, because an example is what people can copy** (1 Peter 5:3; John 13:15).
45. **Serve from a secured position, not toward one** (John 13:3–4).
46. **The hierarchy is not abolished by service** (John 13:13). Master and Lord, _and_ footwasher.
47. **Do not threaten** (Ephesians 6:9). It is the one behaviour named for prohibition.

**On succession**

48. **The founder's final deliverable is a specification** (1 Chronicles 28:11–19).
49. **Preparation is a legitimate and nameable role** (1 Chronicles 22:5).
50. **Name the successor, and charge them personally and conditionally** (1 Chronicles 28:5, 9).
51. **Make the attribution statement at the handover, in public** (1 Chronicles 29:14).
52. **Have the leadership commit first, then the wider group** (1 Chronicles 29:6, 9).
53. **Do not always finish what you started. Prepare it and hand it over** (1 Chronicles 22:5).

**On counsel**

54. **Seek a multitude of counsellors, not one** (Proverbs 15:22).
55. **Apply counsel most rigorously to the highest-stakes decisions** (Proverbs 20:18, "with good
    advice make war").
56. **The load is not to be borne alone** (Genesis 2:18; Exodus 18:18; Numbers 11:17).
57. **A threefold cord is stronger than a pair** (Ecclesiastes 4:12).
