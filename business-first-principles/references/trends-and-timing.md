# Trends and Timing

Reading the times and acting under uncertainty.

## Method and labels

**Text states** / **Lexical** / **Inference** / **Speculation**. Verses from `scripture.ts`,
Strong's definitions verbatim from `lexicon.ts`.

Governing axioms: **A3** (presence precedes production — the brooding before the word), **A8**
(time is a created instrument), **A19** (doing the work reveals the need), **A27** (friction is
structural), **A30** (mortality makes time the binding constraint), **A32** (some states must not
be allowed to become permanent).

**The core distinction in this file.** 1 Chronicles 12:32 names two capacities, not one:
_understanding of the times_ and _to know what Israel ought to do_. They are stated as separate
things and both are required. Most failures of timing are failures of the first — an accurate
reading of the moment without a corresponding action — and most failures of nerve are failures of
the second. This file treats them as distinct skills with distinct failure modes.

## Part 1. The anchor text: Issachar

**Text (1 Chronicles 12:32, KJV)**

> And of the children of Issachar, which were men that had understanding of the times, to know what
> Israel ought to do; the heads of them were two hundred; and all their brethren were at their
> commandment.

### The two capacities

**Text states:** The verse names two things:

1. **"understanding of the times"** — the reading
2. **"to know what Israel ought to do"** — the action

**Text states:** The two are joined by "to," which in the KJV here expresses purpose or result. The
reading exists _in order that_ the action may be known. But the two are distinct capacities: a
person can have one without the other.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The men are described as _understanding_. Not "watchful," not "informed," not
   "prophetic." The word is about comprehension.
2. **Text states:** The object of the understanding is "the times" — a plural noun. **Lexical:**
   the word is **H6256 עֵת (_eth_)** — "time, especially (adverb with preposition) now, when, etc."
   KJV: "[phrase] after, (al-)ways, [idiom] certain, [phrase] continually, [phrase] evening, long,
   (due) season, so (long) as, (even-, evening-, noon-) tide, (meal-), what) time, when." **Text
   states:** This is not the same word as _moed_ (H4150, "an appointment, i.e. a fixed time or
   season") used in Genesis 1:14. _Eth_ is the ordinary word for _time_ or _occasion_.
3. **Text states:** The second capacity is stated with a _know_: "to know what Israel ought to do."
   **Lexical:** **H3045 יָדַע (_yada_)** — "to know (properly, to ascertain by seeing); used in a
   great variety of senses, figuratively, literally, euphemistically and inferentially (including
   observation, care, recognition; and causatively, instruction, designation, punishment, etc.)."
   **Inference:** _yada_ is knowledge by observation and acquaintance, not by deduction. The
   dictionary explicitly includes "ascertain by seeing."
4. **Text states:** The action verb is _do_ — **H6213 עָשָׂה (_asah_)** — "to do or make, in the
   broadest sense and widest application."
5. **Text states:** The subject of the action is the nation: "what Israel ought to do." Not what an
   individual should do. **Inference:** the reading of the times is in service of a collective
   decision.
6. **Text states:** The organisational detail: "the heads of them were two hundred; and all their
   brethren were at their commandment." **Inference:** the capacity is _institutionalised_ — two
   hundred men with this function, and the rest under their command. This is not one wise advisor.
   It is a corps.
7. **Text states (the context):** The chapter lists the tribes and their fighting men who came to
   David at Hebron. **Inference:** the Issacharites' contribution to the war effort was _intelligence
   about timing_, and it was listed alongside troop counts.

### The derivation

**Inference.** Four claims follow:

**1. Understanding the times is distinct from knowing what to do.** The verse names both because they
can be separated. A business can have excellent market intelligence and no plan; it can also have
decisive action and no reading. The first is paralysis with information; the second is speed in the
wrong direction.

**2. Both are required, and they are sequential.** The purpose clause puts the reading before the
knowing. The reading is not the goal; it is the input to the decision.

**3. The reading is a capability, not an inspiration.** Observation 6. Two hundred men, organised,
under command. If it were a prophetic gift it would be one person. It is a _function_.

**4. The reading is about the times, plural.** Observation 2. Not "the future" as a single object.
The word is _eth_ — times, occasions, seasons. **Inference:** the capability is to read the _present
moment_ correctly, and to know what it calls for. This is not forecasting; it is situational
awareness at the level of the whole environment.

### What the text does not say

**Inference.** Three limits, stated so the axiom is not over-applied:

1. **It does not say the reading was correct.** The verse describes the capacity, not a particular
   forecast's accuracy.
2. **It does not say how the reading was done.** No method is given. The text names the capability
   and the organisational structure, and nothing about the technique.
3. **It does not promise that understanding produces a good outcome.** In the chapters that follow,
   the united kingdom under David and Solomon eventually divides. Reading the times well is a
   capability, not a guarantee.

### Business consequences

- **Separate the two functions in the organisation and connect them explicitly.** Observation 6. A
  reading function that does not feed a decision function is a cost centre; a decision function with
  no reading function is a bet.
- **The reading function is a corps, not an advisor.** Observation 6. Two hundred men. **Inference:**
  the capability should be distributed — sales, support, field, finance, and the people closest to
  the customer — rather than concentrated in a strategy team.
- **It is knowledge by observation, not by inference.** Observation 3. _Yada_ is "ascertain by
  seeing." **Inference:** the reading is built from direct contact with the market, not from models
  of it.
- **The output is an action, not a report.** Observation 5. "What Israel ought to do."
- **Plural times.** Observation 4. Not one trend. The environment is a set of simultaneous
  conditions, and they are not the same for every part of the business.

## Part 2. Reading the sky and missing the time

**Text (Luke 12:54-56, KJV)**

> 54. And he said also to the people, When ye see a cloud rise out of the west, straightway ye say,
>     There cometh a shower; and so it is.
> 55. And when ye see the south wind blow, ye say, There will be heat; and it cometh to pass.
> 56. Ye hypocrites, ye can discern the face of the sky and of the earth; but how is it that ye do
>     not discern this time?

**Text (Matthew 16:2-3, KJV)**

> 2. He answered and said unto them, When it is evening, ye say, It will be fair weather: for the sky
>    is red.
> 3. And in the morning, It will be foul weather to day: for the sky is red and lowring. O ye
>    hypocrites, ye can discern the face of the sky; but can ye not discern the signs of the times?

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The forecasting is _accurate_. "and so it is" (Luke 54); "and it cometh to pass"
   (Luke 55). The text grants the skill without qualification.
2. **Text states:** The forecasts are _specific and conditional_: a cloud from the west means a
   shower; a south wind means heat; a red evening sky means fair weather; a red morning sky means
   foul weather.
3. **Text states:** The charge is not that the method is wrong. It is that the method is applied to
   the wrong object: "ye can discern the face of the sky… but how is it that ye do not discern this
   time?"
4. **Text states:** The word is "this time" (Luke) and "the signs of the times" (Matthew). **Text
   states:** In Matthew the word is **G4592 σημεῖον (_semeion_)** — "an indication, especially
   ceremonially or supernaturally." KJV: "miracle, sign, token, wonder." In Luke it is **G2540
   καιρός (_kairos_)** — "an occasion, i.e. set or proper time." KJV: "X always, opportunity,
   (convenient, due) season, (due, short, while) time, a while." **Inference:** _kairos_ is the
   appointed or opportune moment, not _chronos_ (elapsed time). The charge is about missing a
   _window_, not about being late on a schedule.
5. **Text states:** The verdict is "ye hypocrites." **Inference:** the charge is inconsistency — the
   same skill, applied selectively.
6. **Text states:** The capability is _demonstrated and uncontested_. Both passages grant that the
   people can and do read the sky correctly and act on it.

**Inference — the axiom this yields:** **the failure mode is not inability to read; it is the
selective application of a reading skill that is already working.**

The passage does not say the readers are ignorant. It says they are competent at reading one domain
and refuse to apply the same competence to another. The mechanism of the refusal is not stated, and
the obvious candidate is that the second domain has implications they do not want.

**Business consequences:**

- **The reading capability already exists in most companies; it is pointed at the wrong object.**
  Observation 3. Sales teams forecast demand accurately and do not forecast their own market's
  structural change.
- **The charge is inconsistency, not incompetence.** Observation 5. The test is: where else in the
  business do we apply this method, and why not here?
- **The object is the _kairos_, not the schedule.** Observation 4. The failure is missing a window
  that was open, not being late for a date.
- **Test the selective-application hypothesis.** **Inference:** if a business has excellent
  intelligence about one domain and none about an adjacent one, the reason is usually that the
  second domain's findings would require action the business does not want to take.
- **The signs are available.** Observation 6. Both passages assume the information is present. The
  failure is interpretive and volitional, not informational.

## Part 3. The cycles are reliable

### A time to every purpose (Ecclesiastes 3:1-8)

**Text (Ecclesiastes 3:1-8, KJV)**

> 1. To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven:
> 2. A time to be born, and a time to die; a time to plant, and a time to pluck up that which is
>    planted;
> 3. A time to kill, and a time to heal; a time to break down, and a time to build up;
> 4. A time to weep, and a time to laugh; a time to mourn, and a time to dance;
> 5. A time to cast away stones, and a time to gather stones together; a time to embrace, and a time
>    to refrain from embracing;
> 6. A time to get, and a time to lose; a time to keep, and a time to cast away;
> 7. A time to rend, and a time to sew; a time to keep silence, and a time to speak;
> 8. A time to love, and a time to hate; a time of war, and a time of peace.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the structure):** Fourteen pairs, twenty-eight items. Each pair is an antithesis:
   one thing and its opposite, both assigned a time.
2. **Text states:** The opposites are _both legitimate_. Planting and plucking up both have a time.
   Building and breaking down both have a time. **Inference:** the poem does not privilege one side
   of any pair. There is no "good" time and "bad" time; there are _times_.
3. **Text states:** The pairing includes economic and strategic items: plant/pluck up (v. 2),
   break down/build up (v. 3), cast away stones/gather stones (v. 5), get/lose (v. 6), keep/cast
   away (v. 6), war/peace (v. 8).
4. **Text states:** The frame: "To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under
   the heaven" (v. 1). **Text states:** The claim is universal — "every thing," "every purpose."
5. **Text states:** The word for "season" is **H2165 זְמָן (_zeman_)** — "an appointed occasion."
   KJV: "season, time." (Note: the identification of _this verse's_ "season" with H2165 is **not
   verified** — see the verification log. The definition is verified; the verse-level mapping is
   not.) The word for "time" here is **H6256 עֵת (_eth_)** — the same word as in 1 Chronicles 12:32.
   **Inference:** the two passages use the same term for the same concept.
6. **Text states (the conclusion, v. 11):** "He hath made every thing beautiful in his time." Not
   "every thing is beautiful." The beauty is _in its time_.
7. **Text states (the following verse, v. 11):** "also he hath set the world in their heart, so that
   no man can find out the work that God maketh from the beginning to the end." **Text states:** The
   limitation is stated in the same breath as the timing claim. **Inference:** the poem asserts that
   times exist and simultaneously asserts that the whole plan is not visible. The two claims are
   together, not in tension.

**Inference — the axioms this yields:**

- **Timing is a property of the action, not an accident.** Observation 1. Every item has a time. The
  question is not "is this good?" but "is this its time?"
- **The opposite action also has a time.** Observation 2. This is the poem's most useful feature for
  business: for every action you are considering, there is a _counter-action_ with its own season.
  Building up has a time; breaking down has a time. Keeping has a time; casting away has a time.
- **The full plan is not visible.** Observation 7. Which means the reading is always partial, and a
  decision made on a partial reading is the only kind available.

**Business consequences:**

- **Ask which side of the pair the present moment is.** Observation 2. The same company at different
  times should build and should break down.
- **The counter-action is not the failure mode.** Observation 2. Pruning is not the opposite of
  growing; it is a different season.
- **"Is this good?" is the wrong question.** Observation 1. The right one is "is this its time?"
- **Expect to be reading a partial picture.** Observation 7. Which is an argument for reversible
  decisions and staged commitments (A32).
- **Aesthetic judgment is time-bound.** Observation 6. "Beautiful in his time." The same design that
  is right now is wrong in two years.

### Seedtime and harvest shall not cease (Genesis 8:22)

**Text (Genesis 8:22, KJV)**

> While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day
> and night shall not cease.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The statement is a _guarantee_ with a stated duration: "while the earth
   remaineth."
2. **Text states:** Four pairs are named: seedtime/harvest, cold/heat, summer/winter, day/night.
   **Inference:** all four are cyclical, and all four are stated as pairs of opposites that alternate.
3. **Text states:** The verb is "shall not cease." **Text states:** The guarantee is that the
   _cycles continue_, not that any particular cycle is favourable.
4. **Text states:** The promise follows the flood (Genesis 8:21-22) and is a commitment about the
   _regularity_ of the created order after a catastrophic interruption.
5. **Text states (the context):** Genesis 8:21 states the reason: "for the imagination of man's heart
   is evil from his youth." **Inference:** the guarantee of regularity is given _despite_ human
   failure, not because of human merit.

**Inference — the axiom:** **the cycles are reliable, so bet on them.** A business plan that assumes
a permanent summer is a plan against a stated guarantee. A plan that assumes winter arrives is a plan
that can be made.

**Business consequences:**

- **Budget for the cycle, not for the current phase.** Observation 3. The guarantee is that the
  phase changes, not that it stays.
- **The guarantee is a floor for planning confidence.** Observation 1. "While the earth remaineth" is
  a stated duration, which is more than most forecasts offer.
- **The regularity is not conditional on performance.** Observation 5. The cycle continues
  regardless.
- **Seedtime is the input phase and it has a season.** Observation 2. **Inference:** investment
  activity has a season, and it is not the same season as harvesting.
- **This is the ground for countercyclical behaviour.** See Part 4.

### The covenant of day and night (Jeremiah 33:20-21)

**Text (Jeremiah 33:20-21, KJV)**

> 20. Thus saith the LORD; If ye can break my covenant of the day, and my covenant of the night, and
>     that there should not be day and night in their season;
> 21. Then may also my covenant be broken with David my servant, that he should not have a son to
>     reign upon his throne; and with the Levites the priests, my ministers.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The created order is described as a _covenant_: "my covenant of the day, and my
   covenant of the night."
2. **Text states:** The covenant's content is stated: "that there should not be day and night in
   their season." **Inference:** the covenant is about _regularity_ — the alternation in its season.
3. **Text states:** The argument is _a fortiori_: if you cannot break the day/night covenant, then
   the Davidic covenant cannot be broken either. **Text states:** The created order is the _standard
   of reliability_ against which a promise is measured.
4. **Text states:** The word "season" is used again. **Inference:** the regularity includes timing —
   day and night in their season, not merely day and night eventually.
5. **Text states:** The form is conditional and the condition is impossible: "If ye can break…" The
   impossibility of the first is the guarantee of the second.

**Inference.** The passage establishes the created order as the _most reliable thing available_, and
uses it as the reference point for evaluating a promise. This is the strongest statement in Scripture
of the reliability of the cycles.

**Business consequences:**

- **The most reliable reference class is the physical cycle.** Observation 3. **Inference:** when
  building a model of the future, the parts of the environment that follow physical and biological
  cycles are the parts with the highest confidence.
- **"In their season" is part of the covenant.** Observation 4. Timing is included in the guarantee,
  not just occurrence.
- **Use the reliable part as the anchor for the uncertain part.** Observation 3. The reasoning
  pattern is: because X is certain, Y can be trusted. **Inference:** the same structure applies to
  business planning — anchor on the cycles and build the uncertain parts around them.
- **Note the register.** The text calls it a covenant, which is the strongest category of commitment
  the vocabulary offers.

### Time and chance happeneth to them all (Ecclesiastes 9:11)

**Text (Ecclesiastes 9:11, KJV)**

> I returned, and saw under the sun, that the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong,
> neither yet bread to the wise, nor yet riches to men of understanding, nor yet favour to men of
> skill; but time and chance happeneth to them all.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Five pairs, each naming a quality and the outcome it does _not_ guarantee:
   swiftness/race, strength/battle, wisdom/bread, understanding/riches, skill/favour.
2. **Text states:** The list denies the _sufficiency_ of each quality, not its value. The swift do
   win races; they just do not always.
3. **Text states:** The final clause gives the reason: "but time and chance happeneth to them all."
   **Text states:** "Time and chance" — the two factors are named together and they apply
   universally: "to them all."
4. **Text states:** The word for "chance" is **H6294 פֶּגַע (_pega_)** — "impact (casual)." KJV:
   "chance, occurent." And the verb "happeneth" is **H7136 קָרָה (_qarah_)** — "to light upon
   (chiefly by accident); causatively, to bring about; specifically, to impose timbers (for roof or
   floor)." KJV: "appoint, lay (make) beams, befall, bring, come (to pass unto), floor, (hap) was,
   happen (unto), meet, send good speed." **Inference:** the vocabulary is of _encounter_ and
   _impact_, not of randomness in the abstract. Things meet and strike.
5. **Text states:** The observation is introduced as empirical: "I returned, and saw under the sun."
   **Inference:** this is presented as an observation of how things go, not as a principle.
6. **Text states (the surrounding context):** Ecclesiastes 9:10 immediately precedes: "Whatsoever thy
   hand findeth to do, do it with thy might." And 11:4-6 follows in the same book: "He that observeth
   the wind shall not sow." **Inference:** the book's response to the uncertainty is _not_ to stop
   acting. The observation and the command to act sit in the same book, sometimes in the same
   chapter.

**Inference — the axiom:** **effort and outcome are not the same variable.** Excellence improves the
odds; it does not determine the result. This is not a reason to be less excellent — the book commands
the opposite — but it is a reason not to attribute every outcome to the quality of the work.

**Business consequences:**

- **Do not read outcomes back into process quality.** Observation 3. A good process with a bad
  outcome is a bad result, not necessarily a bad process. Attribution error is the failure mode.
- **The five pairs are a list of things that do not guarantee results.** Observation 2. Speed,
  strength, wisdom, understanding, skill. All five are worth having; none is sufficient.
- **"To them all" — the randomness is not targeted.** Observation 3. **Inference:** a business
  cannot infer from its own success that it was favoured, or from its failure that it was penalised.
- **The response is more action, not less.** Observation 6. The same book that observes the
  randomness commands the might.
- **Reserve for the encounter.** Observation 4. The vocabulary is of things _meeting_ you. A reserve
  is a hedge against an encounter, not against a trend.

## Part 4. The seven-year cycle and the countercyclical store

**Text (Genesis 41:29-36, KJV)**

> 29. Behold, there come seven years of great plenty throughout all the land of Egypt:
> 30. And there shall arise after them seven years of famine; and all the plenty shall be forgotten in
>     the land of Egypt; and the famine shall consume the land;
> 31. And the plenty shall not be known in the land by reason of that famine following; for it shall
>     be very grievous.
> 32. And for that the dream was doubled unto Pharaoh twice; it is because the thing is established by
>     God, and God will shortly bring it to pass.
> 33. Now therefore let Pharaoh look out a man discreet and wise, and set him over the land of Egypt.
> 34. Let Pharaoh do this, and let him appoint officers over the land, and take up the fifth part of
>     the land of Egypt in the seven plenteous years.
> 35. And let them gather all the food of those good years that come, and lay up corn under the hand
>     of Pharaoh, and let them keep food in the cities.
> 36. And that food shall be for store to the land against the seven years of famine, which shall be in
>     the land of Egypt, that the land perish not through the famine.

**Note:** the capital mechanics of this passage are worked through in `capital-and-stewardship.md`,
Part 5. This section treats only the _timing_ content.

### The observations that matter for timing

1. **Text states (the cycle is named and bounded):** Seven years of plenty, then seven of famine.
   Both periods are _stated in advance_ and _finite_.
2. **Text states (the transition is abrupt):** "And the seven years of plenteousness… were ended. And
   the seven years of dearth began to come, according as Joseph had said" (41:53-54). **Text states:**
   There is no transition period described.
3. **Text states (the plenty is forgotten):** "all the plenty shall be forgotten in the land of Egypt"
   (v. 30), and "the plenty shall not be known in the land by reason of that famine following"
   (v. 31). **Text states:** The famine is "very grievous" and it _erases the memory_ of the plenty.
   **Inference:** this is a statement about perception, not only about supply. In the famine, nobody
   will remember that there was a plan.
4. **Text states (the doubling as evidence):** "And for that the dream was doubled unto Pharaoh
   twice; it is because the thing is established by God, and God will shortly bring it to pass"
   (v. 32). **Text states:** The repetition is given as the _evidence of certainty_, and it also
   implies urgency: "shortly."
5. **Text states (the plan is countercyclical):** Accumulate during the plenty, distribute during the
   famine. The action is the _opposite_ of the prevailing condition in both phases.
6. **Text states (the plan is timed to the cycle, not to the news):** The accumulation begins in the
   first good year, not after a warning. **Text states:** The plan is fully specified before the
   first year of plenty arrives.
7. **Text states (the outcome):** Egypt had bread when everyone else did not (41:54), and "all
   countries came into Egypt to Joseph for to buy corn" (41:57).

### The derivation

**Inference.** Five claims:

**1. Cycles are readable in advance.** Observation 1. The seven-and-seven structure was disclosed
before it started.

**2. The transition is a cliff, not a slope.** Observation 2. Which means the preparation must be
complete before the turn.

**3. Memory fails during the good phase.** Observation 3. **Inference:** this is the most practically
important observation. The reason companies fail to prepare in a boom is not that they lack the
information; it is that the boom is _self-justifying_. The reserve looks like idle capital (compare
Matthew 25:27), the plan looks pessimistic, and the person arguing for it looks like they are
failing to participate.

**4. The evidence standard is stated.** Observation 4. The dream was doubled, and the doubling is
given as the reason for confidence. **Inference:** the plan's scale should be proportional to the
confidence in the forecast.

**5. The countercyclical action is uncomfortable in both directions.** Observation 5. Accumulating
in a boom means foregoing investment; distributing in a bust means selling at the bottom. Neither is
the consensus action.

### Business consequences

- **Prepare during the good phase or not at all.** Observation 2. There is no window at the turn.
- **The boom is self-justifying, so the reserve argument must be made on principle.** Observation 3.
  A reserve defended on its merits in a boom will lose. It has to be a policy, decided in advance
  (A20).
- **Scale the plan to the confidence.** Observation 4. The doubling justified the 20% rate.
- **The countercyclical position is a competitive advantage.** Observation 7. Egypt became the
  counterparty for the region.
- **The transition will feel sudden even though it was forecast.** Observation 2. A forecast cycle
  does not produce a gradual experience of the turn.
- **Note what "shortly" means.** Observation 4. In the text, the forecast's certainty comes with
  urgency. The plan was executed immediately.

## Part 5. Reconnaissance and the two reports

**Text (Numbers 13:17-20, KJV)**

> 17. And Moses sent them to spy out the land of Canaan, and said unto them, Get you up this way
>     southward, and go up into the mountain:
> 18. And see the land, what it is; and the people that dwelleth therein, whether they be strong or
>     weak, few or many;
> 19. And what the land is that they dwell in, whether it be good or bad; and what cities they be that
>     they dwell in, whether in tents, or in strong holds;
> 20. And what the land is, whether it be fat or lean, whether there be wood therein, or not. And be
>     ye of good courage, and bring of the fruit of the land. Now the time was the time of the firstripe
>     grapes.

### The reconnaissance brief (Numbers 13:17-20)

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The brief is _specific_ and _structured_. Six questions, in pairs:
   - the land: what it is
   - the people: strong or weak
   - the people: few or many
   - the cities: tents or strong holds
   - the land: good or bad
   - the land: fat or lean; wood or not
2. **Text states:** Every question is _binary_: strong/weak, few/many, tents/strongholds,
   good/bad, fat/lean, wood/not. **Inference:** the brief is designed to produce a _comparable
   report_, not a narrative. It is a survey instrument.
3. **Text states:** The instruction is _observational_: "see the land, what it is." Not "assess the
   opportunity" or "evaluate the risk." See what it is.
4. **Text states:** There is a _deliverable_: "bring of the fruit of the land." **Inference:** the
   reconnaissance includes a physical sample.
5. **Text states:** The timing is noted: "Now the time was the time of the firstripe grapes."
   **Inference:** the reconnaissance is conducted at the moment of maximum available evidence — the
   first fruits are in.
6. **Text states:** The charge includes encouragement: "And be ye of good courage."
   **Inference:** the brief anticipates that the reconnaissance will be frightening.
7. **Text states:** The reconnaissance lasts forty days: "And they returned from searching of the
   land after forty days" (13:25).

### The two reports

**Text (Numbers 13:27-33, KJV)**

> 27. And they told him, and said, We came unto the land whither thou sentest us, and surely it
>     floweth with milk and honey; and this is the fruit of it.
> 28. Nevertheless the people be strong that dwell in the land, and the cities are walled, and very
>     great: and moreover we saw the children of Anak there.
> 29. The Amalekites dwell in the land of the south: and the Hittites, and the Jebusites, and the
>     Amorites, dwell in the mountains: and the Canaanites dwell by the sea, and by the coast of Jordan.
> 30. And Caleb stilled the people before Moses, and said, Let us go up at once, and possess it; for
>     we are well able to overcome it.
> 31. But the men that went up with him said, We be not able to go up against the people; for they are
>     stronger than we.
> 32. And they brought up an evil report of the land which they had searched unto the children of
>     Israel, saying, The land, through which we have gone to search it, is a land that eateth up the
>     inhabitants thereof; and all the people that we saw in it are men of a great stature.
> 33. And there we saw the giants, the sons of Anak, which come of the giants: and we were in our own
>     sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The majority report _begins with the truth_: "surely it floweth with milk and
   honey; and this is the fruit of it" (v. 27). The land is as promised.
2. **Text states:** The pivot is one word: "**Nevertheless**" (v. 28). **Text states:** The factual
   observations that follow (strong people, walled cities, Anakim, the list of peoples in v. 29) are
   consistent with the brief. They answer the questions asked.
3. **Text states:** The minority report is an _action recommendation_: "Let us go up at once, and
   possess it; for we are well able to overcome it" (v. 30). **Text states:** Caleb does not dispute
   the facts. He disputes the conclusion.
4. **Text states:** The majority's conclusion: "We be not able to go up against the people; for they
   are stronger than we" (v. 31). **Text states:** The claim is about _relative strength_, which is
   an assessment, not an observation.
5. **Text states:** The majority then goes beyond the brief: "they brought up an evil report of the
   land" (v. 32). **Text states:** The land is now described as "a land that eateth up the
   inhabitants thereof" — which contradicts verse 27 and is not in the brief's questions.
6. **Text states:** The escalation continues: "all the people that we saw in it are men of a great
   stature" and "we saw the giants" (vv. 32-33). **Inference:** the description escalates from "strong
   people" (v. 28) to "men of a great stature" to "giants."
7. **Text states (the self-assessment):** "and we were in our own sight as grasshoppers, and so we
   were in their sight" (v. 33). **Text states:** The second clause is a _claim about the enemy's
   perception_, which the spies could not have known.

### "We were in our own sight as grasshoppers"

**This clause is the passage's diagnostic, and it is worth isolating.**

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The self-assessment is stated first: "we were in our own sight as grasshoppers."
2. **Text states:** And then it is projected: "and so we were in their sight." **Text states:** The
   spies assert what the Canaanites thought of them.
3. **Text states:** There is no report of any interaction with the Canaanites. The forty days
   (13:25) were spent searching the land. **Inference:** the second clause is not observation. It is
   the projection of the first clause.
4. **Text states:** The mechanism is stated with "in our own sight." **Inference:** the distortion
   originates internally and is then externalised as a fact about the world.

**Inference — the axiom this yields:** **self-assessment distorts the reading of the environment.**
The spies did not misreport the facts they were sent to gather. They misreported _themselves_, and
then attributed the misreading to the other party.

**This is the most transferable idea in the passage for a business**, and it is a specific
mechanism rather than a general warning:

1. **The facts were gathered correctly.** Observation 2. The brief was followed.
2. **The distortion entered at the interpretation.** Observation 4.
3. **The distortion was about the self, not the market.** Observation 1. The spies' estimate of
   themselves is the variable that moved.
4. **The internal estimate was then stated as external.** Observation 3. Which makes it
   unfalsifiable from inside.

### The consequence

**Text (Numbers 14:1-4, 26-34, KJV — selected)**

> 1. And all the congregation lifted up their voice, and cried; and the people wept that night.
> 2. And all the children of Israel murmured against Moses and against Aaron: and the whole
>    congregation said unto them, Would God that we had died in the land of Egypt! or would God we had
>    died in this wilderness!
> 3. And wherefore hath the LORD brought us unto this land, to fall by the sword, that our wives and
>    our children should be a prey? were it not better for us to return into Egypt?
> 4. And they said one to another, Let us make a captain, and let us return into Egypt.
> 5. Say unto them, As truly as I live, saith the LORD, as ye have spoken in mine ears, so will I do
>    to you:
> 6. Your carcases shall fall in this wilderness; and all that were numbered of you, according to
>    your whole number, from twenty years old and upward, which have murmured against me,
> 7. Doubtless ye shall not come into the land, concerning which I sware to make you dwell therein,
>    save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun.
> 8. And your children shall wander in the wilderness forty years, and bear your whoredoms, until
>    your carcases be wasted in the wilderness.
> 9. After the number of the days in which ye searched the land, even forty days, each day for a
>    year, shall ye bear your iniquities, even forty years, and ye shall know my breach of promise.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the response):** Weeping, murmuring, and a proposal to reverse course: "Let us make
   a captain, and let us return into Egypt" (v. 4). **Text states:** The recommendation is to return
   to the condition they had been delivered from.
2. **Text states:** The fear is projected onto the family: "that our wives and our children should be
   a prey" (v. 3). **Inference:** the risk to others is used as the reason for the retreat.
3. **Text states (the sentence):** "As ye have spoken in mine ears, so will I do to you" (v. 28).
   **Text states:** The judgment is calibrated to the report: those who said they would die in the
   wilderness die in the wilderness.
4. **Text states:** The two who gave the minority report are excepted by name: Caleb and Joshua
   (v. 30).
5. **Text states (the duration):** "After the number of the days in which ye searched the land, even
   forty days, each day for a year" (v. 34). **Text states:** The sentence is calibrated to the length
   of the reconnaissance. Forty days of searching becomes forty years of wandering.
6. **Text states:** The ten who brought the evil report died by plague (14:36-37), and Joshua and
   Caleb "lived still" (14:38).
7. **Text states:** The minority report was _not_ adopted at the time. It was vindicated by the
   outcome, decades later.

**Business consequences:**

- **The reconnaissance brief should be binary and structured.** Observation 1 of the brief. A
  comparable instrument produces comparable reports.
- **Watch the "nevertheless."** Observation 2. The pivot word is where the interpretation begins,
  and it is usually where the distortion enters.
- **Separate the facts from the recommendation.** Observations 3 and 4. Caleb did not dispute the
  facts; he disputed the conclusion. A report format that requires both, separately, makes this
  visible.
- **Watch the escalation beyond the brief.** Observation 5. The report moved from "strong people" to
  "giants." **Inference:** when a report contains claims that were not asked for and that exceed the
  evidence, that is the signal.
- **Watch self-assessment masquerading as market assessment.** Observation 7. "We were in our own
  sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight." The second clause is unfalsifiable and it is
  presented as fact.
- **The retreat proposal returns to a worse position.** Observation 1. "Let us return into Egypt."
- **The cost of a bad reading is not the reading.** Observation 5. It is the decades.
- **Minority reports need protection.** Observation 7. Caleb and Joshua survived; the ten did not.
  The text records who said what.

## Part 6. Prior experience as preparation

**Text (1 Samuel 17:32-40, KJV)**

> 32. And David said to Saul, Let no man's heart fail because of him; thy servant will go and fight
>     with this Philistine.
> 33. And Saul said to David, Thou art not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him: for
>     thou art but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth.
> 34. And David said unto Saul, Thy servant kept his father's sheep, and there came a lion, and a
>     bear, and took a lamb out of the flock:
> 35. And I went out after him, and smote him, and delivered it out of his mouth: and when he arose
>     against me, I caught him by his beard, and smote him, and slew him.
> 36. Thy servant slew both the lion and the bear: and this uncircumcised Philistine shall be as one of
>     them, seeing he hath defied the armies of the living God.
> 37. David said moreover, The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of
>     the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine. And Saul said unto David, Go, and
>     the LORD be with thee.
> 38. And Saul armed David with his armour, and he put an helmet of brass upon his head; also he armed
>     him with a coat of mail.
> 39. And David girded his sword upon his armour, and he assayed to go; for he had not proved it. And
>     David said unto Saul, I cannot go with these; for I have not proved them. And David put them off him.
> 40. And he took his staff in his hand, and chose him five smooth stones out of the brook, and put them
>     in a shepherd's bag which he had, even in a scrip; and his sling was in his hand: and he drew near to
>     the Philistine.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the objection):** Saul's argument is about _track record_: "thou art but a youth,
   and he a man of war from his youth" (v. 33). **Text states:** The comparison is between David's
   record and Goliath's record.
2. **Text states (the counter-evidence):** David answers with prior experience: the lion and the
   bear. "Thy servant kept his father's sheep, and there came a lion, and a bear" (v. 34).
3. **Text states:** The prior experience is _directly relevant in kind_: an attack on the flock, a
   predator, a fight to the death, and a rescue.
4. **Text states:** David draws the _inference_ explicitly: "this uncircumcised Philistine shall be
   as one of them" (v. 36). **Inference:** the transfer is stated, not assumed. David names the
   analogy.
5. **Text states:** And then the _ground_: "The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion,
   and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me" (v. 37). **Text states:** The mechanism of the
   transfer is the same deliverer, not the same skill.
6. **Text states (the equipment test):** Saul arms David, and David _tests_ the equipment: "he assayed
   to go; for he had not proved it" (v. 39). And he rejects it: "I cannot go with these; for I have
   not proved them."
7. **Text states:** He uses what he has proved: his staff, five stones, and his sling (v. 40).
   **Text states:** "his sling was in his hand" — the instrument he had trained with.
8. **Text states:** The prior experience was _private and unobserved_. The lion and the bear were
   fought while keeping sheep, not in front of an army. **Inference:** the preparation was invisible.

**Inference — the axioms this yields:**

- **Prior experience is the qualification, and the relevance is argued, not assumed.** Observations
  2-4. David named the analogy between the predator and the champion.
- **The preparation happens unobserved.** Observation 8. The lion and the bear were not public.
- **Test new equipment before the engagement.** Observation 6. "I have not proved them."
- **Use the instrument you have proved.** Observation 7. Not the better one that is unfamiliar.
- **The ground of the transfer is stated.** Observation 5. David's confidence is not in his sling
  skill; it is in the deliverer. **Inference:** this is what makes the transfer from a small
  engagement to a large one legitimate rather than presumptuous.

**Business consequences:**

- **Small engagements are the qualification for large ones, and the relevance must be argued.**
  Observation 4. "This will be like that, because…"
- **Do not adopt the incumbent's equipment.** Observation 6. The armour of the previous generation
  is often unusable by the next.
- **Use what you have proved.** Observation 7. A tool you cannot use is worse than a tool you can.
- **The preparation is invisible.** Observation 8. Which means a track record of small, unglamorous
  work is the evidence, and it must be looked for.
- **Distinguish the skill from the ground.** Observation 5. **Inference:** a business that transfers
  confidence from a small win to a large bet needs to name _why_ the transfer is legitimate — the
  same market, the same mechanism, the same capability. Otherwise it is overconfidence with a story.

## Part 7. The anti-hurry axiom

**Text (Isaiah 28:16, KJV)**

> Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD, Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a
> precious corner stone, a sure foundation: he that believeth shall not make haste.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The subject is a _foundation_ — a stone, tried, precious, a corner stone, sure.
2. **Text states:** Four adjectives, in ascending specificity: stone, tried stone, precious corner
   stone, sure foundation. **Inference:** the description is cumulative and it is about _reliability
   under load_. "Tried" means tested.
3. **Text states:** The consequence for the one who believes is stated in the negative: "shall not
   make haste." **Text states:** The verb is not "shall be fast" or "shall be slow." It is _shall not
   hurry_.
4. **Text states:** The consequence is attached to _believing_ — trusting the foundation. **Inference:**
   the hurry is the symptom of not trusting the foundation. A person who trusts the thing they are
   standing on does not need to rush.
5. **Text states (the context, vv. 14-15):** The preceding verses describe a "covenant with death" and
   a refuge of lies, and the hail that will sweep away the refuge. **Inference:** the hurry is
   contrasted with a false security. The alternative to the sure foundation is not slowness; it is a
   different foundation, and the hurry comes from _that_ foundation's inadequacy.
6. **Text states (the context, v. 17):** "Judgment also will I lay to the line, and righteousness to
   the plummet." **Text states:** The same passage uses _measurement instruments_ — a line and a
   plummet — for judgment. **Inference:** the sure foundation and the measuring line belong together.

**Inference — the axiom:** **haste is a symptom of an inadequate foundation, not a property of the
work.** A person who is confident in what they are standing on does not need to hurry. A person who
is not will hurry, and the hurry will not fix the foundation.

**Business consequences:**

- **Hurry is diagnostic, not tactical.** Observation 4. When an organisation is hurrying, the
  question is not "how do we hurry better" but "what are we standing on that we do not trust?"
- **The foundation is "tried."** Observation 2. Which means it has been tested, not merely designed.
  **Inference:** the confidence that removes hurry comes from prior testing.
- **The hurry comes from a false refuge.** Observation 5. An organisation hurrying because it has
  built on a lie will hurry faster as the lie becomes visible.
- **The measuring instruments belong with the foundation.** Observation 6. A line and a plummet.
  **Inference:** a business that cannot measure where it is will not trust its position, and will
  hurry.
- **This is the counterweight to "move fast."** The text does not say speed is wrong. It says the
  person who trusts the foundation _does not make haste_ — which is a statement about _anxiety-driven_
  speed, not about velocity.

### The thoughts of the diligent (Proverbs 21:5)

**Text (Proverbs 21:5, KJV)**

> The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness; but of every one that is hasty only to
> want.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The two subjects are _thoughts_: "the thoughts of the diligent" against the
   thoughts of "every one that is hasty." **Text states:** The contrast is between two modes of
   thinking, not between two speeds of working.
2. **Text states:** The diligent person's thoughts _tend_: "tend only to plenteousness." **Text
   states:** The verb is directional — a tendency, not an arrival.
3. **Text states:** The hasty person's thoughts also tend: "only to want." **Text states:** Both are
   stated as tendencies with certainty ("only"), and both are stated as the natural outworking of the
   thinking, not as a punishment.
4. **Text states:** The English word for the hasty person is "hasty." **Lexical — and stated
   carefully:** three Hebrew entries in Strong's are rendered "hasty" by the KJV — **H213 אוּץ
   (_uts_)** "to press; (by implication) to be close, hurry, withdraw"; **H4116 מָהַר (_mahar_)**
   "properly, to be liquid or flow easily, i.e. (by implication); to hurry (in a good or a bad
   sense)"; and **H7116 קָצֵר (_qatser_)** "short." I **cannot verify which of these stands behind
   Proverbs 21:5**, because the supplied Strong's data has no verse-level tagging. Separately, the
   word behind "make haste" in Isaiah 28:16 is likely **H2363 חוּשׁ (_chush_)** — "to hurry;
   figuratively, to be eager with excitement or enjoyment" — but that mapping is also **unverified**.
   What _is_ verified is that the Hebrew vocabulary for hurry is a family of distinct words, several
   of which carry a sense of _pressure_ or _being narrowed_ (H213: "to press… be narrow"). **Inference:**
   the words are not interchangeable, and the file does not build an argument on the distinction.
5. **Text states:** The two outcomes are _plenteousness_ and _want_ — the same pair used elsewhere in
   Proverbs. **Inference:** the outcomes are the standard vocabulary for sufficiency and
   insufficiency, not for wealth and poverty as such.

**Inference.** Read with Isaiah 28:16, the proverb gives the mechanism: **haste is a mode of
thinking that produces insufficiency, and diligence is a mode of thinking that produces plenty.**
The difference is not effort. Both the diligent and the hasty are working. The difference is whether
the thinking is planned or reactive.

**Business consequences:**

- **Haste is a thinking style, not a speed.** Observation 1. The remedy is not to work slower; it is
  to think differently.
- **"Tend only to" is a tendency, not a guarantee.** Observation 2. The proverb states the direction
  of travel, which is consistent with Ecclesiastes 9:11's observation that the race is not always to
  the swift.
- **The mechanism is stated, not the penalty.** Observation 3. Want is what hasty thinking _produces_,
  not what it is _punished with_. **Inference:** this makes it a design problem — the insufficiency is
  in the reasoning, so it can be corrected by changing the reasoning.
- **The pair is sufficiency and insufficiency.** Observation 5. The stakes are not enrichment.
- **It pairs with the anti-hurry axiom.** Part 7's Isaiah 28:16 identifies the _cause_ of hurry (an
  untrusted foundation); this proverb identifies its _result_ (want).

   metals. **Text states:** And the interpretation is explicit: "These great beasts, which are four,
   are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth" (7:17).

**Inference — the axioms this yield:**

- **Dominance is temporary by construction.** Observation 1. The sequence is given as a series, and
  the first is told it is the first.
- **The later stages are weaker in quality, not stronger.** Observation 2. **Inference:** the
  assumption that the incumbent is at the peak of a progression is a misreading. The metals degrade.
- **The final stage fails from internal incoherence.** Observations 4-5. "They shall not cleave one
  to another."
- **The end is not gradual.** Observation 6. The four are destroyed together, by something external
  to the system.
- **The succession is governed, not emergent.** Observation 7. "He removeth kings, and setteth up
  kings."

**Business consequences:**

- **Assume your category will be superseded.** Observation 1. The question is not whether, but when
  and by what.
- **The incumbent's position is not the top of a ladder.** Observation 2. **Inference:** a company
  that assumes it is at the leading edge of a progression is reading the sequence backwards.
- **Watch for internal incoherence.** Observation 5. "They shall not cleave one to another." A
  conglomerate assembled from parts that do not bond is at the iron-and-clay stage.
- **The disruption may be external to the category.** Observation 6. The stone came from outside the
  image. **Inference:** the threat most likely to end a dominant position is the one that is not
  competing within the same frame.
- **The time horizon is long and the transition is short.** Observation 1 vs. Observation 6. Four
  kingdoms over centuries, destroyed in one event.
- **This is an argument for A32.** Observation 6. Irreversible commitments made on the assumption of
  permanence are commitments made against a stated pattern.

## Part 10. Testing a forecast

**Text (Deuteronomy 18:21-22, KJV)**

> 21. And if thou say in thine heart, How shall we know the word which the LORD hath not spoken?
> 22. When a prophet speaketh in the name of the LORD, if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is
>     the thing which the LORD hath not spoken, but the prophet hath spoken it presumptuously: thou shalt not
>     be afraid of him.

**Text (Amos 3:7, KJV)**

> Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets.

**Text (1 John 4:1, KJV)**

> Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false
> prophets are gone out into the world.

**Text (1 Thessalonians 5:21, KJV)**

> Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The question is asked explicitly: "How shall we know the word which the LORD hath
   not spoken?" (v. 21). **Text states:** The passage supplies the test in response to a direct
   request for one.
2. **Text states (the test):** "if the thing follow not, nor come to pass." **Text states:** The test
   is _outcome_. If the prediction fails, the prediction was not from God.
3. **Text states:** The verdict is stated: "the prophet hath spoken it presumptuously." **Text
   states:** And the instruction: "thou shalt not be afraid of him." **Inference:** the remedy for a
   false forecast is to remove its _authority_, not merely to note its failure. The fear is the thing
   to be dropped.
4. **Text states:** The test is _retrospective_. It can only be applied after the event. **Inference:**
   the passage does not provide a way to evaluate a forecast in advance. It provides a way to
   evaluate the forecaster afterwards.
5. **Text states (Amos):** "Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his
   servants the prophets" (3:7). **Text states:** The claim is that genuine future events are
   _disclosed_, not deduced. **Inference:** this is a claim about the source of the information, not
   about a method.
6. **Text states (1 John):** "believe not every spirit, but try the spirits" (4:1). **Text states:**
   The instruction is to _test_, and the reason given is that false claimants are numerous.
7. **Text states (1 Thessalonians):** "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good" (5:21).
   **Text states:** Two instructions: prove, and hold fast. **Inference:** the testing is not
   scepticism for its own sake; the second half requires retaining what passes.

### The test, assembled

**Inference.** Combining the four passages into a procedure for evaluating a forecast. This is a
synthesis, and it is labelled as such.

| Step | Source               | Question                                                                            |
| ---- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Deuteronomy 18:22    | Did this predictor's prior predictions come to pass?                                |
| 2    | 1 John 4:1           | What is the source of the claim, and is it identifiable?                            |
| 3    | 1 Thessalonians 5:21 | Test it, and then _keep_ what passes. Do not test and discard everything.           |
| 4    | Deuteronomy 18:22    | If it fails, remove the predictor's authority. Do not merely note the failure.      |
| 5    | Amos 3:7             | Is the claim disclosed or deduced? A deduced forecast is a different kind of claim. |

**Business consequences:**

- **The test is retrospective, so keep the record.** Observation 4. A business that does not record
  its forecasts cannot apply the test. **Inference:** the record of past predictions is the single
  most valuable instrument for evaluating present ones.
- **Remove the authority of a failed predictor.** Observation 3. "Thou shalt not be afraid of him."
  A forecaster who was wrong and retains their authority will be wrong again with the same audience.
- **Test, and then keep.** Observation 7. A culture that tests everything and retains nothing has the
  same problem as one that tests nothing.
- **Distinguish disclosed from deduced.** Observation 5. **Inference:** most business forecasting is
  deduction from models, which is a different category of claim from a disclosure, and should be
  labelled differently.
- **"Many false prophets are gone out" is the stated prior.** Observation 6. The base rate of reliable
  forecasts is stated to be low.
- **Expect a method, not an oracle.** **Inference:** the passage supplies a _verification_ procedure
  and no _generation_ procedure. The business equivalent is that you can build a process for
  evaluating forecasts and cannot build one for producing certainties.

## Part 11. Distribution determines yield

### The same seed, four grounds (Matthew 13:3-9)

**Text (Matthew 13:3-9, KJV)**

> 3. And he spake many things unto them in parables, saying, Behold, a sower went forth to sow;
> 4. And when he sowed, some seeds fell by the way side, and the fowls came and devoured them up:
> 5. Some fell upon stony places, where they had not much earth: and forthwith they sprung up, because
>    they had no deepness of earth:
> 6. And when the sun was up, they were scorched; and because they had no root, they withered away.
> 7. And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprung up, and choked them:
> 8. But other fell into good ground, and brought forth fruit, some an hundredfold, some sixtyfold, some
>    thirtyfold.
> 9. Who hath ears to hear, let him hear.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The seed is the same in all four cases. **Text states:** The variable is the
   _ground_.
2. **Text states (four outcomes, distinct mechanisms):**
   - wayside: devoured by birds — the seed is removed before it can take root
   - stony places: sprang up immediately, then scorched — no root, and the speed was deceptive
   - thorns: grew, then choked — competing growth
   - good ground: fruit, at three different rates
3. **Text states:** The stony ground produces _fast_ growth: "forthwith they sprung up" (v. 5).
   **Inference:** the fastest initial response is not the best ground. Speed of germination is
   uncorrelated with yield.
4. **Text states:** The thorns "sprung up, and choked them" (v. 7). **Inference:** the failure was
   not a lack of growth; it was competition from something that grew alongside.
5. **Text states:** Even in the good ground the yields differ: "some an hundredfold, some sixtyfold,
   some thirtyfold" (v. 8). **Text states:** The variation exists _within_ the good category.
6. **Text states:** The same sower sows all four. **Text states:** The text does not describe a
   selection process before sowing.

**Inference — the axioms this yield:**

- **Distribution determines yield, and the same input has different returns in different grounds.**
  Observations 1-2. The seed is constant; the outcome is 0 to 100×.
- **The fastest growth is the least reliable.** Observation 3. "Forthwith they sprung up" precedes
  the withering. **Inference:** early traction is a poor predictor of eventual yield, and a
  business that reads early growth as validation is reading the stony ground.
- **The thorns are the closest failure to success.** Observation 4. The plant grew; something else
  grew faster. **Inference:** this is A27 (friction is structural) in agricultural form — the
  competition is not an obstacle on the path, it is a competing growth.
- **The good ground is still variable.** Observation 5. **Inference:** even after the segment is
  right, there is a 3× spread.
- **The sower sows broadly.** Observation 6. There is no pre-qualification in the parable. This
  connects to Ecclesiastes 11:6 (sow morning and evening).

**Business consequences:**

- **Segment choice is the dominant variable, not effort.** Observation 1. The same effort produces 0
  or 100× depending on the ground.
- **Do not read early traction as validation.** Observation 3. The stony ground looked best in week
  one.
- **Identify the thorns.** Observation 4. Name the competing growth specifically: the incumbent's
  bundling, the customer's internal project, the substitute.
- **Expect a spread even in the good ground.** Observation 5. **Inference:** a plan that assumes
  uniform performance from a correctly-chosen segment is a plan that will miss.
- **Broad sowing is the pattern, and it is expensive.** Observation 6. Three of four grounds
  produced nothing.

### Two growth models (Matthew 13:31-33)

**Text (Matthew 13:31-33, KJV)**

> 31. Another parable put he forth unto them, saying, The kingdom of heaven is like to a grain of
>     mustard seed, which a man took, and sowed in his field:
> 32. Which indeed is the least of all seeds: but when it is grown, it is the greatest among herbs, and
>     becometh a tree, so that the birds of the air come and lodge in the branches thereof.
> 33. Another parable spake he unto them; The kingdom of heaven is like unto leaven, which a woman
>     took, and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (two models, in adjacent verses):**
   - **Mustard seed:** starts smallest, becomes greatest, and _becomes visible_ — birds lodge in it.
   - **Leaven:** _hidden_ in the meal, works invisibly, "till the whole was leavened."
2. **Text states:** The mustard seed's growth is described with a _comparison_: "the least of all
   seeds… the greatest among herbs."
3. **Text states:** The leaven is _hidden_: "which a woman took, and hid in three measures of meal."
   **Inference:** the mechanism is invisible throughout.
4. **Text states:** The leaven's process has a completion: "till the whole was leavened." **Text
   states:** The endpoint is total.
5. **Text states:** Both are offered as descriptions of the same thing — "the kingdom of heaven is
   like…" in both cases. **Inference:** the same subject admits both a visible-growth model and an
   invisible-diffusion model.
6. **Text states:** The mustard seed's growth is _unattended_ after sowing. The man sows and the
   text says nothing about tending. **Inference:** the growth is presented as intrinsic.

**Inference — the axioms this yield:**

- **Two growth models, both valid, and they are structurally different.** Observation 1. One is
  visible and centralised (a tree that birds come to). The other is invisible and distributed (leaven
  through meal).
- **The leaven model cannot be measured during the process.** Observation 3. The growth is hidden
  until the whole is leavened.
- **The mustard model has a visible endpoint and a gathering point.** Observation 1. The birds come
  to the tree. **Inference:** this is a platform or hub model — value accrues at a centre.
- **Both terminate.** Observation 4. "Till the whole was leavened"; "when it is grown."
- **The models are not alternatives.** Observation 5. **Inference:** a business typically runs one
  visibly and one invisibly, and the invisible one is the harder to manage because it produces no
  interim evidence.

**Business consequences:**

- **Know which model you are running, and how each is measured.** Observation 2. A visible-growth
  business is measured by the size of the tree; an invisible-diffusion business has no interim
  metric and must be measured by the _extent of the leavening_ at intervals.
- **The hidden process has no dashboard.** Observation 3. **Inference:** a business running a leaven
  model that tries to build a growth dashboard will build a mustard dashboard and manage the wrong
  thing.
- **The hub model accrues value at a centre.** Observation 1. "The birds of the air come and lodge
  in the branches."
- **Both have a defined endpoint.** Observation 4. This is A14 applied to growth: "the whole was
  leavened" is a completion condition.
- **Do not confuse slow with absent.** Observation 3. The leaven is working while nothing appears to
  be happening.

## Part 12. Acting under uncertainty

### The observer of the wind (Ecclesiastes 11:4-6)

**Text (Ecclesiastes 11:4-6, KJV)**

> 4. He that observeth the wind shall not sow; and he that regardeth the clouds shall not reap.
> 5. As thou knowest not what is the way of the spirit, nor how the bones do grow in the womb of her
>    that is with child: even so thou knowest not the works of God who maketh all.
> 6. In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine hand: for thou knowest not
>    whether shall prosper, either this or that, or whether they both shall be alike good.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the anti-paralysis rule):** "He that observeth the wind shall not sow; and he that
   regardeth the clouds shall not reap" (v. 4). **Text states:** The observer is not described as
   wrong. The observation is described as _preventing action_.
2. **Text states:** The consequence is total: he "shall not sow" — the action never happens.
3. **Text states (the ground):** "As thou knowest not what is the way of the spirit… even so thou
   knowest not the works of God who maketh all" (v. 5). **Text states:** The uncertainty is stated as
   a fact about the limits of knowledge, not as a temporary condition.
4. **Text states (the remedy):** "In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine
   hand" (v. 6). **Text states:** Two sowings, at two different times.
5. **Text states (the reason):** "for thou knowest not whether shall prosper, either this or that, or
   whether they both shall be alike good." **Text states:** Three possibilities are named: this
   prospers, that prospers, or both.
6. **Text states:** The reader is addressed directly with an imperative: "withhold not thine hand."

**Inference.** The passage gives a complete doctrine of action under uncertainty:

1. **Uncertainty is permanent.** Observation 3. Not a data problem.
2. **Waiting for certainty prevents the action entirely.** Observation 1.
3. **The remedy is parallel bets.** Observation 4. Morning and evening.
4. **The outcomes are not exclusive.** Observation 5. Both may succeed.
5. **The command is to act, and it is stated as a prohibition on inaction.** Observation 6.
   "Withhold not thine hand."

**Business consequences:**

- **Waiting for certainty is a decision.** Observation 1. It is a decision to have no harvest.
- **The remedy is parallel bets, not better forecasting.** Observation 3. Since the uncertainty is
  permanent, the response is portfolio structure, not more analysis.
- **Run two attempts, at two times.** Observation 4. **Inference:** the second attempt should be
  structured to succeed if the first fails, and both should be structured to be able to succeed
  together.
- **"Both may be alike good" is an available outcome.** Observation 5. A plan that assumes
  cannibalisation between two bets has assumed the outcome.
- **The command is stated in the negative, and it is about the hand.** Observation 6. "Withhold not
  thine hand." **Inference:** the text treats inaction as the thing requiring a command, not action.

### If the Lord will (James 4:13-15)

**Text (James 4:13-15, KJV)**

> 13. Go to now, ye that say, To day or to morrow we will go into such a city, and continue there a
>     year, and buy and sell, and get gain:
> 14. Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour, that
>     appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away.
> 15. For that ye ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The quoted plan is _specific and complete_: a city, a duration ("continue there a
   year"), the activities ("buy and sell"), and the objective ("get gain"). **Inference:** this is a
   normal business plan, and it is well-formed.
2. **Text states:** The objection is not to the plan's content. It is to the _certainty of its
   grammar_: "ye that say, To day or to morrow we will…"
3. **Text states:** The ground: "Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow" (v. 14).
4. **Text states:** The comparison: "what is your life? It is even a vapour, that appeareth for a
   little time, and then vanisheth away." **Inference:** the argument is A30 — mortality makes time
   the binding constraint, and therefore no plan extends beyond what you control.
5. **Text states:** The prescribed form: "For that ye ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall live,
   and do this, or that" (v. 15). **Text states:** The condition is on the _living_ and the _doing_,
   not only on the outcome.
6. **Text states:** The passage does not prohibit planning. It prescribes the _form_ of the plan's
   statement.

**Inference.** This is the cleanest statement in Scripture of how to plan under uncertainty: make
the plan specific, and state the condition on it. The failure is not the plan; it is the plan stated
as a certainty.

**Business consequences:**

- **The condition is a discipline, not a formula.** Observation 6. The remedy is a habit of mind, not
  a phrase to append.
- **Plan specifically and condition the statement.** Observation 1. The plan in the passage is
  detailed; it is the _assertion_ that is wrong.
- **The condition covers the doing, not only the outcome.** Observation 5. "We shall live, and do
  this, or that."
- **The ground is the brevity of life.** Observation 4. The condition is not a hedge; it is an
  accurate description of the situation.
- **This is the correct form for a forecast.** Observation 2. **Inference:** the difference between
  a forecast and a boast is the presence of the condition, not the specificity.

### Boast not thyself of to morrow (Proverbs 27:1)

**Text (Proverbs 27:1, KJV)**

> Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The prohibition is on _boasting_, not on planning: "Boast not thyself of to
   morrow."
2. **Text states:** The reason: "for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth." **Text states:**
   The unit of uncertainty is _a day_, which is the smallest unit available.
3. **Text states:** The verb is reflexive: "boast not **thyself**." **Inference:** the object of the
   boasting is the speaker's own confidence.
4. **Text states (the comparison):** Proverbs 27:1 sits in a chapter of short observations, and it
   is followed by verse 2: "Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth." **Inference:** the
   theme is self-assessment, not forecasting.

**Inference:** the prohibition is on the _posture_, not the activity. Planning tomorrow is required
elsewhere (Proverbs 6:6-8, the ant provides in summer). Boasting about tomorrow is the failure.

**Business consequences:**

- **The prohibition is on the boast, not the plan.** Observation 1. Compare the ant, which plans and
  does not boast.
- **The uncertainty is daily.** Observation 2. A long-horizon plan is uncertain in its first day.
- **The failure is reflexive.** Observation 3. The thing being asserted is the speaker's own
  reliability.
- **Praise should come from outside.** Observation 4. **Inference:** a company that publishes its own
  certainty is in the position the proverb describes.

### Mercies new every morning (Lamentations 3:22-23)

**Text (Lamentations 3:22-23, KJV)**

> 22. It is of the LORD's mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not.
> 23. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The context is _destruction_: Lamentations is a book of grief over a destroyed
   city. **Text states:** And the statement is that they are "not consumed."
2. **Text states:** The mechanism is stated: "because his compassions fail not."
3. **Text states:** The timing is stated: "They are new every morning." **Text states:** The
   supply is _daily_, not stored.
4. **Text states:** The conclusion is about _faithfulness_: "great is thy faithfulness." **Inference:**
   the attribute named is reliability over time, which is what the daily renewal demonstrates.
5. **Text states:** The word "new" implies replacement. **Inference:** the previous day's supply is
   not carried forward, which means the supply cannot be accumulated.

**Inference — the axiom:** **the daily reset.** The resource that sustains is renewed on a daily
cycle and cannot be stored. This has a direct consequence for how a person or an organisation
approaches a long crisis: the relevant question is not "how will I endure the whole thing" but "is
today's supply sufficient for today."

**Business consequences:**

- **The unit of endurance is the day, not the crisis.** Observation 3. A long downturn is faced in
  daily increments.
- **The supply cannot be stored.** Observation 5. **Inference:** the accumulation strategy that
  works for capital does not work for this resource. This is a limit on the general principle of
  reserves.
- **The statement is made from inside the destruction.** Observation 1. It is not a statement made
  from safety about how one might cope.
- **The attribute named is faithfulness, which is a track record.** Observation 4. **Inference:** the
  ground for confidence in tomorrow's supply is the record of prior supplies.
- **This is the counterweight to Part 4's reserves.** The Joseph plan stores against a seven-year
  famine. Lamentations says the mercy is not storable. **Inference:** the two are about different
  resources, and a business should know which kind it is managing in a given situation.

## Part 13. Entering a culture with something new

**Text (Acts 17:22-31, KJV)**

> 22. Then Paul stood in the midst of Mars' hill, and said, Ye men of Athens, I perceive that in all
>     things ye are too superstitious.
> 23. For as I passed by, and beheld your devotions, I found an altar with this inscription, TO THE
>     UNKNOWN GOD. Whom therefore ye ignorantly worship, him declare I unto you.
> 24. God that made the world and all things therein, seeing that he is Lord of heaven and earth,
>     dwelleth not in temples made with hands;
> 25. Neither is worshipped with men's hands, as though he needed any thing, seeing he giveth to all
>     life, and breath, and all things;
> 26. And hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth, and hath
>     determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation;
> 27. That they should seek the Lord, if haply they might feel after him, and find him, though he be not
>     far from every one of us:
> 28. For in him we live, and move, and have our being; as certain also of your own poets have said, For
>     we are also his offspring.
> 29. Forasmuch then as we are the offspring of God, we ought not to think that the Godhead is like unto
>     gold, or silver, or stone, graven by art and man's device.
> 30. And the times of this ignorance God winked at; but now commandeth all men every where to repent:
> 31. Because he hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man
>     whom he hath ordained; whereof he hath given assurance unto all men, in that he hath raised him from
>     the dead.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the market observation):** "as I passed by, and beheld your devotions, I found an
   altar with this inscription, TO THE UNKNOWN GOD" (v. 23). **Text states:** The observation is
   _specific_ — an altar, and its inscription. He read the market's own artifacts.
2. **Text states:** The observation is _affectionate rather than hostile_: "I perceive that in all
   things ye are too superstitious" (v. 22) is a characterisation, and the following sentence
   immediately gives the evidence and pivots to the argument.
3. **Text states (the bridge):** "Whom therefore ye ignorantly worship, him declare I unto you"
   (v. 23). **Text states:** He starts from _their_ existing object of worship — the unknown god —
   and offers the content. **Inference:** the entry point is something they already have, not
   something he is introducing.
4. **Text states (the quotation of their own sources):** "as certain also of your own poets have
   said, For we are also his offspring" (v. 28). **Text states:** He quotes their poets, not his own
   scriptures, in this setting. And the quotation is used as _evidence_, not decoration.
5. **Text states (the sequence of the argument):** creator (v. 24) → not contained in temples and not
   in need of service (vv. 24-25) → the unity of humanity and the ordering of times and places
   (v. 26) → the purpose, that they should seek him (v. 27) → the quotation from their poets (v. 28)
   → the conclusion about images (v. 29) → the call (v. 30) → the judgment (v. 31).
6. **Text states:** The argument _moves from what they already accept_ to what they do not. The
   created order, the ordering of nations and seasons, and the poets' line are all shared ground.
7. **Text states:** Verse 26 includes the timing claim: "hath determined the times before appointed,
   and the bounds of their habitation." **Text states:** The times and the boundaries of nations are
   stated as _determined_.
8. **Text states (the response):** Some mocked, some deferred, and some believed (vv. 32-34).
   **Text states:** The outcome is mixed, and it is not presented as a failure.
9. **Text states:** Verse 30 states the transition: "And the times of this ignorance God winked at;
   but now commandeth all men every where to repent." **Inference:** the passage has its own
   _kairos_ — a change in what the moment calls for.

**Inference — this is the model for entering a culture with something new.** The transferable
structure, stated as inference:

1. **Observe the market's own artifacts.** Observation 1. Not its demographics — its altars. Read
   what it has actually built and inscribed.
2. **Begin from something they already have.** Observation 3. The entry point is their existing
   practice, not your novelty.
3. **Quote their own sources as evidence.** Observation 4. Not as flattery — as a load-bearing part
   of the argument.
4. **Argue in a sequence from shared premises.** Observation 5. The structure moves from accepted
   ground to contested ground.
5. **Name the moment.** Observation 9. The argument includes a claim about _what time it is_.

**Business consequences:**

- **Read the artifacts, not the demographics.** Observation 1. An altar with an inscription is a
  specific finding. **Inference:** a market entry based on segment data is a different thing from
  one based on what the market has actually built.
- **Find the "unknown god" — the unmet need the market already acknowledges.** Observation 3. The
  Athenians had an altar for what they did not know. **Inference:** the strongest entry point is a
  need the market has already named and not satisfied.
- **Use their sources.** Observation 4. A pitch that cites only your own research is arguing from
  outside.
- **Sequence from shared premises.** Observation 5. **Inference:** the first three quarters of the
  argument should be uncontroversial.
- **Expect a mixed response.** Observation 8. Mockery, deferral, and acceptance. The passage does
  not present the mixed outcome as a failure of the pitch.
- **Have a view on the moment.** Observation 9. The argument includes a claim about what time it is
  — the same capability as 1 Chronicles 12:32.

## Part 14. The harvest is now

**Text (John 4:35, KJV)**

> Say not ye, There are yet four months, and then cometh harvest? behold, I say unto you, Lift up your
> eyes, and look on the fields; for they are white already to harvest.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The conventional wisdom is quoted: "There are yet four months, and then cometh
   harvest." **Text states:** This is an accurate agricultural observation — harvest is four months
   after sowing.
2. **Text states:** And it is _overridden_: "behold, I say unto you."
3. **Text states:** The instruction is physical: "Lift up your eyes, and look on the fields." **Text
   states:** The error being corrected is a failure to _look_.
4. **Text states:** The condition is stated with urgency: "they are white already to harvest."
   **Text states:** "Already."
5. **Text states (the context, vv. 34, 36):** Verse 34: "My meat is to do the will of him that sent
   me, and to finish his work." Verse 36: "And he that reapeth receiveth wages, and gathereth fruit
   unto life eternal: that both he that soweth and he that reapeth may rejoice together." **Text
   states:** The passage includes both the sower and the reaper, and their rejoicing together.
6. **Text states:** The occasion is the Samaritan woman's testimony and the townspeople coming out
   (vv. 28-30, 39-42). **Inference:** the "fields" being referred to are the people approaching.

**Inference — the axiom:** **the standard schedule can be wrong about the present moment.** The
four-month figure is correct in general and wrong here. The error is corrected by looking, not by
recalculating.

**Business consequences:**

- **The general rule can be true and the specific moment can be different.** Observation 1-2. A
  correct industry benchmark does not tell you what is happening in your market now.
- **The remedy is to look, not to model.** Observation 3. "Lift up your eyes, and look on the
  fields."
- **"Already" is the word that matters.** Observation 4. The opportunity is earlier than the
  schedule predicts.
- **The sow-and-reap pair is stated as a partnership.** Observation 5. Both rejoice together, which
  means the reaper's gain is not the sower's loss.
- **The error is costly in the specific way.** **Inference:** if the harvest is ready and you are
  waiting four months, the crop spoils. Being early is a cost; being late here is a total loss.

## Part 15. Intended evil, meant unto good

**Text (Genesis 50:20, KJV)**

> But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is
> this day, to save much people alive.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Two intentions are named, and they are both real: "ye thought evil against me"
   and "God meant it unto good." **Text states:** The verse does not deny the first.
2. **Text states:** The outcome is stated: "to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people
   alive."
3. **Text states:** The scale of the outcome is _large_: "much people."
4. **Text states (the timing):** The statement is made _decades after the event_. Joseph says it to
   his brothers after their father's death (v. 15-19), at the end of the famine and of his career.
   **Inference:** the reading of the event required the whole arc.
5. **Text states:** Joseph's own position: "am I in the place of God?" (v. 19). **Text states:** He
   refuses the judgment seat, and then states the interpretation.
6. **Text states (the earlier statement):** Genesis 45:5 — "Now therefore be not grieved, nor angry
   with yourselves, that ye sold me hither: for God did send me before you to preserve life."
   **Text states:** The same reading, given earlier, at the point of reconciliation.

**Inference.** Three claims:

- **The evil was real.** Observation 1. The verse does not say the brothers meant well.
- **The outcome is not visible in the event.** Observation 4. The reading took decades.
- **The interpretation is not a licence to do evil.** Observation 5. Joseph refuses the judgment
  seat. The verse reports a meaning, not a permission.

**Business consequences:**

- **Do not attempt to read the meaning of an event while inside it.** Observation 4. The
  interpretation required the arc.
- **The evil is not re-characterised.** Observation 1. A post-mortem that describes a betrayal as a
  learning experience has erased the betrayal.
- **The refusal of the judgment seat is part of the pattern.** Observation 5. Joseph declines to
  render the verdict and states the outcome instead.
- **Outcomes are not always proportional to intentions.** Observation 2. A destructive act produced
  a preservative result, and the act is still called evil.
- **This is a post-hoc reading, not a forecast.** **Inference:** the passage is not a promise that
  any particular bad event will produce good. It is a retrospective statement about one event, and
  it is offered by the person who lived it.

## Part 16. Delayed consequence in a market

**Text (Ecclesiastes 8:11, KJV)**

> Because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, therefore the heart of the sons of
> men is fully set in them to do evil.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The mechanism is stated in a causal chain: the sentence is _not executed
   speedily_ → therefore the heart is _fully set_ to do evil.
2. **Text states:** The effect is not "some people are tempted." It is "the heart of the sons of men
   is **fully set**." **Inference:** the delay produces not merely a willingness but a settled
   disposition.
3. **Text states:** The word "therefore" makes the delay the cause. **Inference:** the fault is not
   attributed to human nature alone; the _delay_ is named as the operative factor.
4. **Text states:** The observation is empirical — it is stated as a fact about how people behave
   under a delayed consequence, not as a prescription.
5. **Text states (the context):** Ecclesiastes 8:10 notes that the wicked are buried and forgotten
   and that this too is vanity; verse 12 then states the counter-claim: "Though a sinner do evil an
   hundred times, and his days be prolonged, yet surely I know that it shall be well with them that
   fear God." **Text states:** The delay is real and the eventual outcome is stated, but they are in
   separate verses.

**Inference — the axiom:** **a market with delayed or absent consequences for a behaviour will
produce more of that behaviour, and the increase is a property of the delay, not of the participants'
characters.** This is a structural claim about incentives.

**Business consequences:**

- **A rule without enforcement is a rule that produces the behaviour it forbids.** Observation 1.
  The delay is the cause.
- **The effect is a change in disposition, not a series of individual lapses.** Observation 2.
  "Fully set." **Inference:** by the time the behaviour is widespread, it is no longer a compliance
  problem; it is a norm.
- **The relevant variable is speed of consequence.** Observation 3. Which is a design parameter: how
  long between the act and the cost?
- **Apply this to your own organisation first.** **Inference:** the passage describes a general
  mechanism, and the nearest instance is internal — expense fraud, metric manipulation, quality
  shortcuts, harassment. The question is the lag between the act and the consequence.
- **Also apply it to the market.** A market where a harmful practice is profitable for years will
  fill with that practice, and the entrants are not more wicked than the incumbents; they are
  responding to the same lag.
- **Do not read it as fatalism.** Observation 5. The delay is stated and so is the eventual
  outcome, in the next verse, and the two are held separately.

## Part 17. Operating well in a place that is not home

**Text (Jeremiah 29:5-7, KJV)**

> 5. Build ye houses, and dwell in them; and plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them;
> 6. Take ye wives, and beget sons and daughters; and take wives for your sons, and give your daughters
>    to husbands, that they may bear sons and daughters; that ye may be increased there, and not
>    diminished.
> 7. And seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto
>    the LORD for it: for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the situation):** The addressees are _captives_: "whither I have caused you to be
   carried away captives" (v. 7). **Text states:** And the exile is attributed to God's action: "I
   have caused you to be carried away."
2. **Text states (the instruction):** Six imperatives in two verses: build, dwell, plant, eat, take
   wives, beget, take wives for sons, give daughters to husbands. **Text states:** All are _ordinary,
   long-horizon, domestic activities_.
3. **Text states:** The purpose is stated: "that ye may be increased there, and not diminished"
   (v. 6). **Text states:** The growth is to happen _there_ — in the place of captivity.
4. **Text states:** The final instruction: "seek the peace of the city… and pray unto the LORD for it"
   (v. 7). **Text states:** The word is **H7965 שָׁלוֹם (_shalom_)** — "safe, i.e. (figuratively)
   well, happy, friendly; also (abstractly) welfare, i.e. health, prosperity, peace." KJV: "peace(-able,
   -ably), prosper(-ity, -ous), rest, safe(-ty), welfare, (all is, be) well, wholly."
   **Inference:** _shalom_ is not the absence of conflict; it is the _welfare_ of the city.
5. **Text states:** The reason is stated as self-interest: "for in the peace thereof shall ye have
   peace." **Text states:** The welfare of the host city and the welfare of the exiles are stated as
   linked.
6. **Text states (the context, vv. 8-9):** The passage immediately warns against the false prophets
   and diviners who were predicting a short exile. **Inference:** the instruction to build and plant
   is a direct correction of a _timing forecast_. The people expected a quick return, and the
   instruction assumes a long stay.

**Inference — the axiom:** **operate well in the place you are, on a long horizon, even if it is not
where you intend to end up.** And the reason given is that the welfare of the host and the welfare of
the resident are linked.

**Business consequences:**

- **The instruction corrects a forecast.** Observation 6. The people were told to build because they
  had been told they would leave soon. **Inference:** a business operating in a market it regards as
  temporary will under-invest, and the under-investment is a bet on a timing forecast.
- **The activities are ordinary and long-horizon.** Observation 2. Houses and gardens, not
  monuments.
- **The growth is to happen there.** Observation 3. "That ye may be increased there."
- **Seek the welfare, not just the absence of conflict.** Observation 4. _Shalom_ is the city's
  prosperity, not merely the lack of trouble.
- **The interest is stated as linked, not as altruism.** Observation 5. "In the peace thereof shall ye
  have peace." **Inference:** this is an argument a business can make on its own terms — the local
  operating environment is a shared asset.
- **Note who is speaking and to whom.** Observation 1. The instruction is given to captives by the
  one who sent them into captivity. **Inference:** the passage is not a general endorsement of any
  political arrangement; it is a specific instruction about how to live in a place one did not choose.

## Part 18. Working rules

Inferences, stated as working rules.

**On the two capacities**

1. **Separate the reading from the decision, and connect them explicitly** (1 Chronicles 12:32).
2. **Build the reading function as a corps, not an advisor** (1 Chronicles 12:32). Distribute it
   across the people closest to the market.
3. **Read by observation, not inference** (1 Chronicles 12:32, _yada_ — "ascertain by seeing").
4. **The output is an action, not a report** (1 Chronicles 12:32).
5. **Read the times in the plural.** Different parts of the business face different conditions.
6. **Check where you already read well and are not applying it** (Luke 12:54-56). The failure is
   selective application, not incapacity.
7. **Name the window, not the date** (Luke 12:56, _kairos_).

**On cycles**

8. **Ask which side of the pair the present moment is** (Ecclesiastes 3:1-8). Building or breaking
   down; keeping or casting away.
9. **The counter-action is not the failure mode; it is a different season** (Ecclesiastes 3:1-8).
10. **Budget for the cycle, not the current phase** (Genesis 8:22).
11. **Anchor the uncertain parts of the plan on the reliable cycles** (Jeremiah 33:20-21).
12. **Do not read outcomes back into process quality** (Ecclesiastes 9:11). Time and chance happen to
    all.
13. **Prepare during the good phase or not at all** (Genesis 41:53-54). The transition is a cliff.
14. **Make the reserve a policy, not an argument** (Genesis 41:30-31). The boom is self-justifying and
    will erase the memory of the plan.
15. **Scale the plan to the confidence in the forecast** (Genesis 41:32).

**On reconnaissance**

16. **Write a binary, structured reconnaissance brief** (Numbers 13:17-20). Comparable instruments
    produce comparable reports.
17. **Watch the "nevertheless"** (Numbers 13:28). That is where interpretation enters.
18. **Separate the facts from the recommendation in the report** (Numbers 13:30-31).
19. **Watch for claims that exceed the brief** (Numbers 13:32-33).
20. **Watch for self-assessment masquerading as market assessment** (Numbers 13:33). "We were in our
    own sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight."
21. **Protect the minority report, and record who said what** (Numbers 14:30, 36-38).

**On preparation and nerve**

22. **Argue the relevance of prior experience; do not assume it** (1 Samuel 17:36).
23. **Do not adopt the incumbent's equipment** (1 Samuel 17:39).
24. **Use the instrument you have proved** (1 Samuel 17:40).
25. **Look for the unobserved track record** (1 Samuel 17:34-35). The preparation is private.
26. **Hurry is diagnostic** (Isaiah 28:16). Ask what foundation is not trusted.
27. **The evidence that a small project is on track is that it is being measured** (Zechariah 4:10).

**On the shelf life of positions**

28. **Assume the category will be superseded** (Daniel 2:39).
29. **The incumbent is not at the top of a progression** (Daniel 2:32-33). The metals degrade.
30. **Watch for internal incoherence** (Daniel 2:43). Parts that do not bond.
31. **The disruption is likely to come from outside the category** (Daniel 2:45).
32. **Long transitions, short endings** (Daniel 2:44).

**On forecasting**

33. **Keep the record of past forecasts** (Deuteronomy 18:22). The test is retrospective.
34. **Remove the authority of a failed predictor** (Deuteronomy 18:22). "Thou shalt not be afraid of
    him."
35. **Test, and then keep what passes** (1 Thessalonians 5:21). Not scepticism for its own sake.
36. **Distinguish disclosed from deduced** (Amos 3:7). Label model output as model output.

**On distribution and yield**

37. **Segment choice dominates effort** (Matthew 13:3-9). The same seed yields 0 to 100×.
38. **Do not read early traction as validation** (Matthew 13:5-6). The stony ground was fastest.
39. **Name the thorns specifically** (Matthew 13:7). The competing growth, not a general obstacle.
40. **Expect a spread within the good ground** (Matthew 13:8).
41. **Know whether you are running a mustard model or a leaven model** (Matthew 13:31-33), and do not
    build the wrong dashboard.
42. **Do not despise the day of small things, and check for the measuring instrument** (Zechariah
    4:10).

**On acting under uncertainty**

43. **Waiting for certainty is a decision** (Ecclesiastes 11:4). It is a decision to have no harvest.
44. **Sow morning and evening** (Ecclesiastes 11:6). Parallel bets, not better forecasting.
45. **Both bets may be alike good** (Ecclesiastes 11:6).
46. **Plan specifically; condition the statement** (James 4:13-15). The failure is the certainty of the
    grammar, not the plan.
47. **Boast not; the uncertainty is daily** (Proverbs 27:1).
48. **The unit of endurance is the day** (Lamentations 3:22-23). This resource is not storable.
49. **The general schedule can be wrong about this moment** (John 4:35). Look, do not recalculate.

**On consequence and place**

50. **A rule without enforcement produces the behaviour it forbids, and the effect is a norm**
    (Ecclesiastes 8:11). The design parameter is the lag.
51. **Operate well where you are, on a long horizon** (Jeremiah 29:5-7). The instruction to build was
    a correction of a timing forecast.
52. **Seek the welfare of the place, not just the absence of conflict** (Jeremiah 29:7, _shalom_).
53. **Do not attempt to read the meaning of an event from inside it** (Genesis 50:20).
54. **Do not re-characterise the evil as a learning experience** (Genesis 50:20).

**On entering a culture**

55. **Read the artifacts, not the demographics** (Acts 17:23). Find the altar and its inscription.
56. **Find the need the market has already named and not satisfied** (Acts 17:23). The unknown god.
57. **Quote their sources as evidence** (Acts 17:28).
58. **Sequence from shared premises** (Acts 17:24-29).
59. **Have a view on what time it is** (Acts 17:30).
60. **Expect a mixed response** (Acts 17:32-34).
