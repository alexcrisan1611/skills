# Ethics

The three-part test and how to make an ethics call under pressure.

## Method and labels

**Text states** / **Lexical** / **Inference** / **Speculation**. Verses from `scripture.ts`,
Strong's definitions verbatim from `lexicon.ts`.

Governing axioms: **A20** (abundance precedes restriction; the boundary is few and stated in
advance), **A22** (trust in the stated word is the first target), **A25** (undisclosed failure
produces hiding and blame), **A26** (diagnose before you judge), **A32** (some states must not be
allowed to become permanent).

**One warning, and it is the most important line in this file.** Scripture's ethical material is
addressed to persons in covenant with God, not to firms. A corporation is not a moral agent in the
way a person is; it is a structure through which people act. Every application below therefore
lands on a _person_ — a founder, a manager, a board member, an employee with a decision in front
of them. Where the text speaks to a nation or a king, the application to a company is an analogy
and is labelled as one.

**A second warning.** Per `scripture-foundations`: "Never use Scripture to justify harm,
exploitation, or the overriding of someone's conscience." A reading of any passage below that
produces cruelty has misread something. The keystone governs: love is the first principle of
motive (1 Corinthians 13:1-3).

## Part 1. The test: Micah 6:8

**Text (Micah 6:8, KJV)**

> He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do
> justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?

### The three questions

**Text states:** Three requirements, in three different grammatical forms:

| #   | KJV                         | Form                                 | The question it asks                   |
| --- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| 1   | to do justly                | infinitive of _action_ — **do**      | **What is owed?**                      |
| 2   | to love mercy               | infinitive of _affection_ — **love** | **What is given beyond what is owed?** |
| 3   | to walk humbly with thy God | infinitive of _manner_ — **walk**    | **What do you claim for yourself?**    |

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The three are joined by "and," so all three are required. It is not a menu.
2. **Text states:** They are of three different kinds. _Doing_ is an act; _loving_ is a disposition;
   _walking_ is a continuous manner of life. **Inference:** the three cannot be reduced to one
   another, which is why they function as independent tests.
3. **Text states:** The frame is a question and an answer: "what doth the LORD require of thee,
   but…" **Inference:** the answer is presented as _complete_ — "but" implies this is the whole of
   it, not a summary of a longer list.
4. **Text states:** The addressee is "O man" — a person, not an institution.
5. **Text states (the context):** Micah 6:6-7 presents a series of escalating religious offers —
   burnt offerings, calves, thousands of rams, ten thousands of rivers of oil, a firstborn child —
   and verse 8 is the answer to all of them. **Inference:** the three requirements are presented as
   a _replacement_ for elaborate religious performance, not a supplement to it.

### Why three independent questions

**Inference.** The three questions are independent in a specific way: an action can pass one and
fail another.

- **Justice without mercy** is the exact performance of the contract and nothing more. It is
  correct and cold. It is not condemned by question 1; it fails question 2.
- **Mercy without justice** is generosity that does not pay what is owed. It feels kind and leaves
  the obligation unmet. It fails question 1 while passing question 2.
- **Either without humility** is the assumption that you are the one who decides. A person who has
  done justly and loved mercy but claims the standing to judge their own case has failed question 3.

**This is why the test has three questions and not one.** Most ethical frameworks have a single
axis — consequences, or rules, or character. The Micah test asks three different questions, and the
answers can disagree. When they disagree, the disagreement is the finding.

**A worked example of disagreement.** A supplier is late on a delivery. The contract says you may
impose a penalty.

- **Question 1 (what is owed):** the penalty is owed. The contract says so.
- **Question 2 (what is given beyond):** the supplier's lateness was caused by a flood, and the
  penalty would end the business. Mercy would waive it.
- **Question 3 (what do you claim):** if you waive it, are you doing so because it is right, or
  because you want the supplier to be indebted to you and dependent on your favour? The second
  motive is a claim on them.

**Inference:** the third question is the one that catches the other two. Justice can be a cloak for
cruelty; mercy can be a cloak for control. The humility question asks who you think you are in the
transaction.

### The lexemes

**Lexical (verbatim).**

- **H4941 מִשְׁפָּט (_mishpat_)** — "properly, a verdict (favorable or unfavorable) pronounced
  judicially, especially a sentence or formal decree (human or (participant's) divine law,
  individual or collective), including the act, the place, the suit, the crime, and the penalty;
  abstractly, justice, including a participant's right or privilege (statutory or customary), or
  even a style." KJV renderings include "judgment, just(-ice, -ly), (manner of) law(-ful), manner,
  measure, (due) order, ordinance, right, sentence."
  - **Inference:** "do justly" is not a call to a feeling. _Mishpat_ is a _verdict_ — a
    determination of what is due. The word carries "right" and "due order" and even "measure."
    Question 1 is asking for a determination, not a sentiment.
- **H2617 חֵסֵד (_chesed_)** — "kindness; by implication (towards God) piety; rarely (by
  opposition) reproof, or (subject.) beauty." KJV: "favour, good deed(-liness, -ness), kindly,
  (loving-)kindness, merciful (kindness), mercy, pity, reproach, wicked thing."
  - **Inference:** _chesed_ is the word behind "mercy" here, and it is the same word used of God's
    covenant loyalty. It is not pity from a distance; it is _loyal kindness within a relationship_.
    Question 2 is not "feel sorry for them." It is "what does loyalty give beyond the contract?"
  - **Inference:** the KJV "mercy" and the Hebrew _chesed_ are not identical. _Chesed_ carries
    loyalty and covenant obligation, which is stronger than the English.
- **H6800 צָנַע (_tsana_)** — "to humiliate." KJV: "humbly, lowly."
  - **Text states:** This is the word rendered "humbly" in Micah 6:8. **Inference:** the root sense
    is _to humble oneself_, i.e. to lower. Not "modest" as a temperament — an active self-lowering.
    Question 3 is asking what you do with your own standing.
  - **Caveat:** the identification of this verse's "humbly" with H6800 is **not verified** (see the
    verification log). The definition is verified; the verse-level mapping is not.

### What the test does not cover

**Inference.** Three limits, stated so the test is not over-applied:

1. **It does not tell you what is just.** It tells you to _determine_ what is just. The
   determination requires the other material in this file — the wage rules, the measurement rules,
   the gleaning rules.
2. **It does not resolve conflicts between the three.** It gives you three questions and expects
   you to hold the disagreement. Where they conflict, the text does not supply a tie-breaker.
3. **It is a summary, not a code.** The prophets use it as a compression of the law, not as a
   replacement for it. Micah 6:8 answers the question "what does the LORD require" in three lines;
   the rest of this file is what those three lines mean in commerce.

## Part 2. The commerce chapter: Leviticus 19:9-18

**Text (Leviticus 19:9-18, KJV)**

> 9. And when ye reap the harvest of your land, thou shalt not wholly reap the corners of thy
>    field, neither shalt thou gather the gleanings of thy harvest.
> 10. And thou shalt not glean thy vineyard, neither shalt thou gather every grape of thy vineyard;
>     thou shalt leave them for the poor and stranger: I am the LORD your God.
> 11. Ye shall not steal, neither deal falsely, neither lie one to another.
> 12. And ye shall not swear by my name falsely, neither shalt thou profane the name of thy God: I
>     am the LORD.
> 13. Thou shalt not defraud thy neighbour, neither rob him: the wages of him that is hired shall
>     not abide with thee all night until the morning.
> 14. Thou shalt not curse the deaf, nor put a stumblingblock before the blind, but shalt fear thy
>     God: I am the LORD.
> 15. Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor
>     honor the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour.
> 16. Thou shalt not go up and down as a talebearer among thy people: neither shalt thou stand
>     against the blood of thy neighbour: I am the LORD.
> 17. Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour,
>     and not suffer sin upon him.
> 18. Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt
>     love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the LORD.

**This chapter is the densest ethical material in the Pentateuch, and it is largely commercial.**
Ten of the eighteen verses touch property, wages, judgment, or dealing. It is the single most useful
chapter in Scripture for a business ethics code, and it is organised as a list, not an argument.

### The gleaning rule (vv. 9-10)

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Three prohibitions on _complete_ harvesting: do not reap the corners, do not
   gather the gleanings, do not glean the vineyard, do not gather every grape.
2. **Text states:** The purpose is stated: "thou shalt leave them for the poor and stranger."
3. **Text states:** The mechanism is _leaving_, not _giving_. The owner does not harvest everything
   and then distribute. The owner _stops short_ and the poor come and take.
4. **Text states:** The beneficiaries are named: "the poor and stranger." **Inference:** the
   resident foreigner is included, who has no land and no family claim.
5. **Text states:** The rule is not conditioned on the harvest's size. There is no clause saying
   "in a good year." **Inference:** the obligation is a fixed percentage of the field — the corners
   — regardless of the yield.
6. **Text states:** The ground given is not a reason but an identity: "I am the LORD your God."

**Inference.** The gleaning rule establishes a specific design: **the provision for the poor is
built into the productive process, not added to it afterwards.** It is a yield reduction, not a
charitable deduction. And it preserves the recipient's _agency_: the poor person gleans, which is
work, rather than receiving a distribution, which is dependence.

**Business consequences:**

- **Build the provision into the process, not into the philanthropy.** Observation 3. A margin set
  aside at the point of production is different from a donation made from the proceeds.
- **Leave the recipient with agency.** Observation 3. The poor come and gather; they are not
  handed a result. **Inference:** a programme that removes the recipient's work removes something
  they need.
- **Set it as a fixed rule, not a discretionary amount.** Observation 5. The corners are defined in
  advance (A20).
- **Include the stranger.** Observation 4. The person with no claim on the community is named in
  the rule.

### The defrauding and wage rules (v. 13)

**Text states:** "Thou shalt not defraud thy neighbour, neither rob him: the wages of him that is
hired shall not abide with thee all night until the morning."

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Three prohibitions in one verse: do not defraud, do not rob, do not hold wages
   overnight.
2. **Text states:** The third is a _specific instance_ of the first two. **Inference:** the verse
   structure states the general rule and then a concrete case, which suggests the case is the one
   the author most expects to be violated.
3. **Text states:** The deadline is stated: "all night until the morning." The wages are due the
   same day.
4. **Text states:** "Defraud" is the general term. See `capital-and-stewardship.md`, Part 6, for the
   James 5:4 development of the same theme.

**Inference.** Withholding wages is placed in the same verse as fraud and robbery. That is a
classification, not a caution. A payment term is an ethics decision.

**Business consequences:**

- **Payment terms are an ethics issue, not a treasury issue.** Observation 4. The classification is
  explicit.
- **The named case is the one most likely to be violated.** Observation 2. The text anticipates
  that employers will hold wages.
- **"We cannot pay" and "we are holding it" are different.** The prohibition is on retention.

### The deaf and the blind (v. 14)

**Text states:** "Thou shalt not curse the deaf, nor put a stumblingblock before the blind, but
shalt fear thy God: I am the LORD."

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Two prohibitions, and both target a person who _cannot detect the harm_.
2. **Text states:** The deaf person cannot hear the curse. The blind person cannot see the
   stumbling block. **Inference:** the harm is real but undetectable by the victim.
3. **Text states:** The enforcement clause is _not_ a penalty. It is "but shalt fear thy God."
   **Inference:** no human court can enforce this, because the victim cannot report what happened.
   The rule is enforced only by the fear of God.
4. **Text states:** The pattern connects to verse 13 (withheld wages — the worker may not be able to
   compel payment) and to verse 15 (the poor and the mighty in judgment).
5. **Text states (the modern application, stated as inference):** the categories are _anyone who
   cannot detect or resist the harm_. This includes the person who cannot read the contract, the
   user who cannot see the tracking, the employee who cannot check the calculation, the customer
   who cannot evaluate the claim.

**Inference — this is the single most transferable verse in the chapter for a modern business.**
It states that the standard of conduct toward a person does not depend on their ability to detect
the violation.

**Business consequences:**

- **The test is what you would do if the person could see it.** Observation 2. If the practice
  depends on the victim's inability to detect it, verse 14 is directly in view.
- **Unenforceable obligations are still obligations.** Observation 3. The absence of a complainant
  does not mean the absence of a wrong.
- **Dark patterns are verse 14 violations.** Confusing cancellation flows, hidden fees, opt-out
  defaults, obscured terms. The user cannot detect the harm at the point of decision.
- **The list of "blind" parties in a business is long.** Shareholders who cannot see the accounts,
  regulators who cannot see the process, junior staff who cannot see the incentive structure, the
  next buyer of an asset who cannot see its condition.

### Righteous judgment (v. 15)

**Text states:** "Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of
the poor, nor honor the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour."

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Two symmetric prohibitions: do not favour the poor, do not favour the mighty.
   **Inference:** the prohibition is on _partiality in either direction_, which rules out both
   class-based sympathy and class-based deference.
2. **Text states:** The positive requirement is "in righteousness shalt thou judge thy
   neighbour." The standard is righteousness, not balance.
3. **Text states:** The verse is a single prohibition against "unrighteousness in judgment,"
   illustrated by two cases. **Inference:** the two cases are examples, not the whole rule.
4. **Text states:** Verse 35 extends the same principle to measurement: "Ye shall do no
   unrighteousness in judgment, in meteyard, in weight, or in measure." **Text states:** The same
   word — unrighteousness — is applied to commercial measurement. **Inference:** the text treats
   a false scale as a form of unjust judgment.

**Business consequences:**

- **Favouritism runs both ways.** Observation 1. A discount for a customer because they are
  sympathetic is the same defect as a discount for a customer because they are powerful.
- **The rule is one rule, not two.** Observation 3. "Do no unrighteousness in judgment" is the
  standard; the two cases are illustrations.
- **Measurement is judgment.** Observation 4. See Part 3.

### The talebearer and the blood (v. 16)

**Text states:** "Thou shalt not go up and down as a talebearer among thy people: neither shalt
thou stand against the blood of thy neighbour: I am the LORD."

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Two prohibitions: the talebearer (gossip, rumour-mongering) and standing
   against the neighbour's blood.
2. **Text states:** The talebearer is described with motion: "go up and down." **Inference:** the
   activity is _circulation_ — the spread of information, not the invention of it.
3. **Text states:** The second prohibition — "stand against the blood" — is about failing to act
   when a neighbour's life is at stake. It is the duty-to-act rule (see Part 9).
4. **Text states:** The two are placed in the same verse. **Inference:** the connection is that
   both involve _the neighbour's exposure_. Gossip exposes; standing by exposes.
5. **Text states (the modern analogue, as inference):** circulating an unverified claim about a
   colleague or a competitor is the first prohibition; watching a harm develop and doing nothing is
   the second.

**Business consequences:**

- **The prohibition is on _circulation_, not only on invention.** Observation 2. Passing on a
  rumour you did not create is the named activity.
- **Doing nothing while a harm develops is a separate violation.** Observation 3. See Part 9.
- **The two are in one verse for a reason.** Observation 4. Both are about exposure.

### Hatred, rebuke, vengeance (vv. 17-18)

**Text states:** "Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy
neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him. Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the
children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the LORD."

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The internal state is legislated: "thou shalt not hate thy brother **in thine
   heart**." **Inference:** the prohibition reaches motive, not only action.
2. **Text states:** The remedy for the hidden hatred is _speech_: "thou shalt in any wise rebuke
   thy neighbour." **Inference:** the rebuke is the alternative to the hatred. Silent resentment is
   what the verse forbids; direct address is what it requires.
3. **Text states:** The phrase "in any wise" intensifies the obligation — rebuke, certainly.
4. **Text states:** The purpose is "and not suffer sin upon him" — the rebuke exists to prevent the
   neighbour from continuing in the wrong.
5. **Text states:** Two further prohibitions: "shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge." **Inference:**
   the grudge is prohibited alongside the vengeance, which means the _retained_ resentment is itself
   a violation, whether or not it is acted on.
6. **Text states:** The chapter closes with the summary: "but thou shalt love thy neighbour as
   thyself." **Text states:** Jesus cites this verse, with Deuteronomy 6:5, as the summary of the
   whole law: Matthew 22:37-40 — "Jesus said unto him, Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all
   thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. This is the first and great
   commandment. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. On these
   two commandments hang all the law and the prophets."

**Inference — and this is the passage's sharpest management consequence:** the prohibition on
hatred _in the heart_ is paired with a _command to speak_. The verse does not offer a third
option between resentment and confrontation. The available options are: rebuke the neighbour, or
hate him in your heart. Silence is the second one.

**Business consequences:**

- **Silent resentment is the forbidden state.** Observation 2. An organisation that trains people
  to be "professional" by not raising concerns has created the condition the verse forbids.
- **The rebuke is an obligation, not an option.** Observation 3. "In any wise."
- **The purpose of the rebuke is the neighbour's good.** Observation 4. Not the discharge of your
  own frustration.
- **Grudges are prohibited separately from vengeance.** Observation 5. A manager who does not
  retaliate but keeps a record has not complied.
- **This is the mechanism behind A25.** The failure to speak is what produces the hiding and the
  blame.

## Part 3. Measurement integrity

### The statutes

**Text (Proverbs 11:1, KJV)**

> A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.

**Text (Proverbs 20:10, KJV)**

> Divers weights, and divers measures, both of them are alike abomination to the LORD.

**Text (Proverbs 20:23, KJV)**

> Divers weights are an abomination unto the LORD; and a false balance is not good.

**Text (Deuteronomy 25:13-16, KJV)**

> 13. Thou shalt not have in thy bag divers weights, a great and a small.
> 14. Thou shalt not have in thine house divers measures, a great and a small.
> 15. But thou shalt have a perfect and just weight, a perfect and just measure shalt thou have:
>     that thy days may be lengthened in the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee.
> 16. For all that do such things, and all that do unrighteously, are an abomination unto the LORD
>     thy God.

**Text (Leviticus 19:35-36, KJV)**

> 35. Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment, in meteyard, in weight, or in measure.
> 36. Just balances, just weights, a just ephah, and a just hin, shall ye have: I am the LORD your
>     God, which brought you out of the land of Egypt.

**Lexical (verbatim).**

- **H3976 מֹאזֵן (_mozen_)** — "(only in the dual) a pair of scales." KJV: "balances."
- **H68 אֶבֶן (_eben_)** — "a stone." KJV renderings include "plummet, stone(-ny), (divers)
  weight(-s)." **Text states:** the word for a weight is the word for a stone — weights were
  literally stones.
- **H374 אֵיפָה (_eyphah_)** — "an ephah or measure for grain; hence, a measure in general." KJV:
  "ephah, (divers) measure(-s)."
- **H8255 שֶׁקֶל (_sheqel_)** — "probably a weight; used as a commercial standard." KJV: "shekel."
- **H4820 מִרְמָה (_mirmah_)** — "fraud." KJV: "craft, deceit(-ful, -fully), false, feigned, guile,
  subtilly, treachery."
- **H8267 שֶׁקֶר (_sheqer_)** — "an untruth; by implication, a sham (often adverbial)." KJV:
  "without a cause, deceit(-ful), false(-hood, -ly), feignedly, liar, lie, lying, vain (thing),
  wrongfully."

### Why measurement is singled out

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The verdict is unusually strong. The word is _abomination_ — three times in
   Proverbs (11:1; 20:10; 20:23) and once in Deuteronomy (25:16). **Inference:** this is the
   strongest negative category the vocabulary offers, and it is applied to a _scale_.
2. **Text states:** The specific prohibition is _duplicity_: "divers weights, a great and a small"
   (Deut 25:13); "divers weights, and divers measures" (Prov 20:10). **Text states:** The offence is
   _having two sets_ — one for buying and one for selling. The dishonest merchant is not using a
   broken scale; he is using the correct scale for the wrong party.
3. **Text states:** The requirement is stated in the positive: "a perfect and just weight, a perfect
   and just measure shalt thou have" (v. 15). The word is _perfect_ — complete, whole, not adjusted.
4. **Text states:** The same word — _unrighteousness_ — governs Leviticus 19:15 (judgment) and
   19:35 (measurement). **Inference:** the text treats them as one category.
5. **Text states:** Deuteronomy 25:15 attaches a promise: "that thy days may be lengthened in the
   land." **Inference:** honest measurement is connected to _longevity_, which is a business
   consequence as well as a personal one.
6. **Text states (the oracle in Amos):** Amos 8:5 gives the practice a motive clause: "Saying, When
   will the new moon be gone, that we may sell corn? and the sabbath, that we may set forth wheat,
   making the ephah small, and the shekel great, and falsifying the balances by deceit?" **Text
   states:** The intent is stated plainly: make the measure smaller and the price larger.
7. **Text states (Habakkuk):** "For the stone shall cry out of the wall, and the beam out of the
   timber shall answer it" (Habakkuk 2:11). **Inference:** the wrong measurement is embedded in the
   _structure_ and will testify.

**Inference — the axiom this yields:** measurement integrity is not a compliance matter. It is the
thing that makes every other number trustworthy, and the text treats its violation as an
abomination rather than a violation.

**Why:** a business is a system of measurements. If the measurements can be set by the party with
the interest, then every number produced by the system is suspect, and no decision made from them
can be trusted. The false balance is not one lie; it is a _lie generator_.

### The modern equivalents

**Inference, stated as an application list.** The principle is _duplicity in measurement_ — a
different standard for the other party than for yourself.

| Ancient                           | Modern analogue                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Divers weights in the bag         | Two sets of books; a management metric and an investor metric that differ                                          |
| The ephah made small              | Short-weighting a delivery; a "unit" that quietly shrinks                                                          |
| The shekel made great             | A price that varies by the buyer's inability to check                                                              |
| Falsifying the balances           | Revenue recognition timing; a churn definition that excludes the churned                                           |
| The meteyard                      | A "monthly" that is sometimes 5 weeks; a "seat" that means something different in the contract than in the product |
| Two sets of measures in the house | A metric used in the board deck and a different one used in the operating review                                   |

**Business consequences:**

- **The definition of a metric is an act of measurement, and it is subject to this rule.** A churn
  definition chosen to make churn look low is a small ephah. See A7 (naming is an act of authority).
- **The test is duplicity, not inaccuracy.** Observation 2. An inaccurate scale used consistently
  is a different problem from a scale that changes with the counterparty.
- **The strongest form of the rule is "one standard, published."** Observation 3, "perfect and
  just."
- **Watch the metric that appears only in one document.** The board metric and the operating metric
  should be the same number.
- **The structural consequence is longevity.** Observation 5. Honest measurement is connected to
  staying in business.

## Part 4. Exodus 23:1-9: the courtroom and the crowd

**Text (Exodus 23:1-9, KJV)**

> 1. Thou shalt not raise a false report: put not thine hand with the wicked to be an unrighteous
>    witness.
> 2. Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil; neither shalt thou speak in a cause to decline
>    after many to wrest judgment:
> 3. Neither shalt thou countenance a poor man in his cause.
> 4. If thou meet thine enemy's ox or his ass going astray, thou shalt surely bring it back to him
>    again.
> 5. If thou see the ass of him that hateth thee lying under his burden, and wouldest forbear to
>    help him, thou shalt surely help with him.
> 6. Thou shalt not wrest the judgment of thy poor in his cause.
> 7. Keep thee far from a false matter; and the innocent and righteous slay thou not: for I will not
>    justify the wicked.
> 8. And thou shalt take no gift: for the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the
>    righteous.
> 9. Also thou shalt not oppress a stranger: for ye know the heart of a stranger, seeing ye were
>    strangers in the land of Egypt.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the sequence):** The passage opens with _speech_ — "Thou shalt not raise a false
   report" (v. 1) — before it addresses the judgment itself. **Inference:** the integrity of the
   process begins with what is said before the decision, not with the decision.
2. **Text states (the crowd):** "Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil; neither shalt thou
   speak in a cause to decline after many to wrest judgment" (v. 2). **Text states:** The
   prohibition covers both _doing_ evil with the crowd and _speaking_ with the crowd to bend a
   judgment.
3. **Text states:** The word for crowd is "multitude" and "many." **Inference:** the mechanism is
   numerical pressure, which operates independently of argument.
4. **Text states:** Verse 3 prohibits the opposite partiality: "Neither shalt thou countenance a
   poor man in his cause." **Text states:** Combined with verse 6 ("thou shalt not wrest the
   judgment of thy poor"), the passage forbids favouring the poor _in judgment_ while requiring
   their protection. **Inference:** the two are not contradictory; the rule is that judgment is
   impartial, and the poor are protected by other means (the gleaning rules, the wage rules).
5. **Text states (the enemy's ox):** Verses 4-5 require returning a straying animal and helping a
   fallen one, both belonging to "thine enemy" and "him that hateth thee." **Text states:** And
   verse 5 explicitly forbids the _passive_ option: "and wouldest forbear to help him, thou shalt
   surely help with him."
6. **Text states (the bribe):** "And thou shalt take no gift: for the gift blindeth the wise, and
   perverteth the words of the righteous" (v. 8). **Text states:** The stated mechanism is
   _cognitive_: the gift blinds. Not "corrupts" — _blinds_.
7. **Text states (the stranger):** "Also thou shalt not oppress a stranger: for ye know the heart
   of a stranger, seeing ye were strangers in the land of Egypt" (v. 9). **Text states:** The ground
   is _experience_: "ye know the heart of a stranger."

**Inference.** The passage gives a compact ethics process:

1. **The report must be true** (v. 1). Before the decision, the information.
2. **The crowd is not evidence** (v. 2). Numerical pressure is not argument.
3. **Partiality is prohibited in both directions** (vv. 3, 6).
4. **The passive option is closed** (v. 5). Failing to help is a violation, not a neutral act.
5. **The gift blinds** (v. 8). The problem with a bribe is that it _works_ — on the recipient's
   judgment, not on their honesty.
6. **The stranger is protected on the ground of your own history** (v. 9).

**Business consequences:**

- **"Everyone is doing it" is not an argument.** Observation 3. The text treats the multitude as a
  pressure source, not a legitimacy source.
- **The gift blinds rather than corrupts.** Observation 6 is the sharpest point in the passage: the
  bribe does not require a corrupt recipient. It operates on a person who believes they are
  impartial. **Inference:** the correct policy is therefore a bright-line gift policy, not a
  judgment call about each gift's value — because the judgment is what the gift degrades.
- **Failing to act is a violation.** Observation 4. "Thou shalt surely help with him."
- **Sympathy is not impartiality.** Observations 4 and 5 of the passage: the poor are protected by
  structural rules (gleaning, wages) rather than by favour in judgment.
- **The stranger's protection is grounded in memory.** Observation 7. "Ye know the heart of a
  stranger." **Inference:** the obligation is not derived from an abstract principle but from a
  shared history of having been the outsider.

## Part 5. Unequal yoking and mixed seed

**Text (Deuteronomy 22:9-11, KJV)**

> 9. Thou shalt not sow thy vineyard with divers seeds: lest the fruit of thy seed which thou hast
>    sown, and the fruit of thy vineyard, be defiled.
> 10. Thou shalt not plow with an ox and an ass together.
> 11. Thou shalt not wear a garment of divers sorts, as of woollen and linen together.

**Text (Leviticus 19:19, KJV)**

> Ye shall keep my statutes. Thou shalt not let thy cattle gender with a diverse kind: thou shalt
> not sow thy field with mingled seed: neither shall a garment mingled of linen and woollen come
> upon thee.

**Text (2 Corinthians 6:14-17, KJV)**

> 14. Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness
>     with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness?
> 15. And what concord hath Christ with Belial? or what part hath he that believeth with an infidel?
> 16. And what agreement hath the temple of God with idols? for ye are the temple of the living God;
>     as God hath said, I will dwell in them, and walk in them; and I will be their God, and they shall
>     be my people.
> 17. Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the
>     unclean thing; and I will receive you,

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Three agricultural and domestic prohibitions: mixed seed in a vineyard, an ox
   and an ass yoked together, mixed fabric in a garment.
2. **Text states:** The stated consequence in Deuteronomy 22:9 is _defilement_: "lest the fruit of
   thy seed which thou hast sown, and the fruit of thy vineyard, be defiled."
3. **Text states:** The prohibitions are about _combination_, not about any of the components. Seed
   is permitted; a vineyard is permitted; a yoke is permitted. The _mixing_ is prohibited.
4. **Text states (the ox and the ass):** The two animals are of different strength and gait.
   **Inference:** the image is of two things that cannot work together without one being harmed.
   An ass yoked to an ox will be dragged.
5. **Text states:** Paul takes the yoke image and applies it to relationships: "Be ye not unequally
   yoked together with unbelievers" (2 Cor 6:14). **Text states:** He follows with five rhetorical
   questions, each pairing opposites: righteousness/unrighteousness, light/darkness,
   Christ/Belial, believer/infidel, temple/idols.
6. **Text states:** The conclusion is a command to separate: "Wherefore come out from among them,
   and be ye separate" (v. 17).
7. **Text states (the caution):** The passage does not specify which relationships are in view, and
   faithful readers differ on whether it covers business partnerships, marriages, or only cultic
   association. **I am not going to manufacture certainty on this.** What the text states is the
   principle of the yoke and the list of incompatibilities.

**Inference.** The transferable principle, stated carefully: **some combinations cannot be
harmonised, and attempting the combination damages the weaker party and defiles the yield.** The
image of the ox and the ass is the operational one — it is not about purity, it is about what
happens when two things of different strength and different direction are bound together.

**Business consequences:**

- **The yoke image is the useful one, and it is about mechanism, not purity.** Observation 4. A
  partnership between parties of radically different strength, pace, or values will damage the
  weaker one, and the damage is mechanical rather than moral.
- **Test the combination against the yield.** Observation 2. The stated consequence is defilement
  of the _fruit_ — the output. The question is not "is this partner bad?" but "what does the
  combination produce?"
- **Watch for partnerships that look like synergy and behave like a yoke.** Observation 3. The
  components are each fine; the mixing is the problem.
- **The passage does not settle which relationships are covered.** Observation 7. Applying 2
  Corinthians 6:14 to a commercial joint venture is a defensible inference, not a stated
  requirement, and it should be labelled as such.

## Part 6. The golden rule

**Text (Matthew 7:12, KJV)**

> Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them: for
> this is the law and the prophets.

**Text (Luke 6:31, KJV)**

> And as ye would that men should do to you, do ye also to them likewise.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The rule is _positive_: "do ye even so to them." Not "do not do to others." The
   command is to act, not to refrain.
2. **Text states:** The standard is _your own preference_: "whatsoever ye would that men should do
   to you." **Inference:** the test requires you to actually know what you would want, which is not
   automatic.
3. **Text states:** The scope is _comprehensive_: "all things whatsoever."
4. **Text states:** Matthew attaches a claim: "for this is the law and the prophets." **Text
   states:** The rule is presented as the _summary_ of the ethical material, the same way Micah 6:8
   is.
5. **Text states (the context, Luke 6):** Luke's version is embedded in a passage about loving
   enemies and lending without expectation of return (Luke 6:27-35). **Inference:** the rule is
   being applied in an asymmetric situation, not between equals.
6. **Text states (the context, Matthew 7):** Matthew's version is in the Sermon on the Mount, and
   the "therefore" connects it to the preceding passage about asking, seeking, and knocking (7:7-11),
   which is about a father giving good gifts to his children. **Inference:** the standard is
   _parental_ generosity, not transactional fairness.

**Inference.** The rule is often criticised as requiring you to know another person's preferences,
or as treating everyone as though they wanted what you want. The text's own context answers part of
this: in both gospels the rule is set inside a passage about _asymmetric generosity_ — loving
enemies, giving to those who cannot repay, a father giving to a child. The rule is not "treat
everyone identically." It is "act toward others with the generosity you would want directed at you."

**Business consequences:**

- **The rule is a floor for treatment, not a specification of preferences.** Observation 2. It
  establishes the minimum standard of generosity, not the exact form of the benefit.
- **It applies asymmetrically.** Observation 5. The rule is most demanding where the other party
  cannot reciprocate.
- **"This is the law and the prophets" is a claim about sufficiency.** Observation 4. When a
  situation is genuinely unclear, the rule is intended to be enough.
- **The standard is parental, not transactional.** Observation 6. Which is a much higher bar than
  "do as you would be done by" usually implies.
- **It is a test you can run on a policy before you ship it.** Would I accept this term if I were
  the other party, given my position?

## Part 7. Speech

### Reconcile first (Matthew 5:23-24)

**Text (Matthew 5:23-24, KJV)**

> 23. Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath
>     ought against thee;
> 24. Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and
>     then come and offer thy gift.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The condition is that _the other party_ has something against you: "thy brother
   hath ought against thee." Not that you have something against them. **Inference:** the obligation
   is triggered by the other person's grievance, whether or not you think it is valid.
2. **Text states:** The required action interrupts a religious duty: "Leave there thy gift before
   the altar, and go thy way." **Inference:** reconciliation outranks the ritual.
3. **Text states:** The sequence is explicit: "first be reconciled… and then come and offer."
4. **Text states:** The initiative is yours: "go thy way." You go to them.

**Business consequences:**

- **The obligation is triggered by their grievance, not your judgment of it.** Observation 1. A
  customer who believes they were wronged has a claim on your attention regardless of whether the
  belief is accurate.
- **Reconciliation outranks the scheduled activity.** Observation 2. The gift is left at the altar.
- **You initiate.** Observation 4. The burden of the first move is on the party who noticed.

### Yea, yea; nay, nay (Matthew 5:37)

**Text (Matthew 5:37, KJV)**

> But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of
> evil.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The instruction is about "your communication." **Lexical note:** the KJV
   "communication" here is the Greek λόγος (_logos_) family — _speech_, not the modern sense of
   exchanged messages. (See `scripture-foundations` on the 1611 drift of "conversation"; here the
   word is about what you say.)
2. **Text states:** The standard is the doubled affirmative and the doubled negative — "Yea, yea;
   Nay, nay." **Inference:** the doubling is emphasis: yes means yes, plainly and without
   reinforcement.
3. **Text states:** Anything beyond this "cometh of evil." **Inference:** the addition of oaths,
   hedges, and assurances is the problem. It implies that the plain statement is insufficient.
4. **Text states (the context, vv. 33-36):** The passage is about oaths — swearing by heaven, by
   earth, by Jerusalem, by one's own head. **Inference:** the instruction is to remove the
   _escalation apparatus_, not to forbid promises.

**Inference — this is the operational core of A22.** Reputation is attacked with questions. The
defence is a plain word that does not need support.

**Business consequences:**

- **Plain statements, no escalation.** Observation 3. A commitment that needs "I really mean it" is
  already compromised.
- **Hedges are a tell.** Every qualifier in a promise is an exit. A promise with four qualifiers is
  not a promise.
- **This does not forbid contracts.** Observation 4. It forbids the rhetoric of assurance layered
  on top of a plain statement.
- **It is the internal standard that makes the external one work.** A company whose internal
  communication is full of hedges will produce external communication full of hedges.

### The seven abominations (Proverbs 6:16-19)

**Text (Proverbs 6:16-19, KJV)**

> 16. These six things doth the LORD hate: yea, seven are an abomination unto him:
> 17. A proud look, a lying tongue, and hands that shed innocent blood,
> 18. An heart that deviseth wicked imaginations, feet that be swift in running to mischief,
> 19. A false witness that speaketh lies, and he that soweth discord among brethren.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Seven items, and the structure escalates: six, then seven. **Inference:** the
   seventh is singled out.
2. **Text states:** The list is ordered by _body part_: eyes (proud look), tongue (lying tongue),
   hands (innocent blood), heart (wicked imaginations), feet (swift to mischief), and then two
   final items that are not body parts.
3. **Text states:** Three of the seven are about _speech_: a lying tongue (v. 17), a false witness
   that speaketh lies (v. 19), and sowing discord among brethren (v. 19). **Inference:** speech is
   over-represented, and the seventh — sowing discord — is the climax.
4. **Text states:** The seventh is _sowing discord among brethren_. **Inference:** the list ends
   not with a violent act but with a relational one, and it is described as _sowing_ — a deliberate
   act of planting something that grows.
5. **Text states (the parallel):** Proverbs 12:22 — "Lying lips are abomination to the LORD: but
   they that deal truly are his delight." **Text states:** The same category — abomination — applied
   to lying, with the positive counterpart stated.

**Inference:** the list ends with the relational consequence, which suggests that the author treats
the destruction of trust between people as the most serious item in the set.

**Business consequences:**

- **Speech is three of the seven.** Observation 3. Any ethics framework for a company must
  foreground what people say.
- **Sowing discord is the climax.** Observation 4. It is deliberate, it is agricultural, and it
  grows. **Inference:** the damage is not the incident but the crop.
- **The body-part structure makes the list checkable.** Observation 2. Look at the eyes, the
  tongue, the hands, the heart, the feet. Each is observable.
- **The positive counterpart is stated elsewhere.** Observation 5. "They that deal truly are his
  delight." The goal is not the absence of lying but the presence of dealing truly.

## Part 8. The prophets on commerce

### Jeremiah 22:13: the house built by unrighteousness

**Text (Jeremiah 22:13-14, KJV)**

> 13. Woe unto him that buildeth his house by unrighteousness, and his chambers by wrong; that useth
>     his neighbour's service without wages, and giveth him not for his work;
> 14. That saith, I will build me a wide house and large chambers, and cutteth him out windows; and
>     it is cieled with cedar, and painted with vermilion.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The woe is pronounced on a _builder_: "him that buildeth his house."
2. **Text states:** The means are named: "by unrighteousness" and "by wrong." **Inference:** the
   house is not merely built _alongside_ the wrong; it is built _by_ it. The wrong is the funding
   source.
3. **Text states:** The specific wrong is stated: "that useth his neighbour's service without
   wages, and giveth him not for his work."
4. **Text states:** Verse 14 describes the scale: a wide house, large chambers, cut windows, cedar
   ceiling, vermilion paint. **Inference:** the elaborateness of the building is the measure of the
   withheld wages. The description is a _diagnostic_.
5. **Text states (the contrast):** Verse 15 asks, "Shalt thou reign, because thou closest thyself in
   cedar? did not thy father eat and drink, and do judgment and justice, and then it was well with
   him?" **Text states:** The comparison is with a predecessor who ate, drank, and did judgment and
   justice.

**Business consequences:**

- **Watch the build.** Observation 4. Jeremiah's diagnostic is that the elaborateness of the
  construction measures the withheld compensation. A company expanding while its payables stretch
  should expect the comparison.
- **The wrong can be the funding source, not just a side effect.** Observation 2. "By
  unrighteousness" is a statement about the capital structure.
- **The specific named wrong is unpaid service.** Observation 3. Same category as Leviticus 19:13
  and James 5:4.
- **The alternative is stated.** Observation 5. Judgment and justice, and then "it was well with
  him."

### Isaiah 1:17 and 58:3-11: the real audit

**Text (Isaiah 1:17, KJV)**

> Learn to do well; seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow.

**Text (Isaiah 58:3-11, KJV)**

> 3. Wherefore have we fasted, say they, and thou seest not? wherefore have we afflicted our soul,
>    and thou takest no knowledge? Behold, in the day of your fast ye find pleasure, and exact all your
>    labours.
> 4. Behold, ye fast for strife and debate, and to smite with the fist of wickedness: ye shall not
>    fast as ye do this day, to make your voice to be heard on high.
> 5. Is it such a fast that I have chosen? a day for a man to afflict his soul? is it to bow down
>    his head as a bulrush, and to spread sackcloth and ashes under him? wilt thou call this a fast,
>    and an acceptable day to the LORD?
> 6. Is not this the fast that I have chosen? to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy
>    burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that ye break every yoke?
> 7. Is it not to deal thy bread to the hungry, and that thou bring the poor that are cast out to
>    thy house? when thou seest the naked, that thou cover him; and that thou hide not thyself from
>    thine own flesh?
> 8. Then shall thy light break forth as the morning, and thine health shall spring forth speedily:
>    and thy righteousness shall go before thee; and the glory of the LORD shall be thy rereward.
> 9. Then shalt thou call, and the LORD shall answer; thou shalt cry, and he shall say, Here I am.
>    If thou take away from the midst of thee the yoke, the putting forth of the finger, and speaking
>    vanity;
> 10. And if thou draw out thy soul to the hungry, and satisfy the afflicted soul; then shall thy
>     light rise in obscurity, and thy darkness be as the noon day:
> 11. And the LORD shall guide thee continually, and satisfy thy soul in drought, and make fat thy
>     bones: and thou shalt be like a watered garden, and like a spring of water, whose waters fail not.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the complaint):** The people ask why their fasting is not recognised: "Wherefore
   have we fasted, say they, and thou seest not?" (v. 3). **Inference:** they believe they are
   compliant.
2. **Text states (the answer):** "Behold, in the day of your fast ye find pleasure, and exact all
   your labours" (v. 3). **Text states:** The religious observance coexists with _exacting labour
   from others_. **Inference:** the audit looks at the labour practices, not the observance.
3. **Text states:** The fast is further characterised: "ye fast for strife and debate, and to smite
   with the fist of wickedness" (v. 4). **Inference:** the religious activity is instrumentalised
   for conflict.
4. **Text states (the real fast, vv. 6-7):** Six items:
   - loose the bands of wickedness
   - undo the heavy burdens
   - let the oppressed go free
   - break every yoke
   - deal bread to the hungry, bring the poor that are cast out into thy house, cover the naked
   - "and that thou hide not thyself from thine own flesh"
5. **Text states:** Four of the six are about _release from bondage_: bands, burdens, oppression,
   yoke. **Inference:** the audit is weighted toward the removal of imposed constraint, not toward
   the giving of goods.
6. **Text states (the promises, vv. 8, 9, 10, 11):** light, health, righteousness going before,
   the glory of the LORD as a rereward, answered prayer, guidance continually, satisfaction in
   drought, fat bones, a watered garden, a spring whose waters fail not.
7. **Text states:** Verse 9 restates the condition with three items: "If thou take away from the
   midst of thee the yoke, the putting forth of the finger, and speaking vanity." **Inference:**
   the yoke, the pointing finger, and empty speech — an imposed burden, an accusation, and a lie.
8. **Text states:** Verse 7 ends with a clause about _not hiding_: "and that thou hide not thyself
   from thine own flesh." **Inference:** the failure mode is avoidance, and it is named.

**Inference — the audit.** The passage is an audit framework. It says: your religious metrics are
not the ones being reviewed. The ones being reviewed are: did you release the burdens you imposed,
did you feed the hungry, did you house the displaced, did you stop pointing the finger, did you
stop speaking vanity, did you stop hiding.

**Business consequences:**

- **The audit looks at imposed burdens first.** Observation 5. Four of six items are about release,
  not donation. A company's own constraints on others are the primary subject.
- **"Exact all your labours" is the specific charge.** Observation 2. The religious observance and
  the labour practice are in the same verse, and the labour practice is the problem.
- **The "pointing finger" is an item.** Observation 7. Accusation is on the list.
- **"Speaking vanity" is an item.** Observation 7. Empty speech.
- **Hiding is the failure mode.** Observation 8. "Hide not thyself from thine own flesh."
- **The promises are business-relevant.** Observation 6: guidance, satisfaction in drought, and a
  spring that does not fail. **Inference:** the text presents ethical practice as connected to
  resilience, not merely to reward.

### Amos 5:11-12 and 8:4-6: the small ephah

**Text (Amos 5:11-12, KJV)**

> 11. Forasmuch therefore as your treading is upon the poor, and ye take from him burdens of wheat:
>     ye have built houses of hewn stone, but ye shall not dwell in them; ye have planted pleasant
>     vineyards, but ye shall not drink wine of them.
> 12. For I know your manifold transgressions and your mighty sins: they afflict the just, they take
>     a bribe, and they turn aside the poor in the gate from their right.

**Text (Amos 8:4-6, KJV)**

> 4. Hear this, O ye that swallow up the needy, even to make the poor of the land to fail,
> 5. Saying, When will the new moon be gone, that we may sell corn? and the sabbath, that we may set
>    forth wheat, making the ephah small, and the shekel great, and falsifying the balances by deceit?
> 6. That we may buy the poor for silver, and the needy for a pair of shoes; yea, and sell the
>    refuse of the wheat?

**Observations (Amos 5):**

1. **Text states:** The charge is stated as _treading upon the poor_ and _taking burdens of wheat_
   from him.
2. **Text states:** The consequence is _inversion of use_: "ye have built houses of hewn stone, but
   ye shall not dwell in them; ye have planted pleasant vineyards, but ye shall not drink wine of
   them." **Inference:** the assets are built and then not enjoyed. The penalty is the loss of use,
   not the loss of the asset.
3. **Text states:** Verse 12 names three specific wrongs: afflicting the just, taking a bribe, and
   turning aside the poor in the gate. **Text states:** "in the gate" is where legal judgment
   happened. **Inference:** the third is a denial of access to justice.

**Observations (Amos 8):**

4. **Text states:** The motive is quoted directly: "When will the new moon be gone, that we may sell
   corn? and the sabbath, that we may set forth wheat?" **Inference:** the speaker resents the rest
   days because they stop commerce. This is the most explicit statement in Scripture of an
   anti-rest commercial motive.
5. **Text states:** Three mechanisms, in one clause: "making the ephah small, and the shekel great,
   and falsifying the balances by deceit."
   - **Text states:** The _ephah_ is the measure of what is sold — made small.
   - **Text states:** The _shekel_ is the weight of money — made great.
   - **Text states:** The _balances_ are falsified.
     **Inference:** all three operate in the same direction — the buyer gets less and pays more.
6. **Text states:** The result clause: "That we may buy the poor for silver, and the needy for a
   pair of shoes" (v. 6). **Inference:** the mechanism converts a small measurement advantage into
   the acquisition of _people_.
7. **Text states:** The final item: "yea, and sell the refuse of the wheat." **Inference:** the
   quality is degraded as well as the quantity.

**Business consequences:**

- **Measurement manipulation is a _portfolio_ of small adjustments.** Observation 5. Three
  mechanisms, all in the same direction, all small. **Inference:** this is what measurement
  manipulation looks like in practice — not one big fraud but a set of small, consistent biases.
- **The direction of all the biases is the tell.** Observation 5. The ephah small, the shekel
  great. A single metric that moves in the company's favour is a coincidence; a set of them moving
  together is a policy.
- **The penalty is the loss of use.** Observation 2. The houses are built and not dwelt in.
  **Inference:** the consequence of the practice is that the assets become useless to their owners.
- **Access to justice is a named wrong.** Observation 3. "Turn aside the poor in the gate."
- **Resenting the rest day is a stated symptom.** Observation 4. **Inference:** this connects to
  A15. A business that resents its own non-productive intervals is showing the motive Amos names.

### Micah 3:11: judging for reward

**Text (Micah 3:11, KJV)**

> The heads thereof judge for reward, and the priests thereof teach for hire, and the prophets
> thereof divine for money: yet will they lean upon the LORD, and say, Is not the LORD among us?
> none evil can come upon us.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Three roles, each with a price: heads (judge for reward), priests (teach for
   hire), prophets (divine for money). **Inference:** the list covers the judicial, the
   instructional, and the advisory functions.
2. **Text states:** The three are parallel and the point is the same: each function is being
   performed _for_ a payment that determines its output.
3. **Text states:** The second clause is the sharper one: "yet will they lean upon the LORD, and
   say, Is not the LORD among us? none evil can come upon us." **Inference:** the religious
   confidence is used as _insurance against consequence_. The same people who take the payment
   assume they will not be held to account.
4. **Text states:** The word for the judge's payment is "reward," the priest's is "hire," the
   prophet's is "money." **Inference:** the variation in vocabulary suggests these are the ordinary
   payments for these functions, not bribes as such. The wrong is that the payment determines the
   judgment.

**Inference.** The passage identifies a specific corruption: **a professional whose output is
shaped by who is paying.** It is not the taking of money that is condemned — priests and prophets
were supported. It is that the payment determines the answer.

**Business consequences:**

- **The test is not whether money changed hands; it is whether the payment shaped the output.**
  Observation 4. Paid advice is not the problem. Advice whose content tracks the payer is.
- **This applies to every advisory function in a company.** Auditors, consultants, analysts,
  rating agencies, in-house counsel, and any function whose answer matters to the person paying.
- **The three functions map onto three business roles.** Judge = the person who rules on disputes;
  priest = the person who teaches the standard; prophet = the person who forecasts. Each can be
  captured by the payment.
- **The confident clause is the diagnostic.** Observation 3. "None evil can come upon us" — the
  assumption of immunity is presented as a symptom of the corruption, not a comfort.
- **Structural remedies are better than exhortations.** **Inference:** the passage does not say
  "judge more honestly." It says the practice is a problem. The remedy is to remove the payment's
  control over the output — separation of the payer from the beneficiary, term limits, rotation,
  independent funding.

### Habakkuk 2:9-11: the stone out of the wall

**Text (Habakkuk 2:9-11, KJV)**

> 9. Woe to him that coveteth an evil covetousness to his house, that he may set his nest on high,
>    that he may be delivered from the power of evil!
> 10. Thou hast consulted shame to thy house by cutting off many people, and hast sinned against thy
>     soul.
> 11. For the stone shall cry out of the wall, and the beam out of the timber shall answer it.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The motive is stated: security. "That he may set his nest on high, that he may
   be delivered from the power of evil." **Inference:** the accumulation is for protection, and it
   is described as covetousness anyway.
2. **Text states:** The means: "by cutting off many people" (v. 10). **Inference:** the security is
   purchased at the cost of other people's destruction.
3. **Text states:** The consequence is _against the builder's own house_: "Thou hast consulted shame
   to thy house." The thing built for the family becomes the source of the family's shame.
4. **Text states (the image):** "For the stone shall cry out of the wall, and the beam out of the
   timber shall answer it" (v. 11). **Text states:** The building materials testify. The wall and
   the beam speak to each other.
5. **Text states:** The structure of the image: the stone _cries out_, and the beam _answers it_.
   **Inference:** it is a call and response — the testimony of the building is a conversation
   between its parts.

**Inference — the axiom:** **the built thing records how it was built, and the record is not
destroyable by the builder.** Every structure carries its own history in a form that will testify.

**Business consequences:**

- **The building is a witness.** Observation 4. The artifact records the process. This is true of
  physical buildings, codebases, cap tables, and cultures.
- **The motive does not sanctify the means.** Observation 1. The purpose was security for the
  family — a legitimate aim — and the passage still calls it "evil covetousness."
- **The consequence lands on what was being protected.** Observation 3. The house built for
  security becomes the source of shame.
- **Testimony is structural, not investigative.** Observation 5. The wall does not need to be
  interrogated. **Inference:** the implication for practice is that anything you build will be
  legible to whoever comes after, and the parts will corroborate each other.

## Part 9. The duty to act

### Withhold not good (Proverbs 3:27-28)

**Text (Proverbs 3:27-28, KJV)**

> 27. Withhold not good from them to whom it is due, when it is in the power of thine hand to do it.
> 28. Say not unto thy neighbour, Go, and come again, and to morrow I will give; when thou hast it by
>     thee.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The obligation is bounded by _capacity_: "when it is in the power of thine hand
   to do it." **Inference:** the rule does not require what you cannot do; it requires what you can.
2. **Text states:** The beneficiaries are "them to whom it is due." **Inference:** the good is
   _owed_, not merely available. The verse is about a debt, not a donation.
3. **Text states:** Verse 28 names a specific evasion: the deferral. "Go, and come again, and to
   morrow I will give." **Text states:** The clause "when thou hast it by thee" removes the
   justification — the means are already present.
4. **Text states:** The prohibited speech is quoted directly. **Inference:** the deferral is a
   _script_ — a thing people say.

**Inference:** the failure mode this targets is not refusal. It is _deferral_, which looks like
agreement and functions as refusal.

**Business consequences:**

- **The deferral is the named sin.** Observation 3. "We'll get to it next quarter" when the means
  are present.
- **The obligation is bounded by capacity, not by willingness.** Observation 1. Which also means a
  genuine lack of capacity is not a violation — but it must be genuine.
- **"Due" is the standard, not "nice."** Observation 2. This is about what is owed.
- **The script is recognisable.** Observation 4. When you hear the deferral sentence, the verse has
  already been written.

### Deliver them that are drawn unto death (Proverbs 24:11-12)

**Text (Proverbs 24:11-12, KJV)**

> 11. If thou forbear to deliver them that are drawn unto death, and those that are ready to be
>     slain;
> 12. If thou sayest, Behold, we knew it not; doth not he that pondereth the heart consider it? and
>     he that keepeth thy soul, doth not he know it? and shall not he render to every man according to
>     his works?

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The duty is triggered by a person in imminent danger: "them that are drawn unto
   death, and those that are ready to be slain."
2. **Text states:** The evasion is anticipated and quoted: "Behold, we knew it not" (v. 12).
3. **Text states:** The refutation is not evidential but interior: "doth not he that pondereth the
   heart consider it?" **Inference:** the claim of ignorance is tested against what was actually in
   the heart, not against what was on record.
4. **Text states:** The consequence is stated as a general rule: "shall not he render to every man
   according to his works?"
5. **Text states:** The verse structure is conditional and incomplete — "if thou forbear… if thou
   sayest…" — and the apodosis is a series of rhetorical questions. **Inference:** the sentence is
   deliberately left unfinished, which is a rhetorical device that leaves the verdict to the reader.

**Business consequences:**

- **"We knew it not" is not a defence.** Observation 2-3. The text anticipates the exact excuse.
- **The test is what was available to be known, not what was recorded.** Observation 3.
  **Inference:** an organisation with the information somewhere in its systems has knowledge, even
  if no one has read it.
- **The duty is to deliver, not to report.** Observation 1. The obligation is action.
- **The excuse is judged by the heart, not the process.** Observation 3. A process that produced no
  knowledge is not exculpatory if the knowledge was there.

### Your enemy's ox (Exodus 23:4-5)

**Text (Exodus 23:4-5, KJV)**

> 4. If thou meet thine enemy's ox or his ass going astray, thou shalt surely bring it back to him
>    again.
> 5. If thou see the ass of him that hateth thee lying under his burden, and wouldest forbear to help
>    him, thou shalt surely help with him.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The obligation applies to the property of "thine enemy" and "him that hateth
   thee." **Inference:** the duty is explicitly decoupled from the relationship.
2. **Text states:** The verb is intensified: "thou shalt **surely** bring it back"; "thou shalt
   **surely** help with him." **Inference:** the doubling removes discretion.
3. **Text states:** Verse 5 anticipates the reluctance: "and wouldest forbear to help him." **Text
   states:** The text names the inclination and then overrides it.
4. **Text states:** The help is _with him_: "thou shalt surely help **with him**." **Inference:**
   the assistance is collaborative, not a rescue performed at a distance. You do it alongside the
   person who hates you.
5. **Text states:** The pattern is repeated by Jesus: Matthew 5:44 — "But I say unto you, Love your
   enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which
   despitefully use you, and persecute you."

**Business consequences:**

- **The duty to a competitor is real.** Observation 1. A competitor's stranded customer, a
  competitor's data breach, a competitor's regulatory problem — the text decouples the obligation
  from the relationship.
- **"Wouldest forbear" is anticipated and overridden.** Observation 3. The reluctance is normal and
  is not a licence.
- **The help is collaborative.** Observation 4. Not a rescue that establishes superiority, but
  assistance rendered alongside.
- **The doubling is a design feature.** Observation 2. Where the text expects resistance, it
  doubles the verb. **Inference:** the legal drafting is anticipating the reader's reluctance, which
  is worth noticing as a pattern.

### The Samaritan who crossed the road (Luke 10:25-37)

**Text (Luke 10:25-37, KJV — selected)**

> 25. And, behold, a certain lawyer stood up, and tempted him, saying, Master, what shall I do to
>     inherit eternal life?
> 26. But he, willing to justify himself, said unto Jesus, And who is my neighbour?
> 27. And Jesus answering said, A certain man went down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among
>     thieves, which stripped him of his raiment, and wounded him, and departed, leaving him half dead.
> 28. And by chance there came down a certain priest that way: and when he saw him, he passed by on
>     the other side.
> 29. And likewise a Levite, when he was at the place, came and looked on him, and passed by on the
>     other side.
> 30. But a certain Samaritan, as he journeyed, came where he was: and when he saw him, he had
>     compassion on him,
> 31. And went to him, and bound up his wounds, pouring in oil and wine, and set him on his own
>     beast, and brought him to an inn, and took care of him.
> 32. And on the morrow when he departed, he took out two pence, and gave them to the host, and said
>     unto him, Take care of him; and whatsoever thou spendest more, when I come again, I will repay
>     thee.
> 33. Which now of these three, thinkest thou, was neighbour unto him that fell among the thieves?
> 34. And he said, He that shewed mercy on him. Then said Jesus unto him, Go, and do thou likewise.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The question is _jurisdictional_: "who is my neighbour?" (v. 29). **Text
   states:** And the motive is stated: "willing to justify himself."
2. **Text states:** The parable does not answer the jurisdictional question. It replaces it with a
   question about _behaviour_: "Which now of these three, thinkest thou, was neighbour unto him
   that fell among the thieves?" (v. 36). **Inference:** the answer reframes the question from "who
   is owed" to "who acted."
3. **Text states (the two passers-by):** The priest "saw him, and passed by on the other side"
   (v. 31). The Levite "came and looked on him, and passed by on the other side" (v. 32).
   **Text states:** The Levite's version includes _looking_. **Inference:** the second one
   investigated and still passed.
4. **Text states (the Samaritan's actions):** A sequence of seven: came where he was, saw him, had
   compassion, went to him, bound up his wounds, set him on his own beast, brought him to an inn,
   took care of him (vv. 33-34).
5. **Text states:** The Samaritan _crosses the road_: "came where he was." **Inference:** the
   movement is toward, in contrast to "passed by on the other side."
6. **Text states (the cost):** He uses his own beast, his own oil and wine, his own time, and two
   pence, and he commits to an open-ended future payment: "whatsoever thou spendest more, when I
   come again, I will repay thee" (v. 35). **Text states:** The commitment is unbounded.
7. **Text states:** The Samaritan is a member of a group in conflict with the victim's group.
   **Inference:** the ethnic hostility is the point of the choice.
8. **Text states:** The final instruction: "Go, and do thou likewise" (v. 37).

**Inference — the axiom:** **proximity is not the criterion; the act is.** The two who passed were
closer in every relevant way — religion, ethnicity, and the road. The one who acted was the
outsider. And the question the text refuses is the one that limits the obligation.

**Business consequences:**

- **"Who is my neighbour?" is the wrong question.** Observation 2. The question is a
  jurisdiction-limiting move, and the parable refuses it.
- **The cost is unbounded.** Observation 6. "Whatsoever thou spendest more… I will repay thee."
  **Inference:** the willingness to commit beyond the known cost is what distinguishes the
  behaviour.
- **The second passer-by _looked_.** Observation 3. Investigation without action is a distinct
  failure mode, and it is worse than the first passer-by's.
- **Proximity is not the criterion.** Observation 7. The one who acted was the one with the least
  natural obligation.
- **The sequence matters.** Observation 4. Compassion precedes the approach; the approach precedes
  the treatment; the treatment precedes the transfer to care; the transfer precedes the open
  commitment. Each step is a decision point.

### The audit of the least (Matthew 25:31-46)

**Text (Matthew 25:31-46, KJV — selected)**

> 31. When the Son of man shall come in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then shall he
>     sit upon the throne of his glory:
> 32. And before him shall be gathered all nations: and he shall separate them one from another, as
>     a shepherd divideth his sheep from the goats:
> 33. For I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat: I was thirsty, and ye gave me drink: I was a
>     stranger, and ye took me in:
> 34. Naked, and ye clothed me: I was sick, and ye visited me: I was in prison, and ye came unto me.
> 35. And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it
>     unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.
> 36. For I was an hungred, and ye gave me no meat: I was thirsty, and ye gave me no drink:
> 37. I was a stranger, and ye took me not in: naked, and ye clothed me not: sick, and in prison, and
>     ye visited me not.
> 38. Then shall they also answer him, saying, Lord, when saw we thee an hungred, or athirst, or a
>     stranger, or naked, or sick, or in prison, and did not minister unto thee?
> 39. Then shall he answer them, saying, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye did it not to one of
>     the least of these, ye did it not to me.
> 40. And these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the audit criteria):** Six situations, listed twice — hungry, thirsty, stranger,
   naked, sick, in prison.
2. **Text states:** The acts are the simplest possible: give meat, give drink, take in, clothe,
   visit, come unto.
3. **Text states:** Neither group is aware of having done anything to _the King_. Both ask the same
   question: "when saw we thee…?" (vv. 37-39, 44). **Inference:** the audit is conducted on acts
   whose significance the actor did not recognise.
4. **Text states:** The identification is the key: "Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the
   least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me" (v. 40).
5. **Text states:** The negative version is equally explicit: "Inasmuch as ye did it not to one of
   the least of these, ye did it not to me" (v. 45). **Inference:** the failure is omission, not
   commission. Nobody in the parable did anything harmful.
6. **Text states:** The criteria are all _personal and physical_: food, drink, welcome, clothing,
   health, imprisonment.
7. **Text states:** "The least" is the category. **Inference:** the audit runs on the smallest
   cases, not the largest.

**Inference — the axiom:** the audit is conducted on acts of ordinary provision to people who
cannot repay, and the failure mode is _omission_. This is the same principle as Exodus 23:5 and
Isaiah 58: the passive option is closed.

**Business consequences:**

- **The failure mode is omission.** Observation 5. Nobody in the parable harmed anyone. This is the
  most uncomfortable feature of the passage for a business, because businesses rarely set out to
  harm.
- **The criteria are ordinary and physical.** Observation 6. Not strategic, not financial. Food,
  water, welcome, clothing, health, presence.
- **The actor does not recognise the significance.** Observation 3. **Inference:** the acts that
  count are not the ones that were designed to count.
- **"The least" is the operating category.** Observation 7. Not the most valuable customer, the
  most promising employee, or the most visible case.
- **It is an audit, and it is scheduled.** Observation 1. The passage presents a future review with
  stated criteria, which is exactly the structure of a scheduled evaluation (A8, A9, A10).

### The shut bowels (1 John 3:17)

**Text (1 John 3:17, KJV)**

> But whoso hath this world's good, and seeth his brother have need, and shutteth up his bowels of
> compassion from him, how dwelleth the love of God in him?

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** Three conditions, cumulative: _has_ the world's good, _sees_ the brother's
   need, and _shuts up_ his compassion.
2. **Text states:** "Seeth" is part of the condition. **Inference:** the obligation is triggered by

**Business consequences:**

- **Awareness triggers the obligation.** Observation 2. This is the same structure as Proverbs

- **Having and seeing and shutting is the named combination.** Observation 1. Any two of the three

- **The verdict is about the internal state, not the act.** Observation 4. The question is not
  "why did you not give" but "how does love remain in you."

## Part 10. Integrity when no one is watching

### Joseph in Potiphar's house (Genesis 39:1-12)

**Text (Genesis 39:1-12, KJV — selected)**

> 1. And Joseph was brought down to Egypt; and Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh, captain of the guard,
>    an Egyptian, bought him of the hands of the Ishmeelites, which had brought him down thither.
> 2. And the LORD was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man; and he was in the house of his
>    master the Egyptian.
> 3. And Joseph found grace in his sight, and he served him: and he made him overseer over his house,
>    and all that he had he put into his hand.
> 4. And he left all that he had in Joseph's hand; and he knew not ought he had, save the bread which
>    he did eat. And Joseph was a goodly person, and well favoured.
> 5. And it came to pass after these things, that his master's wife cast her eyes upon Joseph; and
>    she said, Lie with me.
> 6. But he refused, and said unto his master's wife, Behold, my master wotteth not what is with me
>    in the house, and he hath committed all that he hath to my hand;
> 7. There is none greater in this house than I; neither hath he kept back any thing from me but
>    thee, because thou art his wife: how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?
> 8. And it came to pass, as she spake to Joseph day by day, that he hearkened not unto her, to lie
>    by her, or to be with her.
> 9. And it came to pass about this time, that Joseph went into the house to do his business; and
>    there was none of the men of the house there within.
> 10. And she caught him by his garment, saying, Lie with me: and he left his garment in her hand, and
>     fled, and got him out.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states (the conditions):** Joseph has full authority and no oversight: "he left all that he
   had in Joseph's hand; and he knew not ought he had, save the bread which he did eat" (v. 6).
   **Inference:** the master does not even know what he owns. Joseph's position is the maximum
   possible discretion with the minimum possible supervision.
2. **Text states (the refusal's reasoning):** Joseph gives three grounds: (a) the master's trust —
   "my master wotteth not what is with me in the house, and he hath committed all that he hath to my
   hand" (v. 8); (b) the uniqueness of the exception — "neither hath he kept back any thing from me
   but thee" (v. 9); (c) the theological ground — "how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin
   against God?" (v. 9).
3. **Text states:** The first two grounds are about the _trust_, not about the act. **Inference:**
   Joseph's argument is that the act would violate a specific delegation, and he names the exact
   terms of the delegation.
4. **Text states:** The third ground is against God, not against Potiphar. **Inference:** the
   ultimate accountability is vertical, which is what makes it work when the horizontal
   accountability is absent.
5. **Text states (the persistence):** "as she spake to Joseph day by day, that he hearkened not unto
   her, to lie by her, **or to be with her**" (v. 10). **Text states:** Joseph avoids even being
   present. **Inference:** the boundary includes avoiding the proximity, not just refusing the act.
6. **Text states (the final scene):** "there was none of the men of the house there within" (v. 11).
   **Text states:** The temptation arrives precisely when the supervision is absent.
7. **Text states (the outcome):** He flees, leaving his garment (v. 12). And the result is a false
   accusation and imprisonment (vv. 13-20). **Inference:** the integrity did not produce an
   immediate reward. It produced a prison sentence.

**Inference — the axiom:** integrity is what a person does when the supervision is absent and the
trust is total. Joseph's position is the test case: he had complete authority, no oversight, and a
master who would never have known.

**Business consequences:**

- **The test case is maximum discretion with minimum supervision.** Observation 1. Any role with
  that shape is a Joseph test, and the text shows what passes it.
- **Name the delegation's terms.** Observation 3. Joseph could state exactly what had been
  committed to him and what the single exception was. **Inference:** the ability to state the terms
  of the trust is what makes the refusal possible.
- **The vertical ground is what works when the horizontal one is absent.** Observation 4. The
  master would never have known.
- **Avoid the proximity, not just the act.** Observation 5. "Or to be with her."
- **Integrity does not guarantee an immediate good outcome.** Observation 7. This is important and
  is usually omitted. Joseph did the right thing and went to prison for it.

1. **Text states (the statement):** Abraham said "She is my sister" (v. 2). **Text states (the
   technical truth):** "And yet indeed she is my sister; she is the daughter of my father, but not
   the daughter of my mother" (v. 12). **Inference:** the statement is literally true and materially
   false. This is a half-truth, not a lie.
2. **Text states:** The consequence falls on a third party: Abimelech takes Sarah and is threatened
   with death (v. 3), and his whole nation with him (v. 4). **Text states:** Abimelech's own
   defence: "in the integrity of my heart and innocency of my hands have I done this" (v. 5).
3. **Text states:** God confirms Abimelech's integrity: "Yea, I know that thou didst this in the
   integrity of thy heart" (v. 6). **Inference:** the deceived party is exonerated, and the deceiver
   is not.
4. **Text states:** Abimelech's accusation names the _scope_ of the harm: "thou hast brought on me
   and on my kingdom a great sin" (v. 9). **Inference:** the harm extended beyond the immediate
   transaction to a whole community.
5. **Text states:** Abraham's stated reason: "Because I thought, Surely the fear of God is not in
   this place; and they will slay me for my wife's sake" (v. 11). **Inference:** the motive was
   self-preservation, and it was a _prediction about the other party's character_ that turned out to
   be wrong.
6. **Text states:** The arrangement was _pre-agreed_: "at every place whither we shall come, say of
   me, He is my brother" (v. 13). **Inference:** it was a standing policy, not a moment of panic.
7. **Text states:** Abraham is not condemned in the text. He is rebuked by a pagan king, and God
   protects Abimelech rather than punishing Abraham. **Inference:** the text reports the incident
   without pronouncing on it directly, which makes it harder to use and more instructive.

**Inference — the axioms this yields:**

- **A statement can be technically true and materially deceptive.** Observation 1. The half-truth
  is the specific failure mode.
- **The harm falls on third parties who acted in good faith.** Observation 2-3. Abimelech is
  exonerated; Abraham is not.
- **The prediction about the other party is a rationalisation.** Observation 5. Abraham's fear was
  a forecast, and the forecast was wrong.
- **A standing policy is worse than a one-off.** Observation 6. This was a pre-arranged script.

**Business consequences:**

- **Technically-true statements are the professional failure mode.** Observation 1. A disclosure
  that is accurate and misleading is the specific wrong here.
- **Test the disclosure against what the other party would conclude.** Observation 2. Abimelech
  acted on a reasonable inference from a true statement.
- **Name who bears the cost of the ambiguity.** Observation 4. The harm fell on a nation.
- **"They will do it to us anyway" is a forecast, and forecasts can be wrong.** Observation 5.
  **Inference:** a defensive deception based on a prediction about the counterparty's bad faith
  creates the bad faith it predicted.
- **Pre-agreed scripts are worse.** Observation 6. A rehearsed answer to a foreseeable question is a
  policy, and policies are choices.

## Part 11. Boundaries set before the pressure

### Daniel 1:8: purposed in his heart

**Text (Daniel 1:8, KJV)**

> But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's
> meat, nor with the wine which he drank: therefore he requested of the prince of the eunuchs that he
> might not defile himself.

**Observations:**

1. **Text states:** The decision is internal and prior: "purposed in his heart." **Text states:**
   The purpose precedes the request.
2. **Text states:** The decision is _specific_: the king's meat and the king's wine. Not "I will not
   compromise."
3. **Text states:** The action that follows is a _request_, not a refusal: "therefore he requested of
   the prince of the eunuchs." **Inference:** the boundary is held through negotiation, not
   confrontation.
4. **Text states:** The stated purpose is self-directed: "that he would not defile himself."
5. **Text states (the outcome, vv. 9-16):** The request is granted, a test is proposed by Daniel, and
   the outcome is favourable. **Inference:** the boundary was held without conflict, and Daniel
   proposed the mechanism for demonstrating it.

**Inference:** the pattern is: decide privately and specifically, then negotiate the mechanism
rather than announce the prohibition.

**Business consequences:**

- **Decide before the pressure, and decide something specific.** Observation 2. "I will be ethical"
  is not a boundary; "I will not do X" is.
- **The decision is private first.** Observation 1. "In his heart" — before any announcement.
- **Negotiate the mechanism, not the boundary.** Observation 3. Daniel requested an alternative; he
  did not refuse the system.
- **Propose the test.** Observation 5. Daniel offered a way to verify the alternative worked. This
  is A9 (the maker evaluates) applied to an ethics proposal.

### Daniel 3: the boundary held in public

**Text (Daniel 3:16-18, KJV)**

> 16. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, answered and said to the king, O Nebuchadnezzar, we are not

   `scripture-foundations`. The statement is "we are not anxious to answer you," i.e. we do not need
   to deliberate. Not "we are being careless."
2. **Text states:** They state the possible deliverance: "our God whom we serve is able to deliver

3. **Text states:** And then they state the alternative: "**But if not**, be it known unto thee, O

**Inference — and this is the crucial point about pre-committed boundaries:** the statement "but if

**Business consequences:**

- **The "but if not" clause is the test.** Observation 3. Would you hold this if it cost you the

- **State the boundary briefly.** Observation 1. No elaboration, no defence of the position.
- **Expect the cost to be raised.** Observation 6. The furnace got hotter after the refusal.
- **The boundary should be specific.** Observation 5. Name the act, not the principle.

**Inference — the axiom:** a boundary that is already a daily habit is not a decision made under

**Business consequences:**

- **Make the boundary a habit before it is tested.** Observation 6. The decision is made once and
  then repeated daily, which means it is not re-litigated.
- **The trap will use legitimate means.** Observation 2. A properly enacted rule, an irrevocable

- **Consistency is the defence and the exposure.** Observations 3 and 5. The same regularity that
  made Daniel effective made him a target.
- **An unblemished record removes all other angles.** Observation 8. The opposition had to attack

- **Do not change behaviour in response to the pressure.** Observation 3. "As he did aforetime."

## Part 13. How to run an ethics call

This is the practical section. It is entirely inference — a procedure assembled from the material
above. It is not a scriptural command.

**The situation this is for:** a decision in front of you, with a deadline, where the right answer
is not obvious and someone is pressing.

### Step 1. State the obligation

**Question:** What is owed, to whom, and on what basis?

**Sources:** the contract, the law, the stated promise, the custom of the trade, the reasonable
expectation created by your conduct.

**Apply Micah 6:8 question 1 (do justly).** _Mishpat_ is a verdict — a determination of what is
due. Write it down. "We owe X to Y because Z."

**The failure to avoid:** not being able to state the obligation. If you cannot say what is owed,
you are not in a position to decide whether to depart from it.

**Check the specific statutes:**

- Is there a wage involved? (Leviticus 19:13; James 5:4; Deuteronomy 25:4)
- Is there a measurement? (Proverbs 11:1; 20:10; Deuteronomy 25:13-16)
- Is there a pledge or collateral? (Exodus 22:26-27; Deuteronomy 24:6, 10-13)
- Is there a defenceless party? (Leviticus 19:14; Proverbs 24:11-12)
- Is there a stranger or outsider? (Exodus 23:9; Leviticus 19:10)

### Step 2. Name who bears the cost

**Question:** If we do this, who pays, and can they refuse?

**This is the Leviticus 19:14 test.** The standard of conduct does not depend on the other party's
ability to detect or resist the harm. If the answer is "they will not notice" or "they cannot
complain," that is the finding, not the reassurance.

**Write down:** the party, the cost, and their capacity to refuse. If the cost falls on someone who
cannot refuse, the burden of justification is on you.

**Check the direction of the harm.** The Amos 8 test: are the measures moving in your favour
consistently? A single metric in your favour is a coincidence; a set of them is a policy.

### Step 3. Apply the three questions

| Question                           | Micah 6:8   | What to write down                                          |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| What is owed?                      | do justly   | The obligation, stated as a verdict                         |
| What is given beyond what is owed? | love mercy  | What you would do if loyalty, not the contract, governed    |
| What do you claim for yourself?    | walk humbly | What standing you are assuming, and what it entitles you to |

**Run them in order and write the answer to each.** The disagreement between the three answers is
the actual finding.

**Then ask the reconciliation question (Matthew 5:23-24):** does the other party believe they have
something against us? If so, that is a claim on our attention regardless of whether we think it is
valid.

### Step 4. Decide before the pressure

**This is A20 and Daniel 1:8.** The rule that is invented at the moment of temptation is a
negotiation. The rule that is stated in advance is a boundary.

**Concretely:**

- **Write the boundary down** in the form "we will not do X," not "we will be ethical." (Daniel 1:8
  named the meat and the wine.)
- **State the "but if not" clause.** (Daniel 3:18.) What will you do if holding the boundary costs
  you the account, the round, the job? If there is no answer, the boundary is a bet.
- **Make it a habit before it is tested.** (Daniel 6:10, "as he did aforetime.") A practice that is
  already daily is not a decision made under pressure.
- **Decide it privately first, then negotiate the mechanism.** (Daniel 1:8: "purposed in his heart,"
  then "requested of the prince.") Do not announce the prohibition; propose the alternative.

### Step 5. Write down the boundary in advance

**The deliverable of an ethics call is a written boundary, not a decision.**

**Why written:**

- A boundary that is not written cannot be repeated identically. (Nehemiah 6:4 — four times, "after
  the same manner.")
- A written boundary can be checked against the case that arrives later.
- A written boundary survives the person who wrote it.

**What it should contain:**

1. **The act, named specifically.** Not the category.
2. **The "but if not" — the cost you will accept.**
3. **The reason, stated once.** Not a defence, a reason.
4. **The mechanism for holding it.** What you will say, and to whom. (Daniel requested; Nehemiah
   sent messengers.)
5. **The review date.** When will this be revisited, and by whom? (A8, A14.)
6. **The escalation path.** Who else knows, so the boundary does not depend on one person's nerve?

### Two failure modes to watch for

**1. The ethics call that is actually a cost-benefit analysis with ethical vocabulary.**
Diagnostic: the analysis reaches a conclusion that is convenient, and the reasoning is
retrospective. **Test:** state the obligation (step 1) _before_ knowing what it costs. If the
obligation is only determined after the cost is known, it was never an obligation.

**2. The ethics call that never produces a boundary.**
Diagnostic: the decision is made well, and nothing is written down. The next instance starts from
zero, and the organisation has learned nothing. **Test:** is there a document? If not, this was a
decision, not an ethics call.

### A note on when the three questions conflict

**Inference.** The three questions can disagree, and the text does not supply a tie-breaker. When
they do:

- **Question 3 breaks the tie.** The humility question asks what you are claiming for yourself. In
  a conflict between "what is owed" and "what mercy would give," the deciding factor is usually
  whether the mercy is being extended in order to create an obligation in the other party. If it
  is, the mercy is a claim, and question 3 has failed.
- **If question 3 is clean and questions 1 and 2 still conflict, prefer the party who cannot
  refuse.** This is the pattern across Leviticus 19:14, Proverbs 24:11-12, Isaiah 58, and Matthew 25. The text consistently weights the obligation toward the defenceless.
- **Say that you are deciding.** When the three conflict, the decision is a judgment call, and it
  should be labelled as one rather than presented as the plain requirement. Per
  `scripture-foundations`: "Do not manufacture certainty on disputed matters."

## Part 14. Working rules

Inferences, stated as working rules.

**On the test**

1. **Run all three Micah questions and write down all three answers** (Micah 6:8). The
   disagreement between them is the finding.
2. **Justice without mercy is cold; mercy without justice leaves the obligation unmet; either
   without humility claims standing you do not have.**
3. **Question 3 breaks the tie.** If the mercy is designed to create an obligation in the other
   party, it is a claim, not mercy.
4. **If questions 1 and 2 still conflict, prefer the party who cannot refuse.**

**On specific obligations**

5. **Build the provision into the process, not the philanthropy** (Leviticus 19:9-10). Leave the
   corners.
6. **Leave the recipient their agency** (Leviticus 19:9-10). They glean; you do not distribute.
7. **Payment terms are an ethics issue** (Leviticus 19:13). Withheld wages are in the same verse as
   fraud and robbery.
8. **The standard does not depend on the other party's ability to detect the harm** (Leviticus
   19:14). This is the single most transferable rule in the chapter.
9. **Favouritism runs both ways** (Leviticus 19:15). Discounts for the sympathetic are the same
   defect as discounts for the powerful.
10. **Rebuke, or you will hate in your heart** (Leviticus 19:17). Silence is the second option.
11. **Grudges are prohibited separately from vengeance** (Leviticus 19:18). Not acting on the
    resentment is not compliance.
12. **One standard, published** (Proverbs 11:1; Deuteronomy 25:15). Duplicity is the offence, not
    inaccuracy.
13. **The gift blinds, not corrupts** (Exodus 23:8). Which is why the policy must be a bright line
    rather than a judgment call.
14. **The crowd is not evidence** (Exodus 23:2).
15. **The passive option is closed** (Exodus 23:5; Isaiah 58; Matthew 25). Failing to help is a
    violation.
16. **A duty to a competitor is real** (Exodus 23:4-5). The obligation is decoupled from the
    relationship.
17. **Name who bears the cost, and whether they can refuse** (Leviticus 19:14; Amos 8).
18. **Check the direction of the biases** (Amos 8:5). A set of metrics all moving your way is a
    policy.

**On speech**

19. **Plain statements, no escalation** (Matthew 5:37). A commitment that needs "I really mean it"
    is already compromised.
20. **The obligation to reconcile is triggered by their grievance, not your judgment of it**
    (Matthew 5:23-24).
21. **You initiate** (Matthew 5:24). The burden of the first move is on the party who noticed.
22. **Three of the seven abominations are about speech** (Proverbs 6:17, 19). Sowing discord is the
    climax.
23. **Technically-true statements are the professional failure mode** (Genesis 20:2, 12). Test the
    disclosure against what the other party would conclude.

**On boundaries**

24. **Decide before the pressure, and decide something specific** (Daniel 1:8).
25. **Decide privately first, then negotiate the mechanism** (Daniel 1:8).
26. **State the "but if not" clause** (Daniel 3:18). If holding the boundary only works when the
    outcome is favourable, it is a bet.
27. **Expect the cost to be raised after the refusal** (Daniel 3:19).
28. **Make the boundary a daily habit before it is tested** (Daniel 6:10).
29. **Expect the trap to use legitimate means** (Daniel 6:8).
30. **Repeat the refusal identically** (Nehemiah 6:4). Varying the wording invites another attempt.

**On integrity and accountability**

31. **The test case is maximum discretion with minimum supervision** (Genesis 39:6). Name the
    delegation's terms so the refusal is possible.
32. **Avoid the proximity, not just the act** (Genesis 39:10).
33. **Integrity does not guarantee an immediate good outcome** (Genesis 39:20).
34. **"We knew it not" is not a defence** (Proverbs 24:12). The test is what was available to be
    known.
35. **The deferral is the named sin** (Proverbs 3:28). "Tomorrow" when the means are present.
36. **The building is a witness** (Habakkuk 2:11; James 5:3). Anything you build is legible to
    whoever comes after.
37. **The beneficiaries of a bad system do not fix it** (Revelation 18:9, 15, 17). They mourn it.
38. **Participation is the liability, not proximity** (Revelation 18:4).
39. **Non-resistance is aggravating, not mitigating** (James 5:6).

**On the procedure**

40. **State the obligation before you know what it costs.** If the obligation is only determined
    after the cost is known, it was never an obligation.
41. **The deliverable is a written boundary, not a decision.**
42. **Write the act specifically, the cost you will accept, the reason once, the mechanism, the
    review date, and the escalation path.**
43. **When the three questions conflict, say that you are deciding.** Do not present a judgment
    call as the plain requirement.
