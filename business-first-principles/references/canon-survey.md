# Canon Survey: Principles Distilled Across Scripture

## Provenance and status

This file records a second, wider pass over Scripture, filtered for business use. It came from a separate working session. It stays here because it covers ground that the Genesis 1-3 axioms do not reach: purpose, communication, resilience, and governance.

The filter used to build it had four rules:

- Kept: direct commands about conduct, such as honesty, fairness, and diligence. These transfer as they stand. Rest is kept too, with a note on how Christians differ about the Sabbath (section 8).
- Kept: descriptions of the character of God and of his long-range action, such as patience, long horizons, and forgiveness. These transfer as strategic posture.
- Dropped: redemptive events and doctrine with no business analogue. These are the mechanics of atonement, the resurrection as doctrine, the new birth, the specifics of the covenants, the civil laws of Israel, and apocalyptic timelines.
- Flagged: everything here is by analogy. No chapter below is a management manual. The value is the first principle, never a tactic proved by a single text.

Read this file with `axioms.md` (A1-A34), which carries the main derivation. Where the two files touch the same subject, the axiom numbering is authoritative.

### What was dropped, and why

The survey drops these subjects as a source of business method:

- Atonement and sacrificial law (Leviticus 16, Exodus 12). They are theological, not managerial. One note: `axioms.md` A31 reads Genesis 3:21 as grace: God made the covering, and the aprons that the man and woman made did not last. A31 notes that many readers see there a first picture of the covering in Christ, and it says that the atonement texts give no rule for cash. It grounds cash reserves in Proverbs 21:20 and Genesis 41 instead.
- The resurrection as doctrine (1 Corinthians 15). One verse in that chapter, 15:58, speaks to the work of every believer. It rests on the resurrection, and it is a promise about labor "in the Lord", not a business method. See section 2.
- The new birth (John 3). It does not transfer as method.
- The mechanics of the covenants (Genesis 15, Jeremiah 31). They do not transfer as method. Section 2 uses one image from Jeremiah 31:33, the law written in the heart, by analogy only. It does not use the covenant teaching of the chapter.

These subjects shape why a person works with integrity. They do not generate business method. A few entries below still touch texts about grace, the new birth, and the resurrection (Ezekiel 36:26, 2 Corinthians 5:17, 1 Peter 1:3, 1 Corinthians 15:58). Where they do, the entry names God as the one who acts and labels the business point as an analogy.

## 1. Foundation and first principles

- Settle the fundamentals, then build on them. Hebrews 5:12 rebukes readers who still need someone to teach them "the first principles of the oracles of God" again. Hebrews 6:1 says: "leaving the principles of the doctrine of Christ, let us go on unto perfection; not laying again the foundation". The text does not say "master the fundamentals before you scale". It says to stop laying the foundation again and to move on. Inference: get unit economics, product truth, and cash discipline settled once, so that you do not have to lay them again, and then grow.
- The foundation decides what survives the storm. Matthew 7:24-27. Stress-test the model. What you build on real value survives downturns. What you build on hype does not.
- Know the one foundation. The foundation itself is Christ: "other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ" (1 Corinthians 3:11). Ephesians 2:20 names "Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone", and 1 Peter 2:6 reads Isaiah 28:16 the same way. These texts are about Christ and his church, not about a business, and a value proposition is not a cornerstone. Inference, on separate grounds: a business still needs a few non-negotiables that it will not trade away, such as honesty, fair pay, and a clear purpose.
- Your work will be tested. 1 Corinthians 3:13-15: "the day shall declare it", and "If any man's work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss". The original audience is those who build on the foundation of Christ, and the test is God's judgment. A business use of this text is an analogy. Inference: build as if every result will be examined, because it will be.
- Make the foundation reproducible. 2 Timothy 2:19 says: "the foundation of God standeth sure, having this seal, The Lord knoweth them that are his." The verse grounds the foundation in a seal, not in a written text. 2 Timothy 3:16 is a separate point: Scripture "is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness". Inference: those four functions describe a good SOP (standard operating procedure) and training manual. If the method lives only in the head of one person, it is not a foundation.
- Look to the end, and trust God for the way. Hebrews 11:10 speaks of "a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God." Abraham did not have a blueprint: "he went out, not knowing whither he went" (11:8). The builder is God. Inference: have a clear picture of the end state where you can, and act in faith where you cannot.

See also A5 (formation precedes filling) and A14 (completion is a real state).

## 2. Purpose and the long game

- Vision comes before building. Genesis 1 is methodical: separate, name, organize, then fill. John 1 says that the Word was first. Get clarity of the idea before execution.
- Work is a calling, not only profit. Ecclesiastes 12:13, 1 Corinthians 10:31, Colossians 3:23. "whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God" (1 Corinthians 10:31), and "do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men" (Colossians 3:23). The motive is the glory of God. Excellence and margin follow from it. They do not replace it.
- Think in generations, not quarters. Deuteronomy 6:6-7 commands Israel to teach "these words, which I command thee this day" to their children. In Jeremiah 31:33 God himself will "write it in their hearts", and verse 34 adds "they shall teach no more every man his neighbour". The original audience is Israel, and the content is God's word. A business use of these texts is an analogy. Inference: succession and the passing on of culture deserve the same deliberate care. They are not afterthoughts.
- Have patience over long horizons. Habakkuk 2:3 ("though it tarry, wait for it"). Galatians 6:9 ("in due season we shall reap, if we faint not").
- Labor in the Lord is not in vain. 1 Corinthians 15:58: "Therefore, my beloved brethren, be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord." The "Therefore" rests on the resurrection (15:54-57). It is a promise about the work of the Lord, not a promise of a financial return. Inference: a believer who works "as to the Lord" (Colossians 3:23) does not need a quick payoff to know that the work is worth doing.
- Do not conform to the defaults of the market. Romans 12:1-2. Choose first-principles thinking over copying the industry. Renew the mind. Do not copy the competition.
- Hold plans humbly. Isaiah 55:8-9, James 4:13-15 ("If the Lord will"). Your five-year plan is a hypothesis, not a prophecy.

See also A30 (mortality makes time the binding constraint).

## 3. Integrity and trust

- Behave the same when no one watches. Proverbs 1:7, Job 31. "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge". Accountability to a higher authority is the final compliance program.
- Represent things truthfully. Exodus 20:16, Proverbs 11:1, 16:11 (honest scales). No deceptive marketing, no massaged financials, no misleading metrics.
- Pay what you owe, when you owe it. Exodus 20:15, James 5:4. The cries of the workers whose wages were kept back "are entered into the ears of the Lord of sabaoth." Payables, payroll, and contract terms are ethics, not admin.
- Keep your word, even at a cost. Matthew 5:33-37: "let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay". Reputation compounds on kept promises.
- Justice, mercy, humility. Micah 6:8 is a three-part test for any hard decision: "to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God". Is it just? Do I love mercy here? Am I walking humbly with my God? The third question puts the decision before God, not only before my own conscience.
- Live by faith under pressure. Habakkuk 2:4: "the just shall live by his faith." The faith is trust in God, and Galatians 3:11 uses the verse for justification by faith. A business use of this text is an analogy. Inference: a person who trusts God can hold principles that do not change with market conditions.
- Be transparent, and confess early. 1 John 1:5-9: "if we walk in the light". Admit an error early. A corrected mistake is cheaper than a hidden one.
- Put mercy above ritual. Hosea 6:6, Exodus 34:6-7. Do not sacrifice people to process. Make forgiveness an operating norm, not an exception.

See also A22-A26 (trust, deception, hidden failure, diagnosis before judgment).

## 4. Wisdom and decision-making

- Wisdom is the asset with the highest return. Proverbs 1-9, and 3:13-16: "Length of days is in her right hand; and in her left hand riches and honour." Fund learning to match.
- Seek the counsel of many. Proverbs 11:14, 15:22. Big decisions get several independent inputs.
- Be quick to hear and slow to speak. James 1:19. Listening is a business skill. Do customer discovery before you pitch.
- Question the offer that is too good. Genesis 3. The first bad advice was a half-truth. Check your inputs. Do due diligence against the incentives.
- Stay humble about what you know. Job 38-41 ("Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth?"), Romans 11:33. Models fail. Keep a margin for the unknown.
- Write the vision plainly. Habakkuk 2:2-3. Make it documented, readable, executable, and able to wait. A strategy that nobody wrote down does not transfer.
- Choose what information you take in. Philippians 4:8, Colossians 3:1-2. What leadership consumes shapes what leadership decides.
- Know the context of the market. Acts 17:22-31. Paul studied Athens, quoted its poets, and spoke its language. Know the worldview of the customer before the pitch.
- Hire for character over credentials. 1 Samuel 16:7: "man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart."
- Use a process for disputes. Acts 15. Hear testimony, debate, decide on principle, and communicate the ruling. That is a governance model.

See also A18 (aloneness is not good) and A26 (diagnose before you judge).

## 5. Leadership and people

- Lead by serving. Mark 10:43-45, Philippians 2:1-11. Greatness comes through service. Leaders take the low seat and bear the cost.
- Make love the operating system. 1 Corinthians 13 describes love:
  - It is patient with people.
  - It "envieth not". Inference: no obsession with competitors.
  - It "vaunteth not itself". Inference: no founder ego.
  - It "thinketh no evil" (13:5). Inference: a culture that forgives.
  - It endures. Love outlasts every process.
- Put people over process. Matthew 22:34-40. The second command is "like unto" the first. Treat people as ends, not as resources.
- Keep unity, with no silos. John 17:21, Ephesians 2:19-22: "fitly framed together". One mission, with aligned teams.
- Examine yourself before you criticize. Matthew 7:1-5. Remove your own beam first. That is the foundation of a real culture of feedback.
- Give dignity to every worker. Job 31:13-15. The servant and the master share a Maker. Apply equal standards from top to bottom.
- Give grace and second chances. Luke 15 shows the joy in heaven "over one sinner that repenteth" (15:7), and 2 Corinthians 5:17 says "if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature". Both texts are about God's work in the sinner. A business use of them is an analogy. Inference: win back the lost customer and the failed employee where integrity allows. Restoration beats permanent exile.

See also A18, A21, and A29. The full treatment is in `org-and-labor.md`.

## 6. Execution and diligence

- Faith without works is dead. James 1:22-25, 2:14-17. Choose execution over intention. Results are the evidence.
- Diligence compounds, and get-rich-quick destroys. Proverbs 6:6-11, 21:5 ("The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness"), 28:20 ("he that maketh haste to be rich shall not be innocent").
- Do it with your might. Ecclesiastes 9:10. Give full effort to the task at hand.
- Sowing and reaping is a law. Galatians 6:7-9: "whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap", and "in due season we shall reap, if we faint not." Inputs decide outputs, and the harvest comes later. The passage does not say that you reap more than you sow. The principle applies to capital allocation and to culture.
- Persist, and keep the golden rule. Matthew 7:7-12. Ask, seek, knock. And treat others as you want others to treat you. That verse is the baseline for customer relationships.
- Trust God with the harm done to you. Genesis 50:20: "ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive." God is the one who turns the evil to good. Inference: because God rules over it, a leader can answer harm with a post-mortem, not with revenge or blame.
- Be accountable, and do not shift blame. Genesis 3. The first management failure was passing the buck. Own the miss.

See also A4, A11, and A25.

## 7. Money and stewardship

- Money is a tool, not a master. Matthew 6:24: "Ye cannot serve God and mammon." Profit is fuel, not mission.
- The budget shows what you believe. Matthew 6:19-21: where your treasure is, your heart follows. Follow the money to see the real priorities.
- Honor the LORD first. Proverbs 3:9: "Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase". The firstfruits go to the LORD, not to the business. Verse 10 ("So shall thy barns be filled with plenty") is a proverb about his blessing, not a guaranteed return. Inference, a separate point: set aside generosity and reinvestment before you spend the rest. Margin and investment are prudence. They are not the firstfruits.
- Give without PR. Matthew 6:1-4. Resist charity done for show.
- Keep sowing, and spread the risk. Ecclesiastes 11:1-6. Contested: many readers take 11:1-2 ("Cast thy bread upon the waters", "Give a portion to seven, and also to eight") as a call to generosity, and others as a call to spread risk. Verse 6 is plainer: "In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine hand: for thou knowest not whether shall prosper". Inference: run several small bets inside one committed venture, not one bet with a single point of failure. This does not conflict with A21 in `axioms.md`. A21 is about leaving the old commitment, not about putting every test into one bet.
- Be careful with guarantees and debt. Proverbs 6:1-5, 11:15, 17:18. The warning about surety: do not underwrite a risk that you cannot carry.
- Pay fair wages. James 5:4. Withheld pay is not only a labor issue.
- Contentment is risk management. 1 John 2:15-17. Covetousness drives bad decisions.

See also A13 and A31. The full treatment is in `capital-and-stewardship.md`.

## 8. Resilience, rest, and renewal

- Trials are training. James 1:2-4, Romans 5:3-5. Pressure produces endurance. Do not waste the downturn.
- Rest is part of the design. Genesis 2:2-3, and Exodus 20:8-11, the fourth commandment given to Israel. Contested: Christians differ on how the Sabbath command applies today (see `axioms.md` A15, observation 6). Isaiah 40:31: "But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength". Renewal comes from waiting on the LORD, not from rest alone. The team that never rests breaks.
- Hope in God steadies the mind. 1 Peter 1:3 speaks of "a lively hope by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead", and 1:13 says "gird up the loins of your mind, be sober, and hope to the end for the grace that is to be brought unto you at the revelation of Jesus Christ". The hope is in Christ, not in a plan. Inference: a leader whose hope rests there can face uncertainty soberly.
- Change comes from the inside. Ezekiel 36:26 is God's promise to "the house of Israel" (36:22): "A new heart also will I give you". God gives the new heart. No leader can. A business use of this text is an analogy. Inference: in an organization, lasting change usually starts with what people believe and value, and then reaches the process.
- Restoration is possible. 2 Corinthians 5:17: "if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature". That is God's work in Christ. Inference, by analogy: in an organization, past failure need not fix a person's standing for good.

See also A15 (rest is structural) and A28 (fruitfulness continues at a cost).

## 9. Communication and reputation

- Salt and light. Matthew 5:16: "that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven." The purpose is the glory of God, not marketing. Matthew 6:1 warns against good works done "to be seen of them". Inference: do good work openly and honestly, and let the credit go where the text sends it.
- Simple honesty. Matthew 5:37: "let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay". James 5:12: "let your yea be yea; and your nay, nay". No embellishment.
- Words build or destroy. Proverbs, James 1:19. Be slow to speak, especially as a leader.
- Repetition is internal communication. Deuteronomy 6:7, 2 Peter 1:12-15. Train, retrain, and remind. Once is not enough.
- Context comes before content. Acts 17. Adapt the delivery. Never adapt the substance.

See also A4 (making is by word) and A7 (naming is an act of authority).

## How to read this file without overclaiming

1. Analogy, not command. Isaiah 28:16 is about a foundation stone in Zion, and 1 Peter 2:6 applies it to Christ. This file does not use it as a model for business foundations (see section 1).
2. The filter is a judgment call, and it is fallible. Whoever drew the line between what transfers and what does not transfer can be wrong in places. The line is stated above so that you can challenge it.
3. A verse supports one principle, not every principle that you can attach to it. Do not chain unrelated claims onto the same text.
4. Where this file and the axioms overlap, the axioms win. They were derived with more care, and they carry the fuller grounding.
5. Keep the subject of the verse. Where the text says that God acts, do not make the leader the one who acts. Where the text names Christ, do not put a business in his place.
6. Check the word before you lean on it. The KJV is 1611 English. See `../scripture-foundations/references/kjv-1611.md` and `../scripture-foundations/references/original-language.md`.
