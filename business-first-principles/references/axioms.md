# The Axioms: Full Derivation

This file is the full derivation behind the 34 axioms in `SKILL.md`. It is a reference document, not a devotional. It shows each step from the text to the business consequence, so that a reader can find the weak link and attack it.

## Method and labels

The method has four steps, from `scripture-foundations`: text, then observation, then axiom, then application. Most "biblical business" writing fails at the observation step. It jumps from a verse to a conclusion and does not say what happens in the passage.

Every claim below carries one of four labels. The labels carry weight. Do not remove them.

| Label | Meaning |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Text states | The passage says this, in these words. You can check it against the KJV. |
| Lexical | A claim about the Hebrew or Greek word under the English, checked against Strong's. |
| Inference | My reasoning from the text. It is the move of a fallible reader, and you can disagree with it. |
| Speculation | A claim that goes past what the text and the lexicon support. It is marked so that you can discard it. |

Two rules govern everything here:

1. Applications are inferences, not Scripture. Genesis states what God did. A fallible reader derives the business consequence. "Genesis 1 suggests" is honest. "The Bible commands you to" is not honest, unless the text gives the command.
2. Check before you infer. The KJV is 1611 English. Where a word carries the argument, I checked the Hebrew or Greek word. Where the lexicon did not support an inference, the inference is not in this file.

## Quotation conventions

KJV wording is quoted exactly as `scripture.ts` returns it. The source data uses curly apostrophes. This file shows them as straight apostrophes. Wording, punctuation, and capitalization are otherwise unchanged. Strong's definitions are quoted word for word from the output of `lexicon.ts`, with its own abbreviations (`i.e.`, `etc.`, KJV renderings in brackets).

A block quote gives one verse on each line. The verse number comes first, then the text.

## A note on what "axiom" means here

These are not axioms in the mathematical sense, where a self-evident statement lets you deduce everything else. They are observations that carry weight. Each one is something the text shows, stated at a level where it needs no more justification and where a decision can come from it.

Axioms are for deriving, not for reciting. A quote of A17 is not an argument for funding maintenance. The argument is to show that A17 governs this maintenance decision, and what follows from it here.

## Group I: The source and the substrate

Genesis 1:1-2. These three verses tell who acts, on what material, and in what condition the material starts.

### A1. All value is derived

Grounding text, Genesis 1:1:

> 1 In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.

#### Lexical

- H7225 רֵאשִׁית (_reshith_): "the first, in place, time, order or rank (specifically, a firstfruit)." KJV renders it "beginning, chief(-est), first(-fruits, part, time), principal thing." Speculation: the firstfruit sense is one of the senses that Strong's lists. In Genesis 1:1 the KJV renders the word "beginning", and nothing in the verse makes it mean "firstfruits". Do not build an argument on the firstfruit sense here.
- H1254 בָּרָא (_bara_): "(absolutely) to create; (qualified) to cut down (a wood), select, feed (as formative processes)." The main sense, used absolutely, is "to create". The formative senses apply only to the qualified uses. The idea "out of nothing" is a theological inference from the whole passage. This dictionary entry does not state it.
- H776 אֶרֶץ (_erets_): "the earth (at large, or partitively a land)."
- H8064 שָׁמַיִם (_shamayim_): "the sky (as aloft; the dual perhaps alluding to the visible arch in which the clouds move, as well as to the higher ether where the celestial bodies revolve)."

#### Observations

1. Text states: The chapter opens with an actor and a product. It gives no account of the origin of the actor. The universe is the thing made. It does not originate itself.
2. Text states: The verb is "create". It is a making, not a discovery or a rearrangement by an agent that already existed. Whatever the material is, it comes into being by decision.
3. Text states: Nothing in verses 1-31 credits a created thing with its own existence. The heavens, the earth, the light, the plants, the animals, and man are all objects of the verb. None is its subject.
4. Text states (structure): The chapter is a chain of dependencies. Light depends on the word (v. 3). Land depends on the gathering of the waters (v. 9). Vegetation depends on land (v. 11). Man depends on dust and on breath (2:7). The account presents nothing as independent.

#### Derivation

Inference: Nothing in the account originates itself. So ownership in the ordinary commercial sense is a delegated arrangement, not a natural right. The maker keeps the claim, and the holder holds. This is the derivation: the entity that did not make itself cannot claim absolute title over itself. So it cannot claim absolute title over anything that it holds.

The text does not say "own nothing." It shows a grant (1:29, "Behold, I have given you...") and an assignment (2:15, the man is put into the garden). A grant is real, and the recipient has real use of it. But a grant stays a grant.

Other passages make the same claim in plain words. That is why this is an axiom and not an impression:

Psalm 24:1:

> 1 The earth is the LORD's, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein.

1 Chronicles 29:14:

> 14 But who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able to offer so willingly after this sort? for all things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee.

1 Corinthians 4:7:

> 7 For who maketh thee to differ from another? and what hast thou that thou didst not receive? now if thou didst receive it, why dost thou glory, as if thou hadst not received it?

Deuteronomy 8:17-18:

> 17 And thou say in thine heart, My power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth.
>
> 18 But thou shalt remember the LORD thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth...

James 1:17:

> 17 Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.

#### Business consequences

- Ownership is stewardship. The correct posture toward an asset is responsibility for it, not possession of it. This changes what founders think they do when they sell a company, close a division, or spend cash reserves.
- Entitlement is a factual error, not only a vice. The problem is not that the entitled founder is rude. The founder misdescribes the situation, and the wrong description causes later errors. A person who believes that he earned the market he was born into cannot see when the market changes. His model has no place where the market was ever a gift.
- The "self-made" story is expensive. It removes the feedback loop. If I was owed the good outcome, then someone else is to blame for a bad outcome. To learn, you must admit that you received the inputs.
- It sets the correct attitude to inheritance, windfall, and luck. The inventory of the substrate in the procedure (`SKILL.md` step 2) exists because the first thing to get right is what you received and did not choose.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is not moral grandiosity. It is the inability to learn and the inability to be grateful. In organizations it is a specific pathology: founders who cannot hear that the market changed, because their model has no place for a market that was given.

The companion failure is the inverse, and it is as common. A founder correctly concludes that everything is received, and then concludes that nothing he does matters. The text supports neither conclusion. God puts the man in the garden to work it (2:15).

#### Extending verses

- Psalm 115:16: "The heaven, even the heavens, are the LORD's: but the earth hath he given to the children of men." The grant is real, and it is to men.
- Deuteronomy 8:11-20: the full passage. Prosperity comes, then forgetting, then the warning.
- Proverbs 10:22: "The blessing of the LORD, it maketh rich, and he addeth no sorrow with it."
- Haggai 2:8: "The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, saith the LORD of hosts."
- Matthew 25:14: the goods are delivered to servants. See `capital-and-stewardship.md`.

### A2. The default state is formless and empty

Grounding text, Genesis 1:2:

> 2 And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.

#### Lexical

- H8414 תֹּהוּ (_tohu_): "a desolation (of surface), i.e. desert; figuratively, a worthless thing; adverbially, in vain." KJV: "confusion, empty place, without form, nothing, (thing of) nought, vain, vanity, waste, wilderness."
- H922 בֹּהוּ (_bohu_): "a vacuity, i.e. (superficially) an undistinguishable ruin." KJV: "emptiness, void." Note the pair. _Tohu_ is a desolation of surface, a waste. _Bohu_ is an undistinguishable ruin, a formlessness so complete that you cannot tell the parts apart.
- H2822 חֹשֶׁךְ (_choshek_): "the dark; hence (literally) darkness; figuratively, misery, destruction, death, ignorance, sorrow, wickedness."
- H7307 רוּחַ (_ruach_): "wind; by resemblance breath, i.e. a sensible (or even violent) exhalation; figuratively, life, anger, unsubstantiality; by extension, a region of the sky; by resemblance spirit, but only of a rational being..."

#### Observations

1. Text states: The verse names three conditions before anything is made: formlessness (_tohu_), emptiness (_bohu_), and darkness. They describe the starting material.
2. Text states: The verse does not condemn the three conditions. It passes no judgment on them. They are the state of things.
3. Text states (sequence): Order comes after. Light (v. 3), division (v. 4), naming (v. 5), and the rest are all later acts. Nothing in the chapter describes order as the natural condition that something sometimes disturbs.
4. Text states: At the end of the chapter, God evaluates the whole as "very good" (1:31). So formlessness is not evil. It is unfinished. This distinction matters, and readers lose it easily.

#### Derivation

Inference: The unformed state is the starting condition, not an anomaly. So decay is the baseline, and order is the thing that needs input. Every system drifts toward disorder unless something works against the drift all the time. This applies to a codebase, a relationship, a margin, a supply chain, and a culture.

This is a claim about what to expect. It is not a command to be pessimistic. The text states the condition. The business consequence is mine.

The New Testament makes the same claim about the created order in stronger terms.

Romans 8:20-22:

> 20 For the creature was made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by reason of him who hath subjected the same in hope,
>
> 21 Because the creature itself also shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God.
>
> 22 For we know that the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now.

Hebrews 1:10-12:

> 10 And, Thou, Lord, in the beginning hast laid the foundation of the earth; and the heavens are the works of thine hands:
>
> 11 They shall perish; but thou remainest; and they all shall wax old as doth a garment;
>
> 12 And as a vesture shalt thou fold them up, and they shall be changed: but thou art the same, and thy years shall not fail.

Isaiah 51:6:

> 6 ...for the heavens shall vanish away like smoke, and the earth shall wax old like a garment, and they that dwell therein shall die in like manner...

#### Business consequences

- Budget for entropy. Maintenance does not admit poor design. It is the cost of having a design at all. A plan that assumes a stable baseline assumes a state that the text says does not exist.
- "Unexpected" is usually a category error. Churn, cost overrun, and delay are not surprises if the baseline is decay. When you name the rate of drift, a repeated shock becomes a line item.
- New things decay too. Hebrews 1:11 says that the created order waxes old. It does not say that it was badly made. Aging is not failure.
- It gives a new view of the "clean slate" instinct. Reorganizations, rewrites, and relaunches attract people because they reset the disorder. But they reset it to a different starting disorder, not to zero, and the drift starts again. The text gives no example of a state that stays in order without continued action.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is plans that assume stability. The bill comes as "unexpected" churn, cost, and delay every quarter. Then comes an explanation that treats each one as a single event.

The organization develops a permanent surprise reflex. Every miss becomes a story about a specific bad actor or a specific unlucky event, and no one ever puts a price on the drift.

#### Extending verses

- Ecclesiastes 10:18: "By much slothfulness the building decayeth; and through idleness of the hands the house droppeth through."
- Proverbs 24:30-34: the field grown over with thorns, the broken wall, and the diagnosis.
- Matthew 7:24-27: the house on the rock and the house on the sand. Both are tested, and the test is the same storm.
- Nehemiah 2:13: "...and viewed the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down, and the gates thereof were consumed with fire." Text states: the Babylonian army broke the walls down and burned the city (2 Kings 25:9-10, Nehemiah 1:3). The wall did not decay from neglect. Inference: the wall then stayed broken for generations, because no one rebuilt it. Damage that no one repairs becomes the normal state.

### A3. Presence precedes production

Grounding text, Genesis 1:2:

> 2 ...And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.

#### Lexical

- H7363 רָחַף (_rachaph_): "to brood; by implication, to be relaxed." KJV: "flutter, move, shake." The image is a bird over a nest. It broods. It does not only travel. The KJV "moved upon" is weaker than the dictionary.

#### Observations

1. Text states (sequence): The Spirit moves before God says anything. Verse 2 has the brooding. Verse 3 has the first word. The order is presence, then speech, then product.
2. Text states: The brooding is "upon the face of the waters". It is over the chaotic material, not apart from it. It is attention directed at the real situation, with its formlessness.
3. Text states: The verse records no product during the brooding. It is an act with no deliverable.
4. Text states (vocabulary): The verb is not a verb of planning, command, or measurement. It is a verb of brooding, an image of long, quiet nearness.

#### Derivation

Inference: Attention over the chaos comes before the word that orders it. So the cheapest hour in any project is the hour spent to understand the situation before you act. The maker who speaks first and examines later pays for it in rework.

This is an inference from sequence. The text does not say "examine before acting." It shows an order of operations and leaves the reader to notice it.

The same shape occurs again.

Psalm 46:10 (stillness before knowledge):

> 10 Be still, and know that I am God...

Exodus 33:14 (presence before rest, and rest before the journey):

> 14 And he said, My presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest.

#### Business consequences

- A rush to visible output is the most common way to be slow. Other people can see output. They cannot see understanding. That difference rewards early production again and again.
- Discovery is not a phase that a senior person can skip. The account gives the brooding before the first command. If you skip it, the work does not go away. It moves into rework, where it costs more.
- It argues for a specific budget line. Some work has no artifact: you read the codebase, watch the customer, read the contracts, walk the site, and sit with the data. So this work gets cut first. The axiom says that you must protect it first.
- It separates attention from analysis. Brooding is not a report. It is long nearness to the material. A dashboard is not the same thing as looking.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is rework. Inference: rework is very expensive, because it pays twice for the same result. The second-order failure follows. Teams learn that the reward goes to whoever produces something. So they produce something before they understand the problem, and the organization becomes efficient at building the wrong thing.

#### Extending verses

- Proverbs 18:13: "He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him."
- Proverbs 18:17: "He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him."
- Luke 14:28: "For which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it?" To sit down first is the same sequence.
- Nehemiah 2:12-16: the survey by night before the public announcement.

## Group II: The method of making

Genesis 1:3-31 through 2:1. This group has eleven axioms on how the work is done. It carries the most business weight. It is the only place in Scripture that shows a complete production process from start to end, with its sequence intact.

### A4. Making is by word

Grounding text, Genesis 1:3:

> 3 And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

Text states (repetition, checked by search): The phrase "And God said" occurs ten times in Genesis 1, in verses 3, 6, 9, 11, 14, 20, 24, 26, 28, and 29. No other formula in the chapter repeats at that density. The repetition is the author's mark of what to notice.

#### Observations

1. Text states (sequence): In every case the speech comes before the result. "And God said, Let there be..." comes first, and then "...and there was". It is never the reverse.
2. Text states: The speech is a specification, not persuasion. It does not argue with the darkness or bargain with the waters. It states what shall be.
3. Text states: Most of the speeches are declarations of what will be, not requests or prayers. Two speeches have a stated audience. In 1:28 "God said unto them", to the man and the woman, and in 1:29 God says "I have given you". Inference: the making speeches in 1:3-26 do not name a hearer. The speeches in 1:28-29 are a blessing, a command, and a grant to the humans.
4. Text states (result): The result matches the specification. The chapter shows no gap between what God said and what appeared. "And it was so" is the confirmation formula (vv. 7, 9, 11, 15, 24, 30).
5. Text states (John 1): The New Testament identifies the Word as a person and as the agent of creation: "All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made" (John 1:3).

#### Derivation

Inference: In every instance, specification comes before manifestation. So the clarity of the specification sets the upper limit on the quality of the output. You cannot produce more precision than you specified. Briefs, specs, contracts, promises, and names are production tools, not paperwork.

The chain is tight. An unclear word is not a soft problem. It guarantees an unclear result, because the result comes from the word. The account describes no other input.

#### Business consequences

- Downstream work does not resolve ambiguity in the brief. It makes the ambiguity larger. Each person who reads an unclear spec supplies a different reading. Three readers produce three products.
- Contracts, scopes, and definitions are manufacturing equipment. They belong in the budget with the tools. A team that treats a spec as overhead treats the mold as overhead while it pours metal.
- "Let there be" is a test of specificity. You can check a useful spec. Ask what will count as "and it was so". If nothing that you can observe will confirm or disprove the brief, it is not a spec.
- Names given in advance are part of this. A7 develops the idea. The point here is that the word is the instrument.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is rework. The anti-pattern table in `SKILL.md` names it: specifying by implication leads to rework. The second-order failure is worse. Organizations that under-specify for a long time learn to treat rework as normal. Then they cannot estimate anything.

#### Extending verses

- Psalm 33:9: "For he spake, and it was done; he commanded, and it stood fast."
- Isaiah 55:10-11: "...So shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please, and it shall prosper in the thing whereto I sent it."
- Hebrews 11:3: "Through faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God..."
- Proverbs 18:21: "Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof."
- Proverbs 30:5-6: "Every word of God is pure... Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar." Inference: the implementer does not edit the specification.
- John 1:1-3: the Word as person and agent.

### A5. Formation precedes filling

This axiom gets the longest treatment in this file. The two-pass structure is the thing in Genesis 1 that transfers best, and it is the easiest to get wrong.

Grounding text: Genesis 1:3-31, the six days of work, in order.

#### Text states (the structure, laid out)

| Day | Forming (days 1-3) | Text | Filling (days 4-6) | Text |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| 1 | Light divided from darkness. Day and night named. | 1:3-5 | Lights placed to rule day and night | 1:14-19 |
| 2 | Firmament made. It divides the waters above from the waters below. | 1:6-8 | Fish fill the waters. Fowl fly in the open firmament. | 1:20-23 |
| 3 | Dry land made to appear. Seas gathered. Vegetation brought forth. | 1:9-13 | Land animals and man. The herbs and trees given for meat. | 1:24-31 |

#### Observations

1. Text states: Days 1-3 each set up a domain and its boundaries. The verbs divide and gather: "divided the light from the darkness" (1:4), "let it divide the waters from the waters" (1:6), "let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear" (1:9). Text states (checked by search): the word divide or divided occurs five times in Genesis 1, in verses 4, 6, 7, 14, and 18. Three of the five (verses 4, 6, and 7) are on the forming days. Verses 14 and 18 are on day 4, a filling day, where the lights divide the day from the night.
2. Text states: Days 4-6 each populate a domain that an earlier day set up. The verbs fill: "Let there be lights" (1:14), "Let the waters bring forth abundantly" (1:20), "Let the earth bring forth the living creature" (1:24).
3. Text states (correspondence): The pairs are one to one and in order. Day 4 fills day 1: the lights rule the day and night that God divided on day 1 (1:16, 18). Day 5 fills day 2: fowl fly "in the open firmament" (1:20), the domain named on day 2, and creatures fill the divided waters. Day 6 fills day 3: land animals and man live on the dry land, and their food is the herbs and trees from day 3 (1:29-30).
4. Text states (the end): Only after both passes does God call the whole "very good" (1:31). God called each part good. The "very good" attaches to the completed assembly.
5. Text states (the one exception, stated honestly): Day 3 includes vegetation, and vegetation is arguably contents, not container. At that point the pattern is not perfectly clean. Two readings are available:
   - (a) The vegetation is part of the forming of the land. It is what the land typically brings forth, and it is the provision that the day-6 occupants eat.
   - (b) The pattern is a strong tendency, not a rigid scheme.

   Faithful readers differ. I hold (a). It is a reading, not a statement of the text.

#### Derivation

Inference: The domains come before their occupants. So structure comes first and contents second, in two passes, not one. Build the container before the contents. Build the platform before the features, the roles before the people, the chart of accounts before the spending, and the outline before the prose.

The derivation rests on the correspondence, and the correspondence is the argument. The order of the days is not accidental. The author took care to line them up. A reader who accepts the correspondence must deal with this claim: to fill before you form is a category error, not only a preference about sequence.

Scripture states the same shape a second time, in a book of practical wisdom.

Proverbs 24:3-4:

> 3 Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established:
>
> 4 And by knowledge shall the chambers be filled with all precious and pleasant riches.

The passage has three verbs and three stages: built, then established, then filled. The KJV uses "builded" for the first stage and "established" for the second, and only then does the filling happen. This is the same two-pass shape, stated as a proverb. Short of a command, that is the strongest support available.

#### Business consequences

- To fill before you form does not produce a full thing. It produces clutter. This is the sharpest claim of the axiom. Clutter is not a partial version of order. It is a different state, and it is harder to fix than emptiness. An empty directory is easy to organize. A directory with four hundred badly named files is a project.
- The container is usually cheap and invisible. Examples are the schema, the chart of accounts, the information architecture, the role definitions, and the outline. None of these produces a demo. All of them decide what you can demo later.
- It is the diagnosis for a specific class of stuck company. Some companies have many features and no platform, many hires and no roles, or many products and no portfolio logic. Such a company is not "further along" than an empty one. It is in the clutter state. The forming work must still be done, and now it meets resistance, because the contents are already committed.
- It applies to the second pass too. The filling pass has its own discipline. It fills the domains that were formed, in the order they were formed. Day 4 does not fill the land of day 3 with lights.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is clutter, and then features that you cannot evaluate. You cannot evaluate them because no domain exists in which to judge whether they fit. The anti-pattern table lists this directly: filling before forming leads to clutter and to features that cannot be evaluated.

A second failure is less visible. A team that never built a container does not know that it can build one. It experiences every structural problem as a fixed property of the world.

#### Extending verses

- Exodus 25:9: "According to all that I shew thee, after the pattern of the tabernacle, and the pattern of all the instruments thereof, even so shall ye make it." The pattern comes before the making.
- Hebrews 8:5: "...for, See, saith he, that thou make all things according to the pattern shewed to thee in the mount."
- Proverbs 24:27: "Prepare thy work without, and make it fit for thyself in the field; and afterwards build thine house." Preparation comes before building, in a farming frame.
- Luke 14:28-30: the tower. The counting comes before the foundation, and the failure is a foundation laid without the means to finish.
- Nehemiah 3: Nehemiah assigns the wall by section before anyone builds. The divisions exist before the work does.

### A6. Distinction precedes order

Grounding text, Genesis 1:4:

> 4 And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.

#### Observations

1. Text states: The division comes after the evaluation of the light and before the naming. The sequence is: made, judged good, divided, named.
2. Text states: God divides light from darkness, not light from nothing. Both terms are needed. The division is between two things, not between a thing and its absence.
3. Text states (checked by search): "Divide" or "divided" occurs again and again in the chapter, in 1:4, 6, 7, 14, and 18. Boundaries are a repeated instrument, not a single act.
4. Text states: The division makes a boundary. It does not discover one that already existed. "God divided" is an act.

#### Derivation

Inference: Things become definable when they are separated from what they are not. So meaning requires boundaries. You must be able to say what something is not.

The negative space is the argument. In verse 4 the light was already "good" before the division. The division adds definability. Before it, there is light and darkness. After it, there is Day and Night (v. 5). The category exists only on the far side of the cut.

#### Business consequences

- Scope, segment, and positioning are acts of division. They do not describe existing categories. They are the cuts that create categories.
- A category that no one divided has no meaning. "We serve businesses" is not a segment. "We serve multi-site veterinary practices with 3-20 locations" is a segment. Its meaning comes from what it excludes.
- A business that will not say "no" did not say "yes" to anything. This is the axiom in operational form. The customer that you refuse defines the customer that you serve.
- Boundary work is senior work. It is the one task that you cannot delegate downward, because it defines what the organization is. That is why the anti-pattern table pairs "growth with no stated boundary" with A6 and A20 together.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is scope explosion, and then nobody can say what the business is. The bill is concrete. Sales cannot qualify leads, product cannot set priorities, and hiring cannot write a specification, because the company has no shared cut to reason from. The anti-pattern table says it: growth with no stated boundary leads to scope explosion, and you can no longer say what you are.

#### Extending verses

- Leviticus 10:10: "And that ye may put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean;" Distinction is a duty of the priest.
- Ezekiel 22:26 gives the failure: "they have put no difference between the holy and profane, neither have they shewed difference between the unclean and the clean". The text calls the failure to divide a violation, and the result is that the holy is profaned.
- 2 Corinthians 6:14-17: "Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness?" The passage uses a series of divisions to define a boundary.
- Genesis 1:6-7: the firmament divides waters from waters. This is a boundary inside one substance, not only between two obvious things.

### A7. Naming is an act of authority

Grounding text, Genesis 1:5:

> 5 And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day.

#### Observations

1. Text states: The maker names what he made. He does not ask the light what it is called.
2. Text states (repetition): Naming occurs at the forming steps: Day and Night (1:5), Heaven (1:8), Earth and Seas (1:10). Naming is part of the forming pass, not an appendix.
3. Text states: The names stay. The text records no later revision. Day and Night are still Day and Night at the end of the chapter and through the rest of Scripture.
4. Text states (Genesis 2): God delegates the pattern to man: "whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof" (2:19). God grants the authority to name. He does not keep it only for himself.

#### Derivation

Inference: Naming is how the maker sets what a thing is. So names are decisions, not labels. Whoever names the category defines the terms of competition in it.

The delegation in 2:19 is the sharp part. The text states that the names Adam gave stand. It does not say that they were provisional or open to review. Naming authority is real authority.

#### Business consequences

- The one who names the category sets the terms. If you define "spend management software," you decide the comparison set, and so you decide what people compare you on.
- Metric definitions are acts of naming. Examples are "active user," "churn," "gross margin," and "on-time." Each definition quietly sets what people believe is true, and someone makes each one as a decision. Most organizations run several conflicting definitions at once. That is a failure to use the authority, not a lack of authority.
- Role titles are acts of naming. The name of a role decides what candidates think they apply for and what holders of the role think they are allowed to do.
- Renaming is expensive because it means a new founding. A rename is not a change of label. It changes what the thing is, and the old name keeps its claims alive in contracts, habits, and data.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is not bad names. It is that no one is responsible for names. The bill comes as drift in definitions. Three teams report three different churn numbers. The company starts a rebrand to fix a positioning problem that was really a naming problem. Nobody can answer "what business are we in?" with a sentence that everyone accepts.

#### Extending verses

- Genesis 2:19-20: God delegates naming to the man, and the names stand.
- Genesis 17:5: "Neither shall thy name any more be called Abram, but thy name shall be Abraham; for a father of many nations have I made thee." The name carries the identity.
- Genesis 32:28: "Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel..." The new name marks a new founding.
- Matthew 16:18: "...thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church..." The name comes with the assignment.
- Revelation 2:17: "...and will give him a white stone, and in the stone a new name written, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it."

### A8. Time is a created instrument

Grounding text, Genesis 1:14:

> 14 And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:

#### Lexical

- H4150 מוֹעֵד (_moed_): "properly, an appointment, i.e. a fixed time or season; specifically, a festival; conventionally ayear [sic]; by implication, an assembly (as convened for a definite purpose); technically the congregation; by extension, the place of meeting; also a signal (as appointed beforehand)." The KJV renders it here as "seasons." Note the compound. An appointment, a signal appointed beforehand, and an assembly are the same word. A season is a scheduled meeting, not only a stretch of weather.
- H3974 מָאוֹר (_maor_): "properly, a luminous body or luminary, i.e. (abstractly) light (as an element); figuratively, brightness, i.e.cheerfulness; specifically, a chandelier". This is the word for "lights" in 1:14. The "light" of 1:3 is a different word, H216 אוֹר (_or_): "illumination or (concrete) luminary (in every sense, including lightning, happiness, etc.)". Both words come from the same root, H215.
- H3117 יוֹם (_yom_): "a day (as the warm hours), whether literal (from sunrise to sunset, or from one sunset to the next), or figurative (a space of time defined by an associated term)". Note the second half of the definition: "a space of time defined by an associated term". Nature does not fix the unit. What it is attached to defines it.

#### Observations

1. Text states: God makes the lights, and he makes them for something. They are instruments with stated purposes.
2. Text states: The verse lists the purposes: signs, seasons, days, years. All four are functions of measurement.
3. Text states: God makes these instruments on day 4. That is after the division of day and night on day 1, and after three days are already counted. The account counts time before the clock is in place.
4. Text states (the counting formula): The formula "And the evening and the morning were the ... day" occurs six times, checked by search (1:5, 8, 13, 19, 23, 31). The text counts the day by a formula. It does not take the day from the sky.

#### Derivation

Inference: Calendars, cycles, and seasons are made things, given for a purpose. So cadence is a design choice, not a natural fact. Review rhythm, release rhythm, planning horizon, and the fiscal year are instruments that you can shape.

Observation 3 is the argument for this. On days 1-3 the text counts time by a formula, before the sun and moon exist to define it. The formula is a convention that the maker set. If the count of days is a convention at the very origin of the world, then the count of quarters and sprints is a convention too.

#### Business consequences

- A business with no cadence has no memory and no rhythm of correction. Cadence makes review possible. Without a fixed interval, no point exists at which anyone must look.
- You choose the instruments, so you can choose them badly. An annual planning cycle in a market that moves every month is a badly chosen instrument, not bad luck.
- Use different clocks for different things: weekly for delivery, monthly for cash, quarterly for strategy, annually for the covenant. Genesis 1 has one day-unit. It does not follow that every business process needs the same unit.
- "Seasons" are appointments. The definition of _moed_ includes "an assembly (as convened for a definite purpose)." In the business sense, a season is a meeting scheduled in advance. That is why the axiom connects to A20 (state the boundary before the pressure).

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is no rhythm of correction. Problems grow silently between the rare moments when anyone looks. The entry in the anti-pattern table for this is "A plan with no stopping rule". It leads to endless projects, no learning, and no exit. The mechanism is the same: without a cadence, no moment is scheduled at which anyone reviews the decision again.

#### Extending verses

- Genesis 8:22: "While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease." See `trends-and-timing.md`.
- Psalm 104:19: "He appointed the moon for seasons: the sun knoweth his going down."
- Ecclesiastes 3:1: "To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven:"
- Exodus 20:8-11: the sabbath command rests in plain words on the rhythm of the creation week.
- Galatians 4:4: "But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son..." The timing is a chosen instrument, not an accident.
- Daniel 2:21: "And he changeth the times and the seasons: he removeth kings, and setteth up kings..."

### A9. The maker evaluates the work

Grounding text, Genesis 1:4:

> 4 And God saw the light, that it was good...

Text states (repetition, checked by search): The pattern "God saw ... good" occurs seven times in Genesis 1, in verses 4, 10, 12, 18, 21, 25, and 31. The seventh is different: "And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good."

#### Lexical

- H2896 טוֹב (_towb_): "good (as an adjective) in the widest sense; used likewise as a noun, both in the masculine and the feminine, the singular and the plural (good, a good or good thing, a good man or woman; the good, goods or good things, good men or women), also as an adverb (well)". KJV: "beautiful, best, better, bountiful, cheerful, at ease, [idiom] fair (word), (be in) favour, fine, glad, good (deed, -lier, -liest, -ly, -ness, -s), graciously, joyful, kindly, kindness, liketh (best), loving, merry, [idiom] most, pleasant, [phrase] pleaseth, pleasure, precious, prosperity, ready, sweet, wealth, welfare, (be) well(-favoured)."
- Note what _towb_ covers. It is not only "morally correct". It includes beautiful, pleasant, precious, and ready. Inference: the evaluation is not only an ethical audit. It is also a judgment of beauty and of fitness.

#### Observations

1. Text states (structure): The evaluation is inside the making. It is not a phase after the six days. It occurs again and again inside them.
2. Text states: The evaluator is the maker. It is not a customer, a committee, or a later consumer. The one who made the thing judges it.
3. Text states: The judgment is against an intention, not against a benchmark. "That it was good" means good for what it was for. God made the lights for signs, seasons, days, and years (v. 14), and the judgment relates to that.
4. Text states: The judgment repeats. The work of each day gets its own assessment. The maker does not save judgment for the end.
5. Text states: The judgment is stated and recorded. It is not an unspoken impression.

#### Derivation

Inference: The evaluation is inside the work, repeated, and done by the maker against an intention. So judgment is part of production, and taste is a deliverable.

The strongest form of the derivation is this. The person who builds without assessing is not building. That person accumulates. Accumulation and production differ in one thing: whether anyone judged anything. A pile of work is not a product until someone says "that is good" and means something by it.

#### Business consequences

- Build the assessment into the act. If review happens only at the end, the maker builds blind for the whole time.
- Taste is a hiring criterion and a skill that you can train. _Towb_ includes beautiful and pleasant. So the evaluation includes whether the thing is well made, not only whether it functions. An organization that can judge only function will ship things that work and that people hate.
- "Who judges, and against what intention?" is a required question (`SKILL.md` step 9). Without a stated intention, no one can answer "good".
- Self-assessment does not remove outside assessment. Here the maker judges, but the customer also judges later, and the two can disagree. The axiom says that judgment belongs inside the process. It does not say that the judgment of the maker is final.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is accumulation without assessment. Work ships because it is finished, not because it is good. The bill comes as a portfolio of features that nobody will defend one by one. It also comes as a team that has no words to say that a thing is badly made.

#### Extending verses

- 1 Timothy 4:4: "For every creature of God is good, and nothing to be refused, if it be received with thanksgiving:"
- 1 Corinthians 11:28: "But let a man examine himself..." Examination comes before participation.
- 2 Corinthians 13:5: "Examine yourselves, whether ye be in the faith; prove your own selves."
- 1 Corinthians 9:24-27: the runner, the wrestler, and the discipline of self-assessment. "But I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection: lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway."

### A10. Whole-system review is not the sum of part-reviews

Grounding text, Genesis 1:31:

> 31 And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

#### Observations

1. Text states (the asymmetry): God calls the parts good. He calls only the whole very good. The text uses the intensifier exactly once, and it uses it for the assembly.
2. Text states: The object of the final evaluation is "every thing that he had made". It is the complete set, not a sample and not a sum.
3. Text states: The final evaluation comes after God makes the last item. It is not mixed in with the work. It is a separate act at the end of the sequence.
4. Text states (the counting formula): The text counts the sixth day after God evaluates the whole. The work is complete and evaluated before the day closes.
5. Text states: Nothing in the chapter tells us how the parts combine. The chapter shows parts judged good, then the whole judged very good. It does not claim that the second follows from the first.

#### Derivation

Inference: The whole receives an evaluation that no part received. So parts that are each correct can combine into a failure of the whole. Seven good parts can make one bad product.

Observation 5 carries the weight. The text does not say that the whole is very good because the parts were good. It says that the maker looked at everything and gave a different verdict. That leaves open the possibility that good parts combine badly. The separate review of the whole is evidence that the maker did not assume otherwise.

#### Business consequences

- Review the assembly, not only the components. Examples are end-to-end testing, an integrated P&L (profit and loss statement), review at the level of the portfolio, and the whole customer journey.
- "Every part passed" is not a criterion to ship. Each component can meet its spec while the product is unusable. The API is fast, the UI is clear, and the two disagree about what a customer is.
- Someone must own the whole. By their position, component owners will not notice failures of the combination. A failure of the combination is invisible from inside a component.
- It justifies the expensive review. A review of the whole system costs more for each unit of coverage than a review of parts. The axiom says that it is not redundant, and that argument pays for it.

#### Failure mode when violated

The anti-pattern table gives the shape: a portfolio of things that are each correct and that no one can use. The bill comes as integration cost that the team finds late. It also comes as a specific frustration for executives: every team reports green, and the product does not work.

#### Extending verses

- 1 Corinthians 12:12-27: the body. "If the whole body were an eye, where were the hearing?" and "the eye cannot say unto the hand, I have no need of thee". Parts that each function do not form a working body by themselves.
- Ephesians 4:16: "From whom the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth, according to the effectual working in the measure of every part, maketh increase of the body..." Note "fitly joined together". The joining is separate from the function of each part.
- James 2:10: "For whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, he is guilty of all." The whole is not the sum.
- 1 Corinthians 3:13: "Every man's work shall be made manifest: for the day shall declare it, because it shall be revealed by fire; and the fire shall try every man's work of what sort it is." Note "of what sort it is". It is a judgment of the whole thing.

### A11. Multiplication is commanded, and it reproduces after its kind

Grounding text, Genesis 1:28:

> 28 And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

Text states (the "after his kind" formula, counted by search): The phrase "after his kind" occurs eight times in Genesis 1. It occurs three times in 1:11-12 (the herb and the fruit tree), once in 1:21 (the fowl), and four times in 1:24-25 (the living creature, the beast, and the creeping thing). The variant "after their kind" occurs two more times, in 1:21 (the sea creatures) and 1:25 (the cattle). The kind formula occurs ten times in all.

#### Lexical

- H4390 מָלֵא (_male_): "to fill or (intransitively) be full of, in a wide application (literally and figuratively)." The KJV renders it "replenish."
- H3533 כָּבַשׁ (_kabash_): "to tread down; hence, negatively, to disregard; positively, to conquer, subjugate, violate." KJV: "bring into bondage, force, keep under, subdue, bring into subjection."
- H7287 רָדָה (_radah_): "to tread down, i.e. subjugate; specifically, to crumble off."

#### Observations

1. Text states: God commands multiplication. It is in the imperative: "Be fruitful, and multiply."
2. Text states: A blessing comes with the command: "And God blessed them, and God said unto them..." God gives the command and the blessing together.
3. Text states (the kind formula): What is planted decides what comes up: "whose seed is in itself... after his kind". The seed carries the species.
4. Text states: The mechanism is internal. The seed is "in itself". Reproduction is a property of the thing that reproduces, not a process applied to it from outside.
5. Text states: The command to multiply is given twice: to the sea creatures and the fowl (1:22) and to man (1:28). In 1:11 God speaks to the earth ("Let the earth bring forth"), and he gives no command to the plants. But the plants carry seed "in itself" (1:11-12). Inference: reproduction is a property of created life in general, not a special instruction to humanity only.

#### Derivation

Inference: Two conclusions follow, and they are separate.

First, growth is part of the design. Genesis 1:28 speaks to humans, not to businesses. Inference, by analogy: a business that never reproduces lacks something that the design of living things includes. The command is not "grow if conditions permit." It is "multiply."

Second, what you plant is what you get. The seed is "in itself", and the yield is "after his kind". So culture, standards, and character replicate in hires and subsidiaries with high fidelity. You cannot scale a thing that you do not want more of.

The second conclusion matters most in operation. It says that growth is not neutral about quality. When you scale a bad process, you get a bad process at scale, copied faithfully. The "after his kind" formula is the reason.

#### Business consequences

- Inference, by analogy: growth belongs to the design. It is not an extra. This does not mean growth at any cost or growth in any direction, because A6 and A20 limit what grows. It means that a business with no way to reproduce is incomplete in its design, not only unambitious.
- Audit what replicates before you replicate it. Culture, hiring bar, code quality, customer expectations, and pricing discipline are seeds.
- Franchises, subsidiaries, and second products are tests of fidelity. Whatever the parent does, the copy will reproduce. That includes the things the parent does not want reproduced.
- The seed is internal. You cannot outsource the property of reproduction. A growth strategy that depends on an outside agent to reproduce is not the same as a business that reproduces.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is culture rot, copied faithfully at scale. This is the entry in the anti-pattern table. The bill comes as a company that grew and became a worse version of itself. The flaws are exactly the ones that its founders recognized at ten people and did not act on.

The inverse failure also deserves a name: a refusal to multiply because the thing is not yet perfect. The text commands the multiplication of beings that must still subdue the earth (v. 28) and that will later fall. Perfection is not the precondition.

#### Extending verses

- Genesis 8:22: "While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease."
- Galatians 6:7-8: "Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting."
- Hosea 8:7: "For they have sown the wind, and they shall reap the whirlwind..."
- Matthew 7:16-20: "Ye shall know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles?" This is the kind formula applied to people.
- 2 Timothy 2:2: "And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also." The sentence holds four generations of transmission.
- Proverbs 13:11: "Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished: but he that gathereth by labour shall increase."

### A12. Dominion is delegated and bounded

After A5, this axiom and A17 carry the most weight in this group. The lexical work matters more here than anywhere else in the file. The KJV English "dominion" says less than the Hebrew, and the temptation is to overcorrect.

Grounding text, Genesis 1:26 and 1:28:

> 26 And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.
>
> 28 ...and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

#### Lexical (word for word from Strong's)

- H7287 רָדָה (_radah_): "to tread down, i.e. subjugate; specifically, to crumble off." From a primitive root. KJV renderings: "(come to, make to) have dominion, prevail against, reign, (bear, make to) rule,(-r, over), take."
- H3533 כָּבַשׁ (_kabash_): "to tread down; hence, negatively, to disregard; positively, to conquer, subjugate, violate." KJV renderings: "bring into bondage, force, keep under, subdue, bring into subjection."
- H6754 צֶלֶם (_tselem_): "a phantom, i.e. (figuratively) illusion, resemblance; hence, a representative figure, especially an idol."
- H1823 דְּמוּת (_demuth_): "resemblance; concretely, model, shape; adverbially, like."

#### What the lexicon establishes, and what it does not

The lexicon establishes that both verbs are stronger than "custodianship." _Radah_ is "to tread down, i.e. subjugate". _Kabash_ is "to conquer, subjugate, violate." A reading that makes the grant gentle gardening must explain why the text chose these verbs.

The lexicon does not establish that Genesis 1 commands aggressive extraction of resources. The warning in `scripture-foundations` applies. "The Hebrew is stronger than 'dominion' suggests" is a fair claim with the dictionary open. "The Hebrew proves that Genesis 1 commands aggressive resource extraction" is not a fair claim. Two limits hold the overcorrection in check, and both are in the same three verses:

1. Text states: The grant is given. "Let them have dominion" is a permission, not a seizure. However strong the verb is, the authority is received.
2. Text states: The grant is bounded. The verse lists its objects: fish of the sea, fowl of the air, cattle, all the earth, every creeping thing. And it sits under the frame of the image of God, stated one verse later (1:27, "So God created man in his own image"). Authority used as the image of God is authority used under a description.

So the verbs are stronger than the English, and the scope is narrower than the verbs alone suggest. You need both halves.

#### Observations

1. Text states (structure of the grant): The grant has three elements, and each one is present:
   - (a) An agent: the man, plural "them".
   - (b) A domain: listed item by item.
   - (c) A grantor: "Let them have".
2. Text states: The grant is to "them", in the plural. It is not an individual possession.
3. Text states: The grant covers living things and the earth, not the sky or the heavenly bodies. The lights (v. 14) are not in the grant. The text draws the boundary.
4. Text states: God gives the grant before the fall. It has no curse in it. But the grant is not soft. The KJV renderings of _kabash_ include "force" and "bring into bondage". Inference: the subduing has real force in it, and the boundary and the image of God limit that force. The text does not describe a grant without force.
5. Text states: The image of God is the ground of the grant. Verse 26 gives the reason ("Let us make man in our image... and let them have dominion"), and verse 27 states the image again right after. The authority comes from the resemblance.

#### Derivation

Inference: Two things follow, and they are in tension by design.

First, authority is real. It is not a fiction, a license, or a courtesy. The verbs are strong. Some readings reduce dominion to "stewardship" in the weak sense, which is care without authority. Such a reading does not survive the lexicon.

Second, authority is bounded, and it is delegated. It is real over a domain and under a law. It is granted, not seized. Genesis 1 supplies the list of the domain. Genesis 2:16-17 supplies the law.

Therefore: delegate real authority with real limits, and state both. Authority without limits becomes tyranny. Limits without authority become paralysis. In a growing company, the most expensive misunderstanding is about which of the two the company grants.

#### Business consequences

- Write both halves down. A role description that names only responsibilities and no limits is not a grant. It is a liability. A role description that names only limits is not a grant either.
- The list of the domain matters. A grant is over specific things. "Own the product" is not a domain. "Own the product roadmap for the SMB segment, excluding pricing and packaging" is a domain.
- Authority comes from something, and the text states its source. The ground of the grant is the image of God, a resemblance. In organizational terms, authority rests on what the holder is in relation to the thing governed. That is why a grant of authority to someone who does not understand the domain fails, however clearly the limits are written.
- The grant is plural. "Let them have dominion." Dominion is a shared holding, not a personal one. This connects to A18 (aloneness) and to the material on organization design in `org-and-labor.md`.

#### Failure mode when violated

There are two separate failures, and they are opposites:

- Authority without limits leads to tyranny. The holder does whatever he can. The organization finds the boundaries of the grant by finding where it broke.
- Limits without authority lead to paralysis. The holder escalates everything, because nothing is really his to decide. This failure is more common in growing companies and harder to see, because it looks like diligence.

The anti-pattern table does not list A12 directly. The violation of this axiom shows up as a disease of the org chart, not of a project.

#### Extending verses

- Psalm 8:4-8: "Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet:" The grant is stated again and extended.
- Psalm 115:16: "The heaven, even the heavens, are the LORD's: but the earth hath he given to the children of men." The grant is real, and the sky is not in it.
- Matthew 28:18: "All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth." Authority is again given.
- Romans 13:1: "...there is no power but of God: the powers that be are ordained of God."
- John 19:11: "Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above..." Even hostile authority comes from above.
- Mark 10:42-45: the correction of the pagan model of authority. "whosoever will be great among you, shall be your minister."

### A13. Provision precedes assignment

Grounding text, Genesis 1:29:

> 29 And God said, Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat.

Text states (sequence): God gives the provision in verse 29. That is after the command to multiply and subdue in verse 28, and before the work of tilling in Genesis 2:15. Inside chapter 1, the grant of food comes before the close of the sixth day.

#### Observations

1. Text states: The verb is "give": "Behold, I have given you." It is not "you can buy" or "you can earn". It is given.
2. Text states: The provision is listed and specific: every herb bearing seed, and every tree with fruit that yields seed. The grant is not "whatever you can find."
3. Text states: The provision covers the animals too (v. 30): "And to every beast of the earth... I have given every green herb for meat".
4. Text states: God gives the provision before the man does any recorded work. The first work in the account is in Genesis 2:15, and it comes after the food.
5. Text states (Genesis 2:7): God forms the man from dust and gives him breath. That comes before he puts him in the garden (2:15) and before the command about the trees (2:16-17). The order is provision, then placement, then assignment.

#### Derivation

Inference: The resources are granted before the work is required of the worker. So fund the work before you operate. Fund the work before you demand from it.

A model that requires output before inputs is not disciplined. It is starving. The difference matters. "Disciplined" implies that the inputs exist and that someone holds them back on purpose, for a good reason. The text gives no example of withheld inputs as a technique.

Paul applies the principle to wages directly, and he does it with a quote from the law.

Deuteronomy 25:4:

> 4 Thou shalt not muzzle the ox when he treadeth out the corn.

1 Corinthians 9:9-10:

> 9 For it is written in the law of Moses, Thou shalt not muzzle the mouth of the ox that treadeth out the corn. Doth God take care for oxen?
>
> 10 Or saith he it altogether for our sakes? For our sakes, no doubt, this is written: that he that ploweth should plow in hope; and that he that thresheth in hope should be partaker of his hope.

1 Timothy 5:18:

> 18 For the scripture saith, Thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn. And, The labourer is worthy of his reward.

Text states: Paul reads the law of the ox as written "altogether for our sakes". He applies it to the access of the worker to the fruit of the work during the work. The ox eats while it treads. That is the sharpest form of the axiom.

#### Business consequences

- Working capital is a design input, not a financing afterthought. A plan that needs revenue before it can pay for its inputs assumes the axiom away.
- The ox eats while it treads. The law sets a pattern of payment during the work, not only at the end. The text does not forbid commission-only, equity-only, or deferred compensation structures. But these structures are in tension with it, and they need a justification.
- Founders are not exempt. The most common violation of A13 is self-inflicted: a founder works for nothing for two years and calls it discipline. The text gives provision first to the one who works.
- Provision includes the means, not only the output. "Every herb bearing seed": the provision includes the seed, that is, the capacity to reproduce. Capital that you cannot reinvest is not provision. It is consumption.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is starvation, and specifically the starvation of the best asset. The anti-pattern table does not list A13. But the mechanism appears under A17 and A27: underfunded maintenance, and "every plan must price the thorns." The same reasoning applies. A plan that cannot fund its own inputs is not a hard plan. It is a broken one.

#### Extending verses

- Matthew 6:33: "But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you." Provision follows the priority, but it is promised.
- Philippians 4:19: "But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus."
- Psalm 37:25: "I have been young, and now am old; yet have I not seen the righteous forsaken, nor his seed begging bread."
- 1 Kings 17:4-6: "...I have commanded the ravens to feed thee there." Provision comes before the assignment and goes with it.
- Proverbs 3:9-10: honor with the firstfruits. "So shall thy barns be filled with plenty..."

### A14. Completion is a real state

Grounding text, Genesis 2:1-2:

> 1 Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them.
>
> 2 And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.

#### Lexical

- H3615 כָּלָה (_kalah_): "to end, whether intransitive (to cease, be finished, perish) or transitived (to complete, prepare, consume)". The KJV "finished" (2:1) and "ended" (2:2) both translate this one verb.

#### Observations

1. Text states: The work has a finish. The KJV says "finished" in 2:1 and "ended" in 2:2. Lexical: both English words translate one Hebrew verb, _kalah_ (H3615). The text states the end of the work twice, in two consecutive verses.
2. Text states: The finish is declared. The reader does not infer it from a lack of further activity. The text states it.
3. Text states: The finish is total: "all the host of them." Nothing is left half made.
4. Text states: The work stops and rest begins. Rest is possible only because the work ended. The seventh day is a different kind of day because the six are over.
5. Text states: The KJV uses the same kind of words at the end of the tabernacle ("Thus was all the work of the tabernacle... finished", Exodus 39:32, and "So Moses finished the work", Exodus 40:33). It does the same at the end of the temple ("So was ended all the work", 1 Kings 7:51). Inference: the end of a work is a formula, not an accident of the story.
6. Inference (the New Testament echo): At the end of his work Jesus says "It is finished" (John 19:30). Lexical: the Greek verb there is _teleo_ (G5055), "to end, i.e. complete, execute, conclude, discharge (a debt)". It is not the same word as the Hebrew _kalah_. The two words share a meaning, to end or complete, but not a form. Revelation 21:6 also says "It is done."

#### Derivation

Inference: Completion is a declared state with a boundary on both sides, work on one side and rest on the other. So you must define "done" before you start, and you must mean it.

Scope creep is not ambition. It is a refusal to accept limits that were designed. You cannot evaluate (A9, A10), ship, celebrate, or build on a thing that is never finished. The axiom is not about discipline in the abstract. It says that evaluation and rest both depend on an end point.

#### Business consequences

- Define "done" before you start, and mean it. `SKILL.md` step 8 asks: what condition that you can observe means done, and who declares it? A "done" that no one has the authority to declare is not a definition.
- A stopping rule is part of the specification. The anti-pattern table pairs A8 and A14 for "A plan with no stopping rule". The result is endless projects, no learning, and no exit. The mechanism: with no finish, the evaluation never becomes binding, so no learning happens.
- Rest requires completion. A15 depends on A14. You cannot rest from an unfinished thing. You can only stop, and that is different.
- To ship is an act of closure, not an admission that the thing is incomplete. The text declares the work finished, and the story still continues: a fall in chapter 3, then a flood, then a tabernacle. Finished means that the designed scope is complete.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is endless projects, no learning, and no exit. The secondary failure does more damage. An organization that never declares anything finished never experiences the closure that makes the next thing possible. Its people stop believing that anything will ever be done.

#### Extending verses

- John 19:30: "When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost."
- Revelation 21:6: "And he said unto me, It is done. I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end..."
- Ecclesiastes 3:1: "To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven:" Purposes have times, so they have ends.
- Genesis 1:31: the sixth day closes after God evaluates the whole. Evaluation and completion sit next to each other in the sequence.
- Hebrews 4:3: "For we which have believed do enter into rest, as he said, As I have sworn in my wrath, if they shall enter into my rest: although the works were finished from the foundation of the world."

## Group III: The rhythm

Genesis 2:1-3 and 2:15-25. This group has seven axioms on the pattern of work: rest, work, maintenance, partnership, discovery, boundary, and commitment.

### A15. Work is bounded; rest is structural

Grounding text, Genesis 2:2-3:

> 2 And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.
>
> 3 And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made.

#### Lexical

- H7676 שַׁבָּת (_shabbath_): "intermission, i.e (specifically) the Sabbath." It is an intensive form from H7673 שָׁבַת (_shabath_). The root sense is cessation, not refreshment. What does not happen on the day defines it.
- H7673 שָׁבַת (_shabath_): the root. "to repose, i.e. desist from exertion".

#### Observations

1. Text states (ratio): There are six days of work and one day of cessation. The ratio is given, not earned. Nothing in the text makes the seventh day depend on a completed quota.
2. Text states: God blesses and sanctifies the day. It is not only permitted. It is set apart. Rest is an institution. God sets it apart on the seventh day. That is after he makes man on the sixth day (1:27), and before the text records any human work (2:15).
3. Text states (checked by search): The fourth commandment states the pattern again, on the same ground. Exodus 20:11: "For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it."
4. Text states: The command extends the rest to other people and to animals. Exodus 20:10 names "thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates". Rest is not a personal privilege. It is a structural provision for everyone in the system.
5. Text states: The rest is from work, and the work was finished (A14). The same verse pairs the two.

#### Derivation

Inference: The ratio is given, not earned. So capacity planning must include rest, or the plan is wrong. A plan that works only at full throttle forever is not a plan.

The derivation has a sharp edge. Rest is not a reward for good performance. It does not depend on anything. So it belongs in the plan as a fixed input, like the duty cycle of a machine in a manufacturing plan. It is not a variable to squeeze when the schedule slips.

Scripture extends the same logic past the week to the year and to the half-century.

Exodus 23:10-12:

> 10 And six years thou shalt sow thy land, and shalt gather in the fruits thereof:
>
> 11 But the seventh year thou shalt let it rest and lie still; that the poor of thy people may eat: and what they leave the beasts of the field shall eat. In like manner thou shalt deal with thy vineyard, and with thy oliveyard.
>
> 12 Six days thou shalt do thy work, and on the seventh day thou shalt rest: that thine ox and thine ass may rest, and the son of thy handmaid, and the stranger, may be refreshed.

Leviticus 25 gives the sabbath of the land and the jubilee. See `capital-and-stewardship.md` for the capital consequences of the reset every fifty years.

#### Business consequences

- Burnout is a design violation, not a character flaw. This new view is the main contribution of the axiom. If rest is structural, a person who breaks under a full-throttle plan is evidence that the plan was wrong. It is not evidence that the person was weak. If you treat it as a character flaw, you guarantee that the same plan runs again on the next person.
- The provision of rest is for the whole system, not only the principal. Exodus 20:10 names the servants, the animals, and the foreigner who lives there. In business terms, these are contractors, on-call staff, and the people least able to refuse.
- The fallow year is an instrument of capacity. An idle quarter is not always waste. It can be the seventh year. The question is whether you planned it or it happened by accident.
- Use a duty cycle, not heroism. A team that can hit a date only by working every weekend has a plan with a duty cycle above 100%. That is not a plan.
- Jubilee limits accumulation. Leviticus 25 releases land in the jubilee (25:10-13, 23-28) and releases servants (25:39-41). That is a structural limit on compounding advantage. The release of debts is a different law, the seventh-year release in Deuteronomy 15:1-2. `capital-and-stewardship.md` discusses both at length.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is burnout, attrition, and a cliff. That is the entry in the anti-pattern table for "full-throttle plans with no rest." The cliff is the important part. Inference: attrition often looks gradual, but the loss of the people who hold the plan together is sudden. The bill comes as a sudden collapse that you cannot recover from, not as a slow decline.

#### Extending verses

- Exodus 20:8-11: the fourth commandment, grounded in the creation week.
- Exodus 23:10-12: the sabbath year and the sabbath day, "that thine ox and thine ass may rest, and the son of thy handmaid, and the stranger, may be refreshed."
- Leviticus 25:4: "But in the seventh year shall be a sabbath of rest unto the land, a sabbath for the LORD: thou shalt neither sow thy field, nor prune thy vineyard."
- Leviticus 25:20-22 answers the question of provision: "And if ye shall say, What shall we eat the seventh year?... Then I will command my blessing upon you in the sixth year, and it shall bring forth fruit for three years."
- Mark 6:31: "And he said unto them, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat." Jesus commands the rest in the middle of demand, not after it.
- Ecclesiastes 10:10: "If the iron be blunt, and he do not whet the edge, then must he put to more strength: but wisdom is profitable to direct." Note the structure. The blunt tool costs more strength for the same output, and the correction is a matter of direction, not effort. Time at the whetstone is unproductive time that makes production possible.

### A16. Work precedes the fall

Grounding text, Genesis 2:15:

> 15 And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.

Text states (sequence): This verse is in Genesis 2. The curse on the ground is in Genesis 3:17-19. God gives the work assignment before the fall, in the garden, as part of the design. The curse changes the conditions of work. It does not introduce work.

#### Lexical

- H3240 יָנַח (_yanach_): "to deposit; by implication, to allow to stay." The KJV renders it "put." KJV: "bestow, cast down, lay (down, up), leave (off), let alone (remain), pacify, place, put, set (down), suffer, withdraw, withhold."
- H5647 עָבַד (_abad_): see A17.
- H8104 שָׁמַר (_shamar_): see A17.
- H127 אֲדָמָה (_adamah_): "soil (from its general redness)." Note the wordplay. _Adam_ (man) is taken from the _adamah_ (ground). The man and the material that he works share a name.

#### Observations

1. Text states: God assigns work in the garden, before any curse. The garden is not a resort. It is a workplace with a job description.
2. Text states: The verb of placement means to deposit. God deposits the man, as something placed in the care of a location. Inference: the placement starts a relationship of custody. It is not only a change of address.
3. Text states: The job comes before the prohibition (2:15 before 2:16-17). Work comes before the boundary.
4. Text states: The text describes the garden as abundant. It has "every tree that is pleasant to the sight, and good for food" (2:9), and a river waters it (2:10). The river parts into four heads. The first one goes around "the whole land of Havilah, where there is gold" (2:11-12). The gold is in Havilah, outside the garden, not among the resources of the garden. God assigns work in a place that already has what the man needs. Inference: in the design, work is not mainly a way to survive. It is what a person does who already has provision.
5. Text states: The curse changes the ground ("cursed is the ground for thy sake," 3:17) and the yield ("in sorrow shalt thou eat of it," 3:17). It adds thorns (3:18) and sweat (3:19). It does not add the job.

#### Derivation

Inference: God assigns work before the fall. So work is not the punishment, and it is not only the price of survival. It is where meaning is produced.

The derivation rests on observation 5. The curse changes the conditions, not the existence of the task. If work were the curse, the curse must have introduced it. It did not.

This consequence works against two opposite errors:

- Against the error that work is a curse to escape: a business that treats work as something to minimize for everyone misreads the design.
- Against the error that work is the whole of a person: the same account that assigns the work also gives the sabbath (2:2-3) and the counterpart (2:18-24). Work is one of three.

#### Business consequences

- A business that only pays, that makes nothing anyone can love, will hold people with money and lose them anyway. This is the central business claim of the axiom. If work is where meaning is produced, a job that produces no meaning competes on price alone. That position loses against anyone who offers both.
- Craft is not a luxury. The text describes the garden in terms of beauty (2:9, "pleasant to the sight") and of function ("good for food"). It names both.
- The work must be worth doing, not only paid for. This is the case for caring what the product is, not only what it earns.
- Provision is not the absence of work. Observation 4 matters here. God gives the man an abundant place and a job. The two are not alternatives.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is an organization that reduced work to a transaction. The bill comes as a workforce that leaves for a ten-percent raise. It also comes as an inability to attract anyone who cares about the craft, because nothing in the offer is about craft.

The inverse failure also deserves a name: to treat work as the whole of meaning, so that rest (A15) and partnership (A18) become interruptions.

#### Extending verses

- Ecclesiastes 2:24: "There is nothing better for a man, than that he should eat and drink, and that he should make his soul enjoy good in his labour. This also I saw, that it was from the hand of God."
- Ecclesiastes 3:22: "Wherefore I perceive that there is nothing better, than that a man should rejoice in his own works; for that is his portion..."
- Ecclesiastes 9:10: "Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might; for there is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave, whither thou goest."
- John 5:17: "But Jesus answered them, My Father worketh hitherto, and I work."
- Colossians 3:23: "And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men;"
- Genesis 2:5: "...for the LORD God had not caused it to rain upon the earth, and there was not a man to till the ground." The text presents the lack of a man to work as a lack in the state before the fall. It is parallel to the lack of rain.

### A17. Building and keeping are equal partners

This is one of the two axioms in this group that carry the most operational weight. It rests on two separate Hebrew verbs, which the KJV translates with two ordinary English words.

Grounding text, Genesis 2:15:

> 15 And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.

#### Lexical (word for word from Strong's)

- H5647 עָבַד (_abad_): "to work (in any sense); by implication, to serve, till, (causatively) enslave, etc." From a primitive root. The KJV renderings include: "[idiom] be, keep in bondage, be bondmen, bond-service, compel, do, dress, ear, execute, [phrase] husbandman, keep, labour(-ing man, bring to pass, (cause to, make to) serve(-ing, self), (be, become) servant(-s), do (use) service, till(-er), transgress (from margin), (set a) work, be wrought, worshipper".
  - Note the KJV list. "Dress" and "worshipper" are renderings of the same root. The word for tending a garden is the word for serving God.
  - Note also that the same root gives H5650 עֶבֶד (_ebed_), "a servant". The verb of the gardener and the noun for the servant come from the same root.
- H8104 שָׁמַר (_shamar_): "properly, to hedge about (as with thorns), i.e. guard; generally, to protect, attend to, etc." From a primitive root. KJV renderings: "beward, be circumspect, take heed (to self), keep(-er, self), mark, look narrowly, observe, preserve, regard, reserve, save (self), sure, (that lay) wait (for), watch(-man)."
  - Note: the definition itself uses thorns as the image, "to hedge about (as with thorns)". A hedge is a protective boundary. Speculation: the thorns of the curse in Genesis 3:18 are a different Hebrew word, H6975 קוֹץ (_qots_), "a thorn". So the link between the thorns of keeping and the thorns of the curse is only an English image. It is not a link in the Hebrew. Do not build on it.
  - Note also that the KJV renderings include "watch(-man)". Keeping is a post, not only an activity.

#### Observations

1. Text states: The job has two verbs and two duties, given together. It is not one verb with two aspects. It is two separate words, joined by "and".
2. Text states: God gives both duties to the same person, at the same moment, as the same job. The sentence has no ranking.
3. Text states: The first verb (_abad_) is productive. It works, tills, serves, and brings forth. The second verb (_shamar_) is protective. It hedges, guards, watches, and preserves.
4. Text states (checked by search): _Shamar_ is the verb for the cherubim that guard the way to the tree of life. Genesis 3:24 says "...to keep the way of the tree of life." It is the same word. The verb for the second duty of the gardener describes the guard at the gate after the fall.
5. Text states: Nothing in the verse or in the story around it treats _shamar_ as the lesser duty. God does not tell the man to dress the garden and, as a side matter, also not to lose it.
6. Text states (Proverbs, the failure case): Proverbs 24:30-34 gives the result when someone neglects the second verb, in the exact vocabulary of a farm.

#### Derivation

Inference: God gives the two verbs together as the same job. So creation and maintenance are equal. Maintenance is not junior work.

The derivation rests on observation 2. God gives the two duties in one sentence, to one person, at one time. Any ranking must come from outside the verse. The verse does not contain one.

Scripture states the failure pattern elsewhere in the same shape.

Proverbs 24:30-34:

> 30 I went by the field of the slothful, and by the vineyard of the man void of understanding;
>
> 31 And, lo, it was all grown over with thorns, and nettles had covered the face thereof, and the stone wall thereof was broken down.
>
> 32 Then I saw, and considered it well: I looked upon it, and received instruction.
>
> 33 Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep:
>
> 34 So shall thy poverty come as one that travelleth; and thy want as an armed man.

Text states: The field is still a field. The vineyard is still a vineyard. What failed is the _shamar_: the wall is broken down, and the thorns took the ground. Note that the diagnosis is not total idleness. It is "a little sleep, a little slumber." The failure grows by small steps, and each step is small.

Nehemiah gives the positive image.

Nehemiah 4:17:

> 17 They which builded on the wall, and they that bare burdens, with those that laded, every one with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other hand held a weapon.

Text states: The people build and defend at once. They do not build first and defend later. The same people do both at the same time, and the verse names the two hands separately.

#### Business consequences

- Most businesses die of a failure to keep while they congratulate themselves on how they dress. This is the main claim of the axiom, and it is unflattering on purpose. The wall falls down while the new wing goes up.
- Give "keep" named owners, calendar time, and equal status. The three come as a package. An owner with no calendar time is a name on a list. Calendar time with no owner is hours that nobody uses. Without equal status, either one gets traded away in the first conflict over priorities.
- Maintenance is invisible, so every incentive system underfunds it. This is the friction, and a name for it does not fix it. Someone must hold the line. That means that someone must be accountable for a thing whose success looks like nothing happening.
- The failure grows by small steps. "A little sleep, a little slumber" in Proverbs 24 is the mechanism. No company decides at one moment to stop maintaining something. A series of quarters passes in which nobody got to it.
- Build and defend is a staffing model, not a metaphor. Nehemiah 4:16 splits his servants in half: "the half of my servants wrought in the work, and the other half of them held both the spears". Two functions get two allocations, and both are real.

#### Failure mode when violated

The anti-pattern table states it directly. Shipping with no owner for maintenance leads to rot, and then to a rewrite.

The table in `SKILL.md` also gives a rewrite cost of 5-10 times. That number is an unsourced engineering estimate. It is not a number from Scripture and not a measured fact. The text gives only the shape: the field goes back to weeds, and the wall must be rebuilt, not repaired.

The secondary failure: an organization that cannot tell building from keeping will call maintenance "not real work". Then nobody gives the people who do it credit. Then nobody who has a choice keeps doing it.

#### Extending verses

- Nehemiah 4:17-18: the image of the two hands.
- Proverbs 24:30-34: the neglected field and wall.
- Genesis 3:24: _shamar_ at the gate, after the fall. "...to keep the way of the tree of life."
- Song of Solomon 2:15: "Take us the foxes, the little foxes, that spoil the vines: for our vines have tender grapes." These are small, specific, continuous threats to a growing thing.
- Matthew 24:43: "But know this, that if the goodman of the house had known in what watch the thief would come, he would have watched, and would not have suffered his house to be broken up." The failure is a failure to watch, not a failure to build.
- 1 Chronicles 9:19: "And Shallum the son of Kore, the son of Ebiasaph, the son of Korah, and his brethren, of the house of his father, the Korahites, were over the work of the service, keepers of the gates of the tabernacle: and their fathers, being over the host of the LORD, were keepers of the entry." The guard of access is an inherited office with a named family line.
- 1 Corinthians 3:10-15: build with care, because the work will be tested. "But let every man take heed how he buildeth thereupon."

### A18. Aloneness is not good

Grounding text, Genesis 2:18:

> 18 And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him.

#### Lexical (word for word from Strong's)

- H5828 עֵזֶר (_ezer_): "aid". From H5826 עָזַר (_azar_), "to surround, i.e. protect or aid". The KJV rendering of H5828 is "help". It is a singular noun.
- H5048 נֶגֶד (_neged_): "a front, i.e. part opposite; specifically a counterpart, or mate; usually (adverbial, especially with preposition) over against or before". KJV renderings: "about, (over) against, [idiom] aloof, [idiom] far (off), [idiom] from, over, presence, [idiom] other side, sight, [idiom] to view."
- The KJV phrase "an help meet for him" renders _ezer kenegdo_: an aid who is a counterpart, literally one "over against" him.

Text states (the negative space): This is the first "not good" in the Bible. Genesis 1 calls things good again and again and ends with "very good". After that chapter, the first lack that God names is isolation.

#### Observations

1. Text states: The judgment is "not good". It is not "unfortunate" or "less than ideal". It is the same word of evaluation as in chapter 1, with a negation.
2. Text states: The problem is the aloneness of the man. God states it before the man notices it. God says it and then acts. Only after that does Adam find the gap (2:20, "but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him"). God declared the need before Adam felt it.
3. Text states: The remedy is not a subordinate. The KJV "help meet" is _ezer kenegdo_, an aid who is a counterpart, one set over against him. Lexical: the dictionary gloss for _neged_ includes "a counterpart, or mate".
4. Text states (the sequence): God declares the lack (v. 18) before the naming of the animals (vv. 19-20), and the provision (vv. 21-22) comes after. So the man works, finds the gap, and then receives the counterpart.
5. Lexical (the use of _ezer_, stated with its limits): Strong's defines H5828 as "aid". The word is also part of names that refer to God: Eliezer ("God of help", H410 + H5828), Azriel ("help of God", H5828 + H410), and Ebenezer ("stone of the help", H68 + H5828). Exodus 18:4 explains the name Eliezer directly: "And the name of the other was Eliezer; for the God of my father, said he, was mine help, and delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh".
   - What this establishes: _ezer_ is not a word for a subordinate assistant. Names use it for the help that God gives, and the Psalms use it where God is the help of Israel.
   - What this does not establish: the supplied tools have no Strong's tags at the level of each verse. So I was not able to check word by word that Psalm 121:2, Psalm 124:8, or Deuteronomy 33:29 use H5828. The KJV English of those verses is: "My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth" (Psalm 121:2), "Our help is in the name of the LORD, who made heaven and earth" (Psalm 124:8), and "the shield of thy help" (Deuteronomy 33:29). The evidence from the names is checked. The concordance claim is not. Treat the concordance claim as strongly supported but not checked here.

#### Derivation

Inference: The earliest lack that God names in the design is a person without a counterpart. So partnership and counsel are not luxuries for the insecure. They correct a stated defect.

Two separate derivations follow.

First, the lack is structural. Aloneness is not a mood or a preference. The evaluation of the design names it as "not good". So an organization built around one decisive person is built around a stated defect, however capable that person is.

Second, the counterpart is a counterpart, not an assistant. _Ezer kenegdo_ carries "counterpart" and "mate". An assistant takes direction. A counterpart stands opposite and speaks. The choice of words rules out the reading in which the remedy for aloneness is someone who does what you say.

Text states (the support), Proverbs 15:22:

> 22 Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established.

#### Business consequences

- Decisions made alone are a stated defect, not a heroic style. The founders who most need this axiom find it least persuasive, because their results were good so far.
- A counterpart must be able to disagree. If the second person cannot say no, the defect stays. This is the operational test: did this person ever change the mind of the principal on something material?
- God declares the need before anyone feels it. Observation 2 is the practical point. Adam did not ask for help. God named the lack over him. Founders rarely feel that they lack counsel, because the cost of no counsel is invisible until they pay it.
- Discovery comes through work. See A19: the gap became visible to Adam while he named the animals. People often find what partners they need by doing the work, not by planning the organization.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is one point of judgment that nobody inside can correct. The bill comes as a decision that no one was able to stop. Then the organization cannot explain what happened, because nobody ever wrote the reasoning down or said it aloud.

#### Extending verses

- Proverbs 15:22: "Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established."
- Proverbs 20:18: "Every purpose is established by counsel: and with good advice make war."
- Ecclesiastes 4:9-12: "Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up. Again, if two lie together, then they have heat: but how can one be warm alone? And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken."
- Exodus 17:12: "But Moses' hands were heavy; and they took a stone, and put it under him, and he sat thereon; and Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands, the one on the one side, and the other on the other side; and his hands were steady until the going down of the sun." Two people help, one on each side.
- Numbers 11:16-17: the seventy elders. "...and they shall bear the burden of the people with thee, that thou bear it not thyself alone." God states the problem in the same terms as Genesis 2:18.

### A19. Doing the work reveals the need

Grounding text, Genesis 2:19-20:

> 19 And out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field, and every fowl of the air; and brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof.
>
> 20 And Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the air, and to every beast of the field; but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him.

Text states (sequence): God declares the lack (v. 18) before the work. Adam finds the lack (v. 20) after the work. "But for Adam there was not found an help meet for him": the text reports the negative result as a finding.

#### Observations

1. Text states: Adam does a real task, the naming of every living creature, and the task has a real output ("whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof").
2. Text states: God states the gap in 2:18, before the naming. But the naming is where the gap shows up in Adam's own work: "there was not found an help meet for him" (2:20). Inference: the need was known to God from outside the work. The work made it visible to the worker.
3. Text states: God delegates the work ("brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them") and leaves the result to Adam. God states the need in 2:18, but the text does not record that God told Adam what the naming would show.
4. Text states: The text states the discovery as a failure to find: "there was not found". It does not say "Adam realized". The words are words of search and result.
5. Text states: The provision follows at once (vv. 21-22). The discovery makes the provision understandable.

#### Derivation

Inference: The need became visible to the worker only in the work. So execution is an instrument of discovery, not only of delivery.

The derivation is precise. Adam was able to establish the content of the need (a counterpart, not another creature) only by testing all the alternatives. The naming of the animals was the test. The conclusion "not found" required the work.

#### Business consequences

- The real problem is usually next to the one you set out to solve, and it appears only once you are in motion. This is the core claim of the axiom.
- This is the case for prototypes and for shipping a smaller version sooner. A prototype is not a cheaper version of the product. It is the instrument that shows what the product must be. Its value is the discovery, not the artifact.
- Research does not replace the work. Adam did not theorize about whether a counterpart existed. He worked through the set. To read about a market is not the same as to sell into it.
- It gives a new view of "waste". A prototype that you throw away is not waste if it produced the finding. The finding is the deliverable. This is true only if you capture the finding. So you must design the work to produce findings, not only perform it.
- It connects to A5. The discovery often shows that the container was wrong. That is the argument for building a smaller container first.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is analysis paralysis, and its mirror image: shipping something huge without ever testing whether it was the right thing. Both failures come from the same error. They treat the work only as delivery, and never as the instrument that tells you what to deliver.

#### Extending verses

- Proverbs 27:23: "Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds." Knowledge of the state comes from attention to the real flock.
- Luke 14:28-30: the builder of the tower counts first. The failure shows up in the building.
- Acts 16:6-10: "...they assayed to go into Bithynia: but the Spirit suffered them not." They found the direction in the attempt, not in the plan.
- James 4:13-15: "...ye know not what shall be on the morrow." Discovery limits planning.
- Nehemiah 2:14: "...but there was no place for the beast that was under me to pass." This is a finding from the survey, a physical limit found by riding the route.

### A20. Abundance precedes restriction; the boundary is few and stated in advance

Grounding text, Genesis 2:16-17:

> 16 And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat:
>
> 17 But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.

Text states (the shape): A wide yes ("Of every tree... thou mayest freely eat"), then one clear no ("But of the tree... thou shalt not eat of it"). God states the prohibition before the serpent arrives in chapter 3.

#### Observations

1. Text states (proportion): There is one prohibition, in a garden full of trees. The permission is general and strong, "freely eat". The restriction is a single named exception.
2. Text states: The exception is specific. It is one named tree. It is not "some trees" or "the trees that the LORD did not approve".
3. Text states: God states the consequence with the rule: "in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die." Rule and consequence come together.
4. Text states (timing): God gives the rule in chapter 2. The pressure arrives in chapter 3. Text states: The first move of the serpent is to change the wording of the rule: "Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?" (3:1). The attack is on the rule as stated. That is why a precise statement of the rule matters.
5. Daniel shows the pattern. Daniel 1:8: "But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank: therefore he requested of the prince of the eunuchs that he might not defile himself." Text states: in the verse, "purposed in his heart" comes before the request. Inference: Daniel made the decision before the pressure of the meal reached him.

#### Derivation

Inference: The restrictions are few and given in advance. So make generous permission the default, and keep prohibitions few, explicit, and clear. A rule invented after the temptation arrives is not a rule. It is a negotiation.

The last sentence gives the derivation its force. The serpent does not argue about whether the tree is rightly forbidden. He argues about what God said. A rule stated in advance has a fixed text to defend. A rule invented at the moment of pressure has no text. It has only the judgment of the person under pressure, and that is the least reliable instrument available.

#### Business consequences

- Have few rules, stated clearly and stated early. A policy manual with three hundred rules is not a strong boundary. It is a boundary so diluted that nobody can keep it in mind.
- Make generous permission the default. The garden is mostly yes. Organizations that default to no get compliance and lose initiative.
- Attach consequences to rules. Verse 17 states the penalty together with the prohibition. A rule with no stated consequence is a preference.
- The person under pressure must be able to read the rule. If the person who will face the temptation cannot recite the rule, then in any useful sense nobody stated it in advance.
- Decide the ethics before the deal. This is the operational form. It is the reason that `ethics.md` has a part on boundaries set before the pressure (Part 11). Daniel is the model.

#### Failure mode when violated

The anti-pattern table says: growth with no stated boundary leads to scope explosion, and you can no longer say what you are. The ethics failure is sharper. A boundary negotiated under pressure is not a boundary. So the organization finds its real limits by going past them.

#### Extending verses

- Daniel 1:8: he purposed in his heart beforehand.
- Deuteronomy 30:19: "I call heaven and earth to record this day against you, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live:" The options are stated before the choice.
- Joshua 24:15: "...choose you this day whom ye will serve; whether the gods which your fathers served that were on the other side of the flood, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye dwell: but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD." The options are stated, then chosen, then declared before the outcome.
- Psalm 119:105: "Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path." The rule lights the ground ahead. That is the whole point of stating it before you walk.
- Genesis 3:1: the first move of the serpent is against the wording. The attack goes to the precision of the rule, and that is evidence of its value.

### A21. Commitment requires leaving and cleaving

Grounding text, Genesis 2:24:

> 24 Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.

#### Observations

1. Text states (structure): The verse has three elements, in order: leave, cleave, be one flesh. The union is the third step, not the first.
2. Text states: The leaving is specific: "his father and his mother." It is not "his prior life". A named attachment must be left.
3. Text states: The cleaving is a binding, and the word carries the sense of sticking to. Lexical: H1692 דָּבַק (_dabaq_), "properly, to impinge, i.e. cling or adhere". The two are not parallel options. One is an exit, and the other is an attachment.
4. Text states: The verse states the formula as a general principle ("Therefore shall a man..."), right after the specific case of Adam and Eve. Inference: the formula is the pattern for all later unions, not only a description of the first one.
5. Text states: The text describes the union as a new identity: "they shall be one flesh." It is not a partnership between two unchanged parties. It is a new single thing.

#### Derivation

Inference: A new union requires a real exit and a binding attachment. So new ventures fail when founders try to keep every prior option open.

The sequence is the argument. Leave comes before cleave, and cleave comes before one flesh. A venture that never required anyone to leave anything did not reach the first step. So it cannot reach the third.

Second derivation: a covenant structure produces behavior that a contract cannot produce. The text uses "one flesh", which is a claim about identity, not about terms. A contract governs the behavior of two separate parties. A covenant changes what the parties are. Some behavior follows from identity: sacrifice, patience, and taking a cost without a new negotiation. Terms alone cannot produce it.

#### Business consequences

- If you are not willing to leave something, you did not start anything. The test is concrete: what did this commitment cost me in alternatives that I gave up? A founder who still consults two days a week did not leave.
- Name what you leave. The verse names the father and the mother. It does not only point toward them. Some founders describe their exit as "winding down my other things". They did not name what they leave, so nobody can tell whether it happened.
- Covenant structures do better than contracts where commitment is the bottleneck. Founder agreements, equity with real vesting, non-competes with teeth, and public commitments all work on identity, not on terms. The text suggests why they work when contracts alone do not. Note the legal limit. The law on non-competes varies by state. California (Business and Professions Code section 16600), Minnesota, and North Dakota void most non-compete agreements. Get legal advice before you rely on one.
- The union is a new thing, not an alliance. "One flesh" argues against partnership as two independent companies with a shared roadmap, at least for the commitments that must survive pressure.

#### Failure mode when violated

The anti-pattern table says: if you avoid commitment to keep options open, you get no venture, only a permanent cost of holding options.

The cost of holding options is the key insight. To keep options open is not free. It is a continuous payment, and the payment is attention. Inference, a rough estimate and not a measurement: a person with five open options has about one fifth of the attention available for each.

#### Extending verses

- Ruth 1:16: "And Ruth said, Intreat me not to leave thee, or to return from following after thee: for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God:" She states the leaving in plain words, then the cleaving.
- Luke 9:62: "And Jesus said unto him, No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God."
- 2 Timothy 2:4: "No man that warreth entangleth himself with the affairs of this life; that he may please him who hath chosen him to be a soldier."
- 1 Kings 19:19-21: the call of Elisha, and his response. "And he returned back from him, and took a yoke of oxen, and slew them, and boiled their flesh with the instruments of the oxen, and gave unto the people, and they did eat. Then he arose, and went after Elijah, and ministered unto him." He kills the oxen. He does not keep the prior occupation as a fallback.
- Genesis 12:1: "Now the LORD had said unto Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto a land that I will shew thee:" The verse names the leaving in three circles, each smaller than the last.

## Group IV: The fracture and how to operate in it

Genesis 3. This group has thirteen axioms on failure, friction, and work in a broken environment. Note the change of genre. Chapters 1-2 mostly describe what God made. Chapter 3 is a story of what went wrong, with consequences that God declares.

The rule from `scripture-foundations` applies: "Genesis 3 describes the curse. It does not command thorns. It tells you to expect them." Almost every axiom in this group is an expectation, not a command.

### A22. Trust in the stated word is the first target

Grounding text, Genesis 3:1:

> 1 Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made. And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?

#### Observations

1. Text states: The attack opens on what God said, not on anything material. The first recorded question in the Bible is a question about whether a statement is reliable.
2. Text states: The question distorts the statement. God said "Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it" (2:16-17). The version of the serpent is "Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden". It turns the permission into a prohibition.
3. Text states: The distortion is plausible. It is close enough to the original that the woman must correct it. Inference: so the attack succeeded in starting a discussion about the text, not about the tree.
4. Text states: The reply of the woman (3:2-3) adds to the original: "neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die." The rule that God stated did not include touching. Inference: the drift began before the serpent spoke.
5. Text states: Nothing material is taken in verse 1. Nobody eats fruit. The whole first move is an attack on the credibility of the word.

#### Derivation

Inference: The attack opens on whether what was said is reliable. So the integrity of your promises is the asset most worth defending, and it is the easiest to damage.

The second derivation matters most in operation. People attack a reputation with questions, not with facts. So you cannot defend it with facts alone. The serpent did not assert a falsehood. He asked a question. You cannot refute a question. You can only answer it, and an answer admits that the matter was open.

#### Business consequences

- Say what you will do, do what you said, and correct the record plainly when you cannot. This is a discipline in three parts. People skip the third part, and it is the part that matters. If you do not correct the record, you let a distortion stand.
- Questions are the attack vector. So the defense is a track record, not a rebuttal. A company with ten years of kept promises does not have to answer whether it keeps promises.
- A precise original statement is a defense. Observation 2 is the argument. The distortion of the serpent worked because the original was long enough to paraphrase. Short promises that people can check are harder to distort than long, qualified ones.
- Drift is a separate failure from attack. Observation 4 matters. The serpent did not cause the addition that the woman made to the rule. Organizations drift from their own stated commitments without any adversary, through repetition and paraphrase.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is damage to reputation that evidence cannot repair, because the damage is to the willingness to believe evidence. The bill comes as a permanent discount on everything the company says. It shows up as higher due diligence costs, longer sales cycles, and terms that assume bad faith.

#### Extending verses

- Matthew 4:3-6: the same attack, made against Jesus, and answered from the text: "It is written." Note that the second temptation quotes Scripture (Psalm 91). The attack uses the text, not only works against it.
- John 8:44: "...When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his own: for he is a liar, and the father of it."
- Proverbs 30:5-6: "Every word of God is pure... Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar." Read the addition of the woman in the light of this proverb.
- 2 Peter 3:3-4: "...there shall come in the last days scoffers... And saying, Where is the promise of his coming?" The attack on the promise is the same attack, repeated.
- Genesis 3:4: the escalation, from a question to a flat denial.

### A23. Deception works by denying consequence and promising autonomy

Grounding text, Genesis 3:4-5:

> 4 And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die:
>
> 5 For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.

#### Observations

1. Text states (two levers, both present):
   - (a) Consequence denied: "Ye shall not surely die".
   - (b) Autonomy promised: "ye shall be as gods".
2. Text states: A causal word joins the two levers: "For God doth know that...". The serpent presents the denial of consequence as the reason that the autonomy is available. The structure is: nothing bad will happen, and you will be free.
3. Text states: The claim of autonomy is specific about the mechanism: "your eyes shall be opened... knowing good and evil." It is not a vague promise of improvement. It is a claim about a new capability.
4. Text states: The claim of autonomy also gives a motive to the present authority: "God doth know". It implies that God holds something back. The offer of freedom comes with an accusation against the one who set the boundary.
5. Text states: Both levers are false. The consequence comes (3:16-19). The autonomy comes as expulsion from the garden (3:23-24), followed by a new set of masters.

#### Derivation

Inference: Both levers appear together in the first deception. So an opportunity presented with no downside is being sold to you. An offer of pure autonomy always hides a new master.

The second sentence comes from observation 5. In the real event, the promised autonomy produced subjection to a curse. The offer was not freedom from authority. It was a change of authority. That is why "hides a new master" is the right phrase.

These two signals are the fastest available test for a bad deal, a bad hire, or a bad partner. You can check both in one conversation. What is the downside? Who holds authority over me afterward?

#### Business consequences

- A pitch with no downside has a hidden one. Every real opportunity has a cost, a risk, or a trade. A pitch that names none of them is not optimistic. It is incomplete, and the omission gives it away.
- "You will be your own boss" is a claim about a new boss. The offer of autonomy is suspicious by its structure, because autonomy is never absolute. Ask: authority over what, and under whose terms?
- Apply the test to hires and partners, not only to deals. A candidate who describes the last three roles as the fault of everyone else uses lever (a). A partner who proposes a structure in which nothing limits him uses lever (b).
- Apply it to yourself. The most dangerous version is the deal that you pitch. If your own deck has no downside and no limits, you are running the same play.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is the bad deal that looked clean. The bill comes as an obligation that nobody priced: the personal guarantee, the lock-in, the new controlling shareholder, or the covenant that triggers.

#### Extending verses

- Proverbs 14:12: "There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death."
- 2 Timothy 3:13: "But evil men and seducers shall wax worse and worse, deceiving, and being deceived."
- 2 Corinthians 11:13-15: "For such are false apostles, deceitful workers, transforming themselves into the apostles of Christ. And no marvel; for Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light." The presentation is attractive, and that is the mechanism.
- 1 Timothy 6:9: "But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition." Note "snare": a trap that looks like an opportunity.
- Genesis 3:6: the evaluation of the woman herself: "good for food... pleasant to the eyes... to be desired to make one wise". She accepted the pitch on all three channels (A24).

### A24. Desire runs on three channels

Grounding text, Genesis 3:6:

> 6 And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.

#### Observations

1. Text states (three separate clauses):
   - (a) "good for food": appetite.
   - (b) "pleasant to the eyes": beauty.
   - (c) "to be desired to make one wise": status or standing.
2. Text states: The three come in sequence, as a chain of "and". The evaluation is compound, not single.
3. Text states: The evaluation comes before the act. "And when the woman saw... she took". The assessment on three channels produced the decision.
4. Inference (the New Testament echo): 1 John 2:16 says: "For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world." Inference: the three categories line up with the three clauses of Genesis 3:6, in the same order. Flesh matches appetite, eyes match beauty, and the pride of life matches status. John does not cite Genesis 3. The match is a reading, not a statement of either text. If you accept it, it is the strongest available support that the threefold structure is deliberate.
5. Text states: The act is shared: "and gave also unto her husband with her". The decision spread at once.

#### Derivation

Inference: People decide every purchase, hire, and commitment on all three channels at once. So if you appeal only to need, you lose to whoever appeals to beauty. If you satisfy need and beauty but ignore status, you lose to whoever offers standing.

The derivation rests on observation 2. The three clauses are joined, and all three are present in one evaluation. A seller who addresses one channel competes against sellers who address three.

It works in both directions, and that is the most useful property of the axiom. It is the diagnostic for what you sell. It is also the diagnostic for how someone sells a bad decision to you. The same three questions apply to your own product and to the deal on your desk.

#### Business consequences

- Product, brand, and positioning are three channels, not one. Feature parity plus good design plus no status story loses to a worse product with all three.
- Status is the channel that B2B sellers neglect most often. "It works and it is cheap" addresses appetite only. The standing of the buyer inside the buyer's own organization is channel (c).
- Use it as an audit of your own decisions. Before you sign, ask which channel does the work here. If the honest answer is (b) or (c), you did not do the analysis.
- It explains why buyers reject good deals. A better offer that addresses only appetite loses to a worse offer that also makes the buyer look good.
- It is not a license to manipulate. The rule in `scripture-foundations` applies: "Never use Scripture to justify harm, exploitation, or the overriding of the conscience of another person." The three channels describe how people make decisions. They do not authorize you to exploit the weaknesses that they show.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is losses to worse products. The bill comes as a repeated pattern of losses to competitors that the team thinks are inferior, with no clear explanation. Inside the company, the explanation blames the irrationality of the customer, not the incompleteness of the offer.

#### Extending verses

- 1 John 2:16: the three categories, named. See the inference in observation 4.
- James 1:14-15: "But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death." The mechanism is desire, then act, then consequence.
- Joshua 7:21: the account of Achan follows the same sequence: "When I saw among the spoils a goodly Babylonish garment, and two hundred shekels of silver, and a wedge of gold of fifty shekels weight, then I coveted them, and took them..." He saw, he coveted, he took.
- Proverbs 6:25: "Lust not after her beauty in thine heart; neither let her take thee with her eyelids." This names channel (b) as a channel.
- 1 Timothy 6:9-10: the desire for riches is the mechanism, not the riches.

### A25. Undisclosed failure produces hiding and blame

Grounding text, Genesis 3:10 and 3:12:

> 10 And he said, I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself.
>
> 12 And the man said, The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat.

#### Observations

1. Text states (the chain): Fear, then hiding, then blame. Verse 10 gives the first two, and verse 12 gives the third. The sequence is explicit.
2. Text states: The hiding is physical and specific. Adam and his wife "hid themselves... amongst the trees of the garden" (3:8). Concealment is an action, not a feeling.
3. Text states: The man pushes the blame in two directions at once. He blames the woman ("she gave me") and God ("The woman whom thou gavest to be with me"). The second shows more. He blames the design for the failure.
4. Text states (the contrast): Earlier, Adam made a different kind of statement about the same person: "This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh" (2:23). Now he describes the same relationship as a liability.
5. Inference: The concealment cost more than the failure alone. Text states: verse 7 has the fig leaves, verse 8 has the hiding, and in verse 21 God makes coats. Inference: God covered the failure anyway, at his own cost, after the concealment added fear and blame to it.
6. Text states (the contrast in Proverbs): Proverbs 28:13 says: "He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy."

#### Derivation

Inference: Shame leads to concealment, concealment leads to blame, and blame destroys the ability to learn. So the report of a failure must be cheap, and the person who reports must survive it. A culture that punishes the messenger pays for the silence later.

The engine of the derivation is the last point of observation 3: the blame of the design. Once someone blames the system and not the decision, nothing is left to learn, because no decision is left to examine. The chain of blame is not only unfair. It ends all learning.

Blame is not accountability. This distinction is the sharpest contribution of the axiom. Accountability is owning your part without pushing it onto others. Blame is the pushing. An organization that demands "accountability" while it rewards blame gets more blame and less accountability.

#### Business consequences

- Make the report of a failure cheap, and make sure that the reporter survives it. You need both halves. A cheap report without survival is an interrogation.
- Separate the failure from the report, in plain words and in public. The first person to report a serious problem in front of the whole company sets the price of reporting for the next year.
- Watch for blame aimed at the design. "The process was wrong," "the market changed," and "leadership set impossible targets" are sometimes true. They are always a signal that the speaker did not examine his own part. Both claims can be true. The question is whether the speaker names his own part.
- Note that concealment has a cost. See observation 5. The failure was going to be covered. The concealment only added fear, blame, and a longer delay.

#### Failure mode when violated

The anti-pattern table says: if you punish the bearer of bad news, you discover problems late, and the discovery is worse.

The mechanism is a delay, not a denial. Nobody decides to hide a problem forever. People decide to hide it until they fix it. That is exactly the interval in which the organization was able to help.

#### Extending verses

- Proverbs 28:13: the contrast between covering and confessing.
- Psalm 32:3-5: the physical cost of concealment and the relief of confession. "When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long... I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid."
- 1 John 1:9: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness."
- Acts 5:1-11: Ananias and Sapphira. The failure was the concealment, not the holding back of money. Peter says it in plain words: "Whiles it remained, was it not thine own? and after it was sold, was it not in thine own power?" (5:4). The lie was the sin.
- Joshua 7: the concealment of Achan and its cost to the whole camp.

### A26. Diagnose before you judge

Grounding text, Genesis 3:9, 3:11, and 3:13:

> 9 And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?
>
> 11 And he said, Who told thee that thou wast naked? Hast thou eaten of the tree, whereof I commanded thee that thou shouldest not eat?
>
> 13 And the LORD God said unto the woman, What is this that thou hast done? And the woman said, The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat.

#### Observations

1. Text states (the sequence): The questions come in order:
   - "Where art thou?" (location).
   - "Who told thee?" and "Hast thou eaten?" (source and act).
   - "What is this that thou hast done?" (act, to the second party).

   The order is location, then source, then act.
2. Inference: The questions are open in form. They are not accusations phrased as questions. The text does not say that God lacked the information. A God who knows all things does not ask in order to learn. Inference: the questions give each person the chance to give an account, and they bring the facts into the open before the verdict.
3. Text states: The judgment comes after the man and the woman answer. God pronounces the curses in verses 14-19. Before that, he questions the man and the woman. He does not question the serpent. In 3:14 God curses the serpent at once, with no question and no answer.
4. Text states: God questions the man first and then the woman. Each one gives a separate account. The serpent gets no hearing.
5. Text states: The questions do not change the outcome. The blame that Adam pushes onto others does not prevent the curse. Inference: diagnosis is for accuracy, not for leniency. The purpose is a correct verdict, not a lighter one.

#### Derivation

Inference: The first response to failure is a sequence of questions that establish location, source, and act. So establish the facts before you assign responsibility, and keep the two questions separate. A judgment that runs ahead of the diagnosis produces the wrong verdict and teaches people to hide.

The last sentence connects to A25. A wrong verdict is a cost. A wrong verdict that people can see is wrong is worse. It teaches the organization that the process is arbitrary and that concealment is rational.

Text states (the support):

Proverbs 18:13:

> 13 He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.

Proverbs 18:17:

> 17 He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him.

John 7:51:

> 51 Doth our law judge any man, before it hear him, and know what he doeth?

Deuteronomy 1:16-17:

> 16 And I charged your judges at that time, saying, Hear the causes between your brethren, and judge righteously between every man and his brother, and the stranger that is with him.
>
> 17 Ye shall not respect persons in judgment; but ye shall hear the small as well as the great; ye shall not be afraid of the face of man; for the judgment is God's...

#### Business consequences

- Ask, then weigh. The two activities are separate, and they must come in order.
- Ask about location first. "Where art thou?" is not a rhetorical question. It establishes the facts of the situation before anything else. In a post-mortem, the equivalent is: what really happened, and when did we know?
- Take each account separately. See observation 4. In a group post-mortem where everyone speaks in front of everyone, people perform. The Genesis pattern is one person at a time, then the group.
- Diagnosis is not leniency. Observation 5 is important, and readers often lose it. The purpose of diagnosis is a correct verdict, and a correct verdict can be harsh.
- A failure to diagnose is a failure of management, not of a person. If a manager pronounces a verdict before hearing, the wrong verdict is the fault of the manager. So is the culture of hiding that follows.

#### Failure mode when violated

The anti-pattern table says: judging without diagnosing leads to wrong verdicts and to a team that hides.

The secondary failure is the loss of the ability to diagnose. An organization that punishes accurate reports learns to produce inaccurate ones. Then it cannot tell what is happening.

#### Extending verses

- Proverbs 18:13 and 18:17: above.
- John 7:51: above.
- Deuteronomy 1:16-17: above.
- Deuteronomy 19:15: "One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity, or for any sin, in any sin that he sinneth: at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established." The law requires more than one account.
- 1 Timothy 5:19: "Against an elder receive not an accusation, but before two or three witnesses." The rule applies to leaders too.

### A27. Friction is structural

Grounding text, Genesis 3:17-19:

> 17 And unto Adam he said, Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree, of which I commanded thee, saying, Thou shalt not eat of it: cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life;
>
> 18 Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the herb of the field;
>
> 19 In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the ground; for out of it wast thou taken: for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.

#### Observations

1. Text states: The ground is cursed "for thy sake". The resistance is aimed at the worker, and the text says that it is for him. This is a strange phrase. Notice it. Do not smooth it over.
2. Text states: The ground still produces. It is not barren. It brings forth "Thorns also and thistles" in addition to the herb of the field. The yield continues, and the ratio changes.
3. Text states (duration): "all the days of thy life." The resistance is permanent. It is not a phase, a consequence that expires, or a condition that better technique removes.
4. Text states: The verse names the effort: "In the sweat of thy face". The cost is exertion, not only poor outcomes.
5. Text states: The curse slows the yield. It does not stop it. The ground resists. It does not refuse. Effort still yields bread, but not the bread that it yielded before the curse.
6. Text states (the promise inside the curse): The man still eats the herb of the field. The curse changes the terms of the exchange. It does not cancel the exchange.

#### Derivation

Inference: The environment resists, and the resistance is permanent. So every plan must price the thorns. Name the specific friction as a line item, not as a surprise. Examples are churn, compliance cost, the drag on recruiting, and technical debt. The ROI (return on investment) must cover the weeds. A model that works only in ground with no curse is not a model.

The engine of the derivation is observation 3. If friction were temporary, a plan can reasonably assume that it will clear. It lasts "all the days of thy life". So a plan that leaves it out is a plan for a world that does not exist.

Observation 2 is the second half, and it prevents overcorrection. The ground still yields. A plan that prices the thorns and then assumes that nothing will grow is equally wrong. The correct model is: the yield is real, and the ratio is worse than the design.

#### Business consequences

- Price the friction, with numbers where you can. `SKILL.md` step 5 asks: what will resist this, what will it cost each month, and what is the plan for it?
- Name the specific thorn. A generic risk register is useless. "Churn runs at 1.8% a month, and the replacement cost is $14,000 for each seat" is a priced thorn.
- The ROI must cover the weeds. A margin model that leaves out the cost of friction is a model of a different business.
- Do not confuse the thorn with a broken thing. The resistance of A27 is expected and permanent. The pain of delivery in A28 is different again. The skill is to tell them apart.
- Watch for friction that is invisible because it is spread out. Churn, the overhead of coordination, and the cost of switching context are real costs. They never appear on one line, because they are spread across everything.

#### Failure mode when violated

The anti-pattern table says: if you assume a stable baseline, you get "unexpected" churn, cost, and delay every quarter.

The typical symptom is an organization that has a story for each miss and no rate for the class of miss. Once you name the rate, you can plan for it. Until then, every quarter is a surprise.

#### Extending verses

- Matthew 7:24-27: the storm is not a punishment for the bad builder. Both houses get the same storm. The difference is the foundation.
- 1 Corinthians 3:12-15: "Every man's work shall be made manifest: for the day shall declare it, because it shall be revealed by fire; and the fire shall try every man's work of what sort it is." The test comes to everyone.
- James 1:2-4: "My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing." Note that the text assumes the trial. It is not an exception. And the process must run to completion (A14).
- Ecclesiastes 10:10: the blunt iron. Resistance is a property of the tool, and sharpening manages it.
- Romans 8:22: "For we know that the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now." The resistance covers all creation. It is not local.

### A28. Fruitfulness continues, but delivery costs

Grounding text, Genesis 3:16:

> 16 Unto the woman he said, I will greatly multiply thy sorrow and thy conception; in sorrow thou shalt bring forth children; and thy desire shall be to thy husband, and he shall rule over thee.

#### Observations

1. Text states (the asymmetry): "I will greatly multiply thy sorrow and thy conception". God multiplies both. The output increases, and the pain increases. The curse does not reduce fruitfulness. It multiplies both terms.
2. Text states: The pain is at the delivery: "in sorrow thou shalt bring forth children". It is not in the conception or in the existence of the child. It is in the bringing forth.
3. Text states: The output still comes. Children are still born. The text does not say that the fruit is lost.
4. Text states (the New Testament reading of it): John 16:21 says: "A woman when she is in travail hath sorrow, because her hour is come: but as soon as she is delivered of the child, she remembereth no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world." Text states: Jesus uses this as the model for the grief of the disciples that turns to joy. The pain is real and limited in time, and the joy comes with the delivery.
5. Text states (the pattern applied to work): Galatians 4:19 says: "My little children, of whom I travail in birth again until Christ be formed in you". Paul applies the same image to the work of formation.

#### Derivation

Inference: The output still comes, and the pain is at the bringing forth. So pain is not evidence that a course is wrong, because every real thing has a hard delivery. But pain is also not a license to ignore the signal. Tell the pain of producing apart from the pain of a broken thing. The first is the cost of the work. The second is information.

The derivation turns on observation 2, the location of the pain. Pain at delivery is structural. The curse does not describe pain at other points. So pain at other points needs a different explanation.

#### Business consequences

- Hard launches, hard fundraises, hard hires, and hard migrations are normal. They are the travail. An organization that reads difficulty as a signal to stop will not finish anything.
- But a specific pain that you can diagnose is information. The test is whether the pain is at the delivery or somewhere else. Pain in the middle of a process that must be routine is not travail. It is a symptom.
- Do not use A28 to justify grinding. The axiom says that delivery costs. It does not say that the cost has no limit or that every cost is legitimate. That is the discipline of A27: price the thorns, and if the price is more than the yield, the model is wrong.
- The joy comes with the delivery. John 16:21 says it in plain words: the anguish is forgotten "as soon as she is delivered". This is an argument for finishing (A14). Unfinished travail is pain with no event that makes up for it.

#### Failure mode when violated

There are two opposite failures:

- Quitting at the travail: you abandon things that work because they are hard.
- Grinding through a broken thing: you endure pain that is a symptom, not travail, and you call the endurance a virtue.

The question that tells them apart is the question of A28: is this the pain of producing, or the pain of a broken thing? The first has a delivery date and a known shape. The second does not.

#### Extending verses

- John 16:21: travail and joy, in one sentence.
- Galatians 4:19: travail as formation.
- Acts 14:22: "...that we must through much tribulation enter into the kingdom of God."
- 1 Peter 1:6-7: "...though now for a season, if need be, ye are in heaviness through manifold temptations: That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory..." Note "if need be" and "for a season". The pain is limited and has a purpose.
- Hebrews 12:11: "Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby." Note "afterward", and note "exercised". The yield depends on going through the process, not only on the process happening.

### A29. Authority gets contested

Grounding text, Genesis 3:16:

> 16 ...and thy desire shall be to thy husband, and he shall rule over thee.

#### Observations

1. Text states: The statement comes after the fall. It is part of the declaration of consequences. It describes a change, not the original design. In Genesis 2 the counterpart is an _ezer kenegdo_, a corresponding strength, and the text uses no language of rule.
2. Text states: The verse names two things together: a desire directed at the other, and a rule over the other. Inference: they are in the same sentence, which suggests that they are related.
3. Contested: The phrase "thy desire shall be to thy husband" is truly difficult. Faithful readers differ on whether it describes longing, a desire to control, or something else. Lexical: the word is H8669 תְּשׁוּקָה (_teshuqah_), "a longing". The same construction appears in Genesis 4:7 ("And unto thee shall be his desire"). I will not invent certainty here. The text is not clear enough to settle it, and the axiom does not depend on the answer.
4. Text states: Whatever the exact sense, the verse pairs a desire with a rule. Inference: the result is a hierarchy that becomes a place of struggle. The later accounts show many contests over authority and standing. Sarai and Hagar, Rachel and Leah, and Hannah and Peninnah are rivalries between women in the same household. They are not contests between a husband and a wife, so they do not illustrate this clause directly. They show only that contest marks relationships after the fall in general.
5. Text states (the New Testament correction): The New Testament does not abolish authority. It redefines how authority works. Mark 10:42-45 and 1 Peter 5:1-4 both name the pagan model ("exercise lordship", "being lords over God's heritage") and forbid it.

#### Derivation

Inference: After the fall, hierarchy becomes a place of struggle, not a smooth order. So expect the contest and design for it. Authority that service does not make legitimate becomes domination. Domination produces resistance, not followers.

The derivation gets its force from the word "expect". The verse describes what is, not what ought to be. A leader whom resistance surprises misread the environment, in the same way that a plan that ignores thorns misreads the ground (A27).

Text states (the correction):

Mark 10:42-45:

> 42 But Jesus called them to him, and saith unto them, Ye know that they which are accounted to rule over the Gentiles exercise lordship over them; and their great ones exercise authority upon them.
>
> 43 But so shall it not be among you: but whosoever will be great among you, shall be your minister:
>
> 44 And whosoever of you will be the chiefest, shall be servant of all.
>
> 45 For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.

1 Peter 5:2-3:

> 2 Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind;
>
> 3 Neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being ensamples to the flock.

#### Business consequences

- Expect the contest. People will test authority. That is not a hiring problem or a culture problem. It is the stated condition.
- Service is the mechanism that makes authority legitimate. Both passages give the same alternative. The one who leads serves, and by serving becomes an example, not a lord. This is not a soft recommendation. It is the only alternative to domination that the text offers.
- Domination produces resistance, not followers. The mechanism: a person who complies because he must will comply only while someone watches. Where it is safe, he will work against the arrangement.
- "Being ensamples" is a specific requirement. 1 Peter 5:3 says that the leader is to be an example. An example is something that people can see and copy. So the real behavior of the leader is the instrument of management.
- The disputed phrase does not carry the weight. Observation 3 matters for honesty. The axiom stands on the contest, which is plain. It does not stand on the answer to the difficult clause.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is authority that someone must enforce all the time. The bill comes as compliance without initiative, high turnover in the roles closest to the leader, and an organization that cannot function when the leader is absent.

#### Extending verses

- Mark 10:42-45: above.
- 1 Peter 5:1-4: above.
- John 13:1-17: the washing of feet. "If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you." See `org-and-labor.md`.
- 3 John 1:9-10: "I wrote unto the church: but Diotrephes, who loveth to have the preeminence among them, receiveth us not. Wherefore, if I come, I will remember his deeds which he doeth, prating against us with malicious words: and not content therewith, neither doth he himself receive the brethren, and forbiddeth them that would, and casteth them out of the church." This is the failure case in the New Testament: authority contested, then used to exclude.
- Numbers 16: the rebellion of Korah, the contest in its extreme form.
- 1 Samuel 8: Israel demands a king. It is the contest between the authority that the people requested and the real one.

### A30. Mortality makes time the binding constraint

Grounding text, Genesis 3:19:

> 19 In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the ground; for out of it wast thou taken: for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.

#### Observations

1. Text states: The sentence has an end point: "till thou return unto the ground". An end that the text states as certain limits the working life.
2. Inference: The end point applies to the worker, not to the work. Text states: the man returns to the ground. Inference: the ground stays, so the work outlives the worker. The verse does not say this in plain words.
3. Text states (the pairing): The verse joins labor and mortality in one sentence. They are not separate topics. Inference: the limit on life makes labor a limited quantity.
4. Text states (the response of the wisdom literature): Psalm 90:12 says: "So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom." Text states: the prayer is not for more days. It is for the ability to count them.
5. Text states (the failure case): In Luke 12:16-21 the rich man plans barns with no account of his own end, and God calls him a fool: "But God said unto him, Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee: then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided?"

#### Derivation

Inference: Finite time is the base constraint under every plan. So opportunity cost is the real cost of everything. The correct question is rarely "is this good?" It is "is this the best use of the years it will consume?"

The derivation rests on observation 2: the work outlives the worker. So every commitment moves a slice of a finite life into a thing that will last after the slice is gone. The cost of the commitment is the slice. The question is whether the thing is worth it.

#### Business consequences

- Opportunity cost is the unit of account, not cash and not headcount. You can replace cash. You cannot replace years.
- The real deadline is the one that exists. `SKILL.md` step 1 says: tell the deadline that exists apart from the one that feels urgent. The poor planning of someone else makes most urgency. The anti-pattern: if you decide by urgency, not by deadline, you spend your life on the fires of other people.
- Luke 12:16-21 is the failure mode. The text does not condemn the man for planning or for building. It condemns him for planning with no account of his own end. He speaks to his own soul about a plan with no term: "Soul, thou hast much goods laid up for many years".
- To number your days is a discipline, not a mood. Psalm 90:12 asks for the ability, so it is a skill. The practice is to budget years the way you budget money.
- It applies to the tenure of the founder. A founder who will not name his own end date has the same defect as the rich fool.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is to spend the finite resource on the urgency of someone else. The bill comes as a career that was busy and produced nothing that the person calls his own.

#### Extending verses

- Psalm 90:12: "So teach us to number our days..."
- Psalm 39:4-5: "LORD, make me to know mine end, and the measure of my days, what it is: that I may know how frail I am. Behold, thou hast made my days as an handbreadth..."
- James 4:14: "Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour, that appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away."
- Ephesians 5:15-16: "See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, Redeeming the time, because the days are evil."
- John 9:4: "I must work the works of him that sent me, while it is day: the night cometh, when no man can work."
- Luke 12:15: "...for a man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth."

### A31. Recovery is provided, and it costs something that is not you

Grounding text, Genesis 3:21:

> 21 Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them.

#### Observations

1. Text states: God makes the covering and gives it. "The LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them." The man did not make it and did not ask for it.
2. Text states (the cost): Coats of skins require animals. The text does not say which animals or how many, but it states the material. Inference: the covering required a death that is not the death of the man.
3. Text states (the contrast): In verse 7 the man and the woman "made themselves aprons" of fig leaves. The contrast is explicit: a covering they made themselves, then a covering that God provides. Inference: the provided covering is the one that lasts.
4. Text states (the sequence): The covering comes after the judgment (vv. 14-19) and before the expulsion (v. 23). God gives it to people who already failed and who already received their sentence.
5. Text states: The covering does not depend on anything the man does. The text records no request, no confession, and no penance between verse 20 and verse 21.

#### Derivation

Inference: God covers the failure, and the covering required a life. So failure is survivable, and recovery is never free. Something or someone pays for it. Cash reserves exist to be the thing that pays.

The derivation has two halves, and you need both:

- Failure is survivable. See observation 4. The covering comes after the sentence. A person who already failed still receives clothes.
- Recovery is never free. See observation 2. The coat cost an animal.

The business application follows from the second half. A cash reserve is the thing that pays. Build it before you need it. Treat it as the cost of permission to fail, not as idle capital.

Text states (the doctrinal extension): Scripture states a related pattern for a different kind of covering. It states it first in the law (Leviticus 17:11), and the New Testament states it again (Hebrews 9:22).

Leviticus 17:11:

> 11 For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.

Hebrews 9:22:

> 22 And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.

The business inference is not that the cash reserve of a company is a sacrifice. It is that the structure recurs: covering costs something, and the something is not the person covered. A company that expects recovery to be free misreads the pattern.

#### Business consequences

- Build cash reserves before you need them. A reserve that you gather during the crisis is not a reserve. It is a distress sale.
- Treat the reserve as the cost of permission to fail, not as idle capital. This new view is the contribution of the axiom to the debate about reserves. Cash held for recovery is not underperforming. It does the job that you set it aside for.
- Name what pays: the cash reserve, the insurance policy, the parent company, the customer contract, the patient shareholder. If you name nothing, nothing will pay.
- The covering you make yourself is the first instinct. See observation 3. Fig leaves are what the organization reaches for: a statement, a restructure, a rebrand. They do not last.
- Failure is survivable. This is the counterweight, and you need it. An organization that believes that failure is fatal will hide it (A25). It will also never attempt anything that has a real chance of failure (A11).

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is a failure that nothing covers. Specifically, its cost lands on the wrong party: the supplier, the employee, the customer, or the family. The anti-pattern table does not list A31, but the mechanism shows in A25 and A32.

#### Extending verses

- Isaiah 53:5-6: "But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray..." Another bears the covering.
- 2 Corinthians 5:21: "For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him."
- Romans 5:8: "But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us."
- Psalm 49:7-8: "None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him: (For the redemption of their soul is precious, and it ceaseth for ever:)" The cost is more than the person can pay.
- Leviticus 17:11 and Hebrews 9:22: above.

### A32. Some states must not be allowed to become permanent

Grounding text, Genesis 3:22-23:

> 22 And the LORD God said, Behold, the man is become as one of us, to know good and evil: and now, lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever:
>
> 23 Therefore the LORD God sent him forth from the garden of Eden, to till the ground from whence he was taken.

#### Observations

1. Text states (the stated reason): "lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever". The text explains the expulsion by what it prevents.
2. Text states: The action prevents. It does not punish. God already pronounced the sentence in verses 14-19. The expulsion is a separate act, and its stated purpose is to stop a condition from becoming irreversible.
3. Text states: The condition that God prevents is permanent life in a fallen state. The problem is not the life. It is the permanence.
4. Text states: The prevention is immediate: "Therefore". The text describes no delay between the decision and the act.
5. Text states (the permanence that does apply): Other consequences in this chapter are explicitly permanent: "all the days of thy life" (v. 17), "till thou return unto the ground" (v. 19). Inference: the text separates permanent consequences, which God declares, from permanent states, which God prevents. God puts a gate only on the second.

#### Derivation

Inference: God prevented a bad condition from becoming irreversible. So identify the actions that you cannot undo, and put a gate on them. Examples are irreversible deletions, obligations that grow out of control, contracts with no exit, and architectures that you can never migrate off. Slow these down on purpose. Speed is for reversible decisions.

The derivation rests on observation 1. The text states the reason for the act, and the reason is permanence. A reader who accepts the reason has the principle.

#### Business consequences

- Sort decisions by reversibility, and set the speed to match. This is the operational form of the axiom. For daily management it is the most useful thing in this file. The people closest to a reversible decision must make it fast. Slow down irreversible decisions, escalate them, and review them.
- Name the irreversible set in plain words: deletion of data, publication to customers, signing without an exit, hiring into a role with no way to remove the person, commitments of architecture, public statements, personal guarantees, and issuance of equity.
- Add friction, not prohibition. The text does not say that God destroyed the tree of life. God guarded the way (3:24). The design pattern is access through a gate, not elimination. This connects directly to A33.
- A contract without an exit is an irreversible decision with a signature. The exit clause is the gate.
- The anti-pattern is not "moving fast." It is moving fast on the irreversible set. That is the only place where speed is truly dangerous.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is an irreversible state that someone entered casually. The bill comes as one of these: a commitment that you cannot unwind, a migration that you cannot reverse, a disclosure that you cannot recall, or a deletion that you cannot restore. The same sentence always comes with it: "we did not realize it was permanent."

#### Extending verses

- Hebrews 9:27: "And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment:" Some events happen only once.
- Luke 16:26: "And beside all this, between us and you there is a great gulf fixed: so that they which would pass from hence to you cannot; neither can they pass to us, that would come from thence." This is a state past which no movement is possible.
- Proverbs 4:23: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life." Guard the source.
- Matthew 7:6: "Give not that which is holy unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine, lest they trample them under their feet, and turn again and rend you." This is an irreversible disclosure.
- Revelation 22:11: "He that is unjust, let him be unjust still: and he which is filthy, let him be filthy still: and he that is righteous, let him be righteous still: and he that is holy, let him be holy still." This is a point at which the state is fixed.

### A33. Access is guarded, and the guard is a feature

Grounding text, Genesis 3:24:

> 24 So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.

#### Lexical

- H8104 שָׁמַר (_shamar_): "properly, to hedge about (as with thorns), i.e. guard; generally, to protect, attend to, etc." The KJV renders it "keep." Text states: this is the same verb as the second duty of the man in the garden (Genesis 2:15, "to dress it and to keep it"). The verb for the protective work of the gardener describes the guard at the gate.

#### Observations

1. Text states: The same agent who made the garden places the guard on purpose. The guard did not emerge as a consequence. It is an act.
2. Text states: The guard has a specific function: "to keep the way of the tree of life." Its stated function is not to punish or to frighten. It is to keep a way, an access route.
3. Text states: The guard is active: "a flaming sword which turned every way." The description is of continuous motion. It is not a locked door. It is a watched one.
4. Text states: The guarded thing is the means of life, not the garden as such. God expels the man from the garden (v. 23), but the specific object of the guard is the tree of life.
5. Text states: God sets the guard after the fall (3:24). The cherubim are part of the order that follows the fall. They are not in the order of Genesis 1-2. Inference: God himself sets this access control. So it is not only a human reaction to a fallen society.

#### Derivation

Inference: God himself sets a guard on access after the fall. So security and access control are part of a God-given order in a fallen world, not only a reaction by suspicious people. Inference (wider, and less certain): permissions, locks, and limits belong in the design.

The text shows the guard after the fall, not in the good order of Genesis 1-2. Any claim that access control belongs to the original good order goes past the text. Hold it only as an inference. One point supports it: the job in 2:15 already includes _shamar_, "to keep it", before the fall (A17).

The derivation has two halves.

First, a guard is not a symptom of distrust. See observation 5. The maker placed the guard, and the text states its function positively: to keep a way. A company that treats access control as an insult to its people misreads the model.

Second, the real permanent cost of failure is lost access. See observation 4, and the phrase itself: "to keep the way". What was lost in Genesis 3 was not mainly comfort or a garden. It was the way in. Guard the way back in: to the garden, the market, the account, the trust.

#### Business consequences

- Permissions, locks, and limits belong in the design. They are design elements, not extras that you add after an incident.
- The guard is active, not passive: "a flaming sword which turned every way." A static permission that someone granted once and never reviewed is not a guard.
- Name the guarded asset. What is the tree of life in your business? It can be the production database, the signing keys, the customer list, the brand, or the ability to ship. Guard that, specifically.
- Lost access is the permanent cost. Trust, once lost, is a closed way. That is why A22 matters so much: reputation is an access route.
- The guard is _shamar_, the same word as "keep" in A17. Building and keeping are the same duty (A17), and keeping includes the guard on access. The two axioms are one instruction seen from two angles.

#### Failure mode when violated

The failure is open access in the name of speed or trust. The bill comes as an incident, and then as a second bill: the loss of the access itself. Fixing the first problem cannot restore it.

#### Extending verses

- Matthew 7:6: "Give not that which is holy unto the dogs..."
- Revelation 22:14-15: "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city. For without are dogs, and sorcerers..." Access is a right in plain words, and a gate controls it in plain words.
- Proverbs 4:23: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."
- Matthew 25:10-12: "...and the door was shut... But he answered and said, Verily I say unto you, I know you not." Access can close permanently.
- 1 Corinthians 9:24-27: "But I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection: lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway." Guard your own access.
- Nehemiah 2:20: "...but ye have no portion, nor right, nor memorial, in Jerusalem." Nehemiah denies access in plain words to parties with no standing.

### A34. There is a permanent adversary, and the victory is asymmetric

Grounding text, Genesis 3:15:

> 15 And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.

#### Lexical

- H7779 שׁוּף (_shuph_): "properly, to gape, i.e. snap at; figuratively, to overwhelm". KJV: "break, bruise, cover." This is the verb "bruise" in Genesis 3:15, for both strikes.
- G4937 συντρίβω (_syntribo_): "to crush completely, i.e. to shatter (literally or figuratively)". This is the verb "bruise" in Romans 16:20. It is a different word from _shuph_, in a different language.

#### Observations

1. Text states: God sets up the enmity: "I will put enmity". It is not a natural rivalry that arose. God declares it.
2. Text states: The enmity is ongoing and passes through generations: "between thy seed and her seed". It is a permanent feature, not a single event.
3. Text states (the asymmetry): The verse has two strikes, at two different targets: head and heel. Inference: the targets differ in severity. A strike to the head is decisive. A strike to the heel is painful and survivable.
4. Text states: The one who receives the strike to the heel delivers the strike to the head. The same sentence assigns both. The wound and the victory are connected.
5. Text states: God speaks the verse to the serpent, in the middle of the judgments. Inference: many readers take it as the first promise in Scripture. Text states: God addresses it to the enemy, not to the man.
6. Text states (the New Testament identification): Revelation 12:9 says: "And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world". Romans 16:20 says: "And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly." Lexical: the verb in Romans 16:20 is _syntribo_ (G4937), not the Hebrew _shuph_ (H7779) of Genesis 3:15. Inference: Romans 16:20 echoes the image of Genesis 3:15, with a crushed enemy under the feet. It is not the same verb.

#### Derivation

Inference: Conflict is not a phase. The promised win is to the head, and the cost is to the heel. So competition and opposition last. They are not a passing stage. The long game is the only game.

The engine of the derivation is observation 3. If the two strikes were equal, the instruction is to trade blows. They are not equal. So the strategy is to accept the wound to the heel and to aim at what is decisive. It is a statement about what to absorb and what to attack.

Inference, by analogy only: Genesis 3:15 is a promise about the seed of the woman and the serpent. It is not an instruction about business competitors. The analogy is about where to aim. Aim at the problem, at the root cause, not at a person or a competitor. This fits observation 6 and the rule below: a competitor is not the enemy.

#### Business consequences

- Opposition is permanent. A business that expects competition to end, after the next release, the next raise, or the next regulation, misread the environment. No configuration of the market makes opposition stop.
- Expect the wound to the heel. To be copied, undercut, criticized, sued, and misrepresented is the price. These are not evidence that the strategy is wrong. A strategy that produces no opposition is either trivial or already dead.
- Inference, by analogy: aim at the head of the problem. The heel is survivable, and the head is decisive. In business terms, attack the thing that decides whether the problem continues: the root cause in your own economics, distribution, or key constraint. Do not attack a competitor, and do not spend effort on what only annoys you.
- Do not confuse the adversary with a person. Ephesians 6:12 says in plain words: "For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places." A competitor is not the enemy. If you treat a competitor as the enemy, you get bad strategy and bad ethics.
- The long game is the only game. See observation 2: the enmity passes through generations. A plan that requires the conflict to end within a quarter is not a plan.

#### Failure mode when violated

There are two opposite failures, and both are common:

- Expecting opposition to end. The bill comes as a strategy built on a temporary advantage that someone assumed was permanent.
- Trading blows at the wrong level. You answer in kind: you match a price cut or answer a smear. That is an exchange of strikes at the heel, in a contest that is decided at the head.

#### Extending verses

- 1 Peter 5:8: "Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour:"
- Revelation 12:9: the identification of the serpent.
- Ephesians 6:12: not flesh and blood.
- Matthew 10:16: "Behold, I send you forth as sheep in the midst of wolves: be ye therefore wise as serpents, and harmless as doves." Both qualities at the same time.
- 2 Corinthians 2:11: "Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices." You can know the devices, so you can prepare the defense.
- 1 John 3:8: "...For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil." The strike to the head, stated.
- Romans 16:20: "And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly."
